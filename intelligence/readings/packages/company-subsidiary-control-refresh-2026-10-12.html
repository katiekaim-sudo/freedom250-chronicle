# Who actually owns and controls the companies in the October restart

<!-- plain-english-entry:start -->
## In plain English

**Question:** Which parents, subsidiaries, banks, operating companies,
regulated firms and temporary merger vehicles actually hold the powers behind
the company names that appeared in the October government and money news?

**Answer:** The brand is almost never the whole legal actor. American Express
was reached through three companies and two regulators. Evernorth's merger
closed, but Ripple is a minority shareholder rather than its parent. Securitize
Stocks are entitlements held through Securitize Markets, not direct shares on
the issuers' books. Isabella has every approval for Grand River but the banks
remain separate until the November 2 closing. Vistra's parent, nuclear owner
and nuclear operator are separate, and DOE has not named the loan borrower.

**Evidence checked through:** 2026-10-12T17:20:00-04:00

The detailed record keeps ownership, management, licence, supervision,
contract, custody, recordkeeping and product operation as different edges.

## Governing edge types

| Edge | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| wholly owned subsidiary | legal equity control | ownership of every subsidiary asset by the parent |
| beneficial ownership | voting/investment power under the cited measure | operational management or subsidiary status |
| sole managing member | decision control under an LLC agreement | ownership of every economic unit |
| regulator/supervisor | legal supervisory perimeter | business ownership |
| licence/registration | permission for one legal entity and activity | permission for affiliates or every product |
| contract/custody | named duty under one agreement | corporate control or final settlement |
| partnership/integration | commercial or technical cooperation | ownership, guarantee or completed operation |
| merger approval | regulatory permission to close | legal closing or systems conversion |

## 1. American Express: three legal persons, two federal supervisors

The October 8 orders resolve the group structure relevant to the enforcement:

```text
American Express Company — registered bank holding company
  -> owns and controls American Express Travel Related Services Company, Inc.
       — itself a registered bank holding company
       -> owns American Express National Bank
            — national bank / insured depository institution
```

The Federal Reserve supervises the two holding companies and ordered board,
enterprise BSA/AML and TRS OFAC plans. OCC supervises the national bank and
ordered the bank-level compliance committee and action plan while imposing the
$350 million penalty. The parent order reaches enterprise risk management; it
does not turn the parent or TRS into the bank. The bank's deposits, cards,
customers and SAR duties remain bank-level objects.

Primary sources: [Federal Reserve order](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/files/enf20261008a1.pdf) · [OCC bank order](https://occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-occ-2026-87a.pdf)

## 2. Amidi / Plug and Play / Noematrix: the named penalty leaves one fund unnamed

Treasury penalized **Amidi, LLC**. It says an unnamed controlled foreign entity,
described as a Chinese fund, invested about $92,478 in **Shanghai Qiongche
Intelligent Technology Company Limited (Noematrix)**. Treasury separately says
Amidi is the parent of the organization doing business as **Plug and Play Tech
Center**.

The public chain is therefore:

```text
Amidi, LLC
  -> controls unnamed Chinese fund
       -> made minority portfolio investment in Noematrix
  -> parent of organization doing business as Plug and Play Tech Center
```

The penalty belongs to Amidi for a notification failure. The release does not
establish that Plug and Play made the investment, that Amidi owns Noematrix or
that the investment was prohibited. The exact fund name and ownership
percentage remain public gaps.

Primary source: [Treasury OISP penalty](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0652/)

## 3. Isabella / Grand River: approved, scheduled, not yet combined

The October 6 filing says all regulatory approvals and the Grand River
shareholder vote are complete, with closing expected November 2. Until closing,
the two holding companies and two banks remain separate.

The contract sequence is:

```text
Isabella Bank Corporation
  -> owns Isabella Bank
  -> owns temporary 401 Merger Sub, Inc.

Grand River Commerce, Inc.
  -> owns Grand River Bank

at closing:
401 Merger Sub -> merges into Grand River Commerce
Grand River Commerce -> merges into Isabella Bank Corporation
Grand River Bank -> merges into Isabella Bank
```

Regulatory approval is not the merger. The November 2 closing, legal-effective
time, bank-merger effective time and December systems conversion remain
separate receipts.

Primary sources: [October 6 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/842517/000084251726000227/isba_grcxmergerapprovalxex.htm) · [merger agreement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/842517/000084251726000158/isba_mergeragreementxex21.htm)

## 4. Evernorth: Ripple contributed, but does not parent the public company

The October 9 SEC filing proves the business combination closed. **Evernorth
Holdings Inc.** is the Nevada public company. **Pathfinder Digital Assets LLC**
survived as a wholly owned subsidiary, and the former SPAC survived as another
wholly owned subsidiary. Evernorth Holdings is the sole managing member of
Pathfinder and controls its affairs; other unit holders generally hold economic
rights without voting rights.

Ripple was a contributor and is a shareholder, not Evernorth's parent. At
closing the filing reported:

- Ripple Labs: 7.1% of voting Class A;
- Chris Larsen: 9.9% of voting Class A on the filing's beneficial-ownership
  basis, including Ripple/related-party shares;
- SBI Holdings-linked entities: 39.9% of voting Class A and 9.0% of non-voting
  Class C; and
- Arrington XRP Capital Fund: 19.9% of voting Class A and 91.0% of non-voting
  Class C.

The exchange mechanics expressly capped Ripple and affiliates below 9.9% of
combined voting power. Evernorth also has a BitGo Bank & Trust custody
agreement. None of these facts makes Ripple, SBI, Arrington, BitGo or XRP the
issuer of Evernorth common stock or the sole controller of the public company.

Primary source: [Evernorth October 9 Form 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2092592/000119312526419146/d24481d8k.htm)

## 5. Securitize: product operation runs through regulated subsidiaries

SEC filings name **Securitize Corp.** as the public parent and list its
subsidiaries. The October 8 product release says **Securitize Markets, LLC**
holds the Securitize Stocks security entitlements. Holders are not registered
shareholders of Apple, Microsoft or the other underlying issuers unless they
convert where available. The underlying issuers did not sponsor or endorse the
tokens.

The operative product chain is:

```text
Securitize Corp. — public parent
  -> Securitize Markets, LLC — broker-dealer / ATS; holds entitlement layer
  -> Securitize Transfer Agent, LLC — registered transfer-agent subsidiary
  -> other named fund-service, adviser, identity and regional subsidiaries

Securitize Markets entitlement
  -> backed 1:1 by underlying share
  -> token moves on Solana
  -> Jump Trading supplies launch market making
  -> USDC is the announced transaction currency
  -> issuer transfer agent remains the direct-share register
```

The existing Solana PropAMM and Securitize broker-dealer launch are the current
operating route. NYSE's planned ATS, OKXICE TSV, future 24/7 trading, Aave use
and Ripple Prime support are forward-looking. Solana is the network, not the
security issuer, transfer agent or shareholder record.

Primary sources: [October 8 Securitize release](https://investors.securitize.io/news/news-details/2026/Securitize-Launches-Global-Onchain-Trading-of-U-S--Stocks-with-Security-Entitlements/default.aspx) · [SEC subsidiary list](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2094496/000121390026076444/ea029723901ex21-1.htm)

## 6. Vistra nuclear: parent, owner, operator and federal lender are different jobs

Vistra's SEC subsidiary list names **Energy Harbor Nuclear Generation LLC** and
**Vistra Operations Company LLC** separately. NRC records identify Energy
Harbor Nuclear Generation as owner and Vistra Operations as operator for the
PJM nuclear plants following the 2024 transfer. Vistra Corp. is the public
parent. The NRC's future uprate and licence-renewal schedules attach to Vistra
Operations.

DOE's October 5 conditional commitment names Vistra and three plants but does
not publicly identify the borrower, guarantors, plant-specific allocations or
definitive financing documents. It would be wrong to book $4.2 billion to
Vistra Corp., Energy Harbor Nuclear Generation or each plant without the loan
agreement.

Primary sources: [SEC subsidiary list](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1692819/000169281926000006/vistra-20251231xex211.htm) · [NRC transfer notice](https://www.nrc.gov/cdn/doc-collection-news/2023/23-062.pdf) · [DOE conditional commitment](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-announces-42-billion-investment-boost-nuclear-power-and-help-lower)

## 7. SPR exchanges: the brands are named, the contracting subsidiaries are not

DOE names “ExxonMobil” and “BP America” for exchanges up to two million barrels
each. The reached public record does not include the exchange agreements or
exact borrowing/refining legal entities. ExxonMobil's July redomiciliation also
makes brand shorthand hazardous: ExxonMobil Holdings Corporation is the new
successor registrant while Exxon Mobil Corporation remains primary obligor on
the cited notes.

Do not assign the SPR receivable, crude title or premium-barrel duty to a parent
or operating subsidiary until DOE publishes the contracts. Authorization is
real; the exact corporate counterparty remains unresolved.

Primary sources: [DOE exchange announcement](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-authorizes-strategic-petroleum-reserve-exchange-address-supply) · [ExxonMobil redomiciliation filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2115436/000119312526291990/d71068d8k12b.htm)

## Bottom line

The October company story is not “consolidation everywhere.” It is a more
precise pattern:

- supervisors climb from bank to group without merging legal persons;
- merger structures use temporary subsidiaries and staged extinguishment;
- public-company ownership can be concentrated without a contributor becoming
  the parent;
- regulated products run through exact licensed subsidiaries;
- plant ownership, plant operation and federal borrowing remain separate; and
- government announcements often name brands before publishing the legal
  counterparty.

That is the subsidiary constitution the next banking wave must carry forward.
