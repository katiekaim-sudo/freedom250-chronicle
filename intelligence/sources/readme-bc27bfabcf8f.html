Answer

The April administration proposal would raise estimated total federal outlays from $7.540 trillion in FY2026 to $8.093 trillion in FY2027: +$552 billion, or 7.33%. That is an estimate-to-proposal comparison, not final actual spending or the enacted FY2027 budget. Its underlying funding priorities change more sharply than its total: substantial defense expansion alongside cuts to many civilian agencies. OMB policy table 15-1

As of this cutoff, annual FY2027 funding begins under P.L.119-103, signed September 2. It generally continues FY2026 operating rates until replacement appropriations or December 11, with specified exceptions. New starts and defense production increases are restricted subject to exceptions. Mandatory benefits follow their own current-law provisions. Enrolled law, Division A §§101–106,111, signing

The latest OMB publication is the September 4 Mid-Session Review. It updates current-law mandatory outlays to $4.602 trillion → $4.958 trillion (+7.74%), but does not supply replacement overall spending, revenue or deficit totals. Its narrative incorporates legislation and a requested supplemental. We therefore retain the coherent April comparison and show September’s narrower update separately. MSR, pp1–3

What the budget says about The Thesis

The reviewed proposals support a broad program of institutional redesign, with different directions across agencies: consolidated wildfire responsibility, redistributed education functions, reorganized health agencies, private screening delivery and more flexible foreign-policy funding. Education and HHS explicitly question inherited arrangements and invoke repair, confidence or trust. This gives the thesis more support than budget cuts alone would provide.

The inference remains proportional. A proposal reveals intended governing choices; Congress’s response tests acceptance, and operating evidence tests consequences. Medicare’s growing current-law commitments show substantial continuity alongside redesign. NIST illustrates why a large headline decline must be separated from the nonrecurrence of directed projects before judging changes in continuing capacity. Follow the working thesis assessment and each card’s interpretation section for evidence, alternatives and limits.

What the entity chart adds

  • Defense department versus function: the department’s pure discretionary comparison is $857.6B → $1,100B. OMB’s roughly $1.5T national-defense headline also includes other agencies and a $350B mandatory reconciliation request. CBO estimates $15B of that request’s cash outlays in FY2027; the authorization and spending clocks differ.
  • DHS and its components: the enacted Secure America Act provides $69.545B of FY2026 budget authority available through September 30, 2029. Of that, $26.020B belongs to CBP, $38.525B to ICE, and $5B to the Secretary’s additional authorities. Children are included in the parent total. This is additional to other funding and cannot all be labeled FY2027 spending. The law does not enact the separate $350B Pentagon request. S.2 §§101–104,201–203, June 10 signing
  • HHS and successor functions: AHA and other proposed consolidations need comparable old/new structure tables. A blank predecessor row does not mean the function vanished. The AHA comparable amount is reconstructed under the proposed structure, not a separate FY2026 agency appropriation. The budget does not establish a completed legal reorganization. HHS Budget in Brief
  • Agriculture and Interior: OMB notionally restates the earlier comparison for proposed wildfire transfers. Read both owners together; this is not proof of completed transfer. OMB S-3 footnotes
  • Social Security and Medicare: benefit outlays are separate from agency administration and from trust-fund assets. The $12.7B SSA administrative line is not the $1.775T FY2027 Social Security benefits estimate. OMB MSR
  • Treasury: its departmental budget is not the whole government’s spending simply because Treasury disburses payments, and interest is not departmental operating expense.

Interpretation

Katie’s overhaul hypothesis is supported at the level of proposed allocation and some enacted multiyear funding. It is too broad if interpreted as a completed, uniform rewrite of next year’s annual appropriations. The strongest pattern is selective expansion of security/enforcement capacity alongside civilian-program reductions, while large benefit and interest outlays continue to shape the total. Follow the funding authority, receiving entity, obligation and eventual payment before claiming operating results.

When budgets were prepared and presented

FY2027 preparation began in 2025. September 30, 2026 closes FY2026; it is not the agency submission deadline. Scheduled internal deadlines, public releases and hearings are separate clocks.

  • 2025-09-08 — Initial agency submissions to OMB (OMB scheduled deadline): Unless otherwise specified, for agencies subject to Executive Branch review. Actual internal submissions have not been verified. Legislative/judicial bodies, the Federal Reserve Board, USPS and other specified exceptions do not follow this review requirement. OMB A-11: FY2027 preparation schedule
  • September–December 2025 — Strategy and resource planning (Planning schedule): Full draft strategic plan accompanies the budget request; final strategy draft and initial FY2027 performance-plan draft were scheduled for December 3. Performance targets connect to the funding requested. OMB A-11: strategic and performance planning OMB A-11: performance targets and resources
  • Late November 2025 / January 6, 2026 — Passback and finalization (General process / scheduled lock): OMB normally returns decisions around late November. January 6 was the scheduled final agency database lock. These dates do not establish actual FY2027 passback or submission compliance. OMB A-11: FY2027 preparation schedule
  • 2026-02-02 — President’s submission to Congress (Statutory window endpoint): The statute specifies the first Monday in January through the first Monday in February: January 5–February 2 in 2026. 31 USC 1105: President’s budget submission
  • 2026-04-03 — President’s FY2027 request released (Actual submission): CBO identifies April 3 as the administration’s submission date, 60 days after the February 2 window endpoint. CBO analysis of the FY2027 spending proposals
  • Within two weeks after transmission to Congress — Agency congressional justifications (Publication deadline rule): Public posting is measured from each justification’s transmission, not automatically from April 3. OMB clearance precedes congressional transmission; actual agency transmission dates remain to be established. OMB A-11: congressional justification and public posting
  • 2026-10-01 — FY2027 begins (Fiscal-year boundary): Annual funding currently begins under the CR; replacement appropriations or December 11 end its coverage, with statutory exceptions. FY2027 continuing resolution — enacted text

The public examples do not establish a uniform department presentation day: Defense detailed rollout April 21 and Senate hearing April 30; HHS Ways and Means testimony April 16; Treasury Senate FSGG testimony April 22; Education Senate LHHS testimony April 28; GSA congressional justification published April 3. Each card links the agency source and supporting resources. These are selected examples, not exhaustive hearing calendars.

Resources means the documented preparation and justification material: OMB A-11 instructions, strategy, performance plans, prior-year results, program/account estimates, capital and IT submissions where applicable, and congressional justifications. We have not recovered agencies’ private working papers, actual internal submission receipts, every revision, or OMB passback decisions. A public document’s contents do not prove every internal analytical input used.
