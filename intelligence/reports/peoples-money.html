Provenance & audit (Claude, 2026-07-12). Delivered by Katie; audited before ingestion by an independent verification pass against live primary sources. The packet's method is unusually strong — it separates source classes (10-K vs newsroom vs survey vs agreement), and it distinguishes CONFIRMED CHANGE (a proven before/after) from CURRENT TERM (what a live document says today). That discipline is why the audit could find its seams. Corrections below are BINDING on the derived layer; the body is reproduced verbatim.

① BofA — the finding holds, but the window is different and the packet's "June 2" agreement could not be found. The §12 arbitration/mass-arbitration insertion is REAL and independently corroborated without the Internet Archive: the May 18 update PDF carries the clause verbatim, its scope does reach Zelle, RTP, ACH, wires, bill pay and Erica, and a June 4 2026 Senate letter (Blumenthal/Warren/Johnson) plus NCLC and Public Citizen all describe it as newly adopted after ~17 years without pre-dispute arbitration. BUT: the live agreement at the packet's own URL today reads effective March 23, 2026, contains the word "arbitration" zero times, and banners the May 18 update as forthcoming. So (a) the insertion window is Mar 23 → May 18, 2026, TIGHTER than the packet's Jan 31 → May 18, and (b) the packet's "2026-06-02 CONFIRMED CHANGE" (transfer clocks, Deals rename) is unverified — demoted. Do not carry the June 2 delta. ② U.S. Bank AI clause — DOWNGRADED to CURRENT TERM. The clause is real and verbatim as quoted ("we may use any data that is input by you to further train or develop AI models"), on a page last modified 2026-02-20. But the packet's proof that it was introduced in February rests entirely on two Internet Archive snapshots, which the audit could not open. Provable today: the TERM exists, strongly dated to Feb 2026. Not provable: the CHANGE. Treat as a current term with circumstantial dating until someone re-verifies the snapshots. ③ Franklin — "reverse" is too strong. The filing says the transfer agent holds the private key for hosted wallets and that its blockchain-integrated system is "under the unilateral control of the transfer agent," which "has the ability to correct errors." But it also says a blockchain record "can never be deleted" — the transfer agent corrects an unauthorized transaction by appending an offsetting instruction, not by reversing it. Use "correct by offset," never "reverse." (The finding survives intact and is arguably sharper: the token cannot be un-written, and the custodian still wins.) Also: the cited text says only "the Fund's transfer agent" — it never names Franklin Templeton Investor Services. The attribution is probably right; it is not sourced. ④ Two dates are wrong. The Chase Digital Services Agreement is labeled "2025-11-06" in the packet; the live document says last updated June 2026. The AI-agent language is verbatim correct — the date is not. Any inference that Chase had AI-agent terms as early as Nov 2025 is unsupported. And the Synchrony "2026-01-01" effective date is not established; the CareCredit form carries a 3/2026 revision code. ⑤ One misattribution. Discover's move to Virginia governing law is real, but it comes from the cardmember agreement, not the merger FAQ as the packet states. VERIFIED CLEAN: Chase's third-party-AI-agent definitions and disclaimer; Capital One N.A. as legal Discover issuer from 2025-05-18; Synchrony's batches-of-fifty mass-filing procedure; the substance and scope of the BofA clause.

U.S. banking websites — full entity sweep

Updated: 2026-07-11
Status: live full-site reconnaissance; first institutional pass complete
Scope: what major U.S. banking entities say they are building, buying, researching, testing, growing, shrinking, and preparing for across their own public websites.

Companion legal review: Bank user agreements — legal-entity and contract-change map

Historical-document results: Agreement document requests — resolution log

Governing rule

Consumer debt is one thread produced by the sweep. It is not the sweep's organizing frame.

Each bank remains a separate entity portrait until a dated source joins it to another bank, merchant, fintech, regulator, research partner, or infrastructure provider. Product marketing, investor strategy, internal-data research, technical research, and regulatory disclosure are different source classes and must not be blended.

Source classes

Source class What it can establish What it cannot establish alone
Annual report / 10-K / 10-Q balance-sheet direction, segment strategy, booked investment, portfolio performance, formal risk actual customer experience or undisclosed operating intent
Investor day / earnings transcript management targets, capital allocation, growth plan, experiments management chooses to highlight completed delivery of a target
Newsroom / product page launch, partnership, stated customer offer, rollout date adoption, profitability, credit quality
Internal-data research institute observed behavior in the bank's own de-identified transaction or deposit population the whole U.S. population unless benchmarked and weighted
Commissioned survey stated attitudes of the sampled population actual behavior or causation
Technical research / patents capability being explored or protected production deployment in credit decisions
Customer agreement operative price, fee, term, collection, and account rights how often a term is used

Entity universe

Core national and card entities

JPMorgan Chase; Bank of America; Wells Fargo; Citigroup; Capital One / Discover; American Express; Synchrony; Goldman Sachs; Ally; Bread Financial.

Expansion ring

U.S. Bancorp; PNC; Truist; Citizens; CardWorks / Merrick Bank; Cross River Bank; Fifth Third / Comerica; Franklin Templeton.

The expansion ring is included because it contains distinct growth experiments—merchant acquiring, branch buildout, point-of-sale finance, open banking, and subprime portfolio consolidation—that the largest-bank group alone would miss.

A. JPMorgan Chase & Co. / Chase

Verified growth program

  • Consumer & Community Banking served 86.6 million consumers and 7.4 million small businesses in 2025.
  • Chase's share of proprietary-card spend rose to 12%, more than 300 basis points above 2021.
  • Premium and small-business card accounts have grown at a 12% rate since 2019; the bank is refreshing Sapphire and co-brand products and reintroduced Slate with a 21-month introductory APR.
  • Chase agreed to acquire more than $20 billion of Apple Card balances, with an expected transition of about 24 months from January 2026.
  • Management describes branch building, investments, payments, lending innovation, segmented propositions, pricing, personalization, and real-time data as the continuing consumer growth stack.

Primary sources: JPMorganChase 2025 Consumer & Community Banking letter; Chase — Apple Card announcement

Verified research and experimental program

  • The JPMorganChase Institute uses de-identified bank transaction data to study household cash buffers, investments, BNPL, credit-card spending, and small-business behavior.
  • Chase has tested cash-flow underwriting based on customer deposit data for people without a conventional credit history. It reports approving more than 76,000 new-to-credit customers through that path.
  • The Institute is studying BNPL as both convenience and a liquidity valve, including its visibility gap in traditional credit reports and interaction with mortgage borrowers.
  • Consumer Banking says predictive AI/ML is mature in credit, fraud, operations, marketing, sales optimization, pricing, and personalization; agentic systems are the next stated layer.
  • The firm is separately funding behavioral-science and fraud/scam research. These must remain separate from underwriting research.

Primary sources: JPMorganChase — financial health and cash-flow underwriting; Institute — BNPL and household balance sheets; JPMorganChase 2026 company update

Current factual read

Chase is growing by joining a huge physical and deposit franchise to co-brands, premium loyalty, new-to-credit underwriting, real-time personalization, and acquired card books. The debt-modification story is the back end of that broader acquisition-and-engagement machine.

Open watches

Apple Card conversion; Freedom Rise and deposit-data underwriting volume; pricing/personalization AI; agentic-commerce payment rules; branch and affluent-account growth; Institute findings that become products.

B. Bank of America

Verified growth program

  • Bank of America is extending its physical network toward nationwide coverage while keeping digital delivery central.
  • BofA Rewards opened relationship pricing and benefits to any client with an active personal checking account, making more than 30 million additional clients eligible. Eligible card bonuses range from 10% to 75% based on relationship tier.
  • It launched card referrals, Custom Pay Plan purchase-to-installment conversion, and My Credit monitoring in June 2026.
  • The structure is explicit: use checking as the entry account, connect card and Merrill assets, then reward the full relationship rather than one product.

Primary sources: Bank of America — BofA Rewards expansion; Bank of America — referrals, Custom Pay Plan and My Credit; 2026 CEO shareholder letter

Verified research and data program

  • Bank of America Institute's Consumer Checkpoint uses aggregated internal credit-card, debit-card, and deposit data for a near-real-time view of spending, wages, savings, debt paydown, and household buffers.
  • The Institute is explicitly tracking the K-shaped split by income. Its March 2026 report noted rising minimum card payments at the margin; its May report found lower- and middle-income households easing discretionary spending while higher-income households continued to spend.
  • The bank reports more than 8,100 patent filings, 59 million digital users, and more than 3 billion Erica interactions. AI is embedded in consumer service, CashPro, markets, research intelligence, fraud, operations, and internal workforce tools.

Primary sources: Bank of America Institute — Consumer Checkpoint; May 2026 Consumer Checkpoint; Bank of America — technology and AI

Current factual read

BofA is building a relationship-pricing loop: deposit account → rewards tier → card and installment use → Merrill assets → more benefits. Its Institute simultaneously watches the same customer base for stress and spending changes.

Open watches

Custom Pay Plan terms and uptake; referral-driven account growth; Rewards migration; branch openings; whether Institute measures feed credit-line, pricing, or marketing changes; new patent families tied to payments or underwriting.

C. Wells Fargo

Verified growth program

  • The Federal Reserve asset cap is no longer the main constraint; Wells now frames the period as a shift from remediation toward growth.
  • Its spring 2026 investor material reports new credit-card accounts up nearly 60%, consumer checking openings up more than 15%, and auto originations more than doubling year over year.
  • Wells launched eleven card offerings from 2021 through 2025 and is targeting underpenetrated, high-density markets for branch and client growth.
  • It sold or exited twelve businesses while investing in the remaining core franchise.

Primary sources: Wells Fargo — spring 2026 investor presentation; Wells Fargo 2025 annual report

Verified technology/research posture

  • Wells combined the roles of head of AI and co-CEO of Consumer Banking and Lending under Saul Van Beurden, placing AI leadership inside the consumer operating structure.
  • The stated technology path is cloud migration, agile delivery, branch modernization, mobile adoption, and digital product expansion.
  • The first sweep has not yet located a public, transaction-data consumer institute comparable to JPMorganChase Institute or Bank of America Institute. Wells publishes economic and investment research, but that is a different research class.

Primary source: Wells Fargo — Saul Van Beurden role

Current factual read

Wells may be the clearest regulatory-release growth story: it is adding card accounts, checking relationships, and auto production at unusually high rates after years of constraint and business exits.

Open watches

Card product mix and approval bands; branch-market targets; auto underwriting; AI production use; whether growth outpaces controls; second-quarter results following the asset-cap removal.

D. Citigroup / U.S. Consumer Cards

Verified growth program

  • Citi made U.S. Consumer Cards one of five standalone core businesses, separating Retail Banking into Wealth.
  • The card business spans general-purpose cards, private-label cards, and installment lending for roughly 70 million customers.
  • Citi acquired 13 million card accounts in 2025, maintains twelve strategic card partners, and says partner cardholders spend roughly three times more at those merchants than non-cardholders.
  • Citi is targeting affluent customers and younger generations, expanding its Strata family, and deepening card integration into merchant digital channels.
  • Flex Loan and Flex Pay have recorded double-digit growth.

Primary sources: Citi 2026 Investor Day discussion guide; Citi — U.S. Consumer Cards; Citi Branded Cards

Verified research and AI program

  • Citi commissioned a March 2026 survey of 1,000 U.S. adult credit-card decision-makers on credit confidence, desired tools, balance transfers, installment products, and lower-rate cards. This is marketing/attitude research, not observed transaction behavior.
  • Investor Day says new AI risk models are increasing approval rates by 100 basis points; AI personalization and generative-search work is helping digital sales grow 25%.
  • Citi is preparing for agentic commerce in which customer AI assistants select and execute transactions. Keeping Citi cards top-of-wallet inside those agents is an explicit goal.

Primary sources: Citi Consumer Cards Credit Trends Survey; Citi 2026 U.S. Consumer Cards presentation transcript

Current factual read

Citi is using AI not only to cut costs but to approve more accounts, personalize acquisition, lift digital sales, and place its cards inside merchant and future agentic-shopping flows.

Open watches

Approval-rate lift by portfolio; digital-sales baseline; installment balances; new strategic partners; agentic-commerce standards; whether higher lifetime-value acquisition changes risk mix.

E. Capital One / Discover / Brex

Verified growth program

  • Capital One acquired Discover to join card lending, deposits, merchant acceptance, and a proprietary global payment network.
  • The original deal case expected network growth from moving selected Capital One debit and credit volume onto Discover and substantial operating and marketing synergies.
  • Capital One then agreed to acquire Brex for $5.15 billion; the deal closed in 2026. Brex adds corporate cards, spend-management software, real-time payments, and AI agents for finance workflows.
  • The combined direction extends beyond consumer cards: own the issuer, more of the network, merchant relationships, business payments, spend software, and underwriting layer.

Primary sources: Capital One — Discover acquisition rationale; Capital One — Brex acquisition

Verified technical research program

  • Capital One maintains an applied AI/ML research program with university collaborations and published work in explainable AI, graph networks, anomaly detection, NLP, privacy, synthetic data, imbalanced data, customer embeddings, and ML at scale.
  • Its published agenda explicitly includes sequence modeling for credit risk and explainability for credit decisioning.
  • Capital One says research findings feed its model ecosystem; however, a technical paper is not proof that a particular production credit model uses that method.

Primary sources: Capital One — AI and machine learning; Capital One — ML research agenda

Current factual read

Capital One is assembling a vertically linked payments stack: consumer lending + deposits + Discover network + merchant acceptance + Brex business cards and spend control + cloud/AI underwriting and fraud research.

Open watches

Discover-network volume migration; Brex integration; merchant pricing; graph-model production use; consumer/business data joining; new network products; retained versus sold loan books.

F. American Express

Verified growth program

  • Millennial and Gen Z consumers represented roughly 65% of new global consumer-account acquisitions in 2025 and became the largest share of U.S. consumer spending on AmEx.
  • International was its fastest-growing segment, with 13% FX-adjusted spending growth in 2025.
  • AmEx expanded acceptance to more than 170 million merchant locations and uses premium product refreshes, travel, dining, sports, entertainment, rewards, and annual fees to drive acquisition and engagement.
  • First-quarter 2026 card balances and other loans grew 8%; revenue growth included increased spending, fee income, and net interest income from card balances.

Primary sources: American Express — 2026 chairman's letter; American Express 2026 Q1 Form 10-Q

Verified research program

  • Amex Trendex and its travel, expense-management, and business surveys track the aspirations and spending plans of target customers and corporate decision-makers.
  • The 2026 travel report studies intended trip behavior; the Expense Management Trendex surveyed 500 mid-sized-company finance decision-makers about AI and spend control.
  • This research is tightly aligned with product and merchant strategy: it identifies experiences and business workflows around which AmEx can build cards, acceptance, rewards, and expense products.

Primary sources: American Express 2026 Global Travel Trends; Amex Trendex — expense management and AI

Current factual read

AmEx is not chiefly chasing the broadest borrower pool. It is growing a closed-loop data-and-loyalty system around younger premium customers, merchants, experiences, and business expense flows—while also earning more interest as card balances rise.

Open watches

Young-cohort retention and revolving behavior; premium fee changes; sports/experience partnerships; merchant acceptance; card-balance growth; how survey findings become product refreshes.

G. Synchrony

Verified growth program

  • Synchrony serves more than 70 million active accounts and reports more than $180 billion of purchase volume.
  • It expanded Pay Later to more than 6,200 merchant locations in 2025, including Lowe's and JCPenney.
  • Its own data say merchants offering Pay Later beside revolving credit receive at least 10% average sales lift; Pay Later often brings net-new customers who may later migrate into private-label and co-brand cards.
  • It is investing in Walmart/OnePay, Lowe's commercial cards, and Versatile Credit. Pay Later spans Pay in 4 through monthly loans as long as 84 months; e-commerce products explicitly aim to increase purchasing power, conversion, ticket size, and repeat use.

Primary sources: Synchrony 2025 annual report; Synchrony business financing platform; Synchrony Pay Later

Verified research program

  • Synchrony's Major Purchase studies investigate when financing changes purchase size, affordability, and retailer choice.
  • Vertical research is product-linked. Its 2026 VisionIQ study surveyed 2,000 consumers about how cost and financing affect eye-care decisions; CareCredit had more than 12 million open accounts and $40 billion of available credit.
  • These surveys do more than describe the consumer: they identify purchases deferred by price and the places where embedded financing could turn unmet demand into a completed sale.

Primary source: Synchrony / CareCredit — 2026 VisionIQ study

Current factual read

Synchrony's growth funnel is unusually explicit: find a purchase blocked by price → embed instant credit at checkout or provider office → pay the merchant quickly → retain collection risk → migrate the customer from installment to revolving and then co-brand products.

Open watches

Walmart/OnePay launch; merchant conversion lift; customer migration rates; health-care vertical research; deferred-interest mix; Versatile Credit's multi-lender routing; merchant loss-sharing.

H. Goldman Sachs

Verified growth/shrink program

  • Goldman is leaving mass-market card issuance through the planned Apple Card transfer.
  • Its growth is moving toward Global Banking & Markets and Asset & Wealth Management, including private banking, lending, alternatives, financing, and private credit.
  • Goldman should therefore be tracked as both a consumer-lending exit and a producer/distributor of research and capital for the nonbank credit system.

Primary sources: Goldman Sachs — Apple Card transition; Goldman Sachs 2025 annual report

Verified research program

  • Goldman Sachs Research maintains live work on the U.S. consumer, affordability, discretionary cash flow, defaults, private credit, AI, labor, and macro conditions.
  • In May 2026 it cut its estimate of consumer discretionary-cash-flow growth from 5.1% to 3.7%, with only 0.8% expected for the lowest-income quintile.
  • Its private-credit work is watching redemption requests, valuation opacity, software exposure, defaults, and the growing overlap among bank lending, public credit, and private funds.

Primary sources: Goldman Sachs Research — U.S. consumer and inflation; Goldman Sachs — Cracks in Private Credit

Current factual read

Goldman is retreating from holding ordinary card balances while expanding around wealth, markets, and private-credit plumbing. Its research is especially useful for watching where consumer and corporate debt migrates after banks narrow direct lending.

Open watches

Apple Card close; consumer-platform wind-down; private-credit fundraising and stress; bank-to-fund financing; research changes that precede capital allocation.

I. Ally Financial

Verified growth/shrink program

  • Ally sold its card business and stopped new mortgage originations to focus capital on auto finance, digital deposits, corporate finance, insurance, and investment products.
  • In 2025 it originated $43.7 billion of consumer auto loans from a record 15.5 million applications; 43% of originations were in its highest credit-quality tier.
  • Retail auto and corporate-finance assets grew more than 5%; corporate finance reported 35% portfolio growth.
  • Ally Bank reached $144 billion of retail deposits from 3.5 million primary customers, its seventeenth consecutive year of deposit growth.

Primary sources: Ally 2026 proxy; Ally 2025 annual report

Verified technology/research posture

  • Ally publicly describes generative-AI exploration and maintains a technical publication channel, but the first sweep has not yet identified a public underwriting-research program comparable to Capital One's or a transaction-data institute comparable to JPMorgan's.
  • Its most important research surface may be auto applications, dealer channels, used-car pricing, servicing, and loss forecasting embedded in investor disclosures rather than branded public research.

Primary source: Ally — company and technology posture

Current factual read

Ally is concentrating rather than broadly expanding: remove cards and new mortgages, collect deposits digitally, and redeploy capacity into auto and selected corporate assets.

Open watches

Application-to-origination funnel; dealer concentration; credit-tier mix; used-car prices; repossessions; servicing AI; deposit pricing; corporate-finance growth.

J. Bread Financial / Comenity / Bread Pay

The first sweep has not yet found a large branded public research institute. Bread is building embedded credit around ownership ecosystems—not just a store purchase, but the later subscriptions, service, repair, parts, and accessories that keep a consumer spending inside the partner's orbit.

Ford launch and receivables; Gen Z/Millennial growth; partner economics; installment-to-card migration; Comenity securitizations; loyalty and underwriting changes.

P. Cross River Bank / CRB Group

Verified growth program

  • Cross River's public parent is CRB Group, Inc.; Cross River Bank is the regulated bank supplying embedded lending, card, payment and crypto infrastructure behind partner brands.
  • In March 2026 the parent raised $50 million of common equity to expand AI, crypto, embedded finance, international operations and product partnerships. Cross River said it supported more than 100 technology partners.
  • The bank opened a full-service Delaware branch in February 2026 for consumer and small-business credit origination, deposits, check processing and lending.
  • Its capital business is expanding beyond origination infrastructure: 2025–2026 commitments include up to $360 million of Parafin small-business assets, a $100 million facility backed by Point home-equity-investment contracts, and up to $250 million of Figure crypto-backed loans.
  • Cross River has launched stablecoin payments, Request for Payment, international smart-routed payments and new card-processing partnerships.

Primary sources: Cross River — $50 million growth capital; Delaware branch; Figure commitment

Research and experimental program

Cross River does not present a consumer-research institute comparable with JPMorganChase's. Its experiment record is operational: combining lending, payments, cards and crypto on one core, adding an AI layer, enabling 24/7 fiat settlement through Bitcoin infrastructure, and supplying issuing-bank/BIN sponsorship and compliance services to processors and fintechs. These are product and infrastructure claims, not independent findings about consumer behavior.

Primary sources: Cross River stablecoin platform; Cross River / Thredd issuing partnership

Current factual read

Cross River is building both sides of embedded credit: regulated origination/issuing infrastructure and capital facilities that purchase or finance the resulting assets. The consumer-facing fintech may generate demand, while Cross River can appear as bank, infrastructure provider, warehouse lender or receivable buyer.

Open watches

Partner-specific underwriting and servicing roles; forward-flow performance; crypto-backed-loan terms; stablecoin partner approvals; Delaware origination; regulatory orders; and which party owns each loan after origination.

Q. Franklin Resources / Franklin Templeton

Entity boundary

Franklin Templeton is not a retail bank. Franklin Resources, Inc. is the public parent; investment advisers, distributors, registered funds, 529 plans, custodians, transfer agents and specialist managers are separate legal entities. Its inclusion expands the sweep from creditors to institutions managing, financing, securitizing or tokenizing consumer- and business-credit exposure.

Verified growth program

  • Franklin Templeton reported preliminary AUM of $1.79 trillion at June 30, 2026 and $18 billion of long-term net inflows for the quarter.
  • Alternatives and private markets are core growth engines. The March 2026 quarter included $14.3 billion of alternatives fundraising, of which $13.2 billion was private-market assets.
  • Digital-assets AUM reached approximately $1.7 billion in fiscal 2025, up 75% from the beginning of that year; its Benji tokenized money-market platform added intraday-yield and daily-payout functionality.
  • The firm announced the acquisition of 250 Digital's liquid-crypto strategies and the creation of Franklin Crypto. It said the proposed transaction would use BENJI tokens as part of the consideration.
  • Its five-year plan combines organic seed/co-investment capital, acquisitions where organic scale is insufficient, private-wealth expansion, digital assets, ETFs and AI-driven operating leverage.

Primary sources: Franklin Templeton — June 2026 AUM; March-quarter results; Franklin Crypto announcement

Research and experimental program

Franklin Templeton Institute coordinates market and investment research across specialist managers. The firm says it is applying AI in investment management, sales, marketing and operations to improve the research process and scale distribution. Its digital-assets work is also production infrastructure: Benji, blockchain wallet architecture, tokenized fund shares and node/crypto investment capabilities. Research publications, portfolio decisions and platform engineering must remain separate evidence classes.

Primary sources: Franklin Templeton FY2025 investor presentation; Franklin OnChain Fund SAI

R. Fifth Third Bancorp / Fifth Third Bank / Comerica

Verified growth program

  • Fifth Third closed its Comerica merger on February 1, 2026, creating a bank with approximately $294 billion of assets and a presence in 17 of the 20 fastest-growing large U.S. markets.
  • The strategy explicitly combines Fifth Third's consumer-acquisition and analytical-marketing playbook with Comerica's middle-market, Texas, California, technology and life-sciences franchises.

Cross-entity patterns — promoted only after the factual portraits

1. The new retail bank is a demand-generation machine

The banks are not merely waiting for customers to ask for loans. They are embedding credit inside loyalty programs, merchant checkouts, health-care offices, auto ownership, travel, sports, app prompts, and AI recommendations.

2. Installment conversion is becoming a common middle layer

Revolving cards, BNPL, post-purchase installment conversion, merchant installment loans, and hardship fixed-payment plans form a continuum. The same balance can move from purchase → revolving credit → structured installment → hardship plan → charge-off or sale.

3. Research frequently doubles as market discovery

  • Transaction-data institutes find where cash buffers and spending are moving.
  • Commissioned surveys identify purchases blocked by price or friction.
  • Technical research improves approval, personalization, fraud control, and servicing.
  • Merchant research shows where financing lifts conversion or ticket size.

Research therefore needs a second annotation: public knowledge product or input to customer acquisition/product design.

4. Physical branches are not disappearing

JPMorgan, Bank of America, PNC, Truist, and Wells are all using branches or advisors to enter underpenetrated and fast-growing markets, often paired with AI-driven digital personalization.

5. Credit is migrating toward specialists and vertically integrated stacks

Goldman and Ally exit selected consumer books; Chase, Capital One, Synchrony, Bread, and CardWorks acquire or originate them. The direction is not simple bank retreat. It is sorting by data advantage, merchant access, network ownership, capital appetite, and servicing skill.

6. Agentic commerce is an explicit bank objective

Citi, U.S. Bank, Capital One/Brex, JPMorgan, and Truist are preparing for AI systems that recommend, approve, route, or execute payments. The future “top of wallet” contest may be fought inside software agents rather than in a leather wallet or even a mobile app.
