## The six findings

**1. State-ledger projects have different legal and operating states.** The later July 20 claim audit replaces the blanket retreat account. Korea completed live Phase I transactions and began Hangang Phase II on March 18, 2026. Users held participating-bank deposit claims; BOK money was the separate wholesale settlement asset. Japan retained separate retail-CBDC design and tokenized-reserve experimentation. Europe’s retail digital-euro legislation, issuance decision and wholesale settlement/collateral work are separate objects. A pause, experiment, legal permission, scheduled product and live service do not establish the same state.

**2. The dollar token is a weapon, and it fired.** Bessent stated the doctrine Jun 11, 2025: a $2 trillion dollar-stablecoin market is "reasonable" — a Treasury-demand machine exported by market force. In January 2026 **Tether froze $182M of Venezuela's money** — ~80% of PDVSA's oil revenue settles in USDT, so a private company under US law can now switch off a sovereign's oil income with a database write. The IMF says Nigeria has **dollarized on USDT** ($59B/yr inflows, 88.5% of stablecoin activity). Bolivia's state oil company pays for fuel in Tether because its central bank ran out of dollars. The unbanked world never waited for a CBDC; it picked the dollar token off the street.

**3. Gold matters through ownership, accounts and financing.** The August 23 Gold BOP research shows material gold contributions to external accounts and financing, including Ghana. It does not isolate gold as the sole cause of the cedi’s 41% nominal appreciation. Export value is not automatically central-bank reserve capture, collateralized gold liquidity is not a gold export, and reserve revaluation is not a BOP transaction. The original reserve/custody observations require their own dates and transaction evidence; they do not establish one global gold-to-currency causal mechanism.

**4. The empire strikes at rival plumbing, not rival currencies.** One Trump sentence (100% tariff threat) killed the BRICS common currency — the Rio declaration doesn't contain the word "de-dollarization." But Brazil's **Pix** — a payment rail — drew a Section 301 investigation and a proposed **25% tariff on all Brazilian goods** (statutory deadline **Jul 15, 2026**). Argentina got the carrot: the **ESF** (the standing ESF watch line!) bought pesos outright, opened a $20B swap conditioned publicly on Milei's midterms, and was repaid at a profit by January. El Salvador's Bitcoin Law was gutted by IMF conditionality. Carrot, stick, leash.

**5. Japan and Korea illustrate different bank-led claims and stages.** Japan’s effective reserve option allows up to 50% only for specified trust-beneficiary structures and qualifying short-dated sovereign bonds, subject to remaining-maturity, valuation and cure rules. It is not a blanket JGB backing or purchase requirement. JPYSC was issued by SBI Shinsei Trust Bank on June 24, 2026 inside a limited account-based perimeter. The three megabanks’ common-coin work remained preparation rather than demonstrated live issuance in the July 20 audit. Progmat’s roughly 40-institution repo initiative was a study; the $1.6T figure described the existing market, not committed on-chain volume. Korea’s deposit tokens and wholesale BOK settlement asset are a different architecture.

**6. Institutional adaptation must be tested by role and result.** BIS analysis, IMF surveillance and programs, G7/G20 coordination, national regulation and SWIFT operations are different kinds of activity. A policy recommendation, revised rule, network study or pilot does not establish a global institutional victory or defeat. The later coordination and financial-rails studies trace permission, implementation and end-user outcomes separately; they support comparing adaptation and institutional strain without assuming that every institution chose one state-ledger pole.
