# White House Ballroom Lawfare Map — 2025–2026

## The answer

The White House ballroom fight is legally consequential, but the public phrase
“the lawfare” compresses several different things into one blob.

The central action is *National Trust for Historic Preservation v. National
Park Service*. It began as an APA, planning, environmental and constitutional
challenge. After the district court held that the Office of the Executive
Residence was not an APA agency and that the plaintiff had not pleaded the
right cause of action, the Trust amended. The live preliminary-injunction fight
then became a much sharper **ultra vires authority case**:

1. 40 U.S.C. § 8106 says no building or structure may be erected on federal
   park or public grounds in the District without express congressional authority.
2. 3 U.S.C. § 105(d) authorizes appropriations for the care, maintenance,
   repair, alteration and improvement of the Executive Residence. The dispute
   is whether it also supplies free-standing authority for this new,
   privately financed structure.
3. The NPS Organic Act tells NPS to conserve park scenery and historic objects
   unimpaired and bars derogation unless Congress acts directly and specifically.
4. The project used an NPS donation/trust-fund route and an interagency funding
   mechanism. The dispute is whether those funding powers can carry a project
   that NPS or the Executive Residence otherwise lacks authority to build.

A D.C. Circuit majority accepted the challenger's likelihood-of-success theory
on August 7. Judge Rao dissented on standing, the narrowness of ultra vires
review, statutory meaning, party presentation and national-security equities.
Chief Justice Roberts stayed the operative injunction on August 21 without
giving reasons. The stay changes what may happen on the ground; it does not
erase the appellate opinion or decide the merits.

## The actual case constellation

### 1. *Voorhees v. Trump* — attempted first strike, no merits

- **Court:** D.D.C. No. 1:25-cv-03761.
- **Filed:** October 23, 2025.
- **Object:** A Rule 65 emergency application alleging that demolition and
  construction were moving ahead before NCPC, CFA and historic-review steps.
- **Procedure:** No initiating complaint, filing fee, civil cover sheet or
  complete summons package. The clerk said the case could not proceed.
- **End:** Plaintiffs voluntarily dismissed October 28.
- **Ruling:** None. This case cannot establish that a court rejected or accepted
  its legal theory.

Primary handles: [filed emergency application](https://www.courthousenews.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/voorhees-v-trump-east-wing-renovation-tro.pdf) and [reproduced docket](https://clearinghouse.net/case/47079/).

### 2. *National Trust v. NPS* — the construction-authority case

- **Court:** D.D.C. No. 1:25-cv-04316-RJL; D.C. Cir. Nos. 26-5123/26-5134;
  Supreme Court emergency No. 26A203.
- **Filed:** December 12, 2025.
- **Current object:** A modified preliminary injunction against above-ground
  ballroom construction, with exceptions for below-ground national-security
  facilities, site protection and strictly necessary safety/security work.
- **Current effect:** Stayed by the Chief Justice pending further order.
- **Merits:** Unresolved.

The four D.C. Circuit numbers are stages of this one case, not four lawsuits:
26-5101 was the government's first injunction appeal; 26-5108 the Trust's
cross-appeal; 26-5123 the government's appeal from the clarified injunction;
and 26-5134 the Trust's conditional cross-appeal.

Key primary handles: [second amended complaint](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.287645/gov.uscourts.dcd.287645.50.0.pdf), [March 31 district opinion](https://cdn.savingplaces.org/2026/03/31/17/32/14/0f9ba8ca-a461-4fd2-95e2-542ea3f41de7/Civil%20Action%20No.%202025-4316.pdf), [April 16 clarified opinion](https://cdn.savingplaces.org/2026/04/16/12/55/36/bd2f6ee4-bfb8-45d9-a79b-3af42e58cd59/2026.04.16%20DE%2072%20-%20Memorandum%20Opinion%20%28Clarifying%20PI%29.pdf), [D.C. Circuit opinion](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2026/08/26-5123-2187096.pdf), [government Supreme Court application](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26A203/419029/20260813211858520_August%20Ballroom%20Application%20-%20FINAL.pdf), [Trust opposition](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26A203/419350/20260818115836924_26A203%20Respondents%20Opp.%20to%20App.%20for%20Stay%20of%20Injunction%202026-08-18.pdf), and [government reply](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/26/26A203/419462/20260819113718408_August%20Ballroom%20Reply%20-%20FINAL.pdf).

### 3. *Public Citizen v. NPS* — funding-agreement FOIA case

- **Court:** D.D.C. No. 1:25-cv-04468.
- **Filed:** December 22, 2025, following an October 22 FOIA request.
- **Object:** The agreement among NPS, the Executive Residence and the Trust for
  the National Mall governing the acceptance and transfer of project donations.
- **Result:** The government produced the agreement in April 2026; the case was
  dismissed.
- **Boundary:** This was a disclosure suit. It did not adjudicate the project's
  construction authority or find donor corruption.

Primary handles: [filed complaint](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/ECF-1-Complaint-5.pdf), [case record](https://www.citizen.org/litigation/public-citizen-v-national-park-service/), and [produced October 8, 2025 Philanthropic Support Agreement](https://www.citizen.org/wp-content/uploads/White-House-Ballroom-Funding-Agreement.pdf).

### 4. *Asbestos Disease Awareness Association v. NPS* — asbestos-record FOIA case

- **Court:** D.D.C. No. 1:26-cv-00029-RJL.
- **Filed:** January 7, 2026.
- **Object:** Inspection, hazardous-material, abatement, air-monitoring,
  protection, transport and disposal records connected to East Wing demolition.
- **August 5 ruling:** Partial summary judgment for the requester against
  DOL/NPS/DOI on failure to make timely FOIA determinations; EOP dismissed as a
  non-FOIA agency; EPA summary judgment denied because receipt/exhaustion facts
  were disputed.
- **Current gap:** The ten-working-day determination clock should have expired
  by this cutoff, but no publicly indexed post-opinion filing was located that
  proves compliance or identifies the production. This remains a docket-return
  question.
- **Boundary:** The case establishes a records-timeliness dispute. It does not
  establish asbestos exposure, unlawful handling or a safety violation.

Primary handles: [filed complaint](https://www.washingtonpost.com/documents/2f5e2f7e-81d4-43fe-99d1-e274b41c168f.pdf) and [August 5 opinion](https://cases.justia.com/federal/district-courts/district-of-columbia/dcdce/1%3A2026cv00029/288216/27/0.pdf).

### 5. Campaign Legal Center referral — allegation, not adjudication

On April 6, 2026, Campaign Legal Center asked the U.S. Attorney for D.C. to
investigate whether at least 26 lobbyist employers should have reported
ballroom donations on LD-203 contribution reports. It alleged the project was
an entity established, financed, maintained or controlled by the President and

No public USAO case number, enforcement response or disposition was located.
The accurate state is **administrative allegation / public disposition not

### The precise post-hoc finding

The clean statement is:

> NPS had internal environmental records before demolition, but the public did
> not receive the EA/FONSI until the lawsuit. CFA and NCPC's formal public
> design/site approvals came after demolition and after the core suit was filed.

Do not turn that into “there was no environmental record before demolition.”
Do not turn later CFA/NCPC approval into proof that all timing objections
vanished. And do not say “they built without local permits”: NCPC's official FAQ
says local governments do not review federal property, and NCPC does not review
demolition, general site preparation or below-grade work. [Official NCPC FAQ](https://www.ncpc.gov/files/projects/2025/8733_East_Wing_Modernization_Project_Project_FAQs_Jan2026.pdf).

## Controlling-law fault lines

| Legal object | Challenger / D.C. Circuit majority | Government / Judge Rao dissent | Current state |
|---|---|---|---|
| [40 U.S.C. § 8106](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title40-section8106) | Clear prohibition: this structure on D.C. federal park/public grounds needs express congressional authority. | The section asks whether another statute supplies that authority; it does not decide the meaning of § 105(d). | Strongest challenger text; preliminary appellate acceptance, no final merits judgment. |
| [3 U.S.C. § 105(d)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A3+section%3A105+edition%3Aprelim%29) | Authorization of appropriations for ordinary residence care/alteration/improvement, not free-standing power to build a privately financed ballroom. | “Alteration” and “improvement” can include a major addition; at minimum the text is plausibly broad, defeating extraordinary ultra vires review. | Central statutory-meaning dispute. |
| [54 U.S.C. § 100101](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=2023&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-2023-title54-section100101) | NPS must conserve park/historic values unimpaired; its own EA admits permanent visual/historic effects. | NPS's role is donation/fund administration; presidential official-use proviso and historic practice support authority. | Majority accepted the limit at PI stage; dissent disputes application. |
| NPS gifts and trust funds | A donation route cannot authorize a project outside NPS's statutory purposes. | NPS may accept System-purpose donations; statutory trust funds become appropriated funds. | No audited public transaction-level trail in this package. |
| NCPC/CFA | Review came after demolition and may have been segmented or predetermined. | Final CFA and NCPC approvals now exist; prospective failure-to-submit relief is overtaken. | Timing/record/prejudice questions survive differently from simple non-submission. |
| NEPA | Unpublished, allegedly predetermined EA; no EIS despite admitted permanent harms. | NPS completed EA/FONSI; NCPC adopted a FONSI; NEPA is procedural and judicial review is deferential. | Not the current preliminary injunction's sharpest winning theory. |
| NHPA | Early filings invoked historic-preservation review. | [54 U.S.C. § 307104](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title54-section307104) expressly exempts the White House and its grounds. | Poor core theory for the on-grounds project; NPS separately documented off-site effects. |
| Standing | Trust member's regular recreational/professional use and aesthetic/historic injury suffice for associational standing. | Injury is subjective/generalized or insufficiently imminent; germaneness is doubtful for a congressionally chartered organization. | Major Supreme Court threshold issue. |
| Ultra vires | Clear mandatory statutory limits permit a traditional officer suit where APA review does not reach EOP/Executive Residence. | This route is reserved for action entirely beyond authority; a plausible statutory reading defeats it. | Major Supreme Court threshold issue. |
| Security equities | Injunction expressly permits below-grade and strictly necessary security/safety work. | Government says the whole structure is integrated and delay threatens security; courts should not manage a secure construction site. | Government's strongest equitable argument; its public filing says 65% complete as of August 13, an attributed litigation assertion. |

## The “lawfare” analysis

### What the record supports

The tools were cumulative even when the actors and causes of action differed:

1. **Stop or slow the physical project:** emergency applications and the
   National Trust injunction motion.
2. **Force the hidden administrative record into public view:** the merits suit
   surfaced the unpublished EA/FONSI; Public Citizen obtained the funding agreement;
   ADAO litigated agency-response clocks for demolition safety records.
3. **Move the contest from aesthetics into authority and money:** after the APA
   defendant problem, the Trust pleaded ultra vires construction, funding and
   management-transfer theories.
4. **Create secondary compliance exposure:** the LD-203 referral targeted donor
   reporting; introduced bills targeted authorization, disclosure or limits.
5. **Keep multiple clocks open:** judicial relief, FOIA compliance, agency
   review, legislative authorization and construction progress can move
   independently.

That is a coherent **pressure architecture**. It does not prove a centrally
coordinated campaign. The primary record reviewed here does not establish one
command structure, funding source, joint strategy agreement or shared counsel
across all challengers. “Lawfare” is therefore used here as a description of
law and legal process being used to contest, delay, disclose and delegitimize a
government project—not as a proven conspiracy claim.

### Where the challenger campaign is strongest

- It found a clean statutory hook in § 8106 rather than resting only on taste,
  historic loss or process optics.
- It adapted after the first PI denial by pleading the ultra vires route the
  district judge said was missing.
- The government's description of below-ground and above-ground integration
  changed across stages; the courts noticed the tension.
- NPS's own EA supplied evidence of permanent visual/historic injury.
- The late-public review sequence made irreversibility and judicial-review
  timing concrete rather than abstract.

### Where the government is strongest

- Standing and associational germaneness remain real threshold vulnerabilities.
- Ultra vires review is deliberately extraordinary; “improvement” is not
  facially self-defining at this scale.
- CFA and NCPC have now issued final approvals, narrowing the live procedural
  remedy.
- The project sits inside the President's residence and security perimeter;
  the government has put an integrated national-security account into the
  public Supreme Court record.
- The modified injunction requires a line between ballroom work and necessary
  security work, inviting the institutional-competence objection that a court
  is becoming a construction manager.

## Questions that remain genuinely open

1. Will the full Supreme Court grant or deny the stay application, or leave the
   Chief Justice's interim stay in place while a certiorari petition arrives?
2. Will the Court focus on standing, the narrow ultra vires route, statutory
   meaning, security equities or some combination?
3. What post-August 5 filings show about NPS/DOI/DOL compliance in the asbestos
   FOIA case, and what records were actually produced?
4. Did the U.S. Attorney act on the April 6 LD-203 referral?
5. What is the transaction-level donation, deposit, vetting, obligation,
   transfer and disbursement trail under the October 8 agreement?
6. Did any authorization or restriction bill become enacted after this cutoff?
7. What final merits record will remain after construction progress changes the
   remedial and irreparable-harm posture?

Those are the return gates. Until one fires, the accurate posture is contested
authority, temporarily unrestrained construction and no final merits judgment.
