Status: current dated baseline; estimates remain source- and revision-bound
Purpose: establish the initial world state before cargo- and route-level waves
Bottom line
The current system is not a single supply shock. It is a route-capacity shock,
a crude-versus-products mismatch, a gas/LNG shock and a simultaneous Russia
product/refining shock. The Americas are increasingly important as replacement
supply, but announced Venezuelan capacity cannot be counted as delivered
replacement until field, terminal, lifting and receipt records appear.
Middle East oil
The IEA's September Oil Market Report estimates total Gulf-country oil exports
in August at about 13 million barrels per day, nearly half the pre-war
level. It says crude losses had narrowed to just under 45% with help from
bypass flows and escorted Hormuz movements, while refined products and LPG
remained almost 60%, or 3.7 million b/d, below February. Net Gulf diesel/gasoil
exports averaged 390,000 b/d, a little over one quarter of the pre-war level.
These are IEA estimates, not cargo manifests.
The September EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook estimates 6.7 million b/d of
Middle East crude-production shut-ins in August, up from 5.0 million b/d in
July. Its forecast assumes constrained Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb flows through
the fourth quarter, increased use of pipelines, overland routes and ship-to-ship
transfers, and a broad return toward pre-conflict trade only in 2Q27. Forecast
assumptions are not observed future outcomes.
EIA also reports that August loadings from Saudi Arabia's Yanbu outlet were
about half July's level according to Vortexa estimates, and describes increased
Saudi shipments through Suez plus reported STS workarounds outside the Gulf.
This means the East-West pipeline is a bypass from Hormuz, not an immunity from
Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb risk or downstream vessel constraints.
Gas and LNG
The IEA's Q3 Gas Market Report says LNG through Hormuz had represented almost
20% of global LNG supply before the war. It reported Qatar and UAE LNG loadings
down 35 bcm year over year from March through June, partly offset by 27 bcm of
additional non-Gulf LNG production. Its July forecast assumed progressive
Qatar/UAE recovery through October, but the September shipping monitor still
describes traffic as disrupted and warns about AIS jamming, spoofing and dark
vessels. The July recovery path must therefore remain a forecast until later
loading and transit data confirm it.
Russia
Russia is material in two different ways:
it remains one of the largest crude producers and exporters across Baltic,
Black Sea, Arctic, Pacific and pipeline routes; and
the IEA's September report says intensified Ukrainian attacks disrupted the
refining system and pushed product exports toward a near halt.
The IEA estimates the combined August diesel/gasoil export loss from the Gulf
and Russia at 1.6 million b/d versus February. This cannot be assigned to one
region without the disaggregated series. Russia's crude, products, pipelines,
seaborne cargoes and sanctioned/shadow-fleet lanes will be built separately in
Wave 3.
The current MARAD Black Sea/Sea of Azov military-operations advisory carries a
September 15, 2026 expiry. Reaching the date does not prove risk ended; the next
receipt is an extension, replacement, cancellation or contemporaneous security
record.
Venezuela and the Americas
Venezuela is both current supply and promised future supply. The U.S.
Department of Energy said in January that the United States had begun marketing
Venezuelan crude and products, expected an initial 30–50 million barrels, and
required proceeds to settle first in U.S.-controlled accounts. OFAC's 2026
licenses separately authorize defined oil, gas, diluent, service, PDVSA and
payment conduct.
Late-August and September official statements and SEC filings add five
different future-growth
structures:
NABEP/U.S.: 17 fields, a claimed 65 billion barrels of proven reserves,
planned investment up to $100 billion, U.S. rights to a 35% parent-company
interest, 20% offtake at production cost and first refusal on the balance;
Chevron: updated joint-venture terms, over $7 billion of planned five-year
investment, new Orinoco acreage and a target of roughly 600,000 b/d;
Eni/PDVSA: a signed 25-year Junín 5 productive-participation contract, Eni as
exclusive operator, current field production stated at about 12,000 b/d and
a longer-range national/field development goal.
GeoPark/Bare: an executed 25-year production-participation contract described
in GeoPark's Form 6-K; about 11,000 b/d current gross field production; a 65%
net working interest and 100% funding of approved work programs. The filing
says effectiveness is still subject to approvals, authorizations, regulation
and sanctions compliance.
Nabors: a signed multi-year rig contract and one awarded rig expected to
start in early Q1 2027; customer, field, mobilization and first operation are
not yet public in the located filing.
These are not one deal and should not share one status. The company statements
provide stronger contract-state evidence for Chevron, Eni, GeoPark and Nabors
than the
unpublished underlying NABEP/U.S. documents currently available. None by itself
proves future production, a named cargo, payment, 20% U.S. offtake, an SPR
deposit or tax/royalty receipt.

Maritime risk state
As of the cutoff, MARAD lists active advisories for:
Persian Gulf, Strait of Hormuz and Gulf of Oman attacks on commercial vessels
(effective September 9, 2026);
Red Sea, Bab el-Mandeb, Gulf of Aden, Arabian Sea and Somali Basin Houthi
attacks (currently expiring September 22, 2026);
Gulf of Aden/Arabian Sea/Indian Ocean piracy and kidnapping;
Gulf of Guinea piracy and armed robbery;
Black Sea/Sea of Azov military operations (currently expiring September 15,
2026).
An advisory is a risk/control object. It is not a traffic-volume series or
proof that every vessel avoided the area.
Data-access ruling
The strongest public near-real-time aggregate now located is the IEA Middle
East Maritime Chokepoints Shipping Monitor, built on IMF PortWatch/UN Global
Platform AIS data. It is suitable for aggregate movement direction and shock
comparison, with explicit AIS limitations. It is not a public cargo-title or
payment ledger.
Raw/near-raw global AIS remains access-controlled or commercial in many useful
forms. Commercial services such as Vortexa, Kpler, LSEG, MarineTraffic and
VesselFinder may improve voyage and cargo estimates, but any use must preserve
provider, method, access date, vessel identity quality and uncertainty. We will
not reverse-engineer a paid estimate into false primary-source certainty.
