Trump-Xi visit produced a trade board and product lists, but no signed security pact

The September 2026 state visit did not produce a signed 'strategic stability' agreement; that is still a leader-level political framework from May 2026. On trade, both governments published matching texts for a Board of Trade and two product lists worth about $30 billion each way, but the tariff cuts still need legal instruments, a start date and customs instructions.

What we found

  • In May 2026 Trump and Xi agreed to build a 'constructive China-U.S. relationship of strategic stability.' On September 24 Xi said they reached 'common understanding on many issues.' Neither side published a signed text with obligations, entry into force or dispute rules.
  • On September 27-28 the White House/USTR and China's commerce ministry (MOFCOM) published parallel versions of the Board of Trade working procedures, named principals, a quarterly deputies' meeting schedule, '30-for-30' terms of reference and two approved product lists.
  • No effective date or customs instruction for the tariff cuts was found by the September 28 cutoff.
  • The Oval Office and Cabinet Room meetings were closed to press, and no transcript was found. China's mention of a 'new joint arrangement' is one side's readout, and the dinner remarks were transcribed from White House video, not an official transcript.

Why it matters

The trade lane shows a new two-country body set up through matching agency publications, with real effect waiting on each country's domestic tariff process. It is a clear case of an announced deal versus an operating one.

What to watch

  • pending · U.S. and Chinese tariff instruments, a shared effective date and customs instructions.
  • pending · First Board of Trade meeting records under the quarterly deputies' schedule.
  • pending · Any signed strategic-stability text or military crisis-communication instrument.
