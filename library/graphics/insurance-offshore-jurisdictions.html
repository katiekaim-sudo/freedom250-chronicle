Freedom 250 · Foreign relations and world money · Insurance

Which foreign places matter for U.S. insurance, and how is each recognized under U.S. rules?

Seven places in the study's jurisdiction table: the European Union, the United Kingdom, the Bermuda, the Cayman Islands, the Japan, the Switzerland and the United States itself. The NAIC list available at the cutoff identifies EU member states and the UK through covered agreements; Bermuda, Japan and Switzerland are listed as qualified-jurisdiction reciprocal jurisdictions. Bermuda's listing is limited to named commercial and long-term insurer classes. Cayman is not listed in that document. The U.S.–EU agreement entered into force on April 4, 2018; the U.S.–UK agreement entered into force on December 31, 2020. In 2026 Treasury's Federal Insurance Office signed a memorandum with Bermuda's regulator and a cooperation statement with Cayman's; they are not covered agreements, reciprocal-jurisdiction designations, treaty approvals, licences or guarantees.

  • The study's jurisdiction table is a comparison of principal interfaces, not a census of where every insurer or reinsurer is domiciled; no counts or dollar amounts by place are shown.
