What big banks are building, and the fine print behind it

Big banks are growing by tying checking, cards, installment loans, payments and customer data into one relationship, and using AI to approve and personalize. The fine print is moving too: new arbitration and AI-data terms, and card balances changing hands between issuers.

What we found

  • Chase agreed to take over more than $20 billion of Apple Card balances, with about a 24-month transition from January 2026. Goldman Sachs is leaving mass-market card lending as a result.
  • Capital One bought Discover, then agreed to buy Brex for $5.15 billion (closed in 2026), joining cards, deposits, a payment network, merchant acceptance and business spend software. Capital One, N.A. has been the legal Discover issuer since May 18, 2025.
  • Bank of America added pre-dispute arbitration and mass-arbitration rules to its account agreement, reaching Zelle, RTP, ACH, wires, bill pay and its Erica assistant, after about 17 years without it. The audit pins the change to between March 23 and May 18, 2026.
  • U.S. Bank's terms say it may use data you type in to train or develop AI models. The term is real, but when it was added is not proven. Chase's digital agreement, updated June 2026, now defines third-party AI agents.
  • Citi says new AI risk models raise approval rates by 100 basis points. Chase reports approving more than 76,000 people with no credit history by using their deposit data instead.

Why it matters

The consumer money system is being reworked inside existing banks: merged networks, installment products, AI approvals and new contract terms shift who controls disputes and data without any new law. These are separate business decisions, not a coordinated plan.

What to watch

  • pending · The Apple Card move from Goldman to Chase over the roughly 24-month transition.
  • pending · Any regulator or court response to Bank of America's new arbitration clause, which drew a June 4, 2026 Senate letter.
