Freedom 250 · Digital money · Getting dollars back

If you want dollars back from a stablecoin or tokenized fund, who pays you, how fast, and what can stop it?

It depends on the route, and only the proposed federal rule names a speed. Of the 7 routes the library compares, the issuer or fund pays you back for USDC (Circle, for eligible holders) and USYC (Circle's USDC Teller, whose available liquidity is shown in real time; larger and same-day requests go through support procedures). For BUIDL, the routes are an announced exchange of fund shares for Ripple's RLUSD and trades with whitelisted counterparties on UniswapX, which gives "no assurance of liquidity or pricing." On CHIPS, XRPL and Swift it is participant positions, trading liquidity or banks. What can stop you: holder eligibility, verification, jurisdiction and bank payout timing for USDC; undisclosed bank terms on Swift; withdrawal rules on CHIPS. The Fed's proposal would give issuers no more than 2 business days to redeem after a valid request; it is not final.

What's still uncertain

  • A research room within Crypto Hub. Compare the same funding questions without treating bank rules, product terms and market quotes as equivalent.
  • Selling a fund share or a token to someone else is not the same as redeeming it with the issuer; the cards keep the two apart.
  • No route here records an actual payout time; the BUIDL routes are announcements, and current capacity and pricing are not established.
