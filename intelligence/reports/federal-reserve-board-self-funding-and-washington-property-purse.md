# Federal Reserve self-funding and real-estate control map

Updated: 2026-09-30
Status: current workbench research; not promoted to the Chronicle
Scope: Board of Governors funding, Reserve Bank earnings, Washington real
estate, renovation cost claims, procurement, oversight and federal-fiscal
incidence. This is a factual/legal control map, not a finding about motive,
fraud, monetary policy or project value.

Every monetary amount is written in full nominal dollars with commas and no
letter-suffix scaling. Added zeros expand the source's reporting unit; they do
not imply precision beyond the underlying disclosure.

## Short answer

The clean answer is more unusual than either side's shorthand:

> The Board's renovation account is not an annually appropriated taxpayer
> account, and Congress expressly says that the Reserve Bank assessments used
> to fund the Board are not Government funds or appropriated moneys. But the
> money is not ordinary private property either. It is a congressionally
> created, public-purpose central-bank funding stream controlled by a federal
> agency, drawn from Reserve Banks with mixed sovereign and commercial income,
> and economically connected to current or future Treasury receipts.

The strongest description is therefore:

> **Public-purpose, congressionally created, nonappropriated central-bank funds
> under unusually broad Fed control, with the United States as the ultimate
> residual claimant.**

That sentence is a functional synthesis, not a statutory label that applies
identically to every Fed account.

The renovation dispute exposes a real control seam:

- the Board is a federal agency;
- the Board can assess the 12 Reserve Banks for its expenses;
- the Board determines how its obligations, expenses and disbursements are
  approved and paid;
- the Board may acquire Washington property in its own name and exercise
  "sole control" over it;
- Board acquisitions are outside the Federal Acquisition Regulation and the
  ordinary GSA/prospectus/annual-appropriation control chain;
- the full Board approves its own capital plan;
- Congress, GAO, the Fed OIG, independent auditors and planning/historic-review
  bodies still have real but fragmented oversight roles.

The sharper institutional question is not "How can a private company spend
public appropriations?" It is:

> **Why did Congress give a federal monetary authority a self-assessed,
> nonappropriated property purse, and what ex ante control substitutes for the
> appropriations and procurement controls used elsewhere?**

### September 2026 OIG closure

The answer is now substantially less hypothetical. The September 29 OIG
evaluation found no reasonable grounds for a federal criminal referral and no
Board-policy violation warranting an administrative-misconduct finding. It also
found that the Board did not effectively manage and execute the Eccles-1951
construction-manager-at-risk contract.

The Board had not agreed to a project-level guaranteed maximum price by July
2026, four years after construction began and after more than
`$2,000,000,000` had been awarded. It accumulated 84 limited-scope price
packages, paid a 2.95% construction-cost fee without a project-level cap, did
not use its owner representative to independently validate the largest bid
variances, and repeatedly reported the project as "on track" against rebased
budget and schedule references. The OIG said this execution resembled a
higher-risk cost-plus reimbursement contract with a pay-as-you-go approach.

Chairman Kevin Warsh concurred with all seven recommendations. His attached
response makes GSA the Project Executive, directs an independent audit of all
awarded costs, orders GMP negotiations and fixed cost/schedule metrics, and
commits to contract reconciliation and possible reimbursement or credits.
Those are operating-control changes, not yet a signed ceiling, completed audit,
recovery or final-cost receipt.

## I. Claim audit: what does the "$3,000,000,000" claim refer to?

### Ruling

`$3,100,000,000` is a defensible rounded description of the Board's **entire
building-improvement capital portfolio**. It is not the approved budget or
amount already spent for the two buildings currently under construction.

There are three separate public budget objects:

| Object | Current public figure | What it means |

## II. Four entities that "the Fed" shorthand collapses

| Entity | Legal character | Property / money boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Board of Governors | Federal government agency | Owns and controls the Washington campus; has its own audited books; levies assessments on the Reserve Banks |
| Federal Open Market Committee | Statutory monetary-policy committee | Not the owner or capital-budget approver for the Washington buildings |
| Twelve Federal Reserve Banks | Separate federally chartered bodies corporate with governmental, corporate and central-bank characteristics | Hold their own premises and books; earn System income; pay Board assessments |
| Member commercial banks | Required subscribers to Reserve Bank stock | Hold compulsory, nontransferable membership capital; do not own Board buildings or a normal corporate residual |

The Board's 2025 audited statements expressly distinguish the federal-agency
Board from the Reserve Banks and their separate financial statements.

Each Reserve Bank is a separate body corporate under 12 U.S.C. §341. That does
not make the Board a subsidiary of the Reserve Banks or the member banks.

## III. The statutory purse

### 1. Assessment authority

Under **12 U.S.C. §243**, the Board may levy semiannual assessments on the
Reserve Banks sufficient to pay estimated Board expenses and salaries. The
statutory allocation is in proportion to each Reserve Bank's capital stock and
surplus; the assessment then becomes a Reserve Bank expense that reduces its
net earnings.

The same section permits the Board to:

- acquire a Washington site and buildings **in its own name**;
- construct, equip, furnish, maintain, enlarge and remodel them; and
- exercise **sole control** over the buildings and space.

Congress therefore tied the self-funding power directly to the property power.

### 2. The exact legal classification

Under **12 U.S.C. §244**:

- the Board determines how its obligations, disbursements and expenses are
  incurred and paid;
- assessment proceeds may remain on deposit at the Reserve Banks; and
- **"funds derived from such assessments shall not be construed to be
  Government funds or appropriated moneys."**

That last sentence is the strongest legal support for saying the renovation
does not use ordinary taxpayer appropriations.

It does **not** establish that:

- the Board is private;
- the member banks privately own the assessment proceeds;
- the member banks own the Board's buildings;
- every Federal Reserve revenue stream has the same legal classification; or
- Fed spending has no effect on the federal fisc.

The narrow and accurate statement is:

> **Congress directs that Board assessment proceeds "shall not be construed to
> be Government funds or appropriated moneys," while the assessing and spending
> institution is a federal agency exercising powers Congress created.**

## IV. Where the underlying money comes from

The Reserve Banks do not receive a renovation appropriation and then pass it to
the Board. The Board allocates its statutory assessments among them in
proportion to capital stock and surplus. Those assessments are Reserve Bank
expenses paid from their pooled resources and reduce net earnings.

The 2025 combined Reserve Bank statements report approximately:

| Reserve Bank inflow | 2025 amount |
|---|---:|
| Interest on Treasury securities | $106,600,000,000 |
| Interest on agency MBS | $48,000,000,000 |
| Financial-service income | $542,000,000 |
| Reimbursed fiscal-agency services | $934,000,000 |

They also reported approximately:

| Reserve Bank outflow | 2025 amount |
|---|---:|
| Interest paid on reserve balances and similar depository-institution balances | $147,700,000,000 |
| Interest on repo liabilities | $19,700,000,000 |
| Board operations and currency included in combined operating expenses | $2,702,000,000 |
| CFPB | $245,000,000 |

This produces a mixed-source pool:

```text
Treasury and agency-security interest
+ loan and other asset income
+ payment-service fees
+ reimbursed Treasury fiscal-agent work
                         |
                         v
                 12 Reserve Banks
                         |
        +----------------+----------------+
        |                |                |
        v                v                v
 interest expense   Bank operations   Board assessments
 dividends/surplus  priced services   currency + CFPB
        \                |                /
         \_______________|_______________/
                         |
                         v
          excess earnings to Treasury, if any
```

Payment-service users do pay fees, and Treasury reimburses Reserve Banks for
specified fiscal-agent services. But it would be inaccurate to say that the
renovation is a direct $3,000,000,000 bill sent to private businesses or a direct $3,000,000,000
Treasury appropriation. The assessment falls on pooled Reserve Bank earnings.

## V. Who ultimately bears the spending?

### The ordinary positive-earnings case

Reserve Banks ordinarily pay:

1. operating expenses;
2. the statutory member-bank dividend;
3. the capped surplus requirement; then
4. remit remaining earnings to Treasury.

An additional Board expense therefore reduces the residual otherwise available
for Treasury.

### The current deferred-asset case

At year-end 2025, the Reserve Banks reported a **$243,481,000,000 deferred asset**.
When earnings do not cover costs, dividends and surplus requirements:

- the affected Reserve Bank stops remitting while it carries its own deferred
  asset, although another Bank that has extinguished its deferred asset may
  remit even while the System aggregate remains positive;
- the shortfall is recorded as a deferred asset; and
- future net earnings must first recover that balance before normal remittances
  resume.

In calendar 2025:

- the combined loss before remittances was about `$18,761,000,000`;
- Treasury received `$6,281,000,000` of combined cash/System remittances, comprising
  2025 Bank-level remittance provisions and settlement of a prior-year Atlanta
  accrued payable;
- statutory dividends were `$1,688,000,000`; and
- the combined deferred asset grew by `$27,526,000,000`.

The 12-Bank distribution, remittance-clock reconciliation and property scan
are in
`FEDERAL_RESERVE_BANK_BY_BANK_ASSET_AND_EARNINGS_SCAN_2025.md`.

As of July 1, 2026, the Fed reported that the deferred asset had fallen to
about `$236,000,000,000`.

A particular construction payment therefore need not produce a matching
same-year assessment, Reserve Bank loss or deferred-asset increase. Construction
outlays are capitalized on the Board's books; the Board can carry an accumulated
fund balance; and Reserve Banks expense assessments when they are levied. Over
time, assessment financing reduces Reserve Bank net earnings and therefore
reduces current remittances or prolongs the earnings hurdle that must be
recovered before normal Treasury remittances resume.

The clean fiscal-incidence statement is:

> **Not an appropriated taxpayer dollar does not mean economically unrelated to
> taxpayers or Treasury. Board spending reduces Reserve Bank net earnings and
> therefore reduces current remittances or delays future ones.**

### OMB classification

OMB describes the Federal Reserve as self-financing and independent of
Executive budget review. Most Fed operations are nonbudgetary; excess-income
transfers to Treasury are federal receipts. The Board's budget appears in the
Budget Appendix for information, while regional Reserve Bank budgets do not.

This creates an asymmetric federal-budget presentation:

- Treasury interest paid to Fed-held securities appears as an outlay;
- Fed operating and capital spending sits largely outside the budget; and
- only the net remittance returns as a receipt.

## VI. Member-bank stock is not normal private ownership

Member banks must subscribe to Reserve Bank stock. But under 12 U.S.C.
§§282, 287, 289 and 290, that stock:

- is compulsory membership capital;
- cannot be transferred or pledged;
- does not trade;
- is redeemed at paid-in par plus limited accrued dividend;
- receives a statutory dividend rather than the ordinary corporate residual;
- leaves excess earnings and the liquidation residual to the United States.

The 2025 Reserve Bank statements report:

- `$39,152,000,000` capital paid in;
- `$6,785,000,000` statutory surplus; and
- `$45,937,000,000` total Reserve Bank capital.

The member-bank stake is real but narrow. It does not mean JPMorgan, a local
member bank or the banking industry owns the Eccles Building, approves the
Board's renovation budget, or can distribute the Board's accumulated property
value to private shareholders.

The Supreme Court's 2024 CFPB funding decision is an important limiting case.
It treated Federal Reserve System earnings otherwise destined for Treasury as
public funds for constitutional appropriations analysis. That holding concerns
the CFPB's separate statutory funding regime, not the Board's §§243–244
assessment proceeds. It nevertheless makes "all Fed money is private" legally
untenable.

## VII. Property, procurement and control

### Washington Board campus

| Property | Current control state |
|---|---|
| Eccles Building | Board-owned headquarters property |
| 1951 Constitution Avenue / former South Interior Building | GSA transferred custody to the Board in 2018; current OIG and design-review records describe Board ownership |
| Martin Building | Board-owned; main renovation reopened in 2021; later program authority includes related exterior work |
| 1709 New York Avenue | Board retained the property after abandoning the planned full renovation; limited program remains |

The precise 1951 transfer/deed instrument and original Eccles title instrument
remain open document targets. The public custody transfer, current owner
descriptions and Board control are clear; the exact conveyancing instrument is
not yet in this packet.

### Regional Reserve Bank real estate

Reserve Bank-owned premises are held on the relevant Bank's corporate books;
Reserve Banks may also lease property and record leasehold improvements. Local
management and boards originate projects, but Board staff and the full Board
retain supervisory and final-budget roles. Regional property must not be merged
with the Board-owned Washington campus.

The 2025 bank-by-bank annex shows that Atlanta and San Francisco supplied about
90% of the combined `$192,000,000` increase in net Reserve Bank premises and
equipment. Unlike the Board, the Reserve Banks do not publish Statements of
Cash Flows; their audited year-over-year asset movements cannot be read as
cash capital expenditures.

### Procurement exception

The Board's published Acquisition Policy says Board expenditures are not
"acquisitions" governed by the **Federal Acquisition Regulation**. The Fed OIG
has also said the Board is not required to follow the **Public Buildings Act of
1959** or FAR Part 36, although it voluntarily uses GSA and FAR practices as
benchmarks.

Consequences:

- no ordinary annual appropriation for the project;
- no ordinary OMB capital-budget veto;
- no normal GSA prospectus/appropriation chain;
- the Board uses its own acquisition policy;
- full Board approval is the principal ex ante capital control.

This is the most important institutional difference from an ordinary executive
agency project.

## VIII. Oversight lattice

Oversight exists. It is largely audit, reporting and retrospective review rather
than annual ex ante funding permission.

| Overseer | Actual reach | Structural limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Full Board of Governors | Approves the Board capital plan and current project annually; also approves Reserve Bank budgets | The governed institution is its own budget approver |
| Fed OIG | Direct Board jurisdiction; project planning, governance, contracting, cost growth, security and current discretionary-feature review | Does not replace an appropriations committee or approve contracts |
| GAO | May audit the Board and Reserve Banks; real estate and procurement lie outside the monetary-policy/foreign-central-bank exclusions in 31 U.S.C. §714 | Audit and recommendation, not routine ex ante approval |
| Independent auditors | Annual Board and Reserve Bank financial-statement audits required by 12 U.S.C. §248b | Financial-statement assurance is not a full value-for-money judgment |
| Congress | Hearings, document demands, subpoenas, GAO/OIG requests and power to amend §§243–244 | Ordinary annual appropriations leverage is absent |
| President / OMB / GSA | Appointments, public pressure and generally applicable legal tools; GSA was transferor of 1951 | No identified authority to approve, reduce or cancel the Board capital budget |
| NCPC / CFA / SHPO / NPS | Planning, design, environmental and historic review | Not capital-budget or procurement approvers |

### Planning-authority collision

The National Capital Planning Commission treated its September 2, 2021 action
as final-plan approval under the National Capital Planning Act. The Board says
its submission was voluntary and NCPC direction was not binding because §243
grants special property authority notwithstanding other law.

No court, OLC or Attorney General opinion resolving that collision was located.
It should remain an open legal question rather than be flattened into either
"NCPC had veto power" or "there was no external review."

### Historic and environmental review

- CFA approved the comprehensive final design in September 2021, but its
  public-building role is advisory/commentary rather than project funding.
- NEPA review ended in a finding of no significant impact rather than an EIS.
- Historic review found adverse effects on Eccles, 1951 and the Northwest
  Rectangle Historic District; the parties adopted mitigation through an MOA.

Those processes constrain design and mitigation. They do not substitute for
appropriation or value-for-money review.

## IX. What the audit record establishes—and does not

### Established control weaknesses

Prior OIG work found:

- prolonged design and scope changes;
- estimate errors and inconsistencies in earlier Martin planning;
- incomplete documentation and cost-control submissions;
- no established renovation-project governance framework before the
  Eccles–1951 architecture/engineering award;
- missing contractor progress reports or meeting minutes; and
- schedule changes not receiving required approval.

These are genuine management findings, not merely political allegations.

### Findings that cut the other way

OIG also found:

- Board market research and competitive award processes generally aligned with
  its own policies; and
- the 2022 contract-modification process was generally effective, with five
  administrative approval oversights later addressed.

In the audited change-order sample, contractors initially proposed `$47,400,000`;
the Board negotiated the amount to `$38,700,000`.

That evidence does not establish fraud, theft or a sham project. Nor does it
establish that the current budget is reasonable. The live value-for-money
question remains open.

### Current review clock

The OIG evaluation appeared on September 29, 2026. It resolved the criminal and
administrative-misconduct questions within its scope, identified contract,
cost-control, governance and reporting deficiencies, and issued seven
recommendations. It did not determine whether the project's total cost is
reasonable, quantify avoidable loss or independently validate every award.

The live clock is implementation: an agreed GMP or other enforceable ceiling,
the independent cost audit, fixed metrics, contract reconciliation, any
reimbursement or credits, quarterly OIG follow-up, substantial completion and
final project closeout.

| "The project uses the normal FAR/GSA/appropriations stack." | **False** | Board follows its own acquisition regime and special property authority |
| "There is no oversight." | **False** | Congress, GAO, OIG, outside auditors and planning/historic bodies have roles |
| "The oversight is equivalent to annual appropriations control." | **False** | The Board self-assesses and self-approves; much oversight is fragmented or retrospective |
| "Past audits proved fraud or theft." | **Unsupported** | Audits identified governance weaknesses but did not make that finding |
| "Past audits proved the current $2,460,000,000 is good value." | **Unsupported** | Financial/control audits are not a completed value-for-money ruling; current OIG work remains open |

## XI. The principle exposed

The renovation is a useful diagnostic because it removes monetary-policy
complexity. The same institutional design is visible in a tangible asset:

1. Congress creates the entity and the assessment power.
2. The Board sets its own expenses and assesses the Reserve Banks.
3. Congress declares the proceeds nongovernment and nonappropriated.
4. The Board acquires property in its own name and exercises sole control.
5. The Board approves its own capital budget outside ordinary Executive review.
6. The economic cost passes through Reserve Bank earnings and the Treasury
   remittance channel.
7. Accountability arrives through audits, hearings, reporting and possible
   statutory amendment rather than annual funding permission.

This is not a secret privatization. It is a deliberate statutory insulation
architecture.

That architecture creates a double boundary:

- **independence from ordinary presidential/OMB capital-budget approval and
  annual appropriations**, part of the broader insulation intended to protect
  central-bank operations from short-term political control; and
- **reduced ex ante fiscal control**, which can extend beyond monetary policy
  into property, procurement and administration.

The real research question is where that insulation is functionally necessary
and where Congress has allowed monetary-policy independence to carry
unnecessarily into ordinary administration.

This joins directly to the prior Observatory reading in
`KEEP_THE_FED_BIND_ITS_HANDS.md`: the architecture question is not simply
abolish/keep the Fed. It is which hand should be independent, which hand should
be bound, by whom, and at what clock.
