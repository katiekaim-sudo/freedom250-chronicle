C. Authority response

Field Entry
Response state PARTIAL
Exact propositions answered The Chinese Executive Director stated that the accommodative monetary-policy direction was consistent with staff advice and that further RRR/rate cuts remained available. He rejected staff’s preliminary finding that the 2025 external position was stronger than fundamentals, citing trade restrictions, export frontloading and EBA-model limits. He asserted that market forces play the decisive role in RMB formation, that stability is maintained at an adaptive/equilibrium level, and that China has neither need nor intent to obtain trade advantage through depreciation. The statement separately described the financial system as generally sound and risks as under control.
Source/page/date IMF CR 26/44, Executive Director statement dated 2026-02-13: monetary policy, PDF p.115; external assessment/exchange policy, PDF p.116; financial-system response, PDF pp.117–118
Attribution boundary Statement by Zhengxin Zhang, Executive Director for China, presenting the Chinese authorities’ position. It is not IMF staff analysis or the Executive Board summing-up.

F. Six-lane worksheet

Lane Grade One-sentence reason Primary evidence + vintage Persistence evidence Shock evidence Competing explanation Confidence Legal/event link Data gap
L1 Domestic anchor C The price system remains usable, but three years of near-zero CPI, persistent producer-price/GDP-deflator decline and a missing medium-term nominal anchor materially impair price credibility despite identifiable easing capacity. 2025 average CPI 0.0%, GDP deflator -1.0%, output gap -1.0%; CR 26/44 PDF p.65. Staff says real rates remained high and easing had provided limited support, pp.26–27. CPI: 0.2%, 0.2%, 0.0% in 2023–25; GDP deflator: -0.4%, -0.7%, -1.0%; PPI negative for 39 consecutive months and GDP deflator negative for 11 quarters by December 2025, pp.51, 65. Property/LGFV correction propagated weak demand and deflation over several years. Tariff uncertainty adds 2026 growth risk but does not explain the persistent anchor failure, pp.46–47. Authorities cited December CPI 0.8% and core CPI 1.2%, a narrowing PPI decline and additional easing capacity; these may mark early reflation rather than entrenched impairment, pp.114–115. HIGH Article IV §1(i); trade events are context only Updated CPI/PPI weights; usable inflation-expectations series; evidence that late-2025 improvement persisted
L2 Underlying order C Banking and RMB monetary functions continue without generalized fracture, but persistent property/LGFV stress, declining profitability, forbearance and opaque loss recognition materially impair underlying order. 2025Q3 Tier 1 ratio 12.4%, total CAR 15.4%, headline NPL 1.5%; four state banks received RMB 520bn capital injections; forbearance may veil asset quality. PDF pp.17–18, 64. NIM fell from 1.9% in 2022 to 1.4% in 2025Q3; legacy-asset, AMC and LGFV problems persisted while crisis-management/resolution reforms remained incomplete, pp.48, 56, 71–73. Property correction and LGFV debt are the operative stress interval; capital support and debt swaps prevented a generalized break but did not complete balance-sheet repair. Authorities reported CAR 15.36%, NPL 1.52%, strong insurance/securities buffers, improving risks and preserved institutional/market stability, pp.117–118. MEDIUM Article IV §1(ii); no tariff event receives a second score Unmasked asset quality after forbearance; rural/regional-bank distribution; AMC recovery values; usable resolution/ELA framework
L3 Exchange order C The crawl-like managed regime avoided market fracture and retained adequate reserves, but limited flexibility, fix/spot pressure, occasional market shallowness and deficient intervention transparency materially impair shock absorption and adjustment. De jure managed float/±2% band and de facto crawl-like regime, pp.105–106; sizable fix/spot gap during November 2024–May 2025 pressure, pp.14–15; November reserves $3.7tn, about 102% of the capital-control-adjusted ARA metric and assessed adequate, p.75. Staff continued to call for greater flexibility and FX-regime transparency; intervention volumes and duration cannot be reconstructed cleanly from public data, pp.27, 45–47. Depreciation pressure eased after May 2025 trade talks and CNY later appreciated moderately; the regime absorbed the episode without disorder or rapid reserve depletion. Authorities say market forces are decisive, the RMB retains two-way flexibility, stability is adaptive/equilibrium-based and there is no competitive-depreciation intent, pp.29, 116. MEDIUM Article IV §§1–3; capital controls descriptive only; trade events contextual Direct, timely FX-intervention volumes; forward/off-balance positions; sterilization; intervention duration and decision rule
L4 External adjustment C A persistent, material surplus/REER deviation impairs adjustment, but positive NIIP, adequate reserves and a clearly identifiable domestic-demand/flexibility reform path keep the lane functioning. Preliminary 2025 external position “stronger”; CA 3.3% GDP, cyclically adjusted CA 3.2%, norm 0.9%, midpoint gap +2.3%; implied REER gap midpoint -16.4%. PDF pp.74–75. CA rose from 1.4% in 2023 to 2.3% in 2024 and 3.3% in 2025; weak domestic demand, high private saving and low inflation supported the surplus and real depreciation. NIIP remained positive near 20% GDP, pp.65, 74–75. Export frontloading and tariff/trade uncertainty affected 2025 flows, so the assessment is preliminary and explicitly deferred for reassessment in the 2026 ESR; frozen trade events do not themselves move the grade. Authorities reject the EBA conclusion, citing trade restrictions, frontloading, omitted financial-account effects and insufficient country specificity, p.116. MEDIUM Article IV §1(iii): no manipulation finding; all four linked trade events remain untransmitted Final 2026 ESR; full-year partner data; customs/BOP goods-gap reconciliation; financial-account and investment-income sector detail
L5 Current-payment usability A Official Fund evidence states that bona fide current payments and transfers operate free of restrictions and MCPs, with no material operating impairment established; capital and trade controls are excluded. Article VIII §§2/3/4 accepted; exchange system free of MCPs and current-payment/transfer restrictions except separately notified security measures. PDF pp.105–106. Acceptance dates from 1996-12-01; expanded RMB international use provides operating corroboration, while capital controls remain confined as a separate financial-account object. NOT-APPLICABLE: no current-payment shock or arrears episode documented. Tariffs, export licenses and capital controls are not reclassified as Article VIII restrictions. SAFE compliance procedures may create transaction-level friction, but the frozen packet contains no evidence of material denial, discriminatory practice, arrears or undue delay for bona fide current transactions. MEDIUM Article VIII §§2–4; security row separate; all trade events excluded from automatic scoring Itemized security restrictions; current AREAER operating-access detail; transfer-delay/arrears evidence; transaction-level FX-access data
L6 Systemic service C RMB reliably serves a growing China-linked trade, funding, payment and reserve role, but controls, shallow offshore/hedging markets and limited accessible safe assets materially constrain the international job it already occupies. 2024 China trade settlement exceeded 27% for goods and about 32% for services; CIPS volume exceeded RMB 175tn; 2025Q3 global shares were below 3% of trade, about 8% of trade finance and about 2% of reserves. PDF p.55. CIPS volume more than tripled since 2020; offshore lending and emerging-Asia bank use grew, but reserve share remained broadly stable and third-party use remained limited, p.55. Trade tensions encouraged some RMB settlement, but no frozen tariff/export-control event establishes durable systemic-role transmission. The currency need not equal a global reserve issuer to perform its actual role; its China-linked settlement and regional-funding functions may be stronger than the modest global-share figures imply. HIGH IMF freely usable/SDR-basket status, PDF p.106; trade events contextual only Updated BIS/COFER/SWIFT/CIPS measures; third-party transactions excluding Chinese entities; offshore liquidity, hedge depth and safe-asset availability
