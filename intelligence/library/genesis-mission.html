Genesis: a federal AI science platform, mostly still being built

Genesis, launched by executive order in November 2025, links national labs, federal data, AI models and tech companies into one science platform run through DOE. A few pieces work now, but most of the big computers, models and awards are still announced, selected or under construction.

What we found

  • Executive Order 14363 launched Genesis in November 2025. The White House coordinates it, DOE builds the shared platform, and Under Secretary for Science Darío Gil directs it at DOE. The stated goal is to double the productivity and impact of American science and engineering within ten years.
  • The study covers 33 official science challenges and all 278 projects on DOE's July 2026 selection list. The White House cites more than $5 billion in federal commitments.
  • One workflow is shown working: an Oak Ridge plant-imaging campaign of 360 plants over 40 days, with routine data queries taking 3 to 10 seconds. That result comes from the authors' own preprint.
  • Most big systems are not running yet. Lux at Oak Ridge targets October 2026; Discovery is expected in 2028; Argonne's Solstice (100,000 GPUs) and Equinox (10,000 GPUs) are announced designs, not installed. An NNSA secret cloud with AWS is authorized, but Genesis work on it is not yet shown.
  • Named partners include NVIDIA, Oracle, HPE, AMD and AWS; consortium members include Palantir, CoreWeave, Scale AI and Intel. Membership is not proof of signed project deals, and some money figures conflict (GridFM: $11.5 million per DOE, $14.2 million per Brookhaven).

Why it matters

Federal science is being rebuilt as a shared AI and computing platform run through DOE and its labs, with private chip, cloud and AI firms built into the working layer. The agencies keep the legal mandate while the operating machinery is new.

What to watch

  • October 2026 · Whether Lux at Oak Ridge actually goes into operation.
  • pending · Release and testing of the Genesis-Science-1 open science model, and signed award agreements for the selected projects.
  • 2027–2028 · Delivery and acceptance of Discovery and the Los Alamos Mission and Vision systems.
