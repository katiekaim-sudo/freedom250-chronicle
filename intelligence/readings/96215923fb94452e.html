Treasury Payment Fraud and Scams Working Group — Second-Meeting Delta · Freedom 250
Treasury Payment Fraud and Scams Working Group — Second-Meeting Delta
Question
What does the September 30 second meeting establish, what changed from the
inaugural July meeting, and how does the operating working group relate to the
separate congressional TRAPS proposal?
Bottom line
The second meeting is a real continuation receipt, but not yet a deliverable
receipt. At 5:16 p.m. EDT on September 30, Secretary Scott Bessent said Treasury
had convened the second meeting of the Payment Fraud and Scams Working Group
and would continue working with the private sector. His post identified no
participants, agenda, decisions, workstreams, charter, data-sharing instrument,
reporting timetable, regulatory action or technical deployment. No Treasury
readout or partner-agency account was located by the 5:38 p.m. cutoff.
Bessent used the September 9 Xinbi Guarantee action as the proof point. That
action is substantial but belongs to a separate enforcement chain: DOJ says a
court-authorized warrant supported seizure of two wallets holding about $12
million and restraint efforts against 47 additional wallets, producing more
than $52 million restrained; Treasury separately used OFAC sanctions authority
to designate Xinbi, SafeW Technology and Anwen Technology. Restraint, seizure,
blocking, forfeiture, victim return and final adjudication remain different
states.
The most important structural finding is a legal boundary. Treasury's present
working group predates and is not shown to derive from H.R. 4936, the TRAPS Act.
On September 16, the House Financial Services Committee approved an amended
TRAPS bill 49–0. The proposal would require a Treasury-chaired Task Force for
Recognizing and Averting Payment Scams with named federal and private members,
at least three first-year meetings, a one-year public report and annual updates.
The bill was only ordered reported, not enacted, through this cutoff. The
administrative working group and proposed statutory task force have strikingly
similar membership and purposes, but no reviewed source identifies them as the
same legal body or says the bill would ratify the existing group.
Second-meeting public record
The Secretary's September 30 post
establishes only four affirmative facts:
Treasury convened a second meeting on September 30.
The body is still called the Payment Fraud and Scams Working Group.
The Administration treats fraud enforcement as a continuing priority.
Treasury intends to continue working with the private sector.
The post does not identify:
which federal officials or agencies attended;
which financial, telecommunications, technology or victim-support actors
attended;
whether the July participants returned;
an agenda, minutes, votes or agreed recommendations;
a formal charter, membership list or legal authority;
a data-sharing, identity, complaint-routing or fraud-signal protocol;
a rulemaking, supervisory change, fraud hold or reimbursement proposal;
a meeting cadence, next date or public-report deadline; or
an operating result caused by the working group.
The Treasury press-release index listed other September 30 releases but no
working-group readout at the cutoff. The FCC, FTC, Federal Reserve, Treasury and
USTelecom X accounts also had not posted a same-day partner account in the
reviewed search.
Inaugural-meeting baseline
The inaugural meeting took place at Treasury on July 20. Bessent's
July 24 account
named FCC Chairman Brendan Carr, FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson and Federal
Reserve Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman and described a
financial-services, telecommunications and technology coalition.
The strongest participant-specific public record is USTelecom's
same-day industry statement.
It named USTelecom President and CEO Jonathan Spalter and executives from AT&T,
Lumen, T-Mobile, Uniti and Verizon. It emphasized illegal-call and text tracing
through the FCC-recognized Industry Traceback Group.
Carr's official statement accompanying the FCC's July 22 robocall item adds
that the meeting brought together social-media and telecommunications leaders
and discussed ways for government to detect and prevent payment fraud and
scams, with illegal robocalls as an important part of the conversation.
The first-meeting evidence therefore supports a cross-channel design:
impersonation or malicious outreach -> telecom/social platform signal -> consumer action -> payment institution or rail -> tracing, interruption, enforcement and possible recovery
It does not prove that this chain has a shared database, common identifier,
real-time signal exchange, binding service-level standard or unified loss rule.
What the Xinbi example actually proves
Bessent's September 30 post points backward to the coordinated September 9
Xinbi operation rather than announcing a new second-meeting product.
The Treasury release
states that OFAC designated Xinbi Guarantee as a significant transnational
criminal organization and designated SafeW Technology and Anwen Technology for
supporting it. Treasury describes Xinbi as a Chinese-language marketplace
connecting scam-center operators to financial, technology and money-laundering
services and says it processed the equivalent of more than $24 billion in
digital assets and fiat currency since around 2022.
The DOJ release
states that:
the September 7 warrant authorized seizure of Telegram channels hosting the
marketplace;
two Xinbi payment wallets holding about $12 million were seized;
law enforcement sought restraint of 47 additional wallets; and
the coordinated effort restrained more than $52 million in one day.
The defensible chain is:
victim-linked tracing allegations -> court-authorized channel/wallet seizure -> additional-wallet restraint -> OFAC designation and blocking -> forfeiture and victim-return outcomes still separate
The working-group post does not say the group generated the Xinbi lead,
authorized the warrant, performed blockchain tracing, chose the sanctions
targets or caused the private-sector assistance. The operation is evidence of
the kind of public-private and interagency action Bessent wants associated with
the group, not yet evidence of the group's own operating lineage.
The proposed statutory twin: H.R. 4936
The House Financial Services Committee's September 16
markup record
shows H.R. 4936 agreed to by a 49–0 recorded vote after adoption of an amendment
in the nature of a substitute.
The adopted substitute
would, if enacted:
require the Treasury Secretary to establish and chair the Task Force for
Recognizing and Averting Payment Scams within 90 days;
include the CFPB, FCC, FTC, Attorney General, OCC, Federal Reserve, NCUA,
FDIC, FinCEN and Treasury's Under Secretary for Terrorism and Financial
Crimes;
add specified financial-institution, digital-payment-network, consumer,
technology, telecommunications, payment-services, victim and other members;
require at least three meetings in the first year;
direct work on prevention, in-process disruption, information sharing,
operational coordination, reporting, law enforcement, AML, education and
possible legislative or regulatory changes;
require a public report within one year of establishment and annual updates;
and
terminate the task force three years after its initial report.
That text is a proposal, not current authority. Committee approval is not House
passage, Senate passage, enactment, establishment or appointment. The safest
relationship statement is:
existing Treasury administrative working group ~= policy prototype or parallel body; proposed TRAPS task force = possible future statutory constitution
The ~= is an inference from close structural overlap, not an official
identification.
Relationship to the rest of the fraud architecture
Keep four bodies separate unless a source supplies an explicit handoff:
Payment Fraud and Scams Working Group — Treasury-convened cross-sector
forum; two meetings now publicly confirmed.
White House Task Force to Eliminate Fraud / NFDC — benefit-program,
eligibility, federal payment-integrity and criminal-lead architecture under
Executive Order 14395.
DOJ Scam Center Strike Force — enforcement body targeting overseas
scam compounds, infrastructure and cryptocurrency proceeds.
Proposed TRAPS task force — not yet established because H.R. 4936 is not
enacted.
The second-meeting post does not establish membership overlap, shared data,
case referrals, common analytics, transferred authority or one combined fraud
control plane among them.
What would materially change this answer
a Treasury or partner-agency readout naming participants, agenda and agreed
actions;
a charter, membership list, subcommittee structure or meeting cadence;
an executed information-sharing or privacy/security instrument;
a complaint-to-signal-to-payment-interdiction operating record;
a public recommendation, report, rulemaking, supervisory action or named
pilot;
proof tying a working-group signal to a specific enforcement or recovery
result;
House and Senate passage and presidential signature of H.R. 4936 or its
counterpart; or
an official instrument merging, replacing or formally recognizing the
current working group as the TRAPS task force.
