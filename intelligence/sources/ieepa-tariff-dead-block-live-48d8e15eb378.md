# IEEPA — Tariff Dead, Block Live

Updated: 2026-08-15  
Status: factual legal map  
Rule: An emergency is not a tax charter. “Regulate importation” is not “lay duties.” Blocking is a different verb. Section 232 is a different statute. Section 122 is a different statute.

This note is the **emergency-purse** leash. [Judicial Money Map — Source Document](../sources/judicial-money-map-source-document-d2f844afe3d8.html) JM-15 is the docket. ## Short answer

*Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* and *Trump v. V.O.S. Selections, Inc.*, Nos. 24-1287 and 25-250 (Feb. 20, 2026), 607 U.S. ___:

> **Held:** IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs.

The Federal Circuit judgment against the tariffs (No. 25-250) is **affirmed**. The D.D.C. action (No. 24-1287) is **vacated and remanded with instructions to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction**. The tariff challenges belong on the CIT / Federal Circuit path.

Roberts, C.J., announced the judgment. Parts I, II–A–1, and II–B (the statutory holding that “regulate … importation” does not include the power to tax) are the opinion of the Court, joined by Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Barrett, and Jackson. Parts II–A–2 and III (major-questions overlay) are Roberts, Gorsuch, and Barrett only. Kagan, joined by Sotomayor and Jackson, concurred in the judgment on ordinary statutory tools and would not have invoked major questions. Thomas dissented. Kavanaugh dissented, joined by Thomas and Alito. **Judgment 6–3.**

The list in 50 U.S.C. § 1702(a)(1)(B) is investigate, block, regulate, direct and compel, nullify, void, prevent or prohibit — acquisition, holding, use, transfer, importation, exportation, dealing in property. **“Absent from this lengthy list of specific powers is any mention of tariffs or duties.”** Had Congress meant the tariff power, it would have said so, as it does in the statutes that actually grant duties (Trade Expansion Act § 232; Trade Act § 301).

**Same day, operating paper.** EO 14389 (Feb. 20, 2026; 91 FR 9437): the additional **ad valorem duties imposed pursuant to IEEPA** in the listed 2025–26 emergency orders “shall no longer be in effect and, as soon as practicable, shall no longer be collected.” **The national emergencies remain.** Actions that are not those IEEPA duties remain. Section 232 and section 301 duties are **unaffected**. EO 14388 (de minimis suspension) is **unaffected**.

**Same day, different statute.** Proclamation **11012** (Feb. 20, 2026; 91 FR 9339): a 10 percent ad valorem surcharge under Trade Act § 122, 19 U.S.C. § 2132. The statute lets the President proclaim a surcharge **not exceeding 15 percent** for a period **not exceeding 150 days** unless extended by Act of Congress. The proclamation itself: goods entered or withdrawn for consumption on or after 12:01 a.m. EST **February 24, 2026**, through 12:01 a.m. EDT **July 24, 2026**, unless earlier ended or extended by Act of Congress. **No extending Act retrieved at cutoff.** That is not IEEPA. A later CIT judgment on 11012 is a different object, named below.

**Claim cut.** “IEEPA is dead” is **not established**. The tariff use is dead. The block/regulate/nullify machine is the statute Congress wrote. OFAC still runs on it.

**Section 232 is not a backup IEEPA.** The Court used § 232 as the *contrast*: when Congress means duties, it says duties and attaches process. Do not treat a later § 232 action as “the Court allowed the same tariffs under another name” unless that action actually follows § 232.

## 3. What the statute says

**50 U.S.C. § 1701(a).** The § 1702 authorities may be used to deal with an **unusual and extraordinary threat** to national security, foreign policy, or the economy, originating substantially **outside** the United States, if the President **declares** a national emergency for that threat.

**50 U.S.C. § 1702(a)(1)(B)** (as the Court quotes it): the President may, by instructions, licenses, or otherwise,

> investigate, block during the pendency of an investigation, regulate, direct and compel, nullify, void, prevent or prohibit, any acquisition, holding, withholding, use, transfer, withdrawal, transportation, importation or exportation of, or dealing in, or exercising any right, power, or privilege with respect to, or transactions involving, any property in which any foreign country or a national thereof has any interest.

No “tariff.” No “duty.” No “tax.” The Court: “regulate” does not silently include the power to tax. A contrary reading would also collide with Art. I, § 9, cl. 5 (no tax on exports), because the same verb reaches exportation.

**What IEEPA still is.** The blocking and transaction-control statute used for SDN lists, Iranian/Cuban/other emergency property freezes, and OFAC licenses. *Dames & Moore v. Regan*, 453 U.S. 654, is the classic block/transfer object. The 2026 Court says that case “did not involve tariffs at all.” *Van Loon* (JM-13) is a later **property** boundary (immutable code). Different object.

## 4. What the Court did with the two cases

| Case | Court below | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **No. 25-250** *V.O.S. Selections* (importers + 12 States) | CIT summary judgment for plaintiffs; Federal Circuit, en banc, affirmed in relevant part: “regulate … importation” does not authorize unbounded tariffs. 149 F. 4th 1312. | **Affirmed.** |
| **No. 24-1287** *Learning Resources* (two small businesses) | D.D.C. denied transfer to CIT, granted a PI. | **Vacated and remanded to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction.** Wrong court. |

The holding on IEEPA’s meaning is the same object in both. The jurisdictional cut is why D.D.C. does not keep a parallel tariff docket. The hook is **28 U.S.C. § 1581(i)(1)**: the CIT has exclusive jurisdiction of civil actions arising out of any law providing for tariffs or duties. That is why *V.O.S.* was the merits path and *Learning Resources* was dismissed.

**Refunds / liquidations.** The majority does not order a refund, compute a sum, or prescribe a customs-liquidation path. EO 14389 says collection of the listed IEEPA duties shall end as soon as practicable. Whether particular entries are reliquidated is a CIT/CBP fact. **Refunds are not computed here.**

## 5. EO 14389 — what it ends and what it keeps

Signed **2026-02-20**. Published Feb. 25. 91 FR 9437.

**Ends:** additional ad valorem duties imposed **pursuant to IEEPA** in EOs 14193, 14194, 14195, 14245, 14257, 14323, 14329, 14380, and 14382 (as amended). Agencies shall terminate collection as soon as practicable. HTS modifications if needed.

**Keeps:** the national emergencies those orders declared or described. Any non-duty action taken to address those emergencies. Section 232 duties. Section 301 duties. The Feb. 20, 2026 de minimis EO. The Feb. 20, 2026 temporary-import-surcharge proclamation.

Iran’s EO 14382 is on that list. The **duty** dies. The **emergency** does not. The Iran War Powers map already refused to treat 14382 as a force grant. This map refuses to treat the surviving emergency as a hidden tariff.

## 5A. Proc. 11012 — the § 122 instrument (not IEEPA)

White House proclamation of **February 20, 2026**. Published February 25. **91 FR 9339**. FR Doc. 2026-03824.

Finds “fundamental international payments problems” and a “large and serious” U.S. balance-of-payments deficit. Imposes a **10 percent ad valorem** surcharge, **150 days**, effective **12:01 a.m. EST February 24, 2026**, through **12:01 a.m. EDT July 24, 2026**, unless earlier ended or “extended by an Act of the Congress.” Statute cap is 15 percent / 150 days (19 U.S.C. § 2132(a)). Treated as a regular customs duty. Substantial product exceptions (proclamation ¶ 14 / Annexes I–II), including articles already under § 232.

**Extension by Act of Congress: not retrieved at cutoff.** Do not write that the surcharge is still being collected after July 24, and do not write that it died, without a later official instrument.

**Later CIT object — named, not walked.** *Oregon v. United States* / *Burlap and Barrel, Inc. v. United States*, Slip Op. 26-47 (CIT May 7, 2026): summary judgment for the State of Washington and two private importers (Burlap and Barrel, Inc.; Basic Fun, Inc.). The judgment declared Proc. 11012 “invalid as contrary to law” and permanently enjoined it **as to those three importer plaintiffs**. Other state plaintiffs were dismissed for lack of standing. The court **declined a universal injunction**. Stanceu dissented. Slip Op. 26-53 (CIT May 20, 2026) denied the Government’s motion to stay that judgment. A Federal Circuit administrative stay was entered May 12, 2026, pending that court’s consideration of a stay. **Federal Circuit merits, the later stay disposition, and nationwide post–July 24 collection status are not computed here.** This is a § 122 fight. It does not revive IEEPA-as-tariff.

## 7. Entity Theory read

The federal group does not hold one “emergency power.” It holds a **declaration** (NEA — a switch, not a tax charter), a **verb list** (IEEPA), and **separate duty statutes** (§ 232, § 301, § 122) that actually say “duty.”

Congress kept the tax charter. The President used IEEPA as if “regulate importation” were a silent transfer of that charter. The Court read the list and found no such transfer. That is the same error as treating an EO as repeal of an organic act, or a concurrent resolution as a law: **the form has to match the power.**

EO 14389 is the accountant’s tell. Collection of the unauthorized duty stops. The emergency person stays on the books. Blocking can continue. A later § 232 or § 301 action, if it happens, is a **new instrument** with its own predicates. Do not fold it back into “the Court let the tariffs survive.”

The domestic purse (ICA / rescission) and the foreign force grant (War Powers) are still other leashes.
