Federal strategic planning — legal and operating framework
Cutoff: 2026-07-22
Purpose: define what a federal agency strategic plan is, which entities owe one, how it connects to budgets and performance reports, and which dates must remain separate.
Bottom line
The federal strategic-plan system is a recurring management and accountability cycle, not a shelf of vision documents.
Agency Strategic Plan: the administration-term blueprint. The current federal cycle is generally titled FY 2026–2030.
Agency Performance Plan (APP): the two-fiscal-year operating plan that translates strategic objectives into measurable performance goals.
Agency Performance Report (APR): the backward-looking annual report on progress, barriers, and results.
Agency Priority Goals (APGs): a small set of roughly 24-month execution goals for major agencies.
Cross-Agency Priority Goals / President's Management Agenda: the government-wide control layer for goals that cut across agency boundaries.
Learning Agenda / Evidence-Building Plan: the multi-year list of questions the agency needs answered to make better policy and operating choices.
Annual Evaluation Plan: the next fiscal year's evidence-building work.
Strategic Review: the annual management process that tests progress, risks, contributing programs, and prospects for improvement against the strategic objectives.
A strategic goal is a statement of intended direction. It is not proof of funding, implementation, legal authority, milestone completion, or achieved result.
1. Governing law
5 U.S.C. § 306 — agency strategic plans
The current U.S. Code text requires the head of each covered agency to publish a strategic plan on the first Monday in February of the year after a presidential term begins. For the present cycle, that date was 2026-02-02.
The plan must include, among other things:
the agency mission;
general and outcome-oriented goals and objectives;
the tie to federal government priority goals;
the processes, skills, technology, people, capital, information, and other resources needed to reach the goals;
interagency work needed to reach agency and government-wide goals;
congressional and stakeholder input;
the tie between strategic objectives, performance goals, and APGs;
external factors that could affect results;
planned program evaluations; and
where applicable, the Evidence Act learning agenda and capacity assessment.
The plan must cover at least four years following the fiscal year in which it is submitted. An annual performance plan cannot cover a fiscal year outside a current strategic plan.
Entity boundary
For § 306, agency means an Executive agency under 5 U.S.C. § 105, but the statute expressly excludes:
Central Intelligence Agency;
Government Accountability Office;
United States Postal Service; and
Postal Regulatory Commission.
Those entities may publish their own strategic documents, but they do not sit inside this particular § 306 duty. Legislative and judicial bodies likewise need a separate authority/status call. The Observatory entity chart therefore cannot be treated as a list of 379 identical GPRA filers.
5 U.S.C. § 312 — evidence-building plan
Section 312 requires a systematic plan for identifying and addressing priority policy questions and ties an annual evaluation plan to the agency performance-plan cycle. This is why a serious corpus should capture each agency's learning agenda alongside its strategic plan.
Federal Agency Performance Act of 2024
Public Law 118-190, approved 2024-12-23, strengthened the annual strategic-review layer. Agencies must review progress and the likelihood of reaching each covered strategic goal or objective, identify risks and impediments, and identify improvement prospects and strategies for goals at greatest risk.
GAO-26-108516, released in June 2026, found that OMB's August 2025 guidance and the four sampled agencies had not yet fully implemented all new requirements. GAO reported that DHS, Treasury, and GSA planned 2026 strategic reviews, while State had not confirmed that it would conduct one at the time of GAO's work. This is a current implementation finding, not a conclusion about every agency.
2. OMB operating guidance
The current operating manual is OMB Circular A-11, Part 6.
Section 200 — federal performance cycle
A-11 § 200 separates the cycle into:
planning: strategic plans translated into annual performance plans and APGs;
evidence, evaluation, analysis, and review: internal routines using quantitative and qualitative evidence;
reporting: quarterly APG updates and annual performance reports, with core documents posted through Performance.gov and Evaluation.gov.
OMB defines an APP as a public plan for the fiscal year in which it is submitted and the following fiscal year. A performance goal requires an indicator, a target, and a time period. An APG is a limited near-term goal, usually one of two to four, meant to be accomplished within roughly 24 months and to rely mainly on agency implementation rather than new legislation or resources.
OMB also says drafting the Agency Strategic Plan, APP, and APR is an inherently governmental function. Contractors may support the responsible federal officials, but the plans themselves must be prepared by federal employees.
Section 230 — current plan cycle
A-11 § 230 states directly that the plan published in February 2026 must cover at least FY 2026 through FY 2030. It frames the plan as the agency's account of long-term aims, the actions needed to reach them, and the challenges and risks that may block results.
Section 220 — government-wide goals
A-11 § 220 treats the President's Management Agenda (PMA) and Cross-Agency Priority Goals as the government-wide layer. The PMA is revised early in a presidential term; CAP goals coordinate and publicly track work that crosses agency boundaries.
The current President's Management Agenda, issued through OMB Memorandum M-26-03 on 2025-12-08, groups its goals under three headings:
shrink the government and eliminate waste;
ensure accountability for Americans; and
deliver results and buy American.
The live PMA page is the authority for the current goal/objective wording and any later progress updates.

6. Claim controls
The plan says it will is not the agency did.
Current through FY 2026 is not the same as the new FY 2026–2030 plan was published.
Parent plan covers the component is not the component has its own plan.
APG is not the full agency mission; it is a narrow execution subset.
Target is not result.
Budget request is not appropriation and not obligation.
Executive direction is not the same legal state as a completed statutory reorganization.
A strategic plan can outlive the leadership that wrote it; preserve the publication and revision clocks before assigning it to a current officeholder.
