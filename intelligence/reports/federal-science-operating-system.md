## Pre-2025 statutory hinge

### 2024-07-09 — ADVANCE Act supplies fusion definitions and a federal rule clock

**Entity:** Congress; President; Nuclear Regulatory Commission
**Clock:** statute / federal rulemaking
**State after:** Atomic Energy Act definitions amended; December 31, 2027 rule deadline created

Section 205 of Public Law 118-67 added the term `fusion machine` to the Atomic
Energy Act and brought radioactive material produced by a fusion machine into
the byproduct-material definition. It also directs NRC to complete a
technology-inclusive framework, optional for new fusion-machine applicants, by
December 31, 2027. This statutory clock does not itself create a final rule,
an Agreement State framework or a project license.

**Sources:** [Public Law 118-67](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PLAW-118publ67) · [NRC — Fusion and the ADVANCE Act](https://www.nrc.gov/materials/fusion/mass-production-status) · [NRC fusion FAQ](https://www.nrc.gov/materials/fusion/faq)

## 2025

### 2025-01-20 — national energy emergency declared

**Entity:** President; executive departments and agencies  
**Clock:** emergency / energy infrastructure  
**State after:** active national-emergency framework

Executive Order 14156 identifies inadequate energy production, transportation and infrastructure as an unusual and extraordinary threat. It directs agencies to use lawful emergency permitting and other authorities to facilitate energy supply. The order repeatedly limits action to what existing law permits.

**Source:** [White House — Declaring a National Energy Emergency](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/declaring-a-national-energy-emergency/)

### 2025-04-23 — accreditation and institutional-value clock separated from research funding

**Entity:** President; Department of Education; recognized accreditors  
**Clock:** accreditation / Title IV institutional eligibility  
**State after:** policy direction toward student-oriented quality and value

Executive Order 14279 directs the Education Department to realign recognition of accreditors toward high-quality, high-value programs and new educational models. This is an institutional-eligibility and student-aid lane. It is adjacent to, but legally distinct from, agency R&D awards.

**Source:** [White House — Executive Order 14279 PDF](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/eo-14279.pdf)

### 2025-07-23 — qualifying data-center infrastructure receives an acceleration lane

**Entity:** President; Commerce; Energy; Interior; Defense; EPA; CEQ; Permitting Council  
**Clock:** siting / finance / permitting  
**State after:** federal priority and support pathway

Executive Order 14318 defines qualifying projects using alternative thresholds: at least $500 million in committed capital expenditure, more than 100 MW of incremental electric load, protection of national security, or designation by a named Secretary. It directs an initiative that may use loans, guarantees, grants, tax incentives and offtake agreements, plus federal permitting and land actions.

The order covers associated power and technology components. It does not identify every qualifying project or approve any particular parcel.

**Source:** [White House — Accelerating Federal Permitting of Data Center Infrastructure](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/accelerating-federal-permitting-of-data-center-infrastructure/)

### 2025-08-07 — discretionary grant oversight shifts toward priority and progress review

**Entity:** President; OMB; grantmaking agencies  
**Clock:** award design / annual review / termination  
**State after:** senior-appointee review and stronger progress controls directed

Executive Order 14332 directs pre-issuance senior-appointee review of funding opportunities and discretionary awards, annual review for consistency with agency priorities and substantial progress, and revised terms permitting termination when an award no longer advances agency priorities or the national interest. It also directs tighter drawdown authorization and limits on facilities-and-administration costs.

**Source:** [White House — Improving Oversight of Federal Grantmaking](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/08/improving-oversight-of-federal-grantmaking/)

### 2025-11-24 — Genesis Mission chartered

**Entity:** President; DOE; OSTP; OMB; NSTC; participating agencies  
**Clock:** platform / mission / interagency integration  
**State after:** operative executive direction

Executive Order 14363 establishes the Genesis Mission and directs DOE to establish the American Science and Security Platform. The platform is to integrate:

- federal and commercial high-performance computing;
- secure cloud AI environments;
- AI agents and scientific foundation models;
- federal, proprietary, open and synthetic datasets;
- autonomous and AI-augmented experimentation and manufacturing tools.

The order also directs metadata and provenance work; coordinated funding opportunities and prizes; fellowships, internships and apprenticeships; standard partnership, data-use and model-sharing agreements; and policies for ownership, licensing, trade secrets and commercialization of AI-directed experimental results.

Every major command is bounded by applicable law, security requirements and available appropriations.

**Source:** [Federal Register — EO 14363](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/28/2025-21665/launching-the-genesis-mission)

## 2026

### 2026-01-20 — DOE frames Tribes as data-center counterparties

**Entity:** DOE Office of Indian Energy; Tribal Nations and enterprises  
**Clock:** tribal market development  
**State after:** federal opportunity framing

DOE identifies possible tribal roles beyond land leasing: power sales, long-term operations and infrastructure ownership. It also identifies water, community impacts, transmission, interconnection and fiber as constraints. No Tribe is committed by this publication.

**Source:** [DOE — Data Centers: Exploring the Opportunity for Tribes](https://www.energy.gov/indianenergy/articles/data-centers-exploring-opportunity-tribes)

### 2026-01-23 — Fusion Energy Sciences receives enacted FY2026 account funding

**Entity:** Congress; President; DOE Office of Science; Fusion Energy Sciences
**Clock:** appropriation / account allocation
**State after:** enacted FY2026 funding; program and project states remain separate

Public Law 119-74 enacted the FY2026 Energy and Water division. The final
explanatory table allocates $805.657 million to Fusion Energy Sciences:
$634.973 million for research and $170.684 million for U.S. ITER construction.
The account total demonstrates appropriated public research capital. It does
not fund every facility, public-private program or 2030s milestone later mapped
by DOE.

**Sources:** [Public Law 119-74](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ74/pdf/PLAW-119publ74.pdf) · [FY2026 Energy and Water explanatory statement](https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/download/fy26-ewd-jes)

### 2026-01-29 — initial Bull Run fusion-license application reported submitted

**Entity:** Type One Energy; Tennessee Valley Authority; Tennessee Department of Environment and Conservation
**Clock:** project licensing / initial application
**State after:** company-reported initial byproduct-material application submitted

Type One Energy reports that it and TVA made an initial submission in
preparation for licensing the Bull Run project under Tennessee's Agreement
State authority. The announcement says the application lays groundwork for
subsequent submissions. The source does not establish completeness acceptance,
a completed review, an issued license, inspection, operating authorization or
electricity production.

This project clock is parallel to NRC's federal rulemaking clock; NRC states
that commercial fusion development is already occurring in and regulated by
Agreement States.

**Sources:** [Type One — initial Bull Run licensing application](https://typeoneenergy.com/insights-announcements/type-one-energy-initial-licensing-application-bull-run/) · [NRC fusion FAQ](https://www.nrc.gov/materials/fusion/faq)

### 2026-02-26 — NRC opens the proposed federal fusion-machine framework

**Entity:** Nuclear Regulatory Commission
**Clock:** proposed rule / federal licensing framework
**State after:** proposed rule and draft consolidated guidance published

NRC published a proposed technology-neutral framework for fusion machines and
draft consolidated materials-licensing guidance. The 90-day comment period
closed May 27. Proposal, final rule, effective date, license application, issued
license and operating authorization remain separate states. Federal
rulemaking, Agreement State frameworks and individual project applications are
parallel clocks, not one mandatory sequence.

**Source:** [NRC — fusion-machine rulemaking status](https://www.nrc.gov/materials/fusion/rulemaking-status)

### 2026-03-17 — Genesis RFA defines the fusion DCP work program

**Entity:** DOE; Office of Science; Genesis Mission
**Clock:** funding opportunity / application process
**State after:** cross-domain RFA opened; no recipient award or platform access

DOE announced an anticipated $293.76 million across all Genesis subjects, not
fusion alone. The RFA describes Phase I awards of $500,000–$750,000 for nine
months and Phase II awards of $6–$15 million over three years. It makes the
fusion DCP concrete through materials datasets, digital twins, real-time
control, tritium/accountancy models, blanket databases, whole-plant repositories
and remote-maintenance tools. Laboratory work may use Field Work Proposals and
non-laboratory awards may use Other Transaction Authority, so one federal award
database cannot by itself prove complete execution.

**Sources:** [DOE RFA announcement](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-announces-293-million-funding-support-genesis-mission-national-science) · [full RFA](https://science.osti.gov/-/media/grants/pdf/foas/2026/DE-FOA-0003612-000003.pdf)

### 2026-04-20 — grid equipment enters the DPA Title III capacity lane

**Entity:** President; Secretary of Energy  
**Clock:** industrial capacity / grid  
**State after:** DPA section 303 mobilization authority activated for the named capacity field

The presidential determination finds transformers, transmission lines, conductors, substations, high-voltage circuit breakers, power electronics and related inputs essential to national defense. It authorizes purchases, commitments and financial instruments to expand domestic capability.

The object is grid infrastructure and its supply chain—not every project that consumes electricity.

**Source:** [White House — DPA grid-infrastructure determination](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/04/presidential-determination-pursuant-to-section-303-of-the-defense-production-act-of-1950-as-amended-on-grid-infrastructure-equipment-and-supply-chain-capacity/)

### 2026-04-28 — CFS enters the PJM interconnection process

**Entity:** Commonwealth Fusion Systems; PJM Interconnection
**Clock:** project grid application
**State after:** company application publicly announced; fusion independently confirmed among Cycle 1 applicants

CFS says it submitted the proposed ARC/Fall Line plant to PJM's Cycle 1
interconnection process. PJM independently confirmed that fusion was among the
more than 800 Cycle 1 project applications. Application, queue validation,
studies, executed agreement, upgrades, synchronization and export remain

evidence that ARC is connected or operating.

**Sources:** [CFS application announcement](https://cfs.energy/news-and-media/commonwealth-fusion-systems-becomes-first-fusion-company-to-apply-to-pjm-interconnection-the-largest-u.s.-wholesale-electricity-market) · [PJM confirmation](https://insidelines.pjm.com/over-800-new-generation-projects-seek-to-connect-under-pjms-reformed-process/)

### 2026-05-14 — NSF announces X-Labs

**Entity:** National Science Foundation

NSF announces up to $1.5 billion over a decade for independent, milestone-driven research teams outside traditional institutions. ### 2026-06-04 — U.S.–Japan Genesis partnership includes fusion

**Entity:** DOE; Japan MEXT; Japan METI; participating laboratories and institutions
**Clock:** international partnership plan / future appropriations
**State after:** planned five-year collaboration announced

DOE and the Japanese ministries announced a planned combined $1 billion over
five years, split equally and subject to future appropriations. Eleven joint
teams are intended to connect twelve DOE national laboratories, one Office of
Science user facility and twelve Japanese institutions across fusion, quantum,

The announcement is an institutional and funding plan, not proof of a fusion

**Source:** [DOE — U.S.–Japan Genesis partnership](https://www.energy.gov/articles/united-states-and-japan-announce-historic-1-billion-partnership-under-president-trumps)

### 2026-06-09 — DOE finalizes the fusion roadmap

**Entity:** DOE Office of Fusion; Fusion Energy Sciences; industry; national laboratories; universities
**Clock:** strategy / metrics / implementation coordination
**State after:** final Build-Innovate-Grow roadmap published

DOE's 122-page Fusion Science and Technology Roadmap combines public
infrastructure, foundational research, high-performance computing, AI, digital
twins, public-private programs, supply chains, workforce, standards and
commercialization. It maps six technical challenge areas and near-, mid- and
long-term milestones toward private pilot plants and grid delivery in the
mid-2030s.

The roadmap expressly commits no specific funding. Support remains contingent
on future public-private partnerships and congressional appropriations. Its
facility map also distinguishes existing, enhanced, under-construction,
planned and gap-identified capabilities.

On the same date, Tennessee's technology-neutral Agreement State fusion-machine
rules became effective. Type One had already reported an initial project
application on January 29; the effective framework, application, review and
license disposition remain separate states.

**Sources:** [DOE — finalized roadmap release](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-releases-finalized-fusion-science-and-technology-roadmap-accelerate) · [DOE — full roadmap](https://www.energy.gov/documents/fusion-st-roadmap-060926pdf) · [Tennessee — fusion regulations](https://www.tn.gov/environment/news/2026/6/1/tn-powers-up-first-in-the-nation-fusion-regulations-june-9.html)

### 2026-06-18 — FERC opens the six-grid-operator speed-to-power proceeding

**Entity:** FERC; six jurisdictional regional grid operators; transmission owners  
**Clock:** tariff / interconnection / ratepayer protection  
**State after:** show-cause and tariff-reform proceedings opened

FERC directs all six regional operators to justify or reform large-load tariffs. ### 2026-06-19 — WHAM directly converts plasma kinetic energy to electricity

**Entity:** Realta Fusion; University of Wisconsin–Madison; WHAM
**Clock:** physical experiment / power-conversion rung
**State after:** several-hundred-watt-scale direct-energy conversion demonstrated

A converter installed on WHAM drew multiple amps at about 100 volts during
plasma operation. Realta says this could illuminate a few lightbulbs, but also
states that most converted energy was supplied plasma-heating input and that
the experiment was neither net-electricity production nor large-scale
conversion of fusion-born power. This is a physical intermediate rung between
plasma operation and future power conversion, below engineering gain and grid
delivery. The experiment time is not public; the technical release was
published June 30 at 7:00 a.m. ET.

**Sources:** [Realta technical account](https://realtafusion.com/fusion-first-realta-demos-direct-energy-conversion/) · [exact publication clock](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/realta-fusion-becomes-first-commercial-fusion-company-to-convert-plasma-energy-into-electricity-302813632.html)

### 2026-06-20 — NIF records its eleventh ignition

**Entity:** Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory; National Ignition Facility
**Clock:** experimental result / target-gain measurement
**State after:** repeated laboratory ignition record extended

LLNL reports a 7.9 MJ yield with ±0.4 MJ measurement uncertainty and
approximately 3.8 target gain. Target gain compares fusion yield with laser
energy delivered to the target. It does not count the facility's total energy
use and is not engineering breakeven, electricity or grid delivery.

**Source:** [LLNL — achieving fusion ignition](https://lasers.llnl.gov/science/achieving-fusion-ignition)

### 2026-07-21 — `Science: A New Golden Age` transmitted

**Entity:** OSTP Director Michael Kratsios; President  
**Clock:** doctrine / recommendation  
**State after:** official White House design map

The report calls for:

- prioritizing individual scientists over legacy institutions;
- portable fellowships and broader performer eligibility;
- fast grants, long-duration grants, prizes, advance market commitments, regranting and experimental funding mechanisms;
- metascience units and portfolio evaluation;
- industry–university–federal research partnerships;
- open access to federal and university facilities;
- practical technical training and alternative scientific-career paths;
- Genesis-scale compute, data, model and autonomous-laboratory infrastructure.

Its horizon section imagines blockchain contribution records, DAOs, distributed ledgers, smart-contract milestone payments, agent microtransactions and cloud laboratories. The report expressly says the combined system does not yet exist in mature form.

**Source:** [White House — Science: A New Golden Age](https://www.whitehouse.gov/science/)

### 2026-07-21 — FY2028 memorandum converts doctrine into agency instructions

**Entity:** OSTP; OMB; executive departments and agencies  
**Clock:** budget formulation / program implementation  
**State after:** operative administrative guidance

NSTM-5 / M-26-16 directs agencies to reflect the priorities in FY2028 budget submissions. It identifies Genesis support as a central R&D priority and directs attention to scientific foundation models, dataset curation, industrial-scale data capture, open instrument interfaces, standardized data formats, advanced compute and autonomous closed-loop laboratories.

The memorandum also instructs agencies to consider portable direct funding, cross-sector placements, milestone reviews, fast grants, prizes, advanced market commitments, regranting, quadratic funding and eigenfunding. These mechanisms remain bounded by agency authority, conflict rules, scientific rigor and appropriations.

**Source:** [White House — NSTM-5 / M-26-16](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/White-House-Fiscal-Year-2028-RD-Priorities-Memorandum.pdf)

### 2026-08 — National Security Science & Technology Strategy becomes public

**Entity:** President; White House Office of Science and Technology Policy
**Clock:** strategy / doctrine; cover month only; public availability verified 2026-08-17
**State after:** national-security mission, resilience, acceleration and protection layer added to federal S&T doctrine

The 24-page strategy supports the 2025 National Security Strategy, says it
satisfies 42 U.S.C. § 19221 and complements NSTM-5 FY2028 R&D priorities. It
organizes national-security S&T around four qualities—focused, resilient, agile
and secure—and five means: workforce, R&D infrastructure, federal R&D funding,
non-Federal partnerships and allies or partners.

The strategy prioritizes battlefield and homeland missions, a national
resilience architecture, acquisition and regulatory acceleration, research
security, inbound and outbound investment control, export and sensitive-data
protection, development-stage funding logic and later OSTP/NSC CET-specific
plans. Appendix A updates the 14-family CET list; Appendix B maps those
families to battlefield, homeland and transformative needs.

The strategy contains no appropriation, agency-by-agency owner matrix,
implementation deadline, metric or reporting cadence. The cover says only
August 2026. PDF creation metadata dated August 14 is not an authoritative
publication, signature or transmittal clock and must not be point-charted.

**Sources:** [official PDF](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/NSSTS-082026.pdf) · full-reading delta · preserved source record

## Forward clocks created by the source instruments

### Unresolved — § 19221 findings report and Congressional transmittal

Congress records transmission of the 2025 National Security Strategy on
2025-12-04. The 90-day calendar date was 2026-03-04. The public August NSSTS
does not include a signed transmittal, receipt record or the separate findings
report contemplated by 42 U.S.C. § 19221. An earlier or nonpublic submission
may exist; do not convert public non-detection into a final noncompliance ruling.

### 2026-08-21 — Genesis initial-operating-capability target

EO 14363 directs DOE, within 270 days of November 24, 2025 and subject to
available appropriations, to seek an initial operating capability of the
American Science and Security Platform for at least one national challenge.
The August 15 fusion thread does not identify the challenge, platform assets,
users, access rules, completed test or operating result and therefore does not
satisfy this clock by itself.

### 2026-10-19 — qualifying agency action plans due

The memorandum requires the head of each agency with at least $3 billion in FY2026 R&D budget authority to submit an action plan within 90 days. Each plan must identify specific actions, implementation timelines and responsible offices for the four R&D priority-practice groups. October 19 is the calendar date 90 days after July 21; verify any OMB interpretation or extension before treating a later filing as delinquent.

### FY2028 budget process — program requests become appropriations asks

Agency submissions should show proposed shifts in portfolio composition, Genesis contributions, infrastructure investment, talent programs, funding pilots, cost shares and regional co-location. A budget request is not an appropriation, award or operating capability.

### 2026-11-24 — first Genesis annual report due under EO 14363

EO 14363 requires DOE to report within one year of the order and annually thereafter on platform status, integration, user engagement, measurable outcomes, partnerships and authority needs. Public availability is not guaranteed by the order.

### 2027-12-31 — ADVANCE Act fusion-rulemaking deadline

Section 205 of Public Law 118-67 directs NRC to complete a technology-inclusive
regulatory framework, optional for new fusion-machine applicants, by this date.
A final rule, its effective date, Agreement State implementation and individual
project dispositions must still be recorded separately.

## Clock rule

The companion
`FEDERAL_FUSION_DATE_TIME_PLACE_LEDGER_2020-2035.md`
preserves each source clock, timezone, locus, precision defect and later D.C.
chart convention without calculating or interpreting a chart.

The object-level states, eight-company field map and award-accounting chain
live in
`FEDERAL_FUSION_PROJECT_CAPITAL_REGULATORY_REGISTRY_2022-2035.md`.

Never compress these into one “launch” date:

`report -> memorandum -> agency plan -> budget request -> appropriation -> funding opportunity -> award or contract -> platform integration -> test -> operating capability -> verified result -> payment or commercialization`

For fusion, preserve interacting but parallel clocks:

- `communications -> roadmap -> funded mechanism -> verified milestone`;
- `federal statutory direction -> proposed rule -> final rule -> effective rule`;
- `Agreement State framework` and `project application -> completeness -> review -> license -> inspection -> operating authorization`;
- `site control -> permits -> construction -> equipment -> commissioning`;
- `queue/request -> studies -> interconnection agreement -> upgrades -> synchronization -> meter -> export`;
- `reaction -> ignition -> separately measured target/plasma gain -> engineering gain -> heat -> gross electricity -> net electricity -> sustained operation`.

Those lanes converge at a named project's licensed, interconnected and measured
commercial operation; none is evidence that the others have completed.
