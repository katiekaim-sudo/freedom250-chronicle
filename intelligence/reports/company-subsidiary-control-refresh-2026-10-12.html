Who actually owns and controls the companies in the October restart

In plain English

Question: Which parents, subsidiaries, banks, operating companies, regulated firms and temporary merger vehicles actually hold the powers behind the company names that appeared in the October government and money news?

Answer: The brand is almost never the whole legal actor. American Express was reached through three companies and two regulators. Evernorth's merger closed, but Ripple is a minority shareholder rather than its parent. Securitize Stocks are entitlements held through Securitize Markets, not direct shares on the issuers' books. Isabella has every approval for Grand River but the banks remain separate until the November 2 closing. Vistra's parent, nuclear owner and nuclear operator are separate, and DOE has not named the loan borrower.

1. American Express: three legal persons, two federal supervisors

The October 8 orders resolve the group structure relevant to the enforcement:

American Express Company — registered bank holding company
  -> owns and controls American Express Travel Related Services Company, Inc.
       — itself a registered bank holding company
       -> owns American Express National Bank
            — national bank / insured depository institution

The Federal Reserve supervises the two holding companies and ordered board, enterprise BSA/AML and TRS OFAC plans. OCC supervises the national bank and ordered the bank-level compliance committee and action plan while imposing the $350 million penalty. The parent order reaches enterprise risk management; it does not turn the parent or TRS into the bank. The bank's deposits, cards, customers and SAR duties remain bank-level objects.

Primary sources: Federal Reserve order · OCC bank order

2. Amidi / Plug and Play / Noematrix: the named penalty leaves one fund unnamed

Treasury penalized Amidi, LLC. It says an unnamed controlled foreign entity, described as a Chinese fund, invested about $92,478 in Shanghai Qiongche Intelligent Technology Company Limited (Noematrix). Treasury separately says Amidi is the parent of the organization doing business as Plug and Play Tech Center.

The public chain is therefore:

Amidi, LLC
  -> controls unnamed Chinese fund
       -> made minority portfolio investment in Noematrix
  -> parent of organization doing business as Plug and Play Tech Center

The penalty belongs to Amidi for a notification failure. The release does not establish that Plug and Play made the investment, that Amidi owns Noematrix or that the investment was prohibited. The exact fund name and ownership percentage remain public gaps.

Primary source: Treasury OISP penalty

3. Isabella / Grand River: approved, scheduled, not yet combined

The October 6 filing says all regulatory approvals and the Grand River shareholder vote are complete, with closing expected November 2. Until closing, the two holding companies and two banks remain separate.

The contract sequence is:

Isabella Bank Corporation
  -> owns Isabella Bank
  -> owns temporary 401 Merger Sub, Inc.

Grand River Commerce, Inc.
  -> owns Grand River Bank

at closing:
401 Merger Sub -> merges into Grand River Commerce
Grand River Commerce -> merges into Isabella Bank Corporation
Grand River Bank -> merges into Isabella Bank

Regulatory approval is not the merger. The November 2 closing, legal-effective time, bank-merger effective time and December systems conversion remain separate receipts.

Primary sources: October 6 filing · merger agreement

4. Evernorth: Ripple contributed, but does not parent the public company

The October 9 SEC filing proves the business combination closed. Evernorth Holdings Inc. is the Nevada public company. Pathfinder Digital Assets LLC survived as a wholly owned subsidiary, and the former SPAC survived as another wholly owned subsidiary. Evernorth Holdings is the sole managing member of Pathfinder and controls its affairs; other unit holders generally hold economic rights without voting rights.

Ripple was a contributor and is a shareholder, not Evernorth's parent. At closing the filing reported:

  • Ripple Labs: 7.1% of voting Class A;
  • Chris Larsen: 9.9% of voting Class A on the filing's beneficial-ownership basis, including Ripple/related-party shares;
  • SBI Holdings-linked entities: 39.9% of voting Class A and 9.0% of non-voting Class C; and
  • Arrington XRP Capital Fund: 19.9% of voting Class A and 91.0% of non-voting Class C.

The exchange mechanics expressly capped Ripple and affiliates below 9.9% of combined voting power. Evernorth also has a BitGo Bank & Trust custody agreement. None of these facts makes Ripple, SBI, Arrington, BitGo or XRP the issuer of Evernorth common stock or the sole controller of the public company.

Primary source: Evernorth October 9 Form 8-K

5. Securitize: product operation runs through regulated subsidiaries

SEC filings name Securitize Corp. as the public parent and list its subsidiaries. The October 8 product release says Securitize Markets, LLC holds the Securitize Stocks security entitlements. Holders are not registered shareholders of Apple, Microsoft or the other underlying issuers unless they convert where available. The underlying issuers did not sponsor or endorse the tokens.

The operative product chain is:

Securitize Corp. — public parent
  -> Securitize Markets, LLC — broker-dealer / ATS; holds entitlement layer
  -> Securitize Transfer Agent, LLC — registered transfer-agent subsidiary
  -> other named fund-service, adviser, identity and regional subsidiaries

Securitize Markets entitlement
  -> backed 1:1 by underlying share
  -> token moves on Solana
  -> Jump Trading supplies launch market making
  -> USDC is the announced transaction currency
  -> issuer transfer agent remains the direct-share register

The existing Solana PropAMM and Securitize broker-dealer launch are the current operating route. NYSE's planned ATS, OKXICE TSV, future 24/7 trading, Aave use and Ripple Prime support are forward-looking. Solana is the network, not the security issuer, transfer agent or shareholder record.

Primary sources: October 8 Securitize release · SEC subsidiary list

6. Vistra nuclear: parent, owner, operator and federal lender are different jobs

Vistra's SEC subsidiary list names Energy Harbor Nuclear Generation LLC and Vistra Operations Company LLC separately. NRC records identify Energy Harbor Nuclear Generation as owner and Vistra Operations as operator for the PJM nuclear plants following the 2024 transfer. Vistra Corp. is the public parent. The NRC's future uprate and licence-renewal schedules attach to Vistra Operations.

DOE's October 5 conditional commitment names Vistra and three plants but does not publicly identify the borrower, guarantors, plant-specific allocations or definitive financing documents. It would be wrong to book $4.2 billion to Vistra Corp., Energy Harbor Nuclear Generation or each plant without the loan agreement.

Primary sources: SEC subsidiary list · NRC transfer notice · DOE conditional commitment

7. SPR exchanges: the brands are named, the contracting subsidiaries are not

DOE names “ExxonMobil” and “BP America” for exchanges up to two million barrels each. The reached public record does not include the exchange agreements or exact borrowing/refining legal entities. ExxonMobil's July redomiciliation also makes brand shorthand hazardous: ExxonMobil Holdings Corporation is the new successor registrant while Exxon Mobil Corporation remains primary obligor on the cited notes.

Do not assign the SPR receivable, crude title or premium-barrel duty to a parent or operating subsidiary until DOE publishes the contracts. Authorization is real; the exact corporate counterparty remains unresolved.

Primary sources: DOE exchange announcement · ExxonMobil redomiciliation filing

Bottom line

The October company story is not “consolidation everywhere.” It is a more precise pattern:

  • supervisors climb from bank to group without merging legal persons;
  • merger structures use temporary subsidiaries and staged extinguishment;
  • public-company ownership can be concentrated without a contributor becoming the parent;
  • regulated products run through exact licensed subsidiaries;
  • plant ownership, plant operation and federal borrowing remain separate; and
  • government announcements often name brands before publishing the legal counterparty.

That is the subsidiary constitution the next banking wave must carry forward.
