PRWORA Reinterpretation Campaign and State Reporting Map — 1996–2026
In plain English
Question: How did the September 1, 2026 Justice Department opinion change what states must report and know, and which data links and results remain unproved?
Answer: A September 1, 2026 Justice Department legal opinion reads a 1996 welfare-law reporting duty to cover all of a participating state's agencies, not just the welfare office. It also drops the old rule that a final removal order was needed before the state 'knew'. No new procedure or actual report has followed yet.
Evidence checked through: 2026-09-02
The detailed record below keeps the legal wording, source limits and open questions.
Controlling finding
The September 1, 2026 OLC opinion is not an isolated reporting adjustment. It
is the latest object in a coordinated 2025–2026 Executive Branch campaign to
replace several Clinton-era interpretations that had narrowed PRWORA.
Across the campaign, the Administration has revisited:
what counts as a Federal public benefit;
whether mandatory and discretionary spending programs are covered;
which noncitizens remain eligible;
how PRWORA interacts with program-specific law;
which governmental actors must verify and report;
what it means for a government to “know” a person is not lawfully present;
and
how identity and eligibility records can affect benefits, housing, tax
credits, State reporting and later immigration administration.
The September opinion is structurally distinct because it changes the possible
reporting sensor perimeter. The earlier benefit decisions principally
controlled access to money or services. OLC now reads the TANF and SSI reporting
conditions as reaching knowledge held throughout the participating State's
component agencies.

PRWORA § 434, codified at 8 U.S.C. § 1644, prevents Federal, State or local law
from prohibiting government entities from exchanging information with
immigration authorities about an individual's citizenship or immigration
status. It is an anti-restriction rule, not a complete reporting procedure or a
general waiver of every Federal confidentiality statute.
The Balanced Budget Act of 1997 amended the terminology and created a
substantially duplicative TANF provision at 42 U.S.C. § 608(g). Section 611a
continues to use “unlawfully in,” while later language uses “not lawfully
present.” The September opinion does not resolve every consequence of that
drafting history.

1.2 The 1998 Randolph Moss opinion
The August 18, 1998 OLC opinion treated “State” as ambiguous and limited the
TANF and SSI reporting conditions to the agencies administering those programs.
Its reasoning included statutory structure, possible surplusage, legislative
history and Spending Clause notice concerns. The practical concern was that a
broader interpretation could unexpectedly attach a welfare-program funding
condition to schools, motor-vehicle departments and other State bodies that did
not administer the money.
Primary source: 1998 OLC opinion.
1.3 The 2000 interagency implementation notice
SSA, HHS, DOL, HUD and INS published a common implementation notice on
September 28, 2000. It operationalized the narrow interpretation:
the covered entities were the named TANF, SSI and housing administrators;
knowledge required a formal, administratively reviewable finding made in the
specified benefit program;
the finding had to be supported by INS or EOIR, such as by a final removal
order;
a SAVE no-record response or benefit-ineligibility response did not itself
establish unlawful presence;
a benefit agency's finding had no weight outside the benefit context;
reports were due within 45 days after each calendar quarter; and
the published destination was an INS office at 425 I Street NW.
The notice is evidence of a designed reporting pipe. It is not current evidence
of report volume, electronic operation, a maintained address or DHS use.
Primary source: 65 Fed. Reg. 58,301.
1.4 The September 1, 2026 opinion
The new opinion, requested by HHS on June 16, 2026, withdraws the 1998 opinion
and concludes:
“State” means the sovereign State and encompasses its component agencies.
The same meaning applies throughout § 404(b) and (c).
The reporting condition applies across a participating State, not merely in
the TANF or SSI administering office.
The 2000 formal-determination/final-removal-order knowledge threshold was
unsupported.
Actual knowledge means awareness or understanding that leaves no
substantial doubt; DHS notice, an individual's admission, expired or
terminated-status records and facially dispositive documents can support it.
Willful blindness can count, and an agency cannot necessarily avoid
knowledge by refusing readily available information.
Suspicion or a bare reason to believe does not become knowledge.
The reading is prospective. Federal agencies may not alter past agreements
retroactively or recoup old funds on the basis of the new position, and
States may reconsider future program participation.
OLC treats the reporting provisions as Spending Clause conditions and rejects
clear-notice, coercion and relatedness objections. It also states that
noncompliance can place the corresponding program funding at risk. That is a
legal analysis of potential program consequences, not an announced withholding
or termination.
Primary source: September 1, 2026 OLC opinion.

The opinion does not identify a same-day reporting duty. “Every individual” is
the population field; “at least four times annually and upon request” is the
TANF reporting clock.
3. The State and local perimeter
The opinion is strongest for executive departments and agencies that are
components of the participating State. It cites Supreme Court language treating
a State as including local governmental units, which creates meaningful
municipal and school-district exposure. It does not separately classify:
municipalities and counties with distinct legal status;
independent school districts;
public colleges, university systems and affiliated foundations;
State-created authorities and public corporations;
contractors and grantees; or
private entities performing a State program function.
The word “State” is therefore broader, but the legal-person and attribution
questions remain object-specific. A State grant recipient or private school
does not become a State component merely because it receives public money.

5. The existing identity and benefit architecture
The Federal Government does not need a newly announced master database to make
the opinion consequential. It already operates a federated architecture:
SSA Numident and enumeration. A near-universal SSN identity spine can
carry name, birth, citizenship/work-authorization coding, A-number,
documents, fraud indicators and record iterations.
DHS SAVE and person-centric services. Agencies submit biographic and
immigration identifiers through web or system-to-system services. USCIS has
developed person-centric entity-resolution capabilities across DHS and
external data.
State income and eligibility verification. 42 U.S.C. § 1320b-7 requires
specified State programs to use SSNs and immigration-status verification,
with safeguards, notice, a reasonable opportunity to produce evidence and a
fair hearing.
Federal Student Aid. FAFSA applications are matched with SSA; records
containing an A-number are also matched with DHS, and institutions use SAVE
for additional verification.
Treasury Do Not Pay and State hubs. DNP supplies controlled identity,
eligibility and risk responses through APIs, portals, monitoring, payment
verification, PARIS and the unemployment integrity hub.
Agency-specific computer matches. OPM's SSA and DNP notices illustrate
the normal legal form: named systems and populations, specified fields,
a start clock, independent verification and contest rights.
The correct synthesis is a federated Federal Person Record—shared or
reconcilable identifiers across legally distinct systems—not a single
purpose-free database.
6. The SAVE firewall
The immigration-status verification provisions and statutory notes attached to
42 U.S.C. § 1320b-7 contain a critical boundary. The system established for
State benefit verification was not to be used by immigration authorities for
administrative, noncriminal immigration enforcement. The statute also requires
privacy protection and beneficiary process before a status-based adverse
benefit action.
That creates the most important implementation question in this package:
Can PRWORA § 404 reports be routed through SAVE without converting a bounded
benefit-verification service into an administrative immigration-enforcement
feed, or must DHS construct and govern a legally separate outbound channel?
The September opinion does not resolve that question. A State's use of SAVE to
decide eligibility and its separate statutory duty to report qualifying
knowledge may coexist, but their authority, purpose, fields, retention,
correction and downstream-use rules cannot be silently merged.
Primary source: 42 U.S.C. § 1320b-7 and statutory notes.

8. Legal and operational questions the opinion leaves open
Spending Clause and federalism
OLC says the reporting condition is clear, related to the benefit programs and
not coercive. The likely litigation tests are:
whether a funding condition administered by one office clearly reaches
unrelated State components;
whether TANF or SSI funding pressure becomes coercive in a particular State;
whether State-wide reporting is sufficiently related to the funded program;
and
whether the State had adequate prospective choice after the interpretation
changed.
No located merits decision resolves § 611a's State-wide agency perimeter.
Confidentiality and privacy
Section 1644 defeats State or local rules that prohibit exchanging immigration
status information. The opinion does not work through every Federal
confidentiality regime governing the identity attached to that status,
including:
FERPA and education-record restrictions;
HIPAA and program-specific health confidentiality;
42 C.F.R. Part 2 substance-use records;
tax-return confidentiality;
Social Security Act disclosure restrictions;
Title 13 census confidentiality; or
program-specific restrictions on investigative or applicant records.
Names, addresses and identifiers are not identical to the narrow fact of
immigration status. Each proposed disclosure requires its own authority and
conflict analysis.
Due process and correction
The opinion does not prescribe:
notice to the person before reporting;
an opportunity to contest the State's conclusion;
how pending applications or changed status are handled;
how a correction reaches DHS after an erroneous report;
retention, redress or deletion rules;
interagency attribution of knowledge; or
which State official certifies the report.
Federal receipt and downstream action
No located September 2026 object establishes:
a replacement HHS/SSA/DHS notice;
an electronic schema or new destination;
an agreement amendment;
a first State implementation directive;
a first expanded report;
ingestion into a named DHS system;
referral to ICE or another enforcement component; or
an individual benefit, removal or funding outcome.
9. OPM, DNP and the correct architecture claim
OPM's August 26, 2026 DNP notice is a useful proof of architecture but not a
PRWORA implementation. It establishes a scheduled OPM Retirement Services
match with Treasury's DNP Working System for CSRS and FERS eligibility, using
names, SSNs, dates of birth, addresses and payment information. The notice says
the match may not be the sole basis for adverse action and requires independent
verification and process. Its legal start is September 25, 2026.
OPM's separate SSA match beginning no earlier than October 20 concerns earnings,
death indicators and retirement or survivor-benefit limits. Neither object
creates a citizenship registry or State-to-DHS report.
The project-level architecture claim is therefore:
Existing Federal and State systems already resolve identity and eligibility
across program-specific connections. The September OLC opinion broadens the
legal population of State offices whose qualifying knowledge must be
reported. A later implementation object—not the opinion, OPM or DNP—must
create the production State-to-DHS join.
Primary source: OPM–Treasury DNP matching notice.

11. Claim firewall
Unsafe shorthand
Controlled statement
“Congress created a new reporting mandate in 2026.”
Congress enacted the reporting provisions in 1996; OLC changed the Executive Branch's interpretation in 2026.
“Every State must report immediately whenever any employee suspects someone.”
Participating States face periodic or requested reporting for persons the State actually knows are unlawfully present; suspicion alone is insufficient.
“SAVE will now report benefit applicants to ICE.”
SAVE is an operating verification service with an anti-enforcement statutory boundary; no new SAVE reporting workflow was located.
“Every school, DMV and hospital is now definitely covered.”
OLC reaches State components, but local, independent, contractor and confidentiality perimeters remain fact-specific and contested.
“DNP is the reporting pipe.”
DNP is a payment-integrity screening network. No PRWORA-to-DNP or DNP-to-DHS reporting flow is established.
“OPM built the national person database.”
OPM operates bounded workforce and retirement records and matching programs. SSA and DHS supply the central identity/immigration junctions in a federated architecture.
“The opinion proves deportations will follow.”
It changes Executive Branch legal advice; implementation, report receipt, DHS use and any immigration outcome are later clocks.
12. Observatory disposition
The September opinion passes the Research Desk materiality test as a
promotion_review object because it changes legal actor, authority perimeter,
knowledge threshold and potential State-to-Federal information flow inside the
existing Migrant welfare & benefits story.
The broader 2025–2026 sequence should be preserved as the PRWORA
reinterpretation campaign, not split into unrelated welfare, tax, housing and
reporting curiosities. Individual program actions remain separate legal and
operating objects beneath that campaign.
