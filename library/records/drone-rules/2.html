Implementing Section 927 Waiver Process for Certain Unmanned Aircraft Operations

Federal Aviation Administration · April 1, 2026

Notice, not a rule

The FAA explained how it will use FAA Reauthorization Act Section 927 authority to enable UAS operations through waivers rather than full rulemakings or exemptions, expressly invoking EO 14307's drone-dominance goals. It creates a faster, case-by-case path to commercialize drone operations.

Maintained reading (reconciled September 7, 2026)
The April 1, 2026 notice explains FAA’s use of the 2024 Reauthorization Act §927 waiver authority alongside existing exemption and Part 107/Part 91 routes. A waiver may avoid the exemption public-benefit showing and notice/comment process, but FAA retains the safety test, individualized safety analysis and power to deny or redirect an application. The original summary’s claim of a faster commercialization path needs this limit: speed is not guaranteed.

Unleashing American Drone Dominance (FCC Notice of Inquiry)

Federal Communications Commission · April 16, 2026

Notice, not a rule

The FCC sought comment on spectrum and licensing reforms to support drone operations — dedicated drone spectrum, streamlined experimental licensing, and new drone innovation zones and testbeds — in service of EO 14307's goals. It extends drone-dominance policy beyond the FAA into the radio-spectrum layer drones need to fly BVLOS.

Maintained reading (reconciled September 7, 2026)
The instrument is the FCC bureaus’ Public Notice DA 26-314, released April 1 and published April 16, 2026, not a formal Notice of Inquiry as the saved title calls it. It solicited comment on spectrum, experimental licensing, testbeds and coordination. Existing Part 88 arrangements are background; additional spectrum access and innovation-zone ideas in this notice are proposals for consideration. FCC radio authorization and FAA flight permission remain distinct.

Integration of Powered-Lift: Pilot Certification and Operations (SFAR)

Federal Aviation Administration · November 21, 2024

Final rule

The FAA's final powered-lift rule established pilot-certification and operating rules for eVTOL and air-taxi aircraft, including a ten-year Special Federal Aviation Regulation tailored to their varied designs. It is the foundational framework enabling powered-lift integration into the National Airspace System.

Maintained reading (reconciled September 7, 2026)
The November 21, 2024 final rule supplied pilot-training and operating provisions for piloted powered-lift, with permanent amendments and a ten-year Part 194 SFAR. It did not change aircraft type-certification requirements or cover unmanned powered-lift operation. Its main effective date was January 21, 2025; a January 3 correction repaired duplicated tables and a reference. A March 11 notice deferred enforcement until March 20, while recognizing the January effective date.

Streamlining Export Controls for Drone Exports

Bureau of Industry and Security · January 21, 2026

Final rule

Acting under EO 14307, Commerce/BIS issued an interim final rule easing export controls on civil UAVs, allowing less-sensitive commercial drones to ship license-free to most Wassenaar partners and certain cargo and agricultural-spray drones to allies under License Exception STA. A concrete deregulatory follow-through on the drone-dominance order.

Maintained reading (reconciled September 7, 2026)
BIS’s interim final rule took effect January 20, 2026, one day before publication. It changed national-security controls for a defined ECCN 9A012.a.1 subset and expanded License Exception STA eligibility for specified 9A012/9A120 aircraft to A:5 destinations. This implements export-policy direction under existing export-control authority; it is not a general license-free rule for commercial drones.

Executive Order 14307 — Unleashing American Drone Dominance

Executive Office of the President · June 11, 2025

Executive order

Signed June 6, 2025, EO 14307 directed the FAA to issue a proposed rule enabling routine beyond-visual-line-of-sight drone operations within 30 days and a final rule within 240 days, and to accelerate UAS and eVTOL integration into the National Airspace System. BVLOS normalization was the order's first priority, and it also launched an eVTOL Integration Pilot Program.

June 11, 2025

Tags

Maintained reading (reconciled September 7, 2026)
EO 14307, signed June 6 and published June 11, 2025, directed several separate changes: BVLOS rulemaking, an integration pilot, domestic-production measures and export review. Section 4 specifies a 30-day proposed-rule target and a 240-day final-rule target qualified by “as appropriate.” These are instructions, not proof a final rule took effect. Later records establish an NPRM and an eIPP solicitation; the August 2026 FAA returns establish demonstrations.
