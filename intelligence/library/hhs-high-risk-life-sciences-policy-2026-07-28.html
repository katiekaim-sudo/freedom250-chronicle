New federal funding ban on dangerous gain-of-function research

A government-wide policy dated July 20, 2026 and announced July 28 bars federal money for dangerous gain-of-function research and for life-science research involving countries or entities of concern. It does not reach privately funded work, and the review system it requires is not built yet.

What we found

  • Prohibited for federal funding: dangerous gain-of-function research in the U.S. or abroad, and federally funded life-science research involving a country or entity of concern.
  • 'Potential' dangerous work can be funded only after institutional review and a recommendation from a single new federal independent review body. Other risky international research needs a risk-based assessment. Vaccine and countermeasure work is expressly preserved under safeguards.
  • It replaces the 2024 dual-use and enhanced-pandemic-pathogen oversight policy and the older frameworks it covered, but not the Federal Select Agent Program or export-control rules.
  • Deadlines counted from July 20: review body in 90 days (Oct 18, 2026); entity-of-concern list and agency guidance in 120 days (Nov 17, 2026); institution setup and award terms in 180 days (Jan 16, 2027). Penalties can include loss of funding and up to five years of ineligibility.
  • The policy itself says new legislation is needed to stop all such research, including privately funded work. It is the replacement policy EO 14292 (May 5, 2025) called for by September 2, 2025, so it arrived late.

Why it matters

Research oversight is being rebuilt through grant conditions and award terms, the federal funding pipeline, rather than a new statute. That is also why its reach stops at private money.

What to watch

  • 2026-10-18 · Single independent review body due to be set up (Oct 26 if counted from July 28).
  • 2026-11-17 · Country/entity-of-concern list and agency guidance due.
  • 2027-01-16 · Institutions must have review entities in place and awards must carry the new terms.
