Research summary

Federal strategic planning links stated goals to responsible issuers, annual performance plans, budgets, evidence questions and reported results. The July 22 survey distinguishes agency plans, parent coverage, other strategies and source gaps; the August 17 NSSTS addition is a separate national functional strategy. The active Core Missions extension freezes separate 392-row entity and 226-body committee denominators and completes 18 Wave 1 assessments without claiming a whole-government census. The September 18 process spine establishes that the FY 2026–2030 plan is a once-per-presidential-term management cycle and connects mission/program architecture to budget and cost reporting while preserving appropriations, apportionments and allotments as the legal fund-control layer. The September 18 live refresh identifies seven newly final agency-wide plans, three newly located component plans, missed publication forecasts and the still-open cabinet and small-agency queue. The EDGAR control map separates Commission rule/form authority, EBO business ownership, OIT operation, budget/procurement, filer delegation and public-data reuse. Its companion signal analysis connects the initiating data-boundary failure, enterprise support contract, OIG workflow and workforce findings, FDTA standards, identity controls, AI and filing clocks to a provisional modular regulatory-data-plane direction. The procurement companion establishes that the current EDGAR order sits under the OneIT multiple-award vehicle, no matching active public notice was located, and outside technology currently enters most plausibly through a listed prime, a later on-ramp or successor competition, or the SEC's narrow unsolicited-proposal channel. These records establish stated direction, market-access boundaries and bounded inference, not a selected architecture, enactment, new award, completed reorganization or achieved result.
