### Current-payment/MCP operating objects

The reported §2 restrictions include resident-firm MULC/CCL/MEP cross-use, limits on advance import payments, a 90-day delay for related-party services, soybean-import sequencing, legacy dividends/profits, and specified interest/amortization payments. The MCP is the 30% withholding exchange tax on FX used for certain credit-card settlements by users other than resident individuals. [CR 26/105, PDF pp.89–91](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2026/english/1argea2026001.pdf)

## F. Six-lane worksheet

### L1 — Domestic anchor: `D`

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| One-sentence reason | Inflation fell dramatically but remained persistently high, the disinflation process had stalled around the cutoff, and the nominal/operational framework still required substantial repair while tight credit imposed an activity cost. |
| Primary evidence + vintage | End-period CPI: 117.8% in 2024 and 31.5% in 2025; annual inflation about 32½% in early 2026; March 2026 monthly inflation 3.4%; staff called for clarification of the monetary-aggregate anchor and eventual evolution toward greater exchange-rate flexibility and interest-rate tools. |
| Persistence evidence | Inflation exceeded 200% in late 2023 before falling to double digits; the improvement is large but the remaining level and institutional fragility are persistent. Real GDP was -1.3% in 2024 and +4.4% in 2025, with activity slowing and uneven through the second half of 2025. |
| Shock evidence | 2025 political uncertainty produced a sharp money-demand/dollarization shock and depreciation, tightening financial conditions and slowing disinflation; Middle East fuel/regulated-price shocks remained live. |
| Competing explanation | Fiscal discipline, a large cumulative disinflation, more stable expectations, lower pass-through, and renewed 2026 growth could support `C`. |
| Confidence | `MEDIUM` |
| Legal/event link | Article IV `BOARD-CONCERN`; trade events not scored |
| Data gap | CPI weights remain outdated; current-consumption weights and a longer post-shock expectations series could move the grade. |

### L2 — Underlying order: `C`

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| One-sentence reason | The banking system remained liquid, capitalized, and operational, but acute dollarization, shallow domestic finance, rising consumer NPLs, weak peso demand, and large resident assets outside the system materially impair underlying monetary order. |
| Primary evidence + vintage | At February–April 2026 vintages, banks remained liquid and well capitalized with adequate provisioning and comfortable FX liquidity, but credit had stagnated since 2025Q3 and consumer NPLs were rising; the authorities described the financial system as “resilient but shallow.” |
| Persistence evidence | The dollar served as the primary hedge and exceeded 50% of M2 during the 2025 shock; authorities estimated resident U.S.-dollar holdings above US$200 billion, while domestic capital markets remained underdeveloped. |
| Shock evidence | The pre-election dollarization wave stayed substantially inside banks rather than becoming a generalized bank run; no banking fracture or generalized domestic-payment breakdown was reported. |
| Competing explanation | Strong bank liquidity/capital and retained FX deposits could support `B`; the shock did not break intermediation. |
| Confidence | `MEDIUM` |
| Legal/event link | CFMs are contextual only; §2 restrictions score in L5, not again here. |
| Data gap | Current deposit-currency shares, NPL migration, liquidity stress tests, and peso-payment share after April 2026. |

### L3 — Exchange order: `C`

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| One-sentence reason | The widening managed band was functioning and accumulating reserves at the current cutoff, but the regime’s recent stress, policy-driven segmentation, and incomplete buffer restoration remain material persistent impairments. |
| Primary evidence + vintage | De jure/de facto `other managed`; BCRA bought about US$7.5 billion from January through the report cutoff, roughly 20% of traded volume; the peso stabilized, appreciated about 13% in real terms from end-2025, and futures were about 20% below the band ceiling. |
| Persistence evidence | The regime changed from a crawling peg in April 2025; the 2025 risk-off episode pushed the rate toward the upper band and caused major reserve loss before the 2026 purchase program stabilized operation. |
| Shock evidence | Authorities report that the band survived the late-2025 speculative episode and returned to its inner range, but acknowledge decisive Fund and U.S. official support. |
| Competing explanation | Current band operation, two-way flexibility, purchases rather than defensive sales, and subdued futures could support `B`; conversely thin buffers could justify `D`, but that mechanism is assigned primarily to L4. |
| Confidence | `MEDIUM` |
| Legal/event link | MCP is legally recorded but primary performance effect assigned to L5; capital controls remain separate. |
| Data gap | Accessible-market/official-rate spread series, forward-intervention stock, intervention disclosure, and post-April volatility. |

### L5 — Current-payment usability: `D`

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
