Capital letters do not create a second United States

The passport's authority comes from federal law and State Department issuance, not the capitalization on its cover.

What we found

  • The Articles of Confederation give the historic style as The United States of America while varying capitalization elsewhere in the same text.
  • The State Department's commemorative-passport page describes in title case the same country name shown in display capitals on the cover.
  • Federal law assigns passport issuance to the Secretary of State on behalf of the United States; GPO production and document design are separate functions.
  • The Tenth Circuit has expressly rejected the theory that an all-capital rendering names a different legal person.
  • The 1871 Act created a District of Columbia municipal corporation, and 28 U.S.C. § 3002 defines United States only for its debt-collection chapter; neither creates an all-caps national corporation.

Why it matters

It protects a real line in the Entity Theory: the United States and its agencies can have legal personality and different capacities, but those distinctions come from charters, statutes and context. Typography is not a hidden entity switch.

Research summary

United States of America and its all-capital or mixed-capital renderings do not identify separate legal entities. The passport's legal force comes from issuance by the Secretary of State under 22 U.S.C. § 211a; State's own commemorative-passport page describes in title case the wording shown in display capitals on the cover. Federal law can give United States different context-specific meanings, but scope language, definitions, actor and capacity do that work. The Tenth Circuit has expressly rejected the claimed legal split produced by all-capital naming. The 1871 Act incorporated the District of Columbia for municipal purposes, and 28 U.S.C. § 3002 defines United States only for the federal debt-collection chapter; neither creates an all-caps national corporation.
