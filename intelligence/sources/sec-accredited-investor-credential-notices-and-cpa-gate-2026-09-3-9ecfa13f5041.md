# SEC Accredited Investor Credential Notices and the CPA Private-Market Gate

## Question

Did the SEC make licensed CPAs accredited investors on September 30, 2026? If
not, what exactly did the Commission propose, why is the CPA route unusually
important, who would qualify, and what legal and operating gates remain?

## Controlling answer

**No CPA became an accredited investor from this action today.** On September
30, 2026, the Commission issued five notices requesting comment on possible
designation orders under Rule 501(a)(10) of Regulation D. The notices cover:

1. passage of a new FINRA accredited-investor exam;
2. a U.S. CPA license in good standing;
3. a CFA charter in good standing;
4. a U.S. CFP certification in good standing; and
5. a Series 79 license or the combined Series 86 and 87 research-analyst
   license in good standing.

The Commission has **not** issued the final orders. The notices say comments
are due 60 days after Federal Register publication. At this cutoff, the
SEC-issued versions still showed Federal Register date placeholders, so there
is not yet a reliable calendar comment deadline to carry.

The CPA route would be a major conceptual change in investor access even
though it uses authority created in 2020. The SEC would effectively import the
state-governed CPA licensing system into federal securities law as a proxy for
private-market sophistication. An active CPA could qualify personally without
meeting the existing $200,000 individual-income, $300,000 joint-income or $1
million net-worth tests and without taking the proposed FINRA investor exam.

The potential population is large. NASBA reported **650,667 actively licensed
CPAs** as of August 27, 2026, using data from 53 of the 55 U.S. licensing
jurisdictions. The SEC cannot determine how many already qualify by wealth,
income or another credential. It expressly identifies earlier-career CPAs,
CPAs at smaller firms and CPAs in lower-cost regions as people who may be most
affected because their incomes are less likely to clear the existing financial
thresholds.

**Current state:** `Commission notices issued; CPA and companion credentials
not yet designated; public-comment and final-order clocks remain open`.

Rule 501(a)(10), adopted in 2020, already authorizes the Commission to
designate professional certifications, designations and educational
credentials by order after notice and an opportunity for public comment. The
Commission simultaneously designated Series 7, Series 65 and Series 82
licenses in 2020. The September 2026 documents use that existing order process;
they do not amend the text of Rule 501 or replace its income and net-worth
prongs.

The notices repeatedly state that the Commission believes the proposed routes
meet the Rule 501(a)(10) criteria. That is a strong directional signal, but it
is not the legal effect of a final designation order.

## 2. Why the CPA route is such a striking move

The proposal is not limited to audit partners, securities accountants,
valuation specialists, investment-company accountants or CPAs employed in
finance. It would cover a natural person holding **any U.S. CPA license in good
standing**, subject to the Commission's stated good-standing boundary.

That means the proposed access credential would rest on this chain:

`state or territorial CPA law → education and supervised experience →`
`Uniform CPA Examination → active license and continuing education →`
`public license verification → federal accredited-investor status`

The SEC would not independently determine that a particular CPA understands a
particular private company, fund, security, cap table, liquidation preference
or illiquidity risk. It would recognize the profession's licensing system as a
class-wide indicator of sufficient financial sophistication.

This is different from the three licenses designated in 2020. Series 7, 65 and
82 are securities-industry licenses. A CPA license is a broad accounting and
public-protection credential administered across 55 jurisdictions. The SEC is
therefore proposing to move the knowledge-based doorway beyond securities
registration and into a much larger state-licensed profession.

The income comparison makes the practical point. The Commission notice cites
May 2025 median pay of **$83,680** for the broader accountants-and-auditors
occupation, while the individual accredited-investor income threshold remains
$200,000. The occupational figure is not a CPA-only salary measure and cannot
tell us how many CPAs are newly eligible, but it explains why the CPA route
could reach people the wealth tests exclude.

**Our reading:** this is credential portability across legal systems. The SEC
would make a state professional license do double duty as a federal
private-market access passport.

## 3. Who would count as a CPA for this purpose

The notice uses a deliberately bounded category:

- the person must hold a CPA license issued by one of 55 U.S. jurisdictions:
  the 50 states, District of Columbia, Northern Mariana Islands, Guam, Puerto
  Rico or U.S. Virgin Islands;
- the license must be active and in good standing under the relevant
  jurisdiction's system and under the Commission-recognition standard in 17
  CFR 210.2-01(a);
- an inactive or suspended license would not qualify;
- passing the Uniform CPA Examination without obtaining the license would not
  qualify;
- passing the International Qualification Examination alone would not qualify,
  but a person who later receives and maintains a U.S. CPA license could; and
- the status would qualify the CPA personally. It could not be used to buy
  securities on behalf of another non-accredited person.

The Commission's discipline line is surprisingly narrow. A CPA denied the
privilege of practicing before the SEC under Rule 102(e)(1)(i) for lacking the
requisite qualifications would not qualify through the CPA route. But a CPA
denied or suspended from SEC practice under other Rule 102(e) provisions could
still rely on the credential if the CPA remained active and in good standing
under the licensing standard. The notice says broader disqualification would
be unnecessarily punitive.

That produces an important control boundary: **state license status, not every
kind of SEC professional discipline, is the proposed gate.** Commenters may
test whether that line is too permissive or difficult for issuers to apply.

## 4. Why the SEC thinks CPA licensure is enough

The Commission maps CPA licensure onto the four Rule 501(a)(10) attributes:

### Examination and industry-body administration

The AICPA develops and scores the Uniform CPA Examination, while NASBA acts as
a central clearinghouse and the jurisdictions issue licenses. The current exam
has four four-hour sections:

- Auditing and Attestation (`AUD`);
- Financial Accounting and Reporting (`FAR`);
- Taxation and Regulation (`REG`); and
- one discipline chosen from Business Analysis and Reporting (`BAR`),
  Information Systems and Controls (`ISC`) or Tax Compliance and Planning
  (`TCP`).

### Education and supervised experience

Requirements vary by jurisdiction, but the notice groups the principal routes
as:

- a bachelor's degree with an accounting concentration, 30 additional credit
  hours and one year of supervised experience;
- a post-baccalaureate accounting degree and one year of supervised
  experience; or
- a bachelor's degree with an accounting concentration and two years of
  supervised experience.

The third route is the newer 120-hour-plus-two-years model being adopted across
jurisdictions. Variation in state pathways is real; the SEC is relying on the
combined examination, education, experience, license-maintenance and public-
verification structure rather than one nationally identical education rule.

### Continuing competence

Active CPAs must complete continuing professional education, with the issuing
jurisdiction determining the hours and subjects. The Commission treats ongoing
CPE and active-license maintenance as support for continuing sophistication.

### Independent verification

NASBA's CPAverify uses official board data from 53 jurisdictions. Hawaii and
New Mexico are absent from that national database but operate their own public
license searches. The notice therefore concludes that an issuer or other party
can independently verify CPA status.

## 5. What the proposal would change in practice

If the Commission later issues the CPA designation order, an active CPA who is
not otherwise accredited could become eligible to purchase securities in
private offerings that use accredited-investor status as a gate.

The most practical change may be verification. In a Rule 506(c) offering, the
issuer must take reasonable steps to verify that every purchaser is accredited.
Checking an official active-license record could be simpler and less invasive
than reviewing tax forms, brokerage statements, appraisals, debts or written
third-party wealth confirmations.

The designation could also matter where accredited status affects purchaser
limits, disclosure obligations or investment caps, including Rule 506(b),
Regulation A and Regulation Crowdfunding. It could make the pool of eligible
investors larger and reduce issuer search and transaction costs.

It would **not**:

- register or Commission-review a private offering;
- require an issuer or private fund to accept a CPA;
- eliminate fund minimums, suitability checks, transfer restrictions or other
  eligibility rules;
- make the security liquid, accurately valued or safe;
- create public-company disclosure for the investment;
- override a CPA's audit-independence or professional obligations; or
- prove that any particular CPA can bear a total loss.

The notice itself preserves the downside: private offerings generally lack the
additional disclosure supplied by Securities Act registration and can carry
greater illiquidity, agency, adverse-selection and business risks. Audit
professionals would also have to consider independence restrictions before
investing in an audit client.

### Accounting-transition bridge

The proposal also belongs in the Accounting Transition thesis, but only as an
**investor-permission layer**. A verifiable CPA credential could make one
private-offering eligibility check less intrusive and more machine-readable.
It does not authorize an issuance, establish legal title, make a token the
official securityholder record, produce a valid secondary transfer, settle the
cash leg, determine GAAP recognition or supply audit evidence. The governed
cross-package map is `The Private Company On-Chain Transition`.

## 6. The open legal and policy seams

### Credential competence versus investment competence

CPA licensure strongly tests accounting, audit, tax and controls. It does not
necessarily test private-company valuation, venture terms, fund economics,
secondary-market restrictions or portfolio construction. The Commission's
theory is that the credential supports the ability to evaluate risk, not that
every CPA has identical investment experience.

### Uneven state pathways

The same federal result would attach to licenses produced by different state
education, experience, ethics and CPE rules. The Commission treats those
systems as sufficiently comparable at the license level. Commenters may test
whether the variation matters.

### Discipline and status verification

CPAverify is broad but not complete, and state terminology is not uniform. The
notice also preserves eligibility for some CPAs subject to SEC practice bars
so long as their licensing status remains active. Issuers will need a clear,
workable standard if the Commission adopts the route.

### Loss of status and follow-on investments

Commissioner Peirce asked what happens when a person cannot maintain a
credential and later wants to make a follow-on private investment. The notices
do not supply a final answer. Prior purchases do not become newly registered
offerings merely because a credential later lapses, but each later sale must
have its own valid exemption and purchaser-status analysis.

### Size of the actual opening

The gross active-CPA population is known; the newly eligible population is
not. Some CPAs already satisfy income, net-worth, executive, knowledgeable-
employee or existing-license prongs. Some will never seek private investments,
and some offerings will retain high minimums or institutional preferences.

## 7. State and return gates

Re-open this record when any of the following occurs:

1. the five notices publish in the Federal Register and exact comment
   deadlines become available;
2. comments materially challenge or support the CPA competence,
   state-variation, discipline, verification or follow-on-investment theory;
3. the Commission issues, revises or declines a final CPA designation order;
4. the Commission acts separately on any companion credential or the FINRA
   exam;
5. FINRA publishes the exam's final outline, fee, verification system or
   launch date; or
6. SEC or staff guidance explains how credential lapse affects follow-on
   purchases or Rule 506(c) verification.

Do not convert a Federal Register notice, comment letter or Commissioner's
statement into a final designation. The operative event is a Commission order
for the named route.
