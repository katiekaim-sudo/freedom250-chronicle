How U.S. Interest Rates Are Made
One policy decision, several tools, many other rates
The important separation is decision → implementation → observed trade → benchmark → contract → cash payment.
Plain English: the Fed sets the conditions around the shortest-term central-bank money. Markets and lenders then translate those conditions into other prices.
Step 1 · policy
FOMC chooses a target range
The statutory committee—seven Governors plus five voting Reserve Bank presidents—votes on the desired range for overnight federal funds.
Chosen by the FOMC
Step 2 · tools
Fed creates alternatives and backstops
IORB pays eligible reserve holders. ON RRP gives certain cash investors an overnight outlet. Repo and the discount window lend against collateral.
Board + FOMC + New York Fed + Reserve Banks
Step 3 · trades
Institutions actually transact
Banks, FHLBs, dealers, money funds and others move cash, reserves and collateral under different access rules.
Market participants
Step 4 · measurement
Benchmarks are calculated
EFFR is calculated from qualifying unsecured federal-funds trades. SOFR is calculated from qualifying Treasury-repo trades.
New York Fed
Step 5 · contracts
Lenders price the actual borrower
Reference cost, term, credit risk, capital, servicing, options, competition and subsidy all affect the offered rate.
Lender + borrower + governing program

Why EFFR is the operating anchor
Reserve balances are central-bank settlement assets.
The Fed controls the return available on eligible balances through IORB.
Nearby overnight instruments compete for the same cash.
Expected future overnight rates help price longer maturities.
What “base rate” does not mean
Federal funds is not the largest credit market.
Every customer contract does not cite EFFR.
A 0.25-point FOMC move does not force every rate to move 0.25 point.
The Fed does not administratively set Treasury, mortgage or credit-card yields.
Two overnight loops that sound similar but do opposite jobs
In the federal-funds lane, an institution borrows reserves. In ON RRP, a cash investor places cash with the Fed.
“Overnight” means: originated today, due the next business day. Repeated use is a sequence of new transactions, not one immortal loan.
Federal-funds loan
Unsecured institution-to-institution credit
1Payments redistribute reserves
Customer payments, securities settlement, taxes and clearing leave Bank B below its preferred end-of-day buffer—even if the system as a whole has ample reserves.
2A lender sends reserve balances
The transfer settles finally through a Federal Reserve account. Bank B records more reserves and a federal-funds-purchased liability.
3The lender still has credit risk
No specific Treasury or other asset is pledged. Finality of the outgoing payment does not guarantee tomorrow’s repayment.
4Next business day: principal + interest are dueRepay
from inflows, deposits, maturing assets, sales or other funding.Refinance
originate Loan 2 and use its cash to discharge Loan 1.
ON RRP cash placement
Collateralized Federal Reserve investment outlet
1A money fund or other eligible counterparty has cash
It needs a very short, liquid investment—not a loan from the Fed.
2The counterparty sends cash to the Fed
The Fed provides Treasury securities as transaction collateral and records an ON RRP liability.
3The facility supports the policy floor
An eligible investor has less reason to lend privately for materially less after access and balance-sheet costs.
4Next business day: the trade unwinds
Cash plus interest returns and the securities return. The investor may enter a new ON RRP that day.
Daily does not mean emergency
Different banks end each day with different reserve positions.
Some current federal-funds trades reflect arbitrage or regulatory-liquidity motives.
Federal Reserve research attributes more than 90% of current lending to FHLBs.
The cited traditional bank-to-bank lane was about $2–$6 billion per day.
The risk that remains
An unsecured lender lacks an earmarked asset to seize.
A borrower dependent on daily renewal faces rollover risk.
A new loan repaying an old loan does not erase the funding dependence.
ON RRP volume does not show that the Fed “needs money.”
The same dollars can be claims on different issuers

Credit unions deserve their own lane
A lower member loan rate can be ordinary cooperative pricing. Federal credit unions are member-owned, not-for-profit institutions; value may return through lower loan rates, higher savings rates or lower fees.
1 · Charter and membershipWho is legally eligible for the product?
2 · Board policyWho approved the product, underwriting and pricing criteria?
3 · Ceiling, not floorThe FCU general rate cap—currently 18% through September 10, 2027—is a maximum, not a minimum required charge.
4 · Officials and employees differCovered officials generally cannot receive more favorable comparable terms; a genuine employee benefit follows a separate analysis.
5 · Fair lendingMember ownership does not excuse discriminatory or unexplained pricing differences.
6 · Safety, tax and capitalMission-consistent pricing still must cover expected losses, operations and adequate net worth; tax consequences remain transaction-specific.
