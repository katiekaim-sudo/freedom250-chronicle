# Wave 6A — The Chair, the Clause and the Approval Gate

**Research window:** Exact 2026 Aries ingress through exact 2026 Cancer ingress.  
**Evidence cutoff:** June 20, 2026, 11:59 p.m. EDT.  
**Question:** When the new Aries-year regime seeks tighter command, does it replace federal legal persons—or alter who may advise, work, contract, approve and appeal inside the existing body?  
**Finding:** These four orders change the interior control plane more than the charter map. EO 14397 grants a final bounded runway to an advisory council that cannot operate FEMA. EO 14398 turns a conduct rule into a flowed-down contract clause, records-access right, payment-materiality statement and termination/debarment path. EO 14402 makes fixed-price/performance contracting the default and places written justification and high-value approval upstream of other contract types. EO 14410 directly moves listed career positions into Schedule Policy/Career, switching off Chapter 75 procedure for those positions while leaving agencies, merit hiring, retained competitive status and other statutory overlays in place. The packet's thesis is **the regime centralizes control by changing the chair, clause and approval gate through which an old institution acts; legal identity usually survives**.

The institutional ladder is:

`existing legal person → advisory / employment / procurement object → new classification or clause → changed review and approval path → later decision or personnel action → surviving remedy / contest`

## 6A.1 — March 24: FEMA review receives borrowed time, not operating power

[EO 14397](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/27/2026-06075/further-continuance-of-the-federal-emergency-management-agency-review-council) is the third EO in the FEMA Review Council chain. It superseded the prior continuation terms and kept the council alive until ten days after delivery of its required report or May 29, whichever came first. The President's applicable Federal Advisory Committee Act functions were again assigned to Homeland Security under GSA procedure.

This is a precise continuity object:

`existing council → prior deadline approaches → new bounded runway → report/May 29 terminus`

No public final report was isolated by the cutoff. The council could gather evidence and recommend. It could not execute disaster relief, move FEMA appropriations, repeal FEMA's statutes or reorganize the agency by its own vote.

**Maturity.** Continued advisory carrier, then terminal clock; not completed diagnosis or institutional transfer.

## 6A.2 — March 26 through April 24: contractor conduct becomes a payment gate

[EO 14398](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/31/2026-06286/addressing-dei-discrimination-by-federal-contractors) requires a clause prohibiting the order's defined “racially discriminatory DEI activities” in covered federal contracts and contract-like instruments. The clause flows through subcontracts and lower tiers and creates several distinct control rights:

- contractors must furnish information and access to books, records and accounts;
- contractors must report known or reasonably knowable subcontractor conduct and take directed remediation;
- agencies may cancel, terminate or suspend and may pursue suspension or debarment; and
- the clause declares compliance material to federal payment decisions for False Claims Act purposes.

The order also assigns OMB guidance, sector-risk guidance, agency compliance reviews, possible Justice FCA actions and formal FAR amendment.

The FAR Council's April 17 guidance directed use of FAR 52.222-90 in new solicitations and contracts beginning April 24. It also told agencies to make every effort to modify covered existing contracts bilaterally by July 24—outside this packet's evidence cutoff.

**Institutional change.** A political/legal conduct rule acquires contractual privity, record access, flow-down, payment materiality and procurement remedies.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The clause is not itself a finding that any contractor discriminated. Materiality is an element in an FCA pathway, not an automatic false claim, payment stop or judgment. No named EO-attributable termination, debarment or FCA disposition was isolated through June 20.

## 6A.3 — April 30: contract type moves upstream into executive approval

[EO 14402](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/05/2026-08900/promoting-efficiency-accountability-and-performance-in-federal-contracting) makes fixed-price or performance-based contracting the preferred default to the maximum extent consistent with law. It does not make every other contract type illegal.

The immediate decision-right change is specific:

- a contracting officer must justify any non-fixed-price choice in writing to the agency head;
- the agency head must approve non-fixed-price value above $100 million at War, $35 million at NASA, $25 million at DHS and $10 million elsewhere;
- approval may be delegated to an appropriate noncareer employee;
- emergency, disaster, contingency and covered research/pre-production contracts retain exceptions; and
- each agency must review its ten largest covered non-fixed-price contracts and later report to OMB.

This moves the decisive gate before obligation:

`requirement → contract-type choice → written exception → senior approval → solicitation/award → obligation → later performance/payment`

**Maturity.** Operative contract-type leash and review/report instructions. The public record reviewed here did not identify a top-ten conversion, renegotiation, first report or named obligation changed because of the order by the Cancer ingress.

**Continuity.** Title 41, the FAR Council, agency procurement authority, contracting officers, bid-protest forums and lawful non-fixed-price exceptions remain.

## 6A.4 — June 3: the classification seed reaches 4,862 appendix rows

[EO 14410](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/10/2026-11594/implementing-schedule-policycareer-in-the-excepted-service) matures a January 2025 seed. EO 14171 restored and renamed the Schedule F structure, and agencies were told to identify and petition for policy-influencing positions. EO 14410 supplies the first broad signed appendix and directly places its listed positions into Schedule Policy/Career.

The canonical appendix contains **4,861 position-description listing rows plus one NIH class row**. These are position-description objects, not a headcount, list of named people or proof of vacancies. Repeated descriptions can cover more than one chair.

The legal change rests on 5 U.S.C. § 7511(b)(2): Chapter 75 adverse-action procedures do not apply to an employee in a presidentially excepted position determined to be confidential, policy-determining, policy-making or policy-advocating. Agency heads had seven days from June 3 to notify encumbering employees and conform records.

What changed:

- listed positions enter the excepted service;
- Chapter 75 procedure turns off for those positions;
- later position renumbering or organizational realignment does not undo the listing; and
- agency heads may petition OPM for later additions.

What did not change merely because of listing:

- no employee was automatically removed;
- no agency organic act or program function was transferred;
- incumbents who held competitive status retain it even though the position enters the excepted service;
- merit-based hiring remains the rule, with veterans preference as far as administratively feasible; and
- the order does not textually repeal prohibited-personnel-practice or whistleblower statutes.

**Maturity.** This is the packet's deepest direct state change: classification is effective and position-specific. A later removal, bonus, petition, appeal or court case remains its own event.

## The control-plane theorem

The four objects operate at different depths, but they share one topology:

| Surface | Old holder remains | New upstream control |
|---|---|---|
| FEMA review | FEMA and DHS statutes remain | Council lifespan and advisory administration bounded by the President/DHS |
| Contractor conduct | contractor and agency contract remain | mandatory clause, records access, flow-down and payment materiality |
| Contract type | agency and contracting officer still buy | written exception and high-value agency-head/noncareer approval |
| Career policy chair | agency and position still exist | presidential classification removes Chapter 75 procedure for the listed chair |

The regime more often changes **who may proceed, under which record, with whose approval and through which appeal** than it changes the legal person itself.

## Both poles

### Constructive pole — accountable delegation with visible gates

- advisory bodies expire instead of becoming permanent shadow agencies;
- contractors can be held to a flowed-down nondiscrimination rule;
- payment materiality and records access can make compliance auditable;
- senior officials must own exceptions to performance-oriented contracting; and
- genuinely policy-influencing career chairs can be held accountable while remaining merit-based.

### Shadow pole — the gate becomes political command

- an advisory diagnosis can disappear without a public report;
- broad conduct terms can invite intrusive records demands or punishment before adjudication;
- fixed-price defaults can transfer uncertainty to contractors or degrade complex research;
- noncareer approval can pull technical procurement choices toward political control; and
- removing Chapter 75 procedure can turn professional policy advice into obedience while residual remedies become difficult to use.

Both poles remain hypotheses. Named contract actions, personnel notices, removals, appeals, reports and outcomes decide which expression matures.
