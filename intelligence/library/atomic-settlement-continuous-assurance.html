How real-time shared records could change auditing

Blockchain, smart contracts and AI can make books update nearly in real time, but none of them is an audit, and today's SEC reporting and audit opinions are still tied to fixed periods.

What we found

  • Katie's real-time-books idea is directionally right with one correction: transactions can be continuously verifiable, controls continuously executed and evidence continuously examined by AI, but only an independent auditor's engagement and report can call something "audited."
  • Atomic settlement ties both sides of a trade together so both complete or neither does. That removes a class of reconciliation and counterparty risk, but it does not prove goods were delivered, the price was fair or who truly owns the asset.
  • Routine items (cash, token balances, receivables, interest, depreciation, reconciliations) can plausibly update continuously. Estimates, impairments, contingencies, consolidation and disclosures still need judgment, so a fully automatic close is not realistic.
  • The audit likely shifts from sampling and rebuilding past transactions toward testing the systems: code, keys, permissions, outside data feeds, AI models, exceptions and corrections. SEC independence rules still bar the auditor from running or designing the client's books.
  • No general SEC continuous financial-statement regime or PCAOB continuous-audit opinion was found. Reporting is still 10-K, 10-Q and 8-K; continuous public assurance is a future standards question.

Why it matters

The plumbing of accounting is being rebuilt inside existing rules: CFTC and SEC now allow on-chain records, and PCAOB amendments cover auditors analyzing electronic data at scale. The legal audit opinion and SEC reporting calendar have not changed yet.

What to watch

  • pending · The SEC proposing or adopting more frequent or continuously updated company financial-statement reporting.
  • pending · The PCAOB, AICPA or IAASB proposing a continuous-assurance reporting model with defined criteria and report form.
  • pending · A public company publishing versioned, continuously updated GAAP statements with an independent assurance label and full correction history.
  • pending · A real failure (fork, outage, key compromise or AI model error) showing whether the code, data feed, rule, model or a human bore the error.
