## Governing question

Did the Korean equity crash produce a verifiable chain from asset-price losses
through leverage, funding, collateral, clearing/finality or alternative digital
rails?

## Research finding

The saved evidence establishes a severe Korean equity selloff and activated
exchange controls. It does not establish a completed chain into systemic
funding failure, failed settlement or migration into DeFi.

The follow-on sharpened two boundaries that the broad pass had already left
unproved:

1. KOFIA's July 28 credit-financing balances rose while realized forced sales
   fell. That supplies counterevidence to a same-day broker-credit liquidation
   surge; it does not cover every leverage channel. The exact July 29 series
   was unavailable at the parent cutoff and remained unrecovered in the saved
   August 27 check.
2. July 28–29 cash-equity trades were due to settle July 30–31. Completion was
   not yet due at the parent cutoff. The August 27 check still did not recover
   the joined completion/fail record, so settlement remains unadjudicated.

The working causal interpretation, with the measured and unmeasured links separated, is:

> **regional AI/memory-semiconductor repricing → unusually concentrated Korean
> index and large foreign selling → broad price decline and exchange controls
> → higher collateral pressure and possible product rebalancing, but no
> observed July 28 public broker-credit liquidation surge → ordinary BOK repo
> rollover and a corporate-credit warning → post-trade outcome not yet due →
> crypto trades risk-off without a demonstrated Korean DeFi flow.**

The evidence supports an asset-market stress event with collateral pressure.
It does not demonstrate a monetary-system or DeFi-transition event.

## What the BOK operation says

The BOK did not simply add KRW28tn to a standing KRW30tn injection. Its July 16
KRW30tn repo purchase matured on July 28, when a new KRW28tn 14-day operation
was allotted. Bids of KRW30.20tn exceeded supply, showing demand for the
operation: the full KRW28tn offered was allotted, not all KRW30.20tn of bid
demand. Terms retained the signature of ordinary OMO at the 2.75% policy rate.
The replacement was KRW2tn smaller across these two operations alone; other
operations, reserve flows and government balances prevent a systemwide net
liquidity conclusion.

That is consistent with **routine liquidity management under stress**, not
proof of either “nothing happened” or “emergency rescue.”

Sources: [BOK, July 16 terms](https://www.bok.or.kr/portal/bbs/P0001773/view.do?menuNo=200295&nttId=11062947);
