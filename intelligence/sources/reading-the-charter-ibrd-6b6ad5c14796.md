# Reading the Charter — IBRD

Source text: `2012 - Articles of Agreement (verbatim).md` in this folder — all eleven Articles (I–XI) and both Schedules (A, B), current text as amended effective June 27, 2012, fetched per-article from worldbank.org. Every claim below cites the exact Article/Section.

## 1. Immunities & liability shields

Art. VII is the whole game, and it stacks in layers:

- **§2**: full juridical personality — to contract, hold property, sue.
- **§3**: suits against the Bank are allowed only in a court where the Bank has an office, an agent for service, or has issued/guaranteed securities — and **"no actions shall... be brought by members or persons acting for or deriving claims from members."** The Bank's own shareholder-governments (and anyone standing in their shoes) are affirmatively barred from suing it. Whatever forum exists is for third parties only. Same section: pre-judgment, the Bank's property is immune from all seizure, attachment, or execution — a creditor can win in principle but can't touch an asset until final judgment.
- **§4**: post-judgment too — property "wherever located and by whomsoever held" is immune from search, requisition, confiscation, expropriation, or "any other form of seizure by executive or legislative action." Not just courts; legislatures are named and blocked too.
- **§5**: "The archives of the Bank shall be inviolable." Four words, no qualifier, no waiver clause anywhere in the Article.
- **§6**: assets free from "restrictions, regulations, controls and moratoria of any nature" to the extent needed for Bank operations — a currency-control/capital-control exemption baked into the founding law.
- **§8**: governors, EDs, alternates, officers, and employees get personal immunity from legal process for official acts (waivable only by the Bank itself), plus immigration/exchange/travel treatment matching top-rank diplomats of other members.

## 2. Tax exemptions

Art. VII §9 — total-spectrum: (a) the Bank, its assets, property, income, and its "operations and transactions authorized by this Agreement" are immune from **all taxation and all customs duties**, and the Bank is immune even from liability to *collect* a tax on someone else's behalf. (b) no tax on salaries paid to non-local EDs/officials/employees. (c)–(d): no taxation of any kind on Bank-issued or Bank-guaranteed securities (including dividends/interest) where the tax either discriminates against the security for being a Bank security, or where the only jurisdictional hook is the currency/location the security happens to be issued or paid in. That second prong closes the obvious workaround — a state can't tax a World Bank bond just because it's dollar-denominated or serviced through a local bank branch.

## 3. Who appoints, who removes, and for what cause

- **Art. V §2**: Board of Governors — one governor + one alternate per member, five-year term, **"subject to the pleasure of the member appointing him"** — meaning each home government can recall its own governor at will; there's no independence-from-the-appointing-government built in at that layer.
- **Art. V §4**: twelve Executive Directors — five appointed outright by the five largest shareholders, seven elected per Schedule B by everyone else, two-year terms. Voting is per §3: basic votes (equal split of 5.55% of total voting power, post-2012) plus one share-vote per share held — so the "one member, one governor" democratic form conceals a share-weighted vote underneath.
- **Art. V §5**: the President is selected by the Executive Directors, chairs them with no vote except to break ties, and **"shall cease to hold office when the Executive Directors so decide"** — no cause standard, no term protection. The President serves entirely at the EDs' pleasure.
- **Art. VI §2**: the Bank itself can suspend a *member* for failing obligations, by majority of Governors exercising majority voting power — automatic expulsion one year later absent reinstatement.

## 4. Money: self-funding vs. appropriations

No government appropriates the Bank's operating budget. Art. II is the capital engine: $10,000,000,000 authorized stock (§2), each member subscribes per Schedule A, split 20% paid-in/callable-on-demand and 80% callable only to meet the Bank's own borrowing obligations (§5). Art. IV runs the operations off that base plus market borrowings (§1(a)(ii)) and guarantee/interest income, with a self-set commission rate (§4(a), §5(a)) and a self-accumulating Special Reserve (§6) to cover defaults before ever touching a member's callable capital (§7). The U.S. side of funding its own subscription is off-budget too — see `Bretton Woods Agreements Act/Reading the Charter — Bretton Woods Agreements Act.md` §4: Congress capitalized the U.S. subscription as a public-debt transaction with non-interest demand notes, not a line-item appropriation.

## 5. Audit & disclosure carve-outs

Art. V §13: the Bank publishes its own annual report with an audited statement of accounts, and circulates a financial-position summary "at intervals of three months or less" — but (b) any *other* report is published only "as it deems desirable." No GAO exemption or FOIA-equivalent language exists anywhere in the Articles because no U.S. audit regime attaches in the first place absent a waiver — disclosure is what the Bank chooses to disclose, on its own schedule, to its own members (§13(c)).

## 6. Amendment & withdrawal clauses

- **Art. VI §1**: any member can withdraw "at any time" by written notice, effective on receipt — no waiting period, no exit cost specified beyond the settlement-of-accounts machinery in §4 (continuing liability on outstanding loans/guarantees, share repurchase at book value, no payout for six months).
- **Art. VIII**: ordinary amendments need three-fifths of members holding 85% of total voting power (raised from four-fifths by the 1989 amendment — see Layer map). Three provisions are entrenched at **unanimous** consent: the withdrawal right itself (VI §1), the pre-emptive-rights-on-new-capital-stock protection (VIII(b)(ii) — captured in the verbatim file as the "Article U" typo, near-certainly Article II §3(c)), and the liability-limitation clause (II §6). A single dissenting member can block those three, and only those three.

## 7. Emergency powers

Art. VI §5(a): in an emergency, the Executive Directors alone may **temporarily** suspend new lending/guarantee operations pending Board of Governors review — a fast, low-quorum brake the Board can later reverse or ratify. Permanent suspension needs the full majority-of-Governors/majority-voting-power vote under §5(b). No "unusual and exigent circumstances" definitional test exists — "emergency" is undefined and left to the Executive Directors' own judgment.

## 8. The definitions section

There is no stand-alone definitions Article (unlike the IMF's Art. XXXI) — definitions are scattered and load-bearing where they appear. Art. V §4(b) quietly defines "members," for purposes of who counts toward the original five-largest-shareholder director seats, as **"governments of countries whose names are set forth in Schedule A"** — locking the founding-member roster (1944 signatories) into an ongoing structural privilege even as the Bank's membership has grown past 180 countries since. Art. IV §10 does related definitional work by declaring "only economic considerations shall be relevant" to Bank decisions — a self-imposed (and self-policed) definition of what counts as "political" that the Bank alone interprets.

## 9. Succession/perpetuity & dissolution

No fixed corporate lifespan. Art. VI §5 is the full wind-up sequence: permanent suspension of new operations by vote (§5(b)) triggers an orderly-liquidation-only mode; member liability for **uncalled** subscriptions continues "until all claims of creditors, including all contingent claims, shall have been discharged" (§5(c)) — capital-call exposure survives the decision to wind down. Creditors (direct, then contingent) are paid first (§5(d)); only after all liabilities are discharged or provided for can a distribution to members even be considered (§5(e)), and then only pro-rata by shareholding, in a fixed waterfall of the members' own obligations first, then held currency, then gold/acceptable currency, then remaining assets pro-rata (§5(h)).

## 10. Anything strange — the clause you'd never expect

**Art. IX is a self-judging charter.** Any dispute over what the Articles mean — between a member and the Bank, or between members — goes first to the Executive Directors (§(a)), whose decision can be appealed only to the Board of Governors, and **"whose decision shall be final"** (§(b)). No external court, no ICJ referral, nothing outside the institution itself gets the last word on what its own founding document means, while a decision is pending the Bank can act on it anyway. Only a *former* member, or a member during permanent suspension, gets routed to outside arbitration (§(c)) — meaning the self-interpretation privilege applies exactly as long as you're inside the tent. Runner-up strange fact: Art. VII §3's bar on suits "by members or persons acting for or deriving claims from members" means the 180-plus governments that own and fund the Bank are legally the *one* class of plaintiff who can never sue it. And a small institutional-decay wrinkle: the worldbank.org source pages themselves render two errors verbatim into the current official text — "Article U, Section 3 (c)" in Art. VIII(b)(ii) (almost certainly a garbled cross-reference to Article II §3(c)) and "International justice" / "an questions" in Art. IX(c) (almost certainly "International Justice" / "all questions") — reproduced and flagged rather than silently fixed in the verbatim file.

## Layer map

1. **1944 Bretton Woods conference / Articles of Agreement, signed; effective Dec. 27, 1945.** The founding text: purposes (Art. I), $10B authorized capital divided per Schedule A (Art. II), lending conditions (Art. III–IV), governance structure and the original twelve-director layout (Art. V), withdrawal/suspension/dissolution (Art. VI), the full immunity/tax stack (Art. VII), self-interpretation (Art. IX).
2. **→ Dec. 17, 1965 amendment.** Authorized IBRD loans/participations/guarantees to the International Finance Corporation — captured verbatim as Art. III §6, which caps such IFC lending at four times IFC's own unimpaired capital and surplus. The first crack in the Bank's own charter to let it fund a sister institution rather than only member borrowers directly.
3. **→ Feb. 16, 1989 amendment.** Raised the Art. VIII general-amendment threshold from four-fifths to **eighty-five percent** of total voting power — a higher bar to change the rules, meaning whichever bloc of members controls more than 15% of voting power (share votes plus basic votes) holds a standing veto over any future amendment.
4. **→ June 27, 2012 amendment — the voice/governance reform.** Rewrote Art. V §3(a): basic votes changed from a flat 250-per-member allocation to an equal division of 5.55% of the aggregate voting power across all members — a shift from a fixed head-count floor toward a formula that automatically dilutes as membership grows, while leaving share-votes (one vote per share) as the dominant weight. Per the Art. V footnote captured verbatim in this session: "Prior to the amendment each member had 250 basic votes."

## Entity Theory read

The IBRD is a treaty-born bank that wrote itself a legal force field: it can't be sued by the governments that own it (Art. VII §3), its own directors decide what its charter means with no outside court able to overrule them short of a member actually leaving (Art. IX), and its assets are shielded from search, tax, and seizure "by whomsoever held" (Art. VII §4, §9). Who holds the leash: nominally the Board of Governors (Art. V §2), but governors serve "at the pleasure" of their *home* governments, not the Bank — so national capitals control who sits in the room, while share-weighted voting (Art. V §3) means the largest shareholders control what happens once they're there, and the 85% amendment threshold (Art. VIII, since 1989) gives any bloc above 15% of voting power a permanent veto over changing any of it. Does it survive a change of control? Yes, by design — it has already outlived the entire postwar order it was built for, gained an IFC-lending arm in 1965, hardened its own amendment threshold against itself in 1989, and diluted its founding fixed-vote allocation in 2012 without ever touching the immunity stack, the self-interpretation clause, or the bar on member lawsuits. Those three load-bearing walls (Art. VII, Art. IX, Art. VII §3) have not moved once since 1944.
