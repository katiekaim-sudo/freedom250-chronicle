C. Authority response

Field Entry
Response state PARTIAL
Exact propositions answered The authorities broadly concurred with much of staff’s analysis and agreed on the need to rebuild external buffers, but rejected the view that insufficient reserve accumulation constrained disinflation and growth. They characterized the 2025 NIR miss as the result of an exceptional risk-off episode rather than a policy setback, defended the calibration and performance of the exchange-rate band, and rejected inflation targeting as feasible in the near to medium term.

F. Six-lane worksheet

L1 — Domestic anchor: D

Field Entry
One-sentence reason Inflation fell dramatically but remained persistently high, the disinflation process had stalled around the cutoff, and the nominal/operational framework still required substantial repair while tight credit imposed an activity cost.
Primary evidence + vintage End-period CPI: 117.8% in 2024 and 31.5% in 2025; annual inflation about 32½% in early 2026; March 2026 monthly inflation 3.4%; staff called for clarification of the monetary-aggregate anchor and eventual evolution toward greater exchange-rate flexibility and interest-rate tools.
Persistence evidence Inflation exceeded 200% in late 2023 before falling to double digits; the improvement is large but the remaining level and institutional fragility are persistent. Real GDP was -1.3% in 2024 and +4.4% in 2025, with activity slowing and uneven through the second half of 2025.
Shock evidence 2025 political uncertainty produced a sharp money-demand/dollarization shock and depreciation, tightening financial conditions and slowing disinflation; Middle East fuel/regulated-price shocks remained live.
Competing explanation Fiscal discipline, a large cumulative disinflation, more stable expectations, lower pass-through, and renewed 2026 growth could support C.
Confidence MEDIUM
Legal/event link Article IV BOARD-CONCERN; trade events not scored
Data gap CPI weights remain outdated; current-consumption weights and a longer post-shock expectations series could move the grade.

L2 — Underlying order: C

Field Entry
One-sentence reason The banking system remained liquid, capitalized, and operational, but acute dollarization, shallow domestic finance, rising consumer NPLs, weak peso demand, and large resident assets outside the system materially impair underlying monetary order.
Primary evidence + vintage At February–April 2026 vintages, banks remained liquid and well capitalized with adequate provisioning and comfortable FX liquidity, but credit had stagnated since 2025Q3 and consumer NPLs were rising; the authorities described the financial system as “resilient but shallow.”
Persistence evidence The dollar served as the primary hedge and exceeded 50% of M2 during the 2025 shock; authorities estimated resident U.S.-dollar holdings above US$200 billion, while domestic capital markets remained underdeveloped.
Shock evidence The pre-election dollarization wave stayed substantially inside banks rather than becoming a generalized bank run; no banking fracture or generalized domestic-payment breakdown was reported.
Competing explanation Strong bank liquidity/capital and retained FX deposits could support B; the shock did not break intermediation.
Confidence MEDIUM
Legal/event link CFMs are contextual only; §2 restrictions score in L5, not again here.
Data gap Current deposit-currency shares, NPL migration, liquidity stress tests, and peso-payment share after April 2026.

L3 — Exchange order: C

Field Entry
One-sentence reason The widening managed band was functioning and accumulating reserves at the current cutoff, but the regime’s recent stress, policy-driven segmentation, and incomplete buffer restoration remain material persistent impairments.
Primary evidence + vintage De jure/de facto other managed; BCRA bought about US$7.5 billion from January through the report cutoff, roughly 20% of traded volume; the peso stabilized, appreciated about 13% in real terms from end-2025, and futures were about 20% below the band ceiling.
Persistence evidence The regime changed from a crawling peg in April 2025; the 2025 risk-off episode pushed the rate toward the upper band and caused major reserve loss before the 2026 purchase program stabilized operation.
Shock evidence Authorities report that the band survived the late-2025 speculative episode and returned to its inner range, but acknowledge decisive Fund and U.S. official support.
Competing explanation Current band operation, two-way flexibility, purchases rather than defensive sales, and subdued futures could support B; conversely thin buffers could justify D, but that mechanism is assigned primarily to L4.
Confidence MEDIUM
Legal/event link MCP is legally recorded but primary performance effect assigned to L5; capital controls remain separate.
Data gap Accessible-market/official-rate spread series, forward-intervention stock, intervention disclosure, and post-April volatility.

L4 — External adjustment: E

Field Entry
One-sentence reason The external position remained weaker, with very precarious reserve coverage, negative NIR, no durable market access, large FX obligations, and continuing reliance on exceptional official financing despite an identifiable export-led repair path.
Primary evidence + vintage Preliminary 2025 assessment: WEAKER; staff CA gap -1.5% to -3.5% of GDP, midpoint -2.5%; implied REER overvaluation midpoint 15.8%; end-2025 GIR US$27.552 billion and NIR about -US$11.7 billion. The 2025 current account shifted to -1.1% of GDP. External-sector assessment, PDF p.65
Persistence evidence 2025 NIR fell roughly US$9 billion; the external position lacked sustained capital-market access and carried large public-sector FX obligations. The EFF, BIS/PBoC-related financing, repos, and extraordinary U.S. ESF support remained part of the financing architecture.
Shock evidence Since end-2025, agricultural/energy exports, portfolio inflows, and BCRA purchases produced an incipient reserve recovery; GIR reached US$31.9 billion by 2026-04-21 and NIR improved materially but remained negative.
Competing explanation Positive NIIP (+7.1% GDP), strong export prospects, a fiscal anchor, and rapid 2026 reserve purchases could support D; however, operation still depended on exceptional support and controls.
Confidence HIGH
Legal/event link EFF is not itself a failure; the E rests on operating buffers/market access. Trade events remain unscored pending observed transmission.
Data gap Usable versus gross reserves, encumbrances, complete bilateral/repo liabilities, 2026 CA outturn, and durable market-access evidence.

L5 — Current-payment usability: D

Field Entry
One-sentence reason Current international payments remained materially restricted across several transaction classes and one MCP persisted, although access had been substantially liberalized and the system was not generally unusable.
Primary evidence + vintage Officially reported restrictions cover resident-firm cross-use of MULC/CCL/MEP, advance import payments, 90-day related-party service delays, soybean import sequencing, legacy dividends/profits, and specified interest/amortization payments; one 30% withholding-tax MCP remained.
Persistence evidence Restrictions and CFMs date from 2019, though 2023–25 changes largely eased them; virtually all current-payment restrictions for resident individuals were removed, while firm and legacy-flow restrictions persisted.
Shock evidence Liberalization raised trade/capital flows, but staff still found prohibited operations, operating costs, and policy uncertainty; temporary Board approval was requested for BOP reasons.
Competing explanation The remaining restrictions are narrower, temporary, non-discriminatory in staff’s view, and focused on legacy/outflow risks; this argues against E or F.
Confidence HIGH on operating impairment; MEDIUM on formal approval status
Legal/event link Article VIII §2 UNRESOLVED; §3 UNRESOLVED; ER-ARS-IMPORT-PAYMENT
Data gap Formal Board approval wording, transaction-level delay/rejection rates, the share of current payments affected, and subsequent removal clocks.

L6 — Systemic service: NR

Field Entry
One-sentence reason The frozen packet does not provide the minimum official evidence needed to measure the peso’s actual cross-border reserve, invoice, payment, funding, hedge, swap-market, or regional-service role.
Primary evidence + vintage No quantified ARS international-role series appears in the frozen manifest or controlling IMF package.
Persistence evidence NOT-AVAILABLE
Shock evidence NOT-APPLICABLE
Competing explanation ARS clearly continues to serve domestic payment and denomination functions; its limited international role should not be treated as failure.
Confidence HIGH that NR is required
Legal/event link NONE
Data gap Official invoice/payment-currency shares, BIS cross-border ARS positions, regional settlement use, derivatives/funding depth, and reserve holdings.
