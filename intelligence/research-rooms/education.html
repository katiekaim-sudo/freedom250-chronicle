University money now comes with more checks around the payment

Federal education money increasingly checks who the recipient is, what conditions apply, how it reports and whether it passes research-security review, before or around payment. That does not mean every university is under one control system.

What we found

  • The topic is split into four studies: University Funding and Programmable Compliance (July 28, 2026), a Department of Education full sweep (August 4, 2026), Department of War research-security audits (August 17, 2026) and a Learning Agenda Playbook (September 9, 2026).
  • The August 4 sweep covers a call-to-action letter to universities, a Perkins loan servicing transition and a delay in COD enforcement, among other items.
  • The Department of War audits involve institution-level audit and mitigation directives, tied to eligibility and award and payment rules.
  • The study finds no government-wide public-blockchain mandate, and says an audit notice alone does not prove misconduct, an award cut or a payment hold.
  • A related project links a September 1, 2026 OLC opinion on state reporting to existing student-aid data matching. That link stays structural until a specific procedure, data transfer or payment effect is shown.

Why it matters

Control is moving into the payment and reporting machinery: who gets paid, under what conditions and after what checks. The study warns against reading this as one unified system.

What to watch

  • pending · Any specific Education reporting procedure, data transfer or payment effect tied to the September 1, 2026 OLC opinion.
  • pending · Whether a Department of War research-security audit leads to an award or payment action at a named school.

University money is moving to per-payment checks, not blockchain

Federal funding for universities is shifting from yearly reports and audits toward checks on each recipient, award and payment before money goes out. Treasury once tested a private grant ledger, but it touched no real grants, and current rules don't require any particular technology.

What we found

  • Federal sources paid $64.6 billion of the $117.7 billion in FY2024 university research spending (55 percent). HHS alone supplied $35.5 billion.
  • Today's public view is slow: a university's Form 990 can arrive 10.5 months after year-end, and IPEDS finance data can first appear about 18 months after year-end. No federal source links all of a university's legal entities and ID numbers.
  • Treasury's Fiscal Service and GAO built a private, permission-only Ethereum prototype for research grants. It ran outside production, affected no actual grant, and as of 2023 Treasury had no plan to advance it.
  • EO 14249 directs Do Not Pay screening and richer payment data. OMB's May 29, 2026 proposed rule would require eligibility checks before payment and a short written reason with each payment request, but it is still proposed, not final.
  • Research-security certifications already apply: NSF requires institutions with over $50 million a year in federal science support to certify a research-security program.

Why it matters

This fits the thesis closely: Washington is changing how it controls university money through payment systems, screening and grant rules inside existing law, not by a single new statute. The study warns against claiming a blockchain decision that no source shows.

What to watch

  • pending · Whether OMB's May 29, 2026 proposed Uniform Guidance rewrite becomes a final rule, and in what form.
  • pending · Any award term or rule that actually requires a ledger, digital wallet or public record anchor for grants.

Pentagon orders security reviews at 30 unnamed universities

On August 17, 2026 the Department of War said it had ordered 30 unnamed universities to audit their foreign research ties and report by August 31, with future federal research funding at stake. No wrongdoing, funding cutoff or penalty has been shown.

What we found

  • The schools must review academic, financial and research ties to foreign entities of concern (drawn from the Section 1286 list and groups tied to rebranded Confucius Institutes), assess exposure of sensitive or export-controlled research, write mitigation plans that can include ending partnerships, and report to the Department.
  • The 30 schools were not named. The study warns against filling in names from other lists, such as a press-reported 33-school list or a 21-school congressional cohort.
  • The release cited no specific legal authority for the audits. The related laws are narrower: a DoW limit on particular grants with Section 1286-listed partners (P.L. 118-159 § 238, extended through FY2026) and a DFARS funding restriction on schools hosting Confucius-type institutes.
  • Waiver authority under the Confucius Institute restriction ends October 1, 2026, and no waiver applies after that date.
  • The 'future federal research funding' language is broad, but no governmentwide debarment or NSF, NIH or DOE action followed.

Why it matters

Universities are being moved from project-by-project checks to institution-level review using existing defense research-security law and funding eligibility as the lever, not new legislation.

What to watch

  • 2026-08-31 · University reports due to the Department.
  • 2026-10-01 · Confucius Institute restriction waivers end.
  • pending · Any named schools, findings, eligibility decisions or award actions.

Education's August letter to colleges asks; it does not require

A recheck of the Education Department's sites on Aug. 4, 2026 found three things: a letter asking colleges to publish commitments by year-end, a planned move of Perkins loan servicing to a new company, and a delay in enforcing a student-loan reporting field. None is yet a funding condition, a finished transfer or a waiver.

What we found

  • Secretary Linda McMahon signed a three-page letter on Aug. 3, 2026 asking every college to post statements on seven topics, including admissions merit, free speech, cost and foreign influence, before the end of 2026. It cites no legal duty, form, penalty or funding tie.
  • Federal Perkins Loan servicing is moving from ECSI to GCR Servicing. ECSI stops taking new submissions Oct. 1, and the transfer is expected to finish by Dec. 1. That date is a plan, not a done deal.
  • The new 2026–27 Direct Loan field (Enrollment Status Effective Date) is still required. Federal Student Aid pushed enforcement past Aug. 8 to 'later in fall 2026' without a date; if a school leaves it blank, loan-limit rules still apply.
  • GAO's July 16 review of the STATS and earnings-accountability final rule: most of it takes effect July 1, 2027, with two parts effective Aug. 31, 2026.
  • No Education Department entry appeared in the Aug. 2–4 Federal Register.

Why it matters

The letter is only a request now, but its seven topics overlap tools the government already controls, such as student-aid eligibility, civil-rights reviews, accreditation and grants. The study treats a later link to funding as something to watch for, not a fact.

What to watch

  • 2026-10-01 to 2026-12-01 · Whether the Perkins servicing move from ECSI to GCR finishes on time and GCR opens intake.
  • by 2026-12-31 · College responses to the letter, and any later rule, grant term or finding that ties those responses to funding or eligibility.
  • pending · The new enforcement date for the COD reporting field.

Education Department gives states a voluntary planning guide

The Department of Education released a voluntary playbook on September 9 that helps state education agencies tie their goals to evidence and to decisions about programs and money. Pilot states improved their planning, but no student gains or legal transfers are shown.

What we found

  • It was built by OESE, IES and the National Comprehensive Center with eight states: Arkansas, Georgia, Idaho, Kansas, New Hampshire, Tennessee, Utah and Wisconsin.
  • It has six steps, ending with recording a decision to keep, grow, change or stop a program, with a named owner, timing and follow-up checks. Setting up a first agenda is expected to take about three to six months.
  • Georgia surveyed more than 2,600 educators before adding supports for special education teacher retention. Kansas joined several accountability systems into one shared model. Utah used Claude to draft question wording, with people choosing the final text.
  • The fact sheet ties the release to the administration's aim to return education to states and close the department. The playbook itself is voluntary help, not a legal step toward closure.
  • Its capacity scale is for self-reflection only. It is not a ranking, grant test or compliance gate.

Why it matters

It addresses the receiving end of moving federal education work to states: whether states can decide what works and change course. It is a voluntary tool, so it supports the move without carrying out any legal transfer.

What to watch

  • pending · A state publishing an adopted learning agenda with named decision owners and dates.
  • pending · A documented budget, staffing or program decision that came from the process, and outcome data beyond participation counts.
