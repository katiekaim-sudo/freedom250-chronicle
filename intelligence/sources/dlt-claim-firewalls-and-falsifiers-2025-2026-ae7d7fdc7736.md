# DLT Claim Firewalls and Falsifiers, 2025–2026

## Claim firewalls

| Public phrase | Maximum supported claim | Blocked inference |
|---|---|---|
| `tokenized` | a claim or object has a token representation under a named design | token is native title, liquid, collateral-eligible or finally settled |
| `on-chain` | specified state is recorded/executed on a ledger | every legal, cash, custody and accounting state is on that ledger |
| `atomic` | linked technical conditions execute all-or-none under the tested mechanism | legal title/finality, zero liquidity risk, no correction or no insolvency unwind |
| `real value` | real economic/legal value entered a bounded test | recurring market production, scalability, measured benefit or general access |
| `production environment` | operator used production systems/controls for the event | public service is generally open or recurring customer volume exists |
| `launched` / `live` | the issuer/operator describes a service or authorized stage as launched/live | first transaction, recurring usage, full product scope or failure-proven operation |
| `central-bank money` | named design uses or represents a claim anchored to the central bank | direct individual central-bank claim, general wCBDC or same-ledger finality |
| `settled` | a named leg reached a specified technical/operational/legal state | every asset/cash/FX/accounting leg is final |
| `collateral` | an asset is proposed, tested or accepted under named terms | every lender/CCP/central bank recognizes it or a loan/margin credit was granted |
| `interoperable` | two or more systems can exchange specified data/state under a design | fungibility, common finality, common identity, common liquidity or safe failure |
| `immutable` | historic records are difficult/controlled to alter | no freeze, correction, compensating transaction, court remedy or restatement |
| `decentralized` | some validation, operation or access is distributed | legal authority, governance, admission, upgrade and loss are decentralized |

## Case-specific firewalls

- **DTC real production trades ≠ DTC digital cash settlement.** Launch-phase
  transactions are not settled in digitized form; tokenized entitlements begin
  with no DTC collateral or settlement value.
- **SEC staff no-action relief ≠ Commission approval.** It is time-limited
  enforcement posture under stated conditions.
- **Clearstream D7 totals ≠ D7 DLT totals.** Broader digital issuance includes
  non-DLT dematerialized securities.
- **SDX total issuance ≠ total wCBDC settlement.** The wholesale-CBDC subset is
  a continuing pilot with its own volumes and limits.
- **Euroclear D-FMI production ≠ Canton collateral-network production.** One is
  a digital issuance operation; the other was a pilot/design phase.
- **Swift ready for use ≠ completed live settled payment.** Banks were preparing
  pilots and retain final settlement authority outside the ledger.
- **Kinexys aggregate volume ≠ JPM Coin public-chain volume.** Product, currency,
  chain and legal-claim states must be sourced separately.
- **DSS Gate 2 ≠ a live transaction.** It permits bounded activity but does not
  prove an issuance or settlement occurred.
- **Pontes Q3 launch target ≠ live at cutoff.** User testing and a scheduled date
  are not an operative receipt.
- **Agorá's 80-second average ≠ production efficiency.** Scripted windows,
  manual approvals, external account funding and a small controlled sample limit
  the performance inference.
- **mBridge MVP ≠ production global rail.** BIS expressly exited and rejected
  the mature-operating characterization.
- **Project finance adjacency ≠ project finance adoption.** A DLT collateral or
  deposit system does not become a project's funding rail without the named
  instrument, borrower, draw, settlement and accounting chain.

## Counterexamples to the “adapter-only” thesis

The transition cannot be summarized as “the chain is only a front end.”

1. SDX is a regulated DLT CSD with operative securities state.
2. D7 DLT supports DLT-native issuance inside a regulated CSD framework.
3. Helvetia places SNB wholesale central-bank money on the SDX environment for
   same-ledger DvP in a limited pilot.
4. The Eurosystem intends Pontes to extend from T2 synchronization toward a
   public DLT settlement platform with native finality and programmability.
5. Kinexys performs recurring bank-ledger transfers and programmable deposit-
   money workflows, rather than merely displaying a legacy payment.

These cases prove genuine state migration. They do not prove universal
disintermediation or complete migration of title, liquidity, accounting and
failure authority.

## Counterexamples to the “one ledger is inevitable” thesis

1. DTC is explicitly multi-chain and retains an institutional reconciliation
   layer.
2. Swift's ledger is an orchestration layer above bank environments and multiple
   settlement options.
3. LSEG designs for multiple chains and traditional infrastructure.
4. Appia keeps single-ledger and interoperable multi-ledger options open.
5. Central banks warn that a single ledger creates governance, concentration,
   confidentiality, resilience and technology-lock-in problems.
6. Different objects require different issuers and legal regimes even when they
   share technical rails.

## What would strengthen the core synthesis

- multiple major CSDs and banks use compatible rulebooks while retaining local
  legal authority;
- central-bank DLT money becomes routinely available to settle assets on or
  across several ledgers;
- collateral can move between venues without duplicated buffers or
  detokenization;
- shared events feed audited accounting/capital reports with governed
  correction;
- handled incidents show operators, courts and resolution authorities aligning
  the technical and legal state; and
- recurring volumes and measured user benefits grow while legacy infrastructure
  remains as a governed bridge or fallback.

## What would weaken the federated-transition synthesis

| Falsifier | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| one infrastructure becomes the cross-asset authoritative venue | binding law/rulebooks plus recurring issuance, title, cash, collateral and lifecycle volume across jurisdictions |
| central-bank money and major assets coexist natively at scale | production volumes, legal finality, liquidity operations, access and failure handling on the same platform |
| legacy systems materially retire | formal decommissioning, migrated balances/records, participant transition and sustained operation without fallback dependence |
| public permissionless ledgers carry systemic finance without incumbent control | named regulated transactions at scale, clear finality, governance, operational resilience, correction and loss allocation not dependent on a CSD/bank/operator wrapper |
| DLT produces material project-finance transmission | named borrower/instrument, tokenized title/collateral, draw, settled cash, vendor/project use, revenue/debt service and default evidence |
| DLT eliminates reconciliation/accounting duplication | audited multi-entity close showing the shared event is accepted without separate control books while correction and regulatory reporting remain sound |

## What would disprove a specific operating claim

Promote or demote per object, not by sector narrative.

- A first transaction can be disproved by an operator correction, test-only
  classification or absence of the claimed settlement leg.
- “24/7” can be narrowed by conversion windows, maintenance, RTGS closure or
  participant funding limits.
- “Central-bank-money settlement” can be narrowed if the actual holder owns only
  an operator claim backed by an omnibus account.
- “Collateral use” can be narrowed if the transaction was simulated, pledged but
  not financed, or required conversion to a conventional CSD position.
- “Finality” can be narrowed if the technical state remains probabilistic or the
  law/rulebook permits a conflicting insolvency outcome.

## First-failure falsifier suite

The architecture is not proved until at least one case publicly answers:

1. Which record prevailed after a mismatch?
2. Who had power to pause, freeze, reverse or compensate?
3. Did one leg become final before the other failed?
4. What liquidity funded the gap?
5. How were the books corrected?
6. Did a participant/client bear loss, and under which rule?
7. Could the position be redeemed, ported or detokenized?
8. Did the system resume without double-spend, duplicate payment or orphaned
   collateral?

## Net rule

Treat every DLT claim as a vector. A strong receipt in one column does not fill
the others. The research standard is not skepticism for its own sake; it is
making the exact transition visible without erasing what really changed.
