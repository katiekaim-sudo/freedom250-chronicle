Why this exists

The Workbench already held strong Russia-specific research on Ukraine diplomacy, sanctions, oil and gas routes, and the September 2026 U.S. sanctions statute. It did not have a country-level orientation explaining how Russia's presidential-security system, war economy, nuclear status, alliance instruments, global institutions, commodity routes and constrained U.S. relationship fit together.

This package supplies that first map. It routes detailed Ukraine, energy and sanctions claims to their existing owners rather than duplicating them.

Bottom line

There is no single “Russia relationship.” The United States and Russia now interact through a layered system of:

  • diplomatic representation under severe operating constraints;
  • nuclear deterrence and risk management after the expiration of New START;
  • ad hoc Ukraine-war diplomacy without a public final settlement;
  • sanctions, export controls, licenses, waivers and enforcement;
  • commodity and industrial dependencies often rerouted through third countries;
  • multilateral contact at the United Nations and other institutions;
  • narrow operating exceptions, especially the International Space Station;
  • case-specific consular, prisoner and humanitarian channels; and
  • very thin people-to-people infrastructure.

The relationship is therefore less institutionalized than the China relationship and more dependent on unilateral law, multilateral forums, technical exceptions, third-country routes and episodic leader or envoy contact.

Governing boundary

  • Russian official sources establish Russian law, institutions, published acts, data and attributed positions. They do not independently verify battlefield, election, sanctions-effect or treaty-performance claims.
  • U.S. official sources establish U.S. law, policy, actions and attributed assessments. They do not become neutral descriptions of Russian intent merely because they are official.
  • Ukrainian, European and other affected-state sources establish their own positions and acts. They are not substitutes for Russian acceptance or a joint instrument.
  • Multilateral sources establish adopted texts, membership, common definitions and institutional findings within their mandates. A General Assembly resolution is not a bilateral settlement; a treaty membership is not proof of performance.
  • The Russian Federation's domestic territorial claims and internationally recognized borders must be kept in separate columns.
