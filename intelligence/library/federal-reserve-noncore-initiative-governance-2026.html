When the Fed is not the expert owner

The Fed OIG turned one construction failure into an agency-wide rule: for major work outside its core expertise, the Board needs imported delivery skill, independent challenge and controls that survive into the next project.

What we found

  • The Eccles-1951 finding is a capability boundary, not a claim that the Federal Reserve is generally incompetent: monetary, regulatory and supervisory expertise does not automatically create construction or enterprise-technology delivery expertise.
  • The OIG's seventh recommendation calls for an agency-wide framework with full-scope budgets, stated cost limits, KPIs, accountability, materiality-based escalation, oversight criteria and independent senior members.
  • Chairman Warsh accepted the program and directed GSA project leadership, an independent awarded-cost audit, GMP negotiations, fixed metrics, contract reconciliation and permanent standards, but those are directions rather than completed results.
  • The independent audit can connect contractor and subcontractor awards to invoices, cost codes, construction in process and eventual fixed assets; the public financial statements currently show only aggregate construction in process.
  • The decisive durability test is first reuse: whether the framework actually governs planned IT modernization or another named major initiative before costs and schedule drift become irreversible.

Why it matters

This could change how an unusually autonomous, self-funded institution governs work outside its core mandate. The Board would keep legal authority while deliberately separating ownership, specialized execution, independent assurance and senior oversight—a portable control constitution rather than a one-project repair.

What to watch

  • 2026-12-31 or earlier on publication · An operative GSA project-executive instrument, named independent auditor, audit protocol or completed cost-validation result.
  • pending · A signed GMP or enforceable substitute, fixed original/current baselines, fee cap, service reconciliation and any reimbursement or project credit.
  • 2027-03-31 or earlier on publication · An issued agency-wide significant-initiative policy and the Board's 2026 construction-in-process and placed-in-service accounting.
  • pending · First documented use of the framework on IT modernization or another major non-core initiative, followed by measurable outcome evidence.
