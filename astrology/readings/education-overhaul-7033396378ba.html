Schools, students and the education overhaul

Who decides how schools and student aid work as federal services shift toward states, and do students benefit?

The braid so far

This story is larger than the university and science-money braid. The Chronicle's Department of Education legal map, evidence through August 15, says the Department still exists under 20 U.S.C. § 3411. Executive closure direction, staff reductions and interagency service agreements can change who does work without repealing that charter. The March 2026 ED–Treasury agreement bought collection services; it did not transfer title to the student-loan book. A court stay is not a merits holding that the Department was abolished.

The State Learning Agenda Playbook study, evidence through September 9, finds a voluntary method for State education agencies to tie questions and evidence to programme and budget decisions. Pilot cases show changed planning and coordination, not demonstrated student gains or a legal transfer of federal authority. The Education router keeps school/State planning, higher-education funding, research security and student-loan administration on their own clocks.

The forward question is whether a State or local decision maker actually receives power, staff, records and money to act, uses credible evidence to change a school or aid programme, and shows a student result that survives the next leadership change. The constructive route is plural State capacity with visible reasons and remedy; the shadow is a hollow federal shell and many new plans while students, teachers and borrowers cannot tell who is accountable. Ordinary decentralization and uneven local improvement remain strong rivals to a single national overhaul.

This braid begins without authored planetary strands. The 2027 authority/action bridge, 2028 care/record split, 2029 price and 2030 working-bargain sky are forward questions in Capacity or Spectacle, not an automatic education forecast.
