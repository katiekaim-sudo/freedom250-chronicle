# NSSTS Pivotal Third-Party Control Map

**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-08-19  
**Status:** active Workbench research; not vault doctrine; not an investment screen  
**Controlling registry:** `NSSTS_PIVOTAL_THIRD_PARTY_CONTROL_REGISTRY_2026-08-19.json`

## Ruling

The most consequential company on a Science Strategy project may never be the
headline awardee. A third party can still determine whether the project:

- receives an interconnection agreement;
- reaches financial close;
- clears environmental and site-risk review;
- passes electrical and integrated-systems testing;
- receives insurance capacity or a facility certification;
- produces traceable, qualified scientific or manufacturing data; or
- becomes staffed, dispatchable and operable after construction.

This phase therefore maps **decision rights and control records**, not just
technical products.

```text
site and capital proposal
  -> independent study / due diligence / permitting
  -> design and construction
  -> inspection / calibration / commissioning
  -> certification / insurance / owner acceptance
  -> operating authority / market execution
```

The registry adds 30 candidate entities or contracting-entity seams. For every
one, the public-record conclusion is the same: **no named award, task, study or
engagement was found for the target set at the cutoff.** That is a negative
research boundary, not proof that the company is uninvolved. Private
subcontracts, insurer assignments, confidential interconnection work and
pre-award advisory engagements may not be public.

## Five distinct kinds of power

| Control type | What the company can actually do | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Formal gate** | Issue or condition a certification, insurance position, lender reliance record or milestone certificate | E3, Uptime Institute, Intertek, UL, FM |
| **Delegated execution** | Perform a study, solicitation, market function or operating role under authority delegated by an owner, utility or lender | Qualus, Pearl Street, GDS, CAMS, TEA |
| **Acceptance evidence** | Produce the test, calibration, inspection or qualification record used by the formal decision maker | Doble, EPS, Shermco, MBP, Transcat, Micross, Element |
| **Advisory influence** | Shape models, requirements, specifications and risks without issuing final approval | Danovo, PowerGEM, EnerNex |
| **Critical-path supply** | Move, connect, clean or supply equipment and utilities without which installation or stable operation stops | MEI, ChemTreat, Matheson, Barnhart |

These roles should not be collapsed. A testing company can be pivotal without
holding energization authority. An insurer can materially change a design
without issuing a government acceptance. A utility consultant can determine
the upgrades in a study while the utility retains formal approval.

## Highest-priority targets

### 1. Qualus, LLC — the best large-load analogue

NYISO commissioned Qualus for the January 2025 System Impact Study of Micron
New York Semiconductor Manufacturing LLC's White Pine Phase 1 load project.
That study addressed the reliability model, affected systems, required
facilities and applicable NERC, NPCC and NYSRC criteria.

This is the closest public analogue found for a large science or AI load moving
through an actual utility study. Qualus is not thereby assigned to Argonne or a
DOE campus. Promotion requires the target campus, utility, task, completed model
and accepted output.

### 2. E3 Consulting Services, LLC — a financing permission layer

E3, within the NAES family, serves lenders and investors as an independent
engineer. Its work includes fatal-flaw analysis, technical and commercial due
diligence, environmental and NERC review, construction monitoring and milestone
reporting.

This is a different NAES-family role from O&M. If private infrastructure
financing enters a federal AI or power project, an independent engineer can
condition drawdowns and financial close without appearing in the public
technology announcement. The decisive evidence would be the lender assignment,
reliance letter, report and milestone certificate.

### 3. Uptime Institute, Intertek and UL — overlapping but different acceptance paths

**Uptime Institute, LLC** independently evaluates data-center design,
constructed facilities and operations against its Tier system. Its constructed
facility process includes live demonstrations and applies to the installed
configuration, not merely the drawings.

**Intertek Testing Services NA, Inc.** is an OSHA-recognized NRTL. Intertek's
data-center services also span electrical load and power-quality testing,
cooling, enclosure, ICT, cybersecurity and integrated commissioning.

**UL Solutions Inc. / UL LLC** requires an explicit contracting-entity seam.
The public parent, certification entities and task-specific contracting parties
must not be treated as interchangeable. UL can test facilities, modular systems,
electrical equipment, batteries and cooling fluids, but component certification
does not certify a whole facility.

These firms could all be present on the same project while performing different
functions. None currently has a public target engagement.

### 4. Factory Mutual Insurance Company — design influence through underwriting

FM reports protecting about 1,100 data centers representing approximately
$250 billion of insurable value. Its current data-center loss-prevention
material addresses utility failure, standby generation, UPS systems, batteries,
power isolation and liquid cooling.

FM's engineers do not issue the government's project acceptance. But their
findings can become conditions for insurance capacity, lender comfort and
owner risk decisions. That makes underwriting a practical design gate. FM
Approvals LLC must remain separate from Factory Mutual Insurance Company.

### 5. Transcat, Inc. — the defensibility of the measurement itself

Transcat is a particularly strong cross-sector node because it can touch
autonomous laboratories, biomanufacturing and industrial qualification.
ISO/IEC 17025 calibration, NIST traceability, instrument asset management and
IQ/OQ/PQ validation create the evidence that an instrument, utility or facility
was fit for intended use.

A Charles River case documents building-management-system IQ/OQ/PQ, facility
and equipment qualification and clean-gas validation. For any target assignment,
the exact calibration center, method and accreditation scope would still have
to match the required measurement.

### 6. MEI Industrial Solutions — the physical handoff nobody announces

MEI Rigging & Crating, LLC d/b/a MEI Industrial Solutions handles cleanroom
tool moves, rigging, millwrighting, industrial storage, powered test space and
prebuilt utility-module installation. This is the seam between an equipment
purchase and a facility that can actually begin site acceptance testing.

For Natcast, an MEI-like company could surface only after the construction
contract and tool list become visible. Moving a tool into place does not qualify
the process; OEM authorization, cleanroom protocol, utility hookup and SAT are
separate gates.

### 7. ChemTreat, Inc. — operational cooling chemistry

ChemTreat reports supporting more than 200 North American data centers and
documents direct-to-chip cooling treatment at an unnamed DOE national
laboratory. Its work includes pre-operational cleaning, corrosion and fouling
control, biological control, coolant compatibility, monitoring and water reuse.

This is an excellent example of a company that may not build the chiller or
cooling distribution unit but can still determine whether heat transfer and
materials remain reliable after turnover. The unnamed national-lab case cannot
be assigned to Argonne.

### 8. Micross Components, Inc. — packaging transition and trusted qualification

DMEA lists the Micross Advanced Interconnect Technology facility for trusted
post-processing and packaging/assembly, while DLA records qualified
manufacturing and environmental test operations. Micross could bridge
experimental package flows to trusted high-reliability use.

Accreditation is facility- and flow-specific. A Natcast role would require a
Project Agreement or order, selected baseline-flow interface, test vehicle,
acceptance limits and a government-approved report.

### 9. IPS-Integrated Project Services, LLC — making DBPC operable

DBPC will not become an accepted pilot merely because fermentation equipment is
installed. IPS's factory/site acceptance, validation master planning, IQ/OQ/PQ,
SOP, controls and utility-qualification capabilities sit between construction
and fit-for-intended-use operation.

The public evidence supports biopharma and biotech CQV. It does not yet prove a
Hawaii DBPC team or that the same methods translate cleanly into an austere,
deployable bioindustrial environment.

### 10. Doble, EPS and Shermco — electrical permission to energize

- **Doble Engineering Company** produces transformer, bushing, breaker and
  relay diagnostic evidence.
- **Electric Power Systems International, Inc.** performs NETA-style startup,
  ATS, relay, switchgear and substation acceptance testing.
- **Shermco Industries, Inc.** performs LV/MV/HV calibration, repair and field
  verification.

These firms do not generally hold the utility's formal energization decision.
They produce the record the owner or utility uses to decide whether equipment
is safe and ready. No target site order or commissioning package is public.

### 11. Terracon Consultants, Inc. — the pre-award site veto

Terracon reports more than 3,100 data-center projects and combines parcel
screening, environmental review, geotechnical exploration, thermal-resistivity
testing, wetlands/NEPA work, construction materials QA and building-envelope
commissioning.

This is a pivotal *before-the-award* role: soil, water, environmental or routing
conditions can eliminate a site before the headline technology partners are
selected. Portfolio statistics are company-authored, so target promotion needs
a named parcel, authorization and accepted report.

### 12. CAMS — the alternative operator comparator

Consolidated Asset Management Services reports responsibility for more than
100 North American power facilities totaling about 72 GW. Hallador's SEC filing
confirms continuing CAMS O&M and asset-management duties at the 1,080 MW Merom
plant. CAMS also markets a data-center-power lifecycle spanning permitting,
commissioning, mobilization, market participation and 24/7 O&M.

CAMS is the clearest non-NAES comparator for operator insertion after visible
construction. The public record does not connect it to a federal science or
target data-center site.

## What records will expose this layer

The next pass should search for documents that appear *after strategy and before
acceptance*:

1. interconnection study scopes, consultant appendices and CEII-safe cover
   filings;
2. lender technical-advisor appointments, reliance letters and draw
   certificates;
3. environmental, geotechnical and constructability reports tied to a parcel;
4. commissioning plans, issues logs, FAT/SAT protocols and integrated-system
   test scripts;
5. NRTL, Tier, insurer and AHJ findings tied to a particular configuration;
6. tool-move, rigging, high-purity utility and cooling-water packages;
7. calibration scopes, QML/Nadcap records, witness plans and accepted test
   reports; and
8. operator mobilization, NERC registration, market-service and fuel-management
   agreements.

Those records answer the real question: not merely who is capable, but **who is
allowed to examine, condition, certify, move, energize or operate the thing**.
