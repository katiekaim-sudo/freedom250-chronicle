Official study title: International Monetary Transition

Which reserve, settlement, invoicing, FX and stablecoin changes constitute diversification, pricing or actual monetary substitution?

The dollar system is being reworked, not replaced

Countries are spreading reserves, settling some trade in local currencies and testing stablecoins, but these are different moves from actually replacing a currency. The study's deep look at Japan finds its money system being rebuilt through new links and rules, with no sign of replacement or a current crisis.

What we found

  • The Bank of Japan decided on September 18, 2026 to raise its overnight target and deposit rate to 1.25 percent, effective September 24. A September 10 speech by board member Masu said cuts to bond-buying would stop at about ¥2 trillion a month from April 2027.
  • The BOJ's audited fiscal-2025 balance sheet shrank by ¥66.742 trillion. Interest it pays on reserves more than doubled and its payment to the Treasury fell.
  • Japan's yen stablecoin JPYSC is live but small and closed. Both of the BOJ's digital-currency tracks remain pre-production, and a 2021 link between Japanese bonds and Hong Kong central-bank money was retired on September 11 with no reason given.
  • At the September 1 G20 meeting in Asheville, all members present agreed the Chair's Statement except China, which objected to four paragraphs, including those on IMF surveillance and sovereign debt.
  • A related deep dive found no public 50-year Saudi deal to sell oil only in dollars, though a real dollar-oil recycling system exists.

Why it matters

It supports the thesis in the money system: established central banks, finance ministries and payment networks are being rebuilt through new links, rules and digital tools while keeping control of final settlement. The study does not find a coordinated plan to replace the dollar or the yen.

What to watch

  • 2027-04 · Whether the BOJ's bond-purchase cuts stop at about ¥2 trillion a month as signaled.
  • pending · Any move by the BOJ from digital-currency testing to an actual issuance decision.
  • pending · Follow-through on the G20 Asheville work on digital assets and payment modernization.

Research summary

Reserve diversification, regional settlement and trade invoicing remain separate from actual monetary substitution. Bessent's April 2026 IIF scorecard adds the incumbent strategy and a three-way legitimacy contest. The IMF symmetry audit finds candid surveillance of systemic members but operationalized, finance-linked adjustment for borrowers. The July 31 Japan–Korea cases and saved August Japanese confirmation distinguish national intervention and dealer prices from a separate stablecoin quote; the disclosed aggregate does not resolve every trade date, allocation or U.S. funding leg. Korea's observed token price transmission is not net capital flow; Japan's limited production rails are not shown to have supported intervention. The September 1 Asheville G20 Chair's Statement adds agreed work on digital assets and payment modernization, but China expressly objected to four paragraphs and the statement itself is not implementation or monetary substitution. Masu's September 10 BOJ speech supplied an individual hawkish-normalization signal. The September 28 July-meeting minutes and the September 18 decision close the first rate-decision clock: the Board raised the overnight target and complementary deposit rate to 1.25 percent effective September 24. The minutes show bank lending remained strong, major/regional and shinkin outcomes diverged, and no new prudential rule was made; JGB-stock-runoff, bank-level transmission, market-stability and digital-rail clocks remain separate. The Japan Monetary Constitution build uses balance-sheet movement as its empirical spine. Layer 0 finds that the 2021–2026 JGB–HKD link synchronized BOJ-recorded JGB title with HKMA central-bank money without creating a new claim or merging finality; the September 11 retirement is established, while utilization, rationale and successor remain unknown. Layer 1 finds embedded BOJ independence inside a braided Diet–Cabinet–MOF–BOJ–FSA authority structure: the BOJ can operate a JGB auction without buying the debt, execute MOF intervention without deciding it, and settle government funds without authorizing expenditure. Layer 2 finds a multi-book transition: the BOJ's audited fiscal-2025 balance sheet contracted by ¥66.742 trillion; reserve remuneration more than doubled and the Treasury payment fell; sovereign debt and interest expense rose on separate government clocks; and current MOF/FEFSA intervention is material while the latest monthly operation's direction remains undisclosed. Layer 3 finds asymmetric private transmission: bank core margins improved, but shinkin net income fell and regional banks realized ¥1.136 trillion of bond losses; floating borrowers repriced on staged clocks; GPIF absorbed ¥14.753 trillion of domestic bonds while its bond sleeve lost 5.11 percent; and capital, stress tests and delinquency data still show an orderly system. Layer 4 finds private JGB risk-bearing inside public finality: MOF shortened issuance toward investor capacity, recent benchmark auctions remained covered, and repo and JSCC intermediate funding and default risk, while BOJ-NET retains title and yen settlement and the BOJ retains collateral, scarcity-lending and contingent-purchase authority. Layer 5 finds singleness without sameness: cash, BOJ reserves, bank deposits, safeguarded provider balances, trust stablecoins and tokenized deposits share a denomination but not a debtor, ledger, asset or failure path. JPYSC is live but closed-loop and tiny; DCJPY remains an issuing-bank deposit; both BOJ CBDC tracks remain pre-production. The concrete digital-money balance-sheet change is the 2026 capacity to move up to half of a trust-stablecoin reserve from demand deposits into qualifying short sovereign securities or term deposits, adding valuation, liquidity and contribution clocks without giving tokenholders title to the reserve asset. Layer 6 finds many interfaces but few finality rooms: BOJ-NET supplies wholesale yen finality and JGB title; Zengin's large-value RTGS, small-value DNS and More Time clocks can put customer credit ahead of interbank finality; and its collateral and private mutualized loss rules keep liquidity distinct from loss. Zengin-Net's own 2026 diagnosis supports constitutional refactoring under infrastructure strain, but the conditional fiscal-2030 replacement and unresolved settlement asset do not establish crisis or a completed successor. Layer 7 finds mobility without monetary succession: Agorá's real-value tests, Swift's shared commitments, DCJPY, stablecoins, tokenized JGBs and the live Indonesia–Japan QR corridor move coordination and programmable control faster than they move debtor identity, final yen settlement or statutory title. Retail CBDC would create a direct public BOJ claim but remains unauthorized and simulated. The evidence supports constitutional refactoring through adapters, not current monetary replacement or an established legitimacy crisis. Layer 8 finds public continuity and layered loss: BOJ finality and Articles 33/37/38 liquidity sit at the public center, but BOJ credit is not intended to compensate solvency loss; DICJ insurance and resolution, TLAC, JSCC/Zengin waterfalls, fraud restitution and industry contributions allocate cost before legally gated fiscal support. The evidence supports concentrated classification power and bounded legitimacy pressure, while April 2026 capital, funding and stress results argue against a current systemic or legitimacy crisis. The final Japan proof follows six transaction and failure paths and rules for constitutional refactoring without monetary succession: plural risk-bearing inside public finality. It strongly supports concentrated finality and distributed balance-sheet burden, but not current monetary replacement, systemic crisis or an established legitimacy crisis; unequal protection and burden support only a bounded, falsifiable legitimacy-pressure inference. The governed six-lens visual makes the whole constitution, balance-sheet migration, JGB life cycle, payment-finality chain, digital adapters and failure/loss ladder inspectable while preserving the source owners and bounded states. The September 14 UK delta identifies collateral mobility as the near-term centre of the joint FCA–Bank wholesale-tokenisation program and opens a bounded FCA inquiry into eligible physical-gold ownership tokens, while preserving separate title, custody, collateral, settlement, finality, prudential, perimeter and failure clocks. The records support burden migration and constitutional refactoring, but not a present solvency or legitimacy-crisis claim.
