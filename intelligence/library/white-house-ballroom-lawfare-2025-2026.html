Ballroom building goes on while the court fight stays open

The main court case asks whether the White House needed Congress's express approval to build its new ballroom. An appeals court backed a halt, but Chief Justice Roberts paused that halt on August 21, so building may continue with no final ruling on the merits.

What we found

  • The core case is National Trust for Historic Preservation v. National Park Service, filed December 12, 2025. It turns on 40 U.S.C. 8106, which bars new buildings on D.C. federal park grounds without express congressional approval.
  • A district judge granted a preliminary injunction on March 31 and narrowed it April 16. The D.C. Circuit upheld it 2-1 on August 7, with Judge Rao dissenting. Chief Justice Roberts stayed it on August 21 without giving reasons; Supreme Court emergency case 26A203 is still open.
  • East Wing demolition was finished within three days around October 20-23, 2025. The Park Service had environmental records from August, but they became public only in the lawsuit; design and site approvals came later (CFA February 19, NCPC April 2).
  • Around it: an early suit dropped without a ruling, a Public Citizen records suit that got the funding agreement released, an asbestos-records suit, a Campaign Legal Center referral about 26 lobbyist donors, and bills in Congress. None of the bills became law.
  • The July 31, 2025 announcement described a 90,000-square-foot ballroom paid with about $200 million in private money. The government told the Supreme Court it was 65% complete as of August 13, a claim made in a court filing.

Why it matters

It shows how old statutes, courts and records laws become the arena for fights over executive building power. Opponents and the government are both working inside existing legal process; the study finds no proof of one coordinated campaign.

What to watch

  • pending · Whether the full Supreme Court grants or denies the stay, and what issue it focuses on (standing, the narrow ultra vires route, statutory meaning or security).
  • pending · Records produced in the asbestos case, and any U.S. Attorney action on the April 6 donor referral.
  • pending · Any authorization or restriction bill enacted, and a final ruling in the district court.
