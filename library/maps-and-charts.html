If you want dollars back from a stablecoin or tokenized fund, who pays you, how fast, and what can stop it?

It depends on the route, and only the proposed federal rule names a speed. Of the 7 routes the library compares, the issuer or fund pays you back for USDC (Circle, for eligible holders) and USYC (Circle's USDC Teller, whose available liquidity is shown in real time; larger and same-day requests go through support procedures). For BUIDL, the routes are an announced exchange of fund shares for Ripple's RLUSD and trades with whitelisted counterparties on UniswapX, which gives "no assurance of liquidity or pricing." On CHIPS, XRPL and Swift it is participant positions, trading liquidity or banks. The Fed's proposal would give issuers no more than 2 business days to redeem after a valid request; it is not final.

When can an investment adviser hold clients' crypto keys itself under the SEC proposal?

Only as a last resort, and only if the proposal becomes final; the SEC proposed it on October 1, 2026 and no final rule has been issued. For an ordinary advisory client the rule reaches a crypto asset only when it is a fund or security. The adviser may hold the keys itself only when, after a written inquiry, no qualified custodian will hold that particular asset (cost does not count), re-checked at least quarterly, and it must move the asset to a custodian as soon as reasonably practicable once one is available. Only designated supervised people may hold key material, every movement needs at least two of them including a management person, and a technology vendor must not be able to reach the keys or move the assets alone, so a single-employee adviser cannot use the route.

What does the SEC's tokenized-stock exemption allow, and how big can it get?

SEC Release No. 34-106402, in effect September 17, 2026 through September 17, 2031, lets a qualifying U.S. tokenized securities venue trade tokenized versions of listed U.S. stocks in permissioned automated-market-maker pools on a public, permissionless blockchain without being an 'exchange', and lets firms supplying those pools with their own capital avoid 'dealer' status. Every pair must include a tokenized stock. Size is capped at 75 symbols and 0.25% of prior-month average daily volume for S&P 500, Russell 1000 and qualifying LULD ETPs (Tier 1), and 250 symbols and 2.5% for other eligible NMS stocks (Tier 2). It approves no venue, token or chain; custody, transfer-agent, clearing and settlement law still apply.

Can a CPA license make you an accredited investor?

Not yet. On September 30, 2026 the SEC issued Release 33-11446, a notice and request for comment on a possible designation order under Rule 501(a)(10) of Regulation D: a natural person holding an active U.S. CPA license in good standing would qualify personally as an accredited investor, without the income or net-worth tests. What do 'settled' and 'final' really mean for an ACH payment, a tokenized stock and a DTC token?

They mean different things in each system, and "final" in one record does not settle the others. ACH: The Reserve Bank debits the sending bank and credits the receiving bank at the scheduled time. For a credit item, the receiving bank's settlement credit is final and available under Operating Circular 4. Securitize tokenized stock: The product is designed to exchange the CET and USDC atomically on Solana. Solana can make the token transfer technically confirmed while Securitize determines the governed entitlement state. DTC token: The entitlement representation moves between registered participant wallets free of value. DTC's official record recognizes the participant wallet location of the entitlement.

Master account, limited account or payment account: what is the difference?

A master account gives full access to the Fed's payment services and its credit. A limited-purpose account (so far only Kraken's) gives payment access without the Fed's credit, and the proposed payment account would do something similar for a new class of firms. Without any account, a firm reaches the Fed only through a bank that has one.

How do catastrophe bonds and reinsurance carry disaster risk?

Alternative capital adds a funded layer to the reinsurance market. A sponsor transfers a defined risk to a special-purpose vehicle; investors fund collateral and receive a spread; a trigger can reduce principal or release collateral to the sponsor. The original insurer or public program normally remains the direct claimant-facing obligor. A hurricane, flood or earthquake is not itself the security trigger. For ordinary reinsurance the hub's rule is the same: ceded economics do not normally erase the original promise; cession ordinarily does not erase the direct insurer's policyholder obligation or prove that a recovery is due or collectible.

What happens to your policy if your insurer fails?

A domestic insurer does not ordinarily enter the same federal bankruptcy process as an industrial company. State insurance law, the domiciliary commissioner and a supervising state court instead control conservation, rehabilitation or liquidation. How do life insurers' promises turn into private credit and offshore reinsurance?

Policy and annuity money becomes long-dated liabilities that insurers invest: long promises supply investable general-account liabilities, and insurer asset allocation directs trillions into bonds, mortgages, structured assets and alternatives, including through a private credit and structured-asset channel. What changes when an employer moves its pension to an insurer?

Pension risk transfer changes the legal obligor and protection regime, not merely the asset manager.
