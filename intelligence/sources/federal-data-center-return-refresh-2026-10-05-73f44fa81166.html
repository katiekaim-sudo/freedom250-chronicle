Federal Data Center Return Refresh — October 5, 2026

In plain English

Question: Did the federal data-center push become a consolidated program, executed site leases, or operative large-load rules?

Answer: DOE now publicly uses the name American Energy Hubs for its federal-site reuse lane, but no Commerce/OSTP section-3 instrument consolidates all authorities, eligibility and support. Savannah River is still a selection for lease negotiations. Paducah still has selected sponsors and a mapped reuse parcel, but its power agreement remains subject to Kentucky approval and no executed campus lease was located. FERC's national large-load docket remains an advance-rulemaking record, while six section 206 proceedings remain the main federal tariff path.

1. A named DOE lane exists, not one consolidated federal programme

DOE's July Paducah announcement calls the site-reuse lane its American Energy Hubs initiative. DOE's Speed to Power page separately inventories large-load, transmission, technical-assistance and Tribal-energy actions. Those objects make the architecture less inferential than it was in August.

They still do not supply the section 3 object asked for in this return: no located Commerce/OSTP document joins governing authority, programme inventory, applicant eligibility, administrators and each support instrument into one consolidated initiative. DOE's named lane is real; it is not a government-wide fund, entitlement or single approval path.

Primary sources: DOE Paducah partnership, DOE Speed to Power.

2. Savannah River remains at negotiation

NNSA's current public object still says Amentum was selected to enter negotiations for a phased lease. It expressly says selection is not a final lease award and makes any agreement contingent on negotiation, permitting, safety and security review and other federal approvals.

No signed phased lease, site-control instrument, project-specific permit, financing close, notice to proceed or construction record was located. Other Savannah River contracts and construction announced in September belong to the site's nuclear-cleanup and operating missions and cannot be used as receipts for the proposed data center.

Primary source: NNSA Amentum selection.

3. Paducah has a clearer physical plan but not the controlling agreements

DOE's September Community Advisory Board materials now map roughly 785 acres for an AI data center. The July announcement keeps the role split explicit: Brookfield was selected to develop and operate the campus; NextEra was selected to build and own dedicated generation; Big Rivers would provide wholesale service; Jackson Purchase would provide retail service; and Paducah Power would serve as community partner.

The same release says the power-service agreement remains subject to Kentucky Public Service Commission approval. No executed DOE campus lease, final project-specific PSC order, interconnection agreement, generation CPCN, environmental permit or construction notice was located. The Kentucky Commission's already-approved EKPC Data Center Power tariff is a useful cost-separation precedent, not the Paducah service approval.

Primary sources: DOE Paducah partnership, September 2026 Paducah CAB binder, Kentucky PSC data-center tariff discussion.

4. FERC's federal large-load layer is active but not final

RM26-4 remains an advance notice of proposed rulemaking. FERC's April order committed the Commission to act, and its June 18 action opened six separate section 206 proceedings for the regional grid operators to defend or reform their tariffs. Those proceedings are real adjudicative control surfaces.

No final RM26-4 rule or six-proceeding merits disposition was located by the cutoff. State large-load tariffs—including Kentucky's Data Center Power tariff and the many other approved or pending state tariffs noted by FERC—show the cost-causation model moving in parallel. They do not create one national tariff or prove any named data center received service.

Primary sources: FERC RM26-4 docket page, FERC six-proceeding announcement, FERC major orders.
