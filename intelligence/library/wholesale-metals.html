How gold moves—and why a gold token is not collateral yet

The main U.S. metals market is adding more trading hours, including weekends, while keeping its existing clearing and payment rules. The United Kingdom has opened a legal path from owning a specific gold bar toward using a token as protected collateral, but that path is not operating yet.

What we found

  • A September 9, 2026 update sets COMEX's risk and novation rules for weekend trading. Novation is the clearinghouse stepping in as the other side of each trade.
  • Questions still open at that date: how BMO's cash role is accepted and produced, and how conditional trading rebates offered to sovereign funds work.
  • The study traces a chain from a U.S. forced-labor (UFLPA) action through to the LBMA in a Shandong case. COMEX's own decision on whether that metal is eligible is kept as a separate question.
  • A September 14 review of the FCA's tokenised-gold question separates three things: owning the token, title to the physical gold, and control of it as collateral. The FCA paper is a Call for Input, not a rule or a product approval.
  • United Kingdom rules run on separate clocks: current fund rules, final liquidity changes in 2027, new crypto rules expected in 2027, and proposed alternative-investment-fund-manager reforms in 2028.

Why it matters

Gold is being made machine-readable and collateral-ready through existing exchanges, vault rules and regulators, not outside them. That is the thesis in a narrow but concrete market.

What to watch

  • 2027 · UK final fund-liquidity changes and the crypto-perimeter clock that will shape any gold-token product.
  • 2028 · Proposed UK fund-manager (AIFM) reforms, still proposals at the cutoff.
  • pending · COMEX's separate eligibility decision in the Shandong case, and whether BMO's cash role gets settled.
