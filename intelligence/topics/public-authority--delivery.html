Payment integrity is a checkpoint, not a blacklist

Treasury Do Not Pay is a governmentwide shared screening network and federal payment checkpoint. Treasury operates the Working System and controlled access, but each federal or State-administered program must verify a match and apply its own eligibility, notice, contest and recovery authority. A match is therefore not itself a conclusive blacklist, adverse decision, payment stop or recovered dollar.

The fraud and payment-integrity study joins pre-payment identity and eligibility to the weaker post-payment transaction record. Public feeds operate in bounded form, while State, NGO, subrecipient and vendor records remain fragmented. The delivery question is who had authority to decide, which record controlled, what payment actually moved and what remedy followed.

Eligibility architecture changes through records and agencies

The September 1, 2026 OLC opinion changes the Executive Branch position on PRWORA reporting from benefit-agency reporting toward a participating State-wide component-agency duty, while rejecting the older formal-decision/final-order knowledge threshold. The saved study does not establish a replacement reporting procedure, State transmission, benefit termination or enforcement result.

Census/USPS, Education funding and election-integrity materials show the same pattern: an operating test, award condition, data route or public release can be real without becoming one joined enforcement database or a governmentwide outcome. Identity, citizenship, recipient status, award conditions and service access remain distinct records and authorities.

Service delivery is measured at the recipient and operating edge

Education funding is a multi-entity junction in which recipient identity, award conditions, servicing, reporting and research-security eligibility operate before or around payment; it does not establish a public universal funding ledger. Election-integrity review similarly separates voter data, intelligence products, election technology, registration and eligibility, and keeps capability, occurrence and outcome apart.

The branch therefore treats delivery as complete only when a named program or recipient record shows the operative decision, money or service, and the relevant result. A policy release, screening match, postal channel, award, or technology capability is a source object and route to further evidence, not a substitute for delivery.
