Who gets a project accepted?
A project moves through people and records as well as equipment. One company may supply a component, another create the test evidence, and an owner, regulator or contracting official make the final decision. These studies follow those separate roles from site and financing through delivery, testing and operation. Start with the question below. Each chapter keeps its own cases and evidence boundaries; a candidate, comparable project or certificate does not establish a target contract or accepted system.

NSSTS Technology-to-Company Map
Started: 2026-08-18

Governing ruling
This is a standalone research object. It is not part of the Federal Group
and company candidates must not be rendered as children of federal entities.
The map follows this chain:
NSSTS CET family
-> mission or use case
-> required capability or bottleneck
-> federal owner / program / facility
-> acquisition, funding or collaboration path
-> company role
-> exact legal entity
The company edge may be confirmed, inferred or unresolved. Technical fit and
acquisition proximity are graded separately so a famous or technically capable
company is never silently presented as a likely awardee.
Wave 1
Wave 1 covers the buildout layer most likely to make the rest of the strategy
callable:
AI models, secure cloud, high-performance computing and platform integration;
semiconductors, accelerators, memory, advanced packaging and photonics;
scientific data, identity, provenance, engineering software and digital twins;
robotics, autonomous laboratories, instruments, test and measurement; and
physical compute infrastructure, cooling, power coordination and facility modernization.
Wave 2
Wave 2 resolves the physical industrial buildout into three additional lanes:
energy systems, grid hardware, advanced fission and fusion infrastructure;
critical-mineral processing, qualified materials and component supply chains;
advanced manufacturing, industrial digital twins, robotics, metrology,
non-destructive evaluation and supplier qualification.
The governing acceptance objects are energized and field-qualified equipment,
licensed and fueled nuclear systems, origin-compliant material delivered to
specification, and manufacturing processes or parts accepted by a transition
user.
Wave 3
Wave 3 adds three acceptance-gated national-security technology lanes:
biotechnology, biosecurity and biomanufacturing;
quantum computing, networking, sensing, atomic timing and resilient PNT;
space launch and logistics, hypersonics, missile defense and advanced
sensing.
Biotechnology ends at validated screening, scale, cGMP lots or mission and
regulatory acceptance. Quantum and PNT end at government-reproduced benchmarks,
quantitative field results or certified production. Space and hypersonics end
at accepted delivery, checkout, landing, flight, lot or intercept—not at an
award or launch announcement alone.
Wave 4
Wave 4 closes the three remaining principal dedicated NSSTS technology lanes:
communications and networking;
directed energy;
information management and cybersecurity.
Communications ends at accepted on-orbit, field or recurring-service
performance rather than design, production or demonstration alone. Directed
energy ends at accepted live-target, endurance, integration and transition
evidence rather than installation or field assessment. Cyber and information
management end at a funded task, deployed workload, authorization and accepted
operation rather than a vehicle, ceiling, agreement or policy signal.
Cross-sector dependency phase
The completed twelve-sector sweep now supports a second analytical layer:
16 normalized exact-entity recurrence nodes;
15 verified, typed or supported shared rails;
18 bounded propagation scenarios with warning indicators and recheck gates.
This layer distinguishes a real shared capability from corporate portfolio
adjacency, a verified shared rail from a recurring rail type, and a supported
upstream/downstream cross-link from a named supplier contract. Reach is not a
probability score, and no scenario is an investment recommendation.
Priority-company dossier and monitoring phase
The cross-sector layer now feeds two governed operating views:
12 exact-entity priority-company dossiers that preserve program roles,
ownership seams, maturity, adverse evidence, competitors and dated gates;
33 bottleneck monitors covering all 15 shared rails and 18 propagation
scenarios, with explicit promotion, downgrade and expiry tests.
The dossiers are not a ranking. They identify companies that recur at
high-leverage integration, infrastructure, production or transition points.
The monitoring registry is a durable return queue, not an active automation;
automatic_monitoring_active remains false until separately authorized.
Phase 5 — program, facility and contender layer
Phase 5 reverses the company lens. Ten priority federal demand and acquisition
objects now control the analysis, joined to 37 typed company positions, 13
facility records and four historical calibration cases.
The program chain is:
federal demand
-> program office and instrument
-> public performer / contender field
-> facility and capacity join
-> eligibility, test and authorization gates
-> accepted operation or production
This layer distinguishes an operating performer, production awardee, prototype
awardee, R&D awardee, design-pool member, supported candidate and discovery-only
company. It also keeps a verified program site separate from a general company
capability site. The PTS-G design-to-production sequence is the strongest
current back-test: public evidence bounded five serious design teams but did not
publish enough evaluation evidence to rank the two later production selectees
without false precision.
Hidden supply-chain and NAES expansion
The 2026-08-19 expansion adds 27 exact candidate or control nodes concentrated
at the seams a headline-vendor map tends to miss: laboratory A/E and cooling,
federal electrical construction, commissioning, grid control centers,
transformer protection, scientific storage, modular biomanufacturing,
advanced-packaging operation and additive-manufacturing qualification.
NAES is resolved as an acceptance-and-operation membrane rather than an asset
owner, utility, equipment OEM or general transmission builder. Its subsidiaries
remain exact and separate: Olsson carries the active federal electrical and
commissioning evidence; Gridforce carries the operating BA/TOP control-plane
evidence; and Greenberry carries the DOE/NNSA qualified-facility evidence.
The expansion also corrects two Phase 5 states. Natcast holds the final operator
award for NAPMP piloting capabilities, planned for ASU Research Park in Tempe,
while construction, tool installation and qualification remain open. The DBPC
prototype is required to operate in Hawaii in an INDOPACOM-representative
environment, but the selected team, exact site and operator remain undisclosed.
Pivotal third-party control phase
The third-party phase adds 30 entities or contracting-entity seams that may not
win a headline technology or construction award but can still condition whether
a project advances. The controlling objects are decision rights and acceptance
records: large-load studies, lender engineering, environmental review,
commissioning, certification, insurance, calibration, tool move-in, cooling
chemistry, qualification and delegated operation.
Five control types remain distinct: formal gates, delegated execution,
acceptance evidence, advisory influence and critical-path supply. No public
target award or engagement was found for any of the 30 at the cutoff. That is a
negative public-record boundary, not proof of non-involvement; confidential
interconnection work, subcontracts, insurer assignments and pre-award advisory
engagements may remain undisclosed.
Pivotal third-party sector expansion
The sector-expansion phase adds 40 further exact candidates in four balanced
waves: quantum/PNT; space, hypersonics and test ranges; communications and
directed energy; and cyber/information assurance plus critical-minerals,
nuclear and fusion control chains.
The new controlling insight is that most candidates do not hold the final
government decision. They operate the environment, preserve custody, or produce
the calibrated evidence on which that decision depends. The expansion therefore
keeps a formal gate separate from delegated execution, acceptance evidence,
advisory influence and critical-path supply. It also corrects stale range and
test-facility incumbency, including the replacement of RGNext on the East/West
range rail and National Aerospace Solutions at AEDC.
No public Science Strategy target assignment was found for the 40 candidates.
PATH grades are relative to the Science Strategy target, not to an adjacent
federal award or operating contract.
Transaction, failure and return-to-service phase
The 2026-08-19 transaction-trigger phase adds 31 cases across four layers:
Natcast/ASU land and utility control, Argonne/Genesis commercial shadows,
acquisition-to-award entity clocks, and insurance/surety/forensic/restart
controls.
The principal correction is that the planned Natcast PPF site remains publicly
supported by a nonbinding LOI rather than an exposed final site contract. The
land chain runs through ABOR and the legally separate Arizona State University
Research Park, Inc.; Sunbelt Holdings is the park asset manager. Same-campus
JE Dunn, Diamondback Design Group and Applied Materials projects remain
comparators, not PPF assignments.
Aurora supplies the mature Argonne acceptance comparison: an exact subcontract,
seller and lower-tier entity, benchmark, user opening, delivered node-hours and
project closeout. The Genesis Center RFI, Minerva/Metis operating systems and
Solstice/Equinox partnership announcements stop at different earlier states.
The phase also treats failure as a discovery surface. Insurers, adjusters,
forensic engineers, sureties, laboratories and recommissioning firms produce
different evidence and do not inherit one another's decision rights. Shared
ownership is recorded as a structural independence question, not misconduct.

The newly visible official layer
The public record is already richer than a simple speculative field map:
DOE now identifies an official Genesis Mission Consortium and technical
working groups for models, data, HPC/cloud, robotics and workforce.
DOE reported more than $800 million in partner commitments, but the public
aggregate mixes compute, credits, models, infrastructure, expertise,
partnerships and direct funding. It is not a company-by-company award ledger.
The consortium member page names private-sector participants and describes
proposed or contributed roles. Membership is a formal position signal, not a
procurement award.
DOE selected 278 Genesis RFA projects for award negotiations, including 19
company-led selections and 157 participating companies. Selection for
negotiation is not an issued award or obligation.
NNSA's AWS Secret/Restricted Data environment is enterprise-authorized and
intended to host inaugural Genesis workloads. The notice does not establish
that those workloads are running and omits the full contract and security architecture.
Argonne's National Genesis Mission Supercomputing Center of Innovation RFI is
market research for infrastructure, software, services, testbeds and private
financing. It is not a solicitation or award.
Direct DLA magnet orders, AFRL contracts, America Makes selections, DPA
capacity awards, HALEU task orders and advanced-reactor permits now expose a
concrete industrial field beyond official partnership rosters.
Federal AI-campus announcements still do not identify turbine or cooling
OEMs. Those supplier edges remain unresolved.
SMART USA is a closed historical path: Commerce terminated the award and
withdrew federal funding.
DBIMP planning awards, quantum CHIPS letters of intent and QBI Stage B
selections expose important candidates but remain pre-build, pre-final-award
or pre-validation objects.
Space and hypersonic lanes contain dense live awards and tests, while launch,
landing, reusable flight and production award remain distinct from operational
acceptance.
Protected SATCOM and space networking expose direct production and prototype
orders, while launch, checkout, accepted service and recurring operations
remain future objects for most new systems.
Directed-energy JLWS awards establish a live 150-to-500-kW prototype race,
but GAO transition warnings and the absence of a public unclassified program
of record prevent demonstration or installation from becoming production.
Cyber and information-management lanes contain operating zero-trust, cloud,
data and training incumbents alongside prototype, vehicle and policy signals;
task, obligation, ATO and acceptance remain separate.
These distinct objects create a useful evidence ladder:
general capability
-> consortium membership / stated contribution
-> named RFA team or selection for negotiation
-> operative agreement or award
-> delivered and accepted capability
-> production use at a named mission or facility
Quantum and cryptographic acceptance phase
The new acceptance phase follows two different but structurally parallel chains:
quantum component -> traceable measurement -> government V&V
-> manufacturing instrument -> accepted system
PQC implementation -> CAVP -> entropy -> accredited laboratory
-> CMVP / FIPS 140-3 -> product mapping
-> interoperability -> ATO -> production custody
It adds the companies that create calibration, component, module-test, PKI and
authorization evidence without incorrectly treating those evidence producers as
the final government decision authority. Its strongest hidden nodes are Rydberg
Technologies' Quantum Calibration Lab, SRI's Quantum Manufacturing Engineering
Center, the cryogenic and photonic component cohort, Entrust and WidePoint's
federal PKI positions, Crypto4A and SafeLogic's validation transitions, and the
finite NVLAP cryptographic-testing laboratory base.
The phase also preserves a firm public-record no-join: Globus has a demonstrated Genesis workflow and AWS has
an authorized environment intended for Genesis workloads, but no public Genesis-specific PQC
configuration, CBOM, certificate profile, HSM/KMS mapping or ATO evidence was
located as of August 19, 2026.
