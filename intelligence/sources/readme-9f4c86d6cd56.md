# HHS High-Risk Life Sciences Research Policy — July 28, 2026

This package preserves and audits the July 28 public release of the
*United States Government Policy for Stopping High-Risk Life Sciences
Research*. It is a post-cutoff delta to the July 25 federal website sweep and
belongs beside the existing COVID-origin, research-funding and biolab packages.
It does not alter the closed cutoff of Health Benefits Wave 6.

## Short answer

July 28 produced one substantive policy family, not several independent state
changes:

1. a White House-hosted government-wide policy dated July 20;
2. an HHS release announcing it on July 28;
3. an HHS–Education joint statement; and
4. an NIH Director statement about future implementation.

The policy prohibits federal support for dangerous gain-of-function research,
creates review gates for potential DGOF and international research of concern,
and assigns new duties to federal life-science funders, federally funded
institutions and covered principal investigators. It does **not** establish a
universal domestic ban on privately funded DGOF research. The policy itself
says additional statutory, regulatory or legislative action is needed to reach
all U.S. entities and private funding.

## Evidence boundary

- The policy PDF is the controlling policy object.
- The HHS release, joint statement, NIH statement and @HHSGov post are
  announcement or implementation-intent carriers; they do not create separate
  policy events.
- A prohibition on **federal support** is not a criminal prohibition on every
  public or private research actor.
- DGOF, potential DGOF and international research of concern are separate
  policy categories with different funding dispositions.
- A review recommendation is not an award, an operating project or proof that
  risk controls worked.
- Policy date, public-release date, agency-guidance date, institutional
  compliance date, award-term change and observed enforcement remain separate
  clocks.
- The document is a 12-page image-only PDF. It was rendered and visually
  reviewed page by page; no OCR text is treated as authoritative.
