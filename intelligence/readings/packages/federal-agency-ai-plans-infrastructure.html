# Super Intelligence and America.gov executive-order pair

**Evidence cutoff:** September 29, 2026 ET  
**Authority state:** White House texts signed and published on WhiteHouse.gov; Executive Order numbers, Federal Register publication dates, citations, document numbers and official PDFs were not yet available at the cutoff.

## Answer first

The September 29 action is best understood as a **two-order control stack**, not as proof that the Federal Government has declared the arrival of artificial superintelligence.

1. **Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence** changes executive-branch terminology in non-statutory documents. For this order, “Super Intelligence” and “SI” temporarily mean the same technologies already covered by the statutory definition of “artificial intelligence” in 15 U.S.C. 9401(3). The order does not establish a technical capability threshold, a superhuman-performance test or a new statutory category.
2. **Streamlining Access to Government Services Through America.gov** establishes the service-orchestration layer: GSA, the National Design Studio and OMB are to operate America.gov, integrate Login.gov and require covered agencies to expose existing public APIs, dashboards and forms through the platform. This is materially operational, but it preserves agency custody, records, statutory responsibility and adjudicatory authority.

The strongest immediate change is therefore semantic and administrative. The stronger prospective change is architectural: America.gov could become a common front door across high-volume Federal services. Current answer/search functionality is operating; broad authenticated transactions and agency integrations remain future implementation objects.

## The two-order object

| Object | Immediate authority | What changed | What did not change | Next receipt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Super Intelligence order** | Presidential direction to executive departments and agencies, bounded by law and appropriations | Official correspondence, public communications, websites, reports, policy documents and other non-statutory executive-branch documents are to use “Super Intelligence” or “SI” instead of “Artificial Intelligence” or “AI” | Statutes are unchanged; previously issued regulations, Presidential actions, contracts, grants and historical documents need not be altered; existing agency authorities remain | APST legislative proposal due within 60 days, approximately November 28, 2026; later Presidential action or an Act of Congress could supersede the interim definition |
| **America.gov order** | Presidential direction to GSA, National Design Studio, OMB and covered agencies, bounded by law and appropriations | America.gov is established as a unified service front door; Login.gov authentication and agency integration duties are directed; covered services must provide usage/performance data | America.gov is not made the originating agency, system of record or adjudicator; IRS tax filing, Department of War and Intelligence Community services are excluded; in-person, telephone, mail and agency-specific digital routes remain | OMB implementation memorandum due within 90 days, approximately December 28, 2026; covered-service inventories, integration instructions, operating interfaces and observed transactions |

## Definition ruling: a renamed statutory AI category

Section 3(a) of the Super Intelligence order imports the existing definition at 15 U.S.C. 9401(3). That definition describes a machine-based system that, for human-defined objectives, makes predictions, recommendations or decisions influencing real or virtual environments. It is a broad ordinary-AI definition.

The order’s legal construction is therefore:

`SI for this order = technologies and systems already encompassed by statutory AI`

It is **not**:

`SI = demonstrated artificial general intelligence or intelligence exceeding humans across domains`

Section 3(b) confirms that the durable definition question is unresolved. APST must propose legislative language, assess whether SI should modify, expand or supersede the current AI definition, identify conforming statutory amendments and recommend additional executive action. A submitted proposal would still be a proposal, not enacted law.

This is a semantic-control event with real downstream effects on website labels, policy drafting, procurement prose, public communications and search/discovery. It is not a capability certification.

## America.gov control map

The architecture is **federated access with centralized orchestration**, not centralized legal custody.

`individual → America.gov interface → Login.gov authentication → agency API/form/service → originating agency record and adjudication`

| Control | Holder named by the order | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Platform establishment and operation | GSA Administrator, coordinated with National Design Studio and OMB | Subject to law and appropriations |
| Authentication | Login.gov | Does not transfer originating-agency authority |
| Covered-service definition | More than 100,000 users in a 12-month period and available/applicable online | OMB may add or exclude services by memorandum |
| Service integration | Heads of agencies | Existing public APIs, dashboards and digital forms become accessible through America.gov |
| Records and adjudication | Originating agency | Custody, control, statutory responsibility and adjudicatory authority expressly preserved |
| Data access | Originating agency unless Privacy Act or other law provides otherwise | No general cross-agency access authorization |
| Access channels | America.gov plus existing lawful routes | America.gov is a better option, not the only option |
| Performance reporting | Agencies to GSA and OMB | Historical and current usage/performance data, ongoing |

## Current-operation receipts

The live America.gov service presently behaves as a government-source answer engine. It states that it can answer questions from official Government sources without requiring an account. In a bounded test on September 29, it correctly located the companion Super Intelligence order and the imported statutory definition. That proves a current answer/search function; it does not prove authenticated agency transactions.

The platform’s detailed privacy policy supplies a more granular data-flow picture than the front-page promise:

- typed questions are processed and ordinarily remain in browser memory rather than usage analytics, unless a user submits feedback;
- AI search occurs across Government sites in a sandboxed environment;
- document text may be processed in-browser with best-effort personal-information removal;
- voice input can invoke the browser provider and may send audio to that provider;
- operational/security layers receive network and request data, including IP-derived information;
- response and search-result caches, pseudonymous performance telemetry, feedback records and Cloudflare security logs have different retention clocks; and
- the policy says AI providers operate under zero-data-retention terms and may not use interactions for training, sale or advertising profiles.

The user-facing “no conversations” claim is therefore best read as a bounded content-retention claim, not as a claim that no operational, cache, feedback or security record exists.

The Terms of Use also preserve the legal boundary: America.gov does not submit, decide, approve or change a filing, and the originating agency’s determination controls. It warns that generated answers may be inaccurate, incomplete or outdated.

## Clock conflict to preserve

The September 29 White House fact sheet says passport renewal and Medicare enrollment will be available “later this year.” The live America.gov “Coming Soon” page says these services are expected in **early 2027**. Neither statement is proof of completed integration. The live product page is the more specific current delivery signal, but the conflict remains open until an operating transaction or formal schedule resolves it.

## Joined control stack

America.gov sits on top of the earlier Login.gov scaling program in OMB Memorandum M-26-18. That memorandum already required inventories of authenticated public services, agency implementation plans, phased Login.gov deployment, performance measurement, privacy safeguards and replacement of duplicative sign-on paths where requirements are met. The September 29 order adds:

- a unified conversational front door;
- a >100,000-user covered-service threshold;
- GSA/National Design Studio/OMB platform ownership;
- existing API, dashboard and form integration;
- a continuing usage/performance-data feed; and
- a new 90-day OMB implementation memorandum.

The earlier Login.gov deadlines retain their own clocks. The new order does not collapse them into the America.gov 90-day memorandum.

## Claim audit

| Claim | Ruling | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| The Federal Government has legally recognized the arrival of artificial superintelligence | **Not supported** | The operative definition imports the existing broad statutory AI definition and provides no capability threshold |
| The executive branch has been directed to rename AI as SI | **Supported, bounded** | Applies to non-statutory executive-branch documents to the maximum extent permitted by law |
| Federal statutes now use SI instead of AI | **False at cutoff** | Congress has not enacted the proposed definition or conforming amendments |
| America.gov centralizes all Federal records | **False** | The order expressly preserves agency custody/control and disclaims a centralized Federal system of records |
| America.gov can currently complete Federal transactions across agencies | **Not yet supported** | Current answer/search operation is visible; transaction integrations are future implementation objects |
| America.gov is meant to become a common authenticated service layer | **Supported as direction** | The order directs Login.gov integration and covered-service integration through existing interfaces |
| The September 29 launch proves service effects | **Not supported** | Launch, integration, completed transaction, user outcome and effect remain separate clocks |

## Executive Order Hub integration state

Two provisional Chronicle order notes should be maintained because these are distinct Presidential actions with distinct operative objects and clocks. At this cutoff:

- White House text is confirmed for both;
- neither order has an assigned EO number or Federal Register object in the available official record;
- no annexes are present in either White House text;
- Hub records should remain provisional, with blank EO number/citation fields and explicit Federal Register reconciliation gates; and
- authored entity-map keys should wait for distinct EO numbers because a shared date-only provisional key would collide.

When the Office of the Federal Register publishes the orders, reconcile the Executive Order number, publication date, citation, Federal Register document number, official HTML/PDF, plotline rules and any entity map. Do not treat the White House posting date as the Federal Register publication date.
