# Federal Reserve System entity, transaction and software map

Updated: 2026-07-25  
Status: pending workbench research; not yet promoted to the vault  
Evidence cutoff: official records available through 2026-07-25  
Scope: legal entities, control relationships, interdistrict accounting,
System-wide allocations, shared services, software, technology, research,
external reimbursements and international counterparties. This is an
accounting/control map, not a finding of waste, unlawful coordination or
beneficial ownership.

Every monetary amount is written in full nominal dollars with commas and no
letter-suffix scaling. When a source uses a `$1,000,000` or `$1,000` reporting
unit, the displayed source amount is expanded to full dollars. Added zeros do
not create precision beyond the source.

## Short answer

The Federal Reserve does much more than set an interest-rate target and order
paper currency:

- it operates the reserve-account layer for depository institutions;
- it settles high-value, automated-clearing-house, check, securities and
  instant payments;
- it lends to depository institutions and operates emergency facilities when
  authorized;
- it acts as Treasury's bank and fiscal agent;
- it issues, distributes, authenticates and destroys currency;
- it supervises bank holding companies, state member banks, savings-and-loan
  holding companies, certain foreign-bank offices and designated financial
  market utilities;
- it conducts monetary-policy, financial-stability, supervision, payments,
  consumer and regional-economic research;
- it maintains foreign official accounts, foreign-currency investments,
  central-bank swap lines and foreign-official repo services; and
- it runs a large national software, data, cyber, payments and accounting
  infrastructure.

But the user's accounting instinct is still right:

> A full map must ask which legal entity books each cost, which entity directs
> the work, which entity receives the service, who reimburses it, which
> allocations are eliminated in the combined statements, and which outside
> party ultimately bears or benefits from the economics.

The most important correction is:

> **Gross interest income is not the Fed's operating-funding requirement.**

The portfolio exists to implement monetary policy. Its interest income and the
interest paid on reserve and repo liabilities are balance-sheet consequences
of that policy. Operating costs are much smaller and are funded inside the same
earnings mechanism, but they are not the reason the securities portfolio
produces its gross income.

In 2025, the combined Reserve Banks recorded:

| 2025 combined Reserve Bank measure | Full nominal amount |
|---|---:|
| Interest income | $155,299,000,000 |
| Interest expense | $(167,381,000,000) |
| **Net interest expense** | **$(12,082,000,000)** |
| Other income | $3,135,000,000 |
| Operating expenses | $(9,800,000,000) |
| **Reserve Bank loss from operations** | **$(18,747,000,000)** |

So the Banks did not produce excess 2025 interest income because they
"needed" it for software, buildings or research. They actually paid more
interest on monetary liabilities than they earned on monetary assets.

    Banks -->|"fund and host shared operations"| Shared
    Shared -->|"services, allocations and reimbursements"| Banks
    Members -->|"stock subscriptions, deposits and fees"| Banks
    Banks -->|"interest, dividends, credit and payment services"| Members
    Treasury -->|"security interest and fiscal-service reimbursement"| Banks

This is not a parent-subsidiary chart:

- the Board is not consolidated with the Reserve Banks;
- the FOMC is a statutory committee, not a holding company;
- member banks do not hold ordinary residual equity;
- the New York Fed executes SOMA transactions as agent, but the economic
  participation is allocated across all 12 Banks;
- shared organizations such as Federal Reserve Financial Services and Federal
  Reserve Information Technology are operational structures hosted within the
  Reserve Bank network, not necessarily separately incorporated companies; and
- Treasury is outside the Reserve Bank combined reporting entity even though
  it is customer, counterparty, recipient of remittances and ultimate public
  residual claimant.

## II. Main-player control and accounting matrix

| Player | Legal/accounting character | Who directs or appoints | Main money/data relationship | Consolidation treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Congress | Constitutional legislature | Voters and constitutional process | Creates Fed powers, mandates, audit perimeter and remittance rules | Outside all Fed statements |
| Board of Governors | Federal agency with separate GAAP books | Governors nominated by President and confirmed by Senate | Assesses Reserve Banks; regulates and supervises; approves Bank budgets; owns Board assets | Not consolidated with Reserve Banks |
| FOMC | Statutory policy committee | Seven governors plus five Reserve Bank presidents | Directs open-market and foreign-exchange operations; uses Board and Bank staff | No ordinary corporate ownership; no separate consolidated balance sheet identified |
| 12 Reserve Banks | Separate federally chartered bodies corporate using FAM | Each has nine directors; Board appoints three; member banks elect six; Board approves presidents | Hold reserve accounts, own premises, operate services, record allocated SOMA shares, pay assessments | Combined with each other for published combined statements |
| New York Fed / SOMA Desk | One Reserve Bank acting as System agent | FOMC authorizes and directs; Board supervises Bank | Executes domestic and foreign operations; holds SOMA assets and international accounts; allocates participation | NY-only operational booking is participated across Banks; combined presentation removes district geography |
| Member banks | Private regulated banks; mandatory Reserve Bank stock subscribers | Private governance plus bank regulators | Pay capital subscriptions and service fees; hold reserves; receive statutory dividends; elect six Bank directors by classes | External counterparties; stock is not ordinary residual equity |
| FRFS | Integrated Reserve Bank payment-services organization | Reserve Bank enterprise governance under Board oversight | Runs Check, FedACH, Fedwire, National Settlement, FedNow, FedLine and related services | Costs and revenue sit within Reserve Bank books; not identified as a separate consolidated company |
| FRIT / National IT | National shared-technology function hosted at Richmond | System technology governance and Board budget oversight | Supplies national infrastructure and business-line technology; computing and support costs are redistributed across Banks | Hosted inside Reserve Bank reporting; inter-Bank reallocations do not become additional external System expense |
| Product/function offices | Shared operational control nodes hosted at individual Banks | System agreements, committees and Board oversight | Direct services and sometimes control software assets used by all Banks | Asset may sit on host/controller Bank even when development and use are distributed |
| Treasury | Federal department; external party | President, Secretary and Congress | Pays security interest; holds Treasury General Account; reimburses fiscal-agent work; receives remittances | External counterparty |
| Bureau of Engraving and Printing | Treasury bureau | Treasury | Produces notes under Board order; Board reimburses production and capital costs | External supplier; cost reaches Banks through Board currency assessments |
| CFPB | Independent bureau in the Federal Reserve System for statutory funding purposes; separate operations | Director under federal statute | Board requests/allocates funding; Reserve Banks are assessed; CFPB reimburses certain pension/OIG costs | Not consolidated with Reserve Banks |
| OIG | Board/CFPB inspector general | Statutory independent office | Board and CFPB share costs based on work allocation | On Board books, not Reserve Bank combined statements |
| OEB / System retirement plans | System employee-benefit administration and plans | System governance | Atlanta recognizes the System Plan asset/liability; costs and premiums are allocated across participating entities | Allocated to participants; host accounting can obscure ultimate beneficiaries |
| FFIEC | Interagency council, not a Federal Reserve subsidiary | Board, FDIC, NCUA, OCC and CFPB participate | Member assessments plus reciprocal administrative and data-processing reimbursements | Not included in Board financial statements |
| Foreign official institutions and BIS | Foreign central banks, governments and international institution | Their own public authorities; Fed eligibility/terms | Deposits, custody, repo, foreign-currency investments and swap operations centered at New York | External counterparties |
| Main Street LLC and other facility LLCs | Special-purpose/VIE structures with Reserve Bank management and Treasury equity | Board authorization; host Reserve Bank manages; Treasury holds preferred equity | Host Bank loans/purchases; Treasury credit protection/equity | Qualifying VIEs consolidated; intercompany balances eliminated |
| Outside vendors and custodial banks | Private counterparties | Contracts and procurement controls | Cloud, software, consulting, data, tri-party repo custody, hardware and settlement support | External expense, asset or payable; vendor identity is usually absent from public financial statements |

## IV. Income is generated by the monetary balance sheet, not sized to expenses

### Keep the two funding engines separate

The Reserve Banks' monetary-portfolio earnings and the Board's assessments
are different accounting systems:

| 2025 Board of Governors item | Exact audited amount |
|---|---:|
| Assessments for Board operations and capital expenditures | $1,577,000,000 |
| Total Board operating revenues | $1,646,334,004 |
| Board operating expenses | $(1,191,822,781) |
| **Board net income** | **$454,511,223** |
| Net cash from operating activities | $477,672,043 |
| Cash capital expenditures | $(527,426,113) |
| Property, equipment and software, net at 2024 year-end | $1,284,471,516 |
| Property, equipment and software, net at 2025 year-end | $1,769,739,547 |
| **One-year increase in the net asset balance** | **$485,268,031** |
| Cumulative results of operations at 2025 year-end | $1,473,566,444 |

This table explains an accounting point that can otherwise look like surplus
income. The Board assessment explicitly funds both current operations and
capital expenditures. Cash spent on a building or capitalized software is not
fully charged to operating expense immediately; it first becomes an asset and
then reaches expense through depreciation or amortization. The Board therefore
reported $454,511,223 of net income while also spending $527,426,113 of cash on
capital expenditures.

Those are the Board's separate public-agency books, funded by mandatory
assessments rather than ordinary congressional appropriations. They are not
private shareholder funds and should not be mixed with the 12 Reserve Banks'
SOMA interest-income and Treasury-remittance waterfall.

### 2025 income and expense bridge

```text
Interest on monetary assets                            $155,299,000,000
- interest on reserve, repo and other monetary
  liabilities                                          (167,381,000,000)
= net interest expense                                  (12,082,000,000)

+ services, reimbursements, foreign-currency
  translation and other income                            3,135,000,000
- salaries, benefits, premises, equipment, other
  operations, Board/currency and CFPB assessments        (9,800,000,000)
= Reserve Bank operating loss                           (18,747,000,000)
```

This bridge answers “what do they need the income for?”:

1. The interest-income line is generated principally by securities acquired to
   implement monetary policy.
2. The interest-expense line is generated principally by reserve balances and
   repo liabilities used to implement policy and provide monetary control.
3. Operating expenses sit below that spread.
4. Service fees and Treasury reimbursements offset defined operating
   functions.
5. Excess earnings, when present, go to Treasury after expenses, dividends and
   limited surplus.
6. When costs and interest exceed earnings, remittances stop and a deferred
   asset records the earnings hurdle that must be recovered before remittances
   resume.

The portfolio is therefore not economically comparable to an endowment sized
to cover a nonprofit's annual budget. Its scale reflects the amount of central
bank assets and liabilities used to operate monetary policy.

The final 2025 distribution ledger adds another important layer:

| Combined Reserve Bank distribution item | 2025 amount |
|---|---:|
| Net loss before Treasury remittances | $(18,761,000,000) |
| Dividends paid to member banks | $1,688,000,000 |
| Earnings remittances transferred to Treasury | $6,281,000,000 |
| Increase in deferred Treasury-remittance asset | $27,526,000,000 |
| Deferred Treasury-remittance asset at 2024 year-end | $215,955,000,000 |
| Deferred Treasury-remittance asset at 2025 year-end | $243,481,000,000 |

The deferred asset is not cash, a loan to the System or an enforceable
receivable from Treasury. It is the cumulative amount of future net earnings
the Banks must retain before ordinary Treasury remittances resume.

## V. Interdistrict and related-party transaction lanes

### 1. Interdistrict Settlement Account

Each Reserve Bank carries an Interdistrict Settlement Account representing the
cumulative amount due to or from other Reserve Banks. The centralized
accounting system posts the settlement entries.

The account captures:

- cross-District payment activity;
- automated-clearing-house, funds and securities transfers;
- monthly Federal Reserve note clearings;
- priced-service reimbursements between principal and processing Banks;
- shared-service transfers; and
- the annual settlement through gold-certificate accounts.

The 12 individual Interdistrict Settlement Account balances net across the
System. The combined statement therefore removes the Bank-to-Bank geography
that is visible in the individual statements.

Settlement can also be effected through changes in each Bank's SOMA
participation. An ISA movement can therefore change an individual Bank's
allocated securities position without that District having made a cash
purchase or sale. ISA is a clearing/allocation mechanism, not an inter-Bank
loan.

### 2. SOMA domestic participation

The New York Fed executes and holds domestic SOMA transactions, but each
Reserve Bank books an undivided participation:

- domestic securities, repo, reverse repo and related income, premium,
  discount, gain and loss activity are allocated using percentages derived
  from the annual Interdistrict Settlement Account settlement;
- specific securities are not assigned security-by-security to individual
  Banks; and
- the allocation is recalculated annually.

This is why an individual Bank can report a large securities-income or
interest-expense result even though the New York Desk executed the trades.

### 3. Foreign portfolios and central-bank swaps

Foreign-currency investment and central-bank-swap activity is allocated by
each Reserve Bank's share of aggregate capital and surplus at the preceding
year-end, rather than the domestic Interdistrict Settlement Account formula.

This is a different allocation key and must remain a different accounting
lane.

### 4. Priced payment services

Federal Reserve Financial Services manages Check, FedACH, Fedwire Funds,
Fedwire Securities, National Settlement, FedNow and electronic access across
the Banks.

The FAM says:

- principal/host Banks reimburse processing/agent Banks;
- estimated interbank transfers are recorded monthly through the Interdistrict
  Settlement Account and trued to actual cost the following month;
- income from customers is recorded gross, not netted against expense or
  payments to other Banks; and
- the uniform cost-accounting system allocates direct, internal-support,
  nationally provided support and corporate-overhead costs to benefiting
  activities.

The reciprocal Bank-to-Bank receivables, payables and service-cost
reallocations disappear or net in the combined Reserve Bank presentation. The
underlying external payroll, vendor and operating cost remains. These flows
should therefore be called cost allocations or reimbursements—not loosely
called intercompany income.

### 6. Board, currency and CFPB assessments

The Board assesses the Reserve Banks for:

- Board operations and capital;
- currency costs, including Bureau of Engraving and Printing costs; and
- CFPB funding.

These payments are related-System transactions but are **not eliminated** in
the Reserve Bank combined statements because the Board and CFPB are outside
the combined Reserve Bank reporting entity.

Other external flows that survive the 12-Bank combination include Treasury
fiscal-agent reimbursements, depository-institution service revenue, member
bank dividends, Treasury remittances, vendor and payroll spending, external
benefit obligations, and transactions with foreign official and market
counterparties.

The Board's own audited GAAP statements provide the following exact 2025
selected-transaction ledger:

| Assessment or reciprocal charge | 2025 amount |
|---|---:|
| Reserve Bank assessments for Board operations | $1,577,000,000 |
| Reserve Bank assessments for currency expenses | $1,124,810,953 |
| Reserve Bank assessments for CFPB transfers | $245,100,000 |
| **Total assessments levied or to be levied on Reserve Banks** | **$2,946,910,953** |
| OEB assessment charged to the Board | $5,295,000 |
| Board payable to OEB at year-end | $734,031 |
| FFIEC expenses charged to the Board | $4,609,000 |
| Board data-processing and administrative charges reimbursed by FFIEC | $3,199,000 |

Richmond is an especially important host entity. Its 2025 audited statement
says:

- computing and System-support costs were redistributed to the other Reserve
  Banks;
- Richmond received $1,135,000,000 of reimbursement for those costs in 2025,
  compared with $1,052,000,000 in 2024; and
- costs for the Currency Technology Office and System Financial Planning &
  Management were major System services for which Richmond was **not**
  reimbursed by the other Banks.

The $1,135,000,000 is an inter-Bank cost redistribution, not a donation, loan
or additional external System expenditure. It is also not a complete measure
of National IT's external vendor spend. It is evidence that Richmond's own
expense presentation contains a large national shared-service layer.

The FAM adds a procedural detail: Richmond processes each Reserve Bank's Board
assessment through an inter-Fed accounting entry, and the Board maintains its
operating deposit accounts at Richmond.

### 7. Employee-benefit and interagency allocations

Atlanta recognizes the System retirement-plan net asset/liability on behalf of
participants. The service cost is allocated among the Reserve Banks, Board and
other participating entities. The Office of Employee Benefits also initiates
inter-Bank transfers for health-plan premiums.

This is a classic host-entity issue: the legal book holder is not the sole
economic beneficiary.

The FFIEC is a separate interagency example. Its five participating agencies
include the Board and CFPB, but its financial data is not consolidated into
the Board. The Board can both be assessed by the council and earn
reimbursement for services it performs for the council.

### 8. Emergency-facility VIEs

Main Street is managed through Boston with Treasury preferred equity. The
Reserve Bank loan to the VIE and other qualifying intercompany amounts are
eliminated when the VIE is consolidated.

That is a true accounting consolidation lane, unlike the Board-Reserve Bank or
Treasury-Reserve Bank relationships.

### Where the software asset sits

The FAM's software-control rule is unusually important:

> A software project may be developed by one Reserve Bank, installed on
> hardware at another Bank or an outside vendor, and controlled by a product or
> function office hosted at a third Bank.

The controlling or hosting Bank generally records the whole software asset,
including amortization and disposal, even when all 12 Banks use it.

Control indicators include:

- deciding requirements;
- setting implementation and retirement dates;
- operating and supporting the software; and
- granting rights to use it.

Capitalizable costs paid to another Reserve Bank for software development can
be included in the owner's asset. Shared-project costs are transferred to the
books of the Bank that owns or controls the software.

This means a bank-by-bank software balance is **not** a clean measure of that
Bank's local technology consumption. It may identify where the System has
placed accounting ownership.

For every material application, the audit therefore needs four separate
fields:

1. contracting or invoice-paying Bank;
2. controlling product/function office and its host Bank;
3. Bank or vendor that developed and physically hosts the application; and
4. Banks, agencies or business lines to which the cost is ultimately
   allocated.

Under the current FAM, internally developed software is ordinarily capitalized
when the total project cost is $100,000 or more. Externally purchased software
is ordinarily capitalized at $25,000 or more. Meeting a threshold does not
override the underlying asset-recognition and project-stage rules.

### Capitalized versus expensed

| Cost type | Typical FAM treatment |
|---|---|
| Purchased license | Capitalize if asset criteria are met |
| Internal application-development coding, testing and direct implementation | Capitalize |
| Capitalizable work performed by another Reserve Bank | Transfer/include in owner Bank asset |
| Cloud arrangement containing a transferable software license | Account for license element as software |
| Cloud arrangement without a software license | Service contract; qualifying implementation cost may be deferred and amortized over hosting term |
| Preliminary evaluation, training, data conversion and general administration | Expense |
| Maintenance and minor replacement | Expense |
| Post-implementation support | Generally expense |

Capitalized Reserve Bank software is generally amortized over two to five
years. The public statements do not identify the asset by application, vendor,
user group or funding source.

### External contracts and commitments

The public statements reveal external dependence without naming the vendor
ledger:

- Reserve Banks had $260,000,000 of remaining unconditional purchase
  commitments through 2034 for currency-processing-machine maintenance and
  new-equipment development;
- 2025 purchases against those commitments were $83,000,000;
- a pricing notice refers to an unnamed new FedLine Direct network vendor and
  related hardware upgrades; and
- the Board incurred $61,346,020 of contractual and professional fees in 2025.

These are leads for procurement analysis, not proof of affiliate control.

- policy implementation analysis;
- Beige Book and local business/community intelligence; and
- public datasets and publications.

The available budget says 2025 Reserve Bank growth included staffing for
regional economic research. It does **not** establish that most of this money
is passed through to outside nonprofit research organizations.

The narrower audited Board line "Data, news, and research" was $44,291,812 in
2025, compared with $25,434,453 in 2024. Its source-rounded 2025 budget was
$48,900,000. Even this line combines research with purchased data and news
services; it is not a grant total.

The public financial statements also do not provide:

- a recipient-level research-grant ledger;
- outside university or nonprofit contract totals;
- sponsor concentration by research center;
- beneficial-interest or board-overlap schedules for funded organizations; or
- a clean split between employee research, purchased data, outside consulting,
  conferences and grants.

Therefore:

> **Internal research spending is verified. A broad claim that the Fed mainly
> funds outside research arms is not yet supported.**

That hypothesis should be tested through contracts, grants, conference
sponsorships, visiting-scholar programs, data licenses and personnel overlap.

## VIII. International layer

The New York Fed is the main operating node:

- it provides accounts and services to foreign official and international
  account holders;
- it holds foreign-currency deposits with foreign central banks and the Bank
  for International Settlements;
- it invests in selected foreign government obligations;
- it operates the Foreign and International Monetary Authorities repo
  facility;
- it executes foreign-exchange operations; and
- it maintains central-bank liquidity swaps as authorized by the FOMC.

At year-end 2025:

| International-facing item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Reverse repo owed to foreign official and international accounts | $356,205,000,000 |
| 2025 interest expense on those accounts | $15,267,000,000 |
| Maximum 2025 FIMA repo balance | $2,001,000,000 |
| 2025 FIMA repo interest income | $4,000,000 |

These are financial-market and central-bank operations, not the cost of
research or ordinary administration. They explain why the New York operating
perimeter is much larger than a domestic rate-setting office.

## IX. Relationship-closeness test

The nonprofit/high-donor analogy is useful if it is treated as an influence
test rather than an ownership conclusion.

For every organization touching the Fed, score these separately:

| Lens | Question |
|---|---|
| Legal control | Can one party appoint, remove, approve, direct or veto? |
| Funding dependence | What share of the recipient's budget comes from the relationship? Is payment mandatory, contractual or discretionary? |
| Operational dependence | Does one party supply the other's staff, platform, premises, accounting or critical service? |
| Data dependence | Does one party control essential data, models, credentials or access? |
| Personnel overlap | Are there shared officers, directors, fellows, secondees or revolving-door patterns? |
| Risk absorption | Does one party guarantee, capitalize, indemnify or absorb losses for the other? |
| Residual economics | Who receives surplus, bears deficits or captures asset value on wind-down? |
| Exclusivity and duration | Is the relationship recurring, concentrated and difficult to replace? |
| Direction of work | Does the funder set the questions, deliverables, methods, publication rights or implementation path? |
| Accounting treatment | Is it consolidated, eliminated, grossed up, reimbursed, allocated or left off the public entity map? |

### Initial relationship grades

| Relationship | Current grade | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Board ↔ Reserve Banks | **Statutory control and mandatory funding; not parent-subsidiary ownership** | Board supervision, budget approval, assessments, director appointments and standardized accounting |
| FOMC ↔ New York Desk | **Direct agency relationship** | FOMC authorizes and directs; New York executes |
| New York ↔ other Banks for SOMA | **Common economic participation with formula allocation** | New York transacts; all Banks receive undivided interests and income/loss |
| FRIT/FRFS/product offices ↔ Banks | **Shared-service and operational dependency** | Hosted functions serve all Banks through allocations and service agreements |
| Treasury ↔ Reserve Banks | **Sovereign creator/residual claimant plus customer/counterparty; not consolidated control** | Interest, Treasury account, fiscal-agent direction/reimbursement and remittances |
| Member banks ↔ Reserve Banks | **Mandatory capital, customer and governance relationship; not ordinary ownership** | Stock subscriptions, reserve accounts, service fees and director elections without normal residual claim |
| CFPB ↔ Board/Reserve Banks | **Mandatory statutory funding relationship** | Board transfers requested funding and assesses Banks; operations remain separate |
| Outside nonprofit/university ↔ Fed | **Not rateable without recipient-level evidence** | Funding concentration alone is relevant, but grants, contracts, governance overlap and direction must be documented |
| Vendor ↔ Fed | **Contractual dependency varies by system** | Cloud, software, data and custody can create high operational dependence without equity ownership |

## X. Records needed for a true related-party ledger

### Interdistrict and shared service

- Reserve Bank service agreements;
- InterFRB/Interdistrict Settlement Account transaction extracts;
- host, principal, processing and agent Bank cost-reimbursement schedules;
- nationally provided support cost allocations from the Planning and Control
  System;
- product/function-office hosting and governance agreements;
- Federal Reserve Financial Services cost and revenue by host Bank;
- National IT and FRIT allocation schedules by recipient Bank and service.

### Software

- software fixed-asset register by application, owner Bank and host;
- project-office, controller, developer, hosting vendor and user-Bank fields;
- operating software expense by license, cloud, maintenance, contractor and
  data category;
- capitalized labor and other-Bank development charges;
- transfer entries when software ownership moves between Banks;
- amortization, impairment and abandonment schedules;
- contracts and vendor concentration;
- applications funded or reimbursed by Treasury or other agencies.

### Research and outside relationships

- grants, contracts, sponsorships and data purchases by recipient;
- visiting scholar, university center and fellowship funding;
- contractor and consultant payment ledgers;
- Board/director/officer overlap with recipients;
- publication, model, data and intellectual-property control terms;
- repeat funding and recipient budget concentration.

### Consolidation and residual economics

- Bank-by-Bank eliminations used to prepare the combined statements;
- VIE consolidation workpapers;
- Board-versus-Bank related-party schedules;
- Treasury reimbursement and remittance settlement schedules;
- member-bank capital subscription and dividend ledgers;
- foreign official account and service-cost allocation schedules.
