## December 2024 Capricorn seed → June 2026 Capricorn Full phase

The locked June 29, 2026 reading asks whether an obligation can become
measurable and bounded. The Moon-family sequence shows why that question is
harder than simply collecting what is owed. The Capricorn seed begins with
both lights in the seventh house: the problem is planted in an agreement,
counterparty, treaty, rival or open negotiation. At First Quarter, the Moon
moves into the ninth house while Mars becomes the sole final dispositor: law,
doctrine and adjudication become the active hinge. At Full phase, the Moon
falls into the second house opposite the Cancer Sun, Mercury and Jupiter in the
eighth. The original relationship question has become a treasury-versus-shared-
liability accounting.

The working judgment is:

> **An obligation begins as a term between parties, is forced through a legal
> or doctrinal test, and culminates as a question of whose balance sheet must
> carry it. First Quarter opens almost the entire wheel and temporarily gives
> Mars final authority. Full phase closes the field again, but not around the
> seed's old boundary: terms, public need, institutional limit and enforcement
> now form a circuit in which no actor can settle the account alone. The
> obligation may survive while the original legal perimeter does not.**

The source-separated factual comparison supplies a valuable negative control.
Treasury and IRS published a final decentralized-finance broker reporting rule
on the seed date. Congress disapproved that exact rule 101 days later—well
before First Quarter—and declared that it would have no force or effect. A
different custodial-broker reporting regime nevertheless reached operational
reporting in 2026. The seed-date object did **not** become the harvest. The
reporting obligation persisted only after its perimeter changed.

## Source-separated political maturation: the rule dies, the reporting perimeter survives

The official political object was selected independently from the astrology.
The date correspondence is publication-date precision only; no exact signing
or filing time is claimed.

On December 30, 2024, Treasury and IRS published final regulations extending
broker information-reporting rules to certain providers of trading front-end
services in decentralized-finance arrangements. The rule attempted to place a
reporting obligation on a defined noncustodial perimeter.

On April 10, 2025—101 days after the seed and 172 days before First Quarter—
Public Law 119-5 disapproved the rule under the Congressional Review Act. The
enrolled law identifies the December 30 rule at 89 Fed. Reg. 106928 and states
that it “shall have no force or effect.” By the June 29, 2026 Full phase, that
specific rule had been legally dead for 445 days.

But a separate reporting rail survived. The custodial-broker regulations
finalized in July 2024 required gross-proceeds reporting on Form 1099-DA for
transactions beginning January 1, 2025. IRS materials show forms reaching
taxpayers in 2026, and an April 2026 Internal Revenue Bulletin records that
certain custodial brokers had built and implemented systems for 2025 gross-
proceeds reporting. Basis reporting began for certain digital assets acquired
and sold after 2025.

The factual state sequence is therefore not:

`DeFi rule → implementation → harvest`

It is:

`DeFi front-end rule → statutory disapproval / no force → separate custodial
reporting perimeter reaches operation`

That distinction is the analytical value of this family. A loose reading could
say that a seed-date reporting rule “came to fruition” in the 2026 Form 1099-DA
season. The legal objects disprove that statement. The original rule was
extinguished. What matured was an adjacent regime with a different regulated
population and a different custody boundary.

The comparison can therefore support only this bounded observation:

> **The public question of who must carry and disclose the obligation survives,
> while the first legal answer to that question does not. The perimeter is
> redrawn before the family reaches its quarter, and the harvest exposes the
> surviving allocation rather than reviving the repealed rule.**

### Same window, different fates: two perimeters die while one record system acquires operators

The broader December 27, 2024–January 2, 2025 Federal Register window supplies
a useful counter-control. It contains 673 published documents; the Workbench
selected three independently material final rules with later legal or operating
receipts. This is a bounded sample, not an exhaustive review and not three

The exact family object remains Treasury Decision 10021. The control adds:

- the CFPB's December 30 overdraft-credit rule, which Public Law 119-10
  disapproved on May 9, 2025 before its scheduled October 1 effective date;
  and
- the SEC's December 27 EDGAR Next rule, which reached mandatory operation in
  September 2025 through individually credentialed people, typed filer roles,
  delegated authority, multifactor authentication and optional APIs.

The three-object sequence is therefore:

`DeFi reporting perimeter → disapproved`

`large-bank overdraft-credit perimeter → disapproved before operation`

`federal securities-filing identity perimeter → credentialed operation`

This sharpens rather than broadens the comparison. The seed window does not
show a generic victory or defeat for regulation. It distinguishes a proposed
legal perimeter from a durable operating architecture. Two rules die because a
separate legal actor closes their perimeter. One record system survives because
named humans, delegated roles, authentication and machine interfaces can
actually carry it.

That is still only factual context. The EDGAR object is not the Capricorn
family's “real” harvest, and the second repeal is not a second hit. They are
controls against the temptation to treat publication as destiny. The direct
Moon-family comparison remains the DeFi rule's death and the distinct
