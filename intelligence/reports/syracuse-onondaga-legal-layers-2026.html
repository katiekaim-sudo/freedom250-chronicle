Home HeadQuarters — mission, Form 990 and the flow of work

In plain English

Question: What does Home HeadQuarters say it exists to do, and what does its public Form 990 show about how money and work move through it?

Answer: Its mission is to improve housing and neighborhoods for underserved people, starting in Syracuse and extending across Upstate New York. Its latest IRS XML return located in the public indexes reports substantial government-grant revenue, home repair and buyer-assistance activity, and related legal entities that carry out lending, construction, realty, housing and servicing jobs. The return is a map of the nonprofit's own claims and accounts; it does not identify which government award paid for each result.

Evidence checked through: 2026-10-10 (America/New_York). The original Form 990 is tax year 2024 for the fiscal period ending April 30, 2025; the 2024 group audit and October 2026 website are separate dated sources.

The detailed record below keeps the source wording, accounting perimeters and open joins.

Start with the legal filer and its mission

The public IRS Form 990 XML names Home HeadQuarters, Inc., EIN 22-2982267, for May 1, 2024–April 30, 2025. Its Part I short activity line focuses on improving housing and neighborhoods in Syracuse. Its Part III mission narrative describes creating housing and related services for underserved people across Upstate New York. The organization's current About page uses the broader Central and Upstate New York reach and names home financing, development, education and counseling. These are different levels of description in the nonprofit's own record, not proof of a changed charter or permission to spend a specific grant anywhere in Upstate New York. Each award still has its own legal purpose, geography, eligible beneficiary and cost rules. IRS source identity.

This parallels the government entity work: mission identifies the job an institution claims; the grant agreement and program law identify the work that a particular pot of money authorizes. Mission can explain why HHQ seeks a housing award or maintains a loan operation. It cannot turn an announced award into cash, move a city CDBG dollar to Clay, or make an affiliate the legal grantee without an agreement.

The return also reports loan-repayment income, fees and servicing revenue: HHQ is both a program operator and a lender/servicer in different relationships. Original IRS Form 990.

The organization's own control claims

Schedule O says HHQ's board authorizes each contract; staff assign a contract/reporting specialist; program and grant files are reviewed before closing; vouchers from its Blackbaud accounting system are checked against program databases; and staff prepare required grant reports. The organization also says the executive director, finance chief and board leadership review the Form 990 before filing. These are self-reported procedures, not a tested conclusion that every voucher matched, every grant complied or every homeowner received the promised benefit. But they tell us which internal records should exist for a proper government-to-nonprofit trace: contract, restriction, voucher, accounting entry, program case file, report and closeout. Form 990 Schedule O.

These are the nonprofit-side counterpart to the government's legal-entity chart. A grant may be signed by HHQ, a property held by another entity, repairs performed by a construction entity, loans serviced by an LLC and assistance delivered to a household. Control, consolidation, contracting, property title, cash custody and performance are separate edges. The 2024 audited consolidated statements include a wider group and eliminate intragroup balances; they have a different period and reporting perimeter from HHQ's 2024-tax-year Form 990. We must check the exact agreement and property record before tying one government award to one affiliate. Form 990 Schedule R.

Join this to the public-money map

The Syracuse public-money case identifies the government-side awards and program relationships. This 990 establishes HHQ's reported mission, aggregate public-funding share, outputs, grant procedures and related entities. No field in the return maps the $25.9 million government-grant total to Syracuse CDBG, the 2026 New York Vacant Rental award, Onondaga's Bayberry fund or the 2024 SEDCO bridge-loan request. The 2026 awards also fall after this return's April 2025 fiscal cutoff.

The next useful comparison is one exact program on both sides: obtain Syracuse's executed HHQ agreement and activity ID, match the City's draw and payment to HHQ's contract/voucher and program database, then identify the legal HHQ or affiliate account and the homeowner or property result. Schedule O tells us the internal records to request; it does not publish them. A 2025–26 Form 990, when publicly available, can test later aggregate mission and program claims but still will not replace the transaction chain.
