Accounting built from shared business events

Accounting is moving from piecing together scattered records after the fact to building the books from shared business events. Licenses, accounting labels, audit reports and corrections are also being used to decide who may act, invest or rely on the result.

What we found

  • Today one transaction is split across contracts, operating systems, banks, custodians, tax records and the general ledger, then pieced back together at close and audit. The transition makes the business event the starting record and the journal entry a controlled output from it.
  • Smart contracts handle rule-and-arithmetic work well, such as issuing invoices, matching purchase orders to deliveries and invoices, and releasing approved payments. They are weak where facts are disputed, such as real delivery, side deals, impairment, collectibility and valuation.
  • A shared, add-only ledger can curb backdating, silent overwrites, duplicate invoices and orphan entries, but not fake delivery records, side agreements, manipulated reserves or deals kept off the system. The games move; they don't vanish.
  • Standards are changing narrowly: FASB changed crypto measurement and the SEC rescinded SAB 121, while FASB treating some digital assets as cash equivalents was still tentative. A September 24 CFTC staff FAQ confirms that compliant blockchain records can satisfy specified recordkeeping duties without a duplicate off-chain copy kept just because a blockchain is used.
  • Oracle's Swift integration could plausibly cover the whole path from tokenized-deposit event to journal and general ledger. But Oracle has not shown a production mapping, posted journal, close or audit evidence, and its Digital Assets Data Nexus remains planned for fiscal 2027.
  • The people layer is changing too. NASBA's October 1 tracker lists additional CPA-licensure pathways enacted in 44 states plus D.C. and Puerto Rico. The Uniform CPA Exam remains mandatory, but the 120-hour route usually trades 30 extra classroom hours for a second year of experience; course content, experience, ethics, Exam-credit and mobility rules still vary by jurisdiction.
  • Accounting is also permission infrastructure. A credential, classification, control conclusion, audit report or correction has consequence only when a law, contract, regulator, market or counterparty uses that object as a scoped gate. The same CPA license can support state practice, interstate mobility, verification of another investor and a proposed personal accredited-investor route without those permissions becoming interchangeable.

Why it matters

Accounting can become easier to run and harder to alter without a trace, but accruals and judgment do not disappear. The same shift reaches private-company ownership, professional licensing and investor access, so the key tests are who may act, whether experience still builds judgment, whether auditors stay independent and whether corrections reach every dependent report and permission.

What to watch

  • pending · FASB final decisions on treating certain digital assets as cash equivalents and on accounting for crypto transfers (tentative as of July 30).
  • pending · A named Oracle bank and the exact route from a Swift token event into a named book, with posted journals, reversals, reconciliation and auditor evidence.
  • pending · A named private operating company completing the full chain at recurring scale: investor verification, exempt offering, token issuance, official ownership record, transfer, cash settlement, journal, financial statements and independent assurance.
  • 2027 · State boards' operative rules and first licenses under the new routes; the AICPA Profession Ready exposure draft; and the July 2027 CPA Exam platform transition and score schedule.
  • 2026-12-15 · Revised PCAOB AS 2901 and QC 1000 become effective; watch for the first implementation evidence showing how post-issuance engagement deficiencies, unsupported reports and firm remediation propagate into reliance decisions.
