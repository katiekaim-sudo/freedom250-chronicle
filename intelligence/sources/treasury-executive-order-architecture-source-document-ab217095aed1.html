Treasury Executive-Order Architecture — Source Document

Bottom line

The current Executive-Order stack does not establish a Treasury currency, a Treasury-issued stablecoin, an IMF or World Bank exit, a separation of the Federal Reserve from the dollar system, a gold revaluation, or a new Treasury bond-buyback authority.

It does establish a broader Treasury-centered control architecture than the money storyline previously foregrounded. Treasury is being used as a common gate for:

  • international financial-institution voice, vote, contribution, energy-finance, and procurement policy;
  • federal payment verification, classification, accounting, and disbursement centralization;
  • digital-asset infrastructure, GENIUS Act implementation, and Strategic Bitcoin Reserve support;
  • tax-credit eligibility and prohibited-foreign-entity supply-chain tests;
  • housing-market definitions, rulemaking, enforcement, and tax-advantaged construction capital;
  • separate-fund custody for private gifts under the Gold Card program; and
  • sanctions, illicit-finance, investment-security, and foreign-transaction controls.

The strongest factual reading is Treasury as a shared eligibility, routing, accounting, and enforcement layer. That is not the same legal or operational object as Treasury replacing the Federal Reserve or international financial institutions.

Census and corpus diagnosis

A live-vault text census found 71 second-term Executive Order notes from 2025–2026 that mention Treasury. The monetary Executive Order surface foregrounds 35 mapped orders. The difference is not 36 absent maps: many Treasury assignments were captured in other topical waves and therefore did not rise into the main monetary-control story.

Three examples sit in eo_entity_maps_wave10_ceremonial.json, whose label is “Second-term ceremonial and thin orders”:

  • EO 14162 — international climate-finance withdrawal;
  • EO 14376 — institutional-homebuyer definitions and rule review; and
  • EO 14394 — Opportunity Zone and New Markets Tax Credit housing-capital alignment.

EO 14261, EO 14299, and EO 14315 sit under energy. EO 14351's separate Treasury fund sits under immigration. The underlying legal objects were mostly captured; their joined Treasury storyline and later operating returns were under-promoted.

Architecture lanes

1. International development-finance reset

EO 14162 — withdrawal and financial-commitment cease

EO 14162 directed the United States Ambassador to the United Nations, collaborating with State and Treasury, to cease or revoke purported U.S. financial commitments under the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change. It revoked the U.S. International Climate Finance Plan and required Treasury and other agencies to report the policies they rescinded.

This is a climate-finance withdrawal object. It is not withdrawal from the IMF, World Bank, or the United Nations Framework Convention itself.

Primary source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/putting-america-first-in-international-environmental-agreements/

EO 14261 — reversal of the U.S. multilateral-development-bank energy posture

EO 14261 identified Treasury's 2021 Fossil Fuel Energy Guidance for Multilateral Development Banks as rescinded. It directed agencies with loans, guarantees, grants, equity, offtake, or other energy-financing tools to remove discretionary policies discouraging coal finance where legally permitted.

Primary source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/04/reinvigorating-americas-beautiful-clean-coal-industry-and-amending-executive-order-14241/

EO 14299 — affirmative nuclear-financing strategy

EO 14299 gave Treasury a 90-day strategy assignment to use U.S. participation in multilateral development banks to:

  • provide financial and technical assistance for nuclear generation, distribution, and fuel supply;
  • make support available on competitive terms;
  • build institutional capacity to assess and implement nuclear projects; and
  • support U.S.- or allied-standard technology and fuel-supply chains.

Primary source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/05/deploying-advanced-nuclear-reactor-technologies-for-national-security/

Operating return — Treasury Strategic Plan 2026–2030

Treasury's current Strategic Plan converts the three-order sequence into an operating program. Its stated strategies include:

  • leveraging U.S. voice and vote at international financial institutions;
  • pursuing reforms that Treasury considers necessary to justify continued U.S. contributions, subject to appropriations and authorization;
  • increasing U.S. procurement in multilateral-development-bank projects;
  • removing the World Bank prohibition on nuclear-energy support;
  • building nuclear-financing capacity at MDBs; and
  • identifying procurement opportunities for U.S. firms.

The plan identifies World Bank and IMF leadership as a critical Treasury program. The evidence therefore supports institutional repurposing through U.S. leverage, not separation or exit.

Primary source: https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/266/Treasury_Strategic_Plan_2026-2030.pdf

2. Payment, accounting, and data centralization

The previously mapped payment-control braid remains:

EO 14222 payment justification → EO 14247 electronic rails → EO 14249 Treasury screening → EO 14332 grant-draw controls → EO 14395 fraud coordination

Treasury's Strategic Plan supplies the higher-maturity operating intent. It names the Enterprise Data Platform, Payment Integrity, Electronic Federal Tax Payment System modernization, Taxpayer 360, and Core Financial Management Consolidation. Its success criteria include:

  • verifying all Treasury-made federal disbursements before agency certification;
  • expanding Do Not Pay data and federal-program use;
  • classifying federal payments in centralized Treasury accounting systems daily;
  • tying Treasury Account Symbols to all federal payments;
  • verifying funds availability for all federal payments; and
  • migrating non-Treasury disbursing offices with delegated authority to Treasury disbursement.

The plan also asks how effective AI is in taxpayer programs and how effective Do Not Pay is in preventing improper payments. AI and the Enterprise Data Platform are implementation substrates for the payment-control architecture; they do not themselves prove automated payment denial, a public ledger, blockchain settlement, or agent spending authority.

3. Housing-market definition, rulemaking, and enforcement

EO 14376 — original definition leash

EO 14376 directed Treasury to define “large institutional investor” and “single-family home” within 30 days. Other agencies could adopt those definitions when restricting federal approval, insurance, guarantees, securitization, facilitation, and asset sales involving institutional purchasers. Treasury was also directed to review its rules and guidance concerning institutional acquisition or holding of single-family homes.

Primary source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/01/stopping-wall-street-from-competing-with-main-street-homebuyers/

Phase change — Public Law 119-101

Public Law 119-101 became law without the President's signature on July 11, 2026. It supersedes the old map boundary that EO 14376 had not created a private-market purchase-ban statute.

The enacted regime:

  • generally defines a large institutional investor at 350 controlled single-family homes;
  • prohibits covered purchases, subject to detailed statutory exceptions;
  • authorizes Treasury rulemaking in consultation with HUD, FHFA, and SEC;
  • authorizes Treasury, or the Attorney General at Treasury's request, to enforce the prohibition;
  • permits a civil penalty up to $1,000,000 per violation or three times the property's purchase price, whichever is greater; and
  • permits appropriated penalty receipts to be transferred to HUD homeownership programs beginning in fiscal year 2027.

The purchase prohibition and enforcement provisions take effect 180 days after enactment: January 7, 2027. They are repealed 15 years after that effective date.

Primary source: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ101/pdf/PLAW-119publ101.pdf

CBDC adjacency in the same statute

Title XI of Public Law 119-101 amended the Federal Reserve Act to prohibit the Board and Reserve Banks from issuing or creating a retail central-bank digital currency, directly or indirectly through an intermediary. It states an exception for dollar-denominated currency that is open, permissionless, private, and preserves the privacy protections of coins and physical currency. The provision sunsets December 31, 2030.

This is a material legal separation between a Fed retail CBDC and private dollar instruments. It does not create a Treasury stablecoin, authorize a named issuer, provide reserve assets, establish redemption, or create settlement finality.

4. Treasury tax and industrial-eligibility gates

EO 14315 directed Treasury to strictly enforce termination rules for Internal Revenue Code §§ 45Y and 48E wind and solar credits and to implement enhanced prohibited-foreign-entity restrictions.

Primary source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/ending-market-distorting-subsidies-for-unreliable-foreign%E2%80%91controlled-energy-sources/

Public operating returns include:

  • IRS Notice 2025-42, which generally removed the Five Percent Safe Harbor for the relevant deadline and made the Physical Work Test the principal route for covered projects whose construction had not begun before September 2, 2025; and
  • Notice 2026-15, which established interim prohibited-foreign-entity and material-assistance rules while identifying later regulations and safe-harbor tables as separate objects.

Primary sources:

  • https://www.irs.gov/irb/2025-36_IRB
  • https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/treasury-irs-provide-guidance-for-certain-energy-tax-credits-regarding-material-assistance-provided-by-prohibited-foreign-entities-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill

Treasury is therefore operating as a project- and supply-chain-eligibility gate for economically material tax support. A notice is not a project-level credit denial; the next evidence is taxpayer, examination, ruling, litigation, or project-financing impact.

5. Separate Treasury fund for Gold Card gifts

EO 14351 requires Commerce to deposit $1,000,000 individual and $2,000,000 corporate gifts into a separate Treasury fund for Commerce's use in promoting commerce and American industry.

Primary source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/09/the-gold-card/

Commerce reported on January 20, 2026 that $1,300,000,000 had been “committed” through the program. Commitment must remain separate from application, vetting, gift request, receipt, Treasury deposit, obligation, and expenditure.

Primary source: https://www.commerce.gov/news/press-releases/2026/01/first-year-accomplishments-under-secretary-howard-w-lutnick

The likely preexisting accounting substrate is Commerce's gifts-and-bequests fund-symbol system under 15 U.S.C. § 1522, including 13X8501-family receipt and expenditure accounts. The exact Gold Card fund symbol, SF-133, Treasury account statement, receipts, obligations, and expenditures remain unresolved.

6. Opportunity Zone and New Markets Tax Credit housing-capital route

EO 14394 directed Treasury and HUD to evaluate linking federal grants, financing tools, and other incentives to Qualified Opportunity Funds developing and selling single-family homes, and to coordinate Opportunity Zone incentives with the New Markets Tax Credit.

Primary source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/03/removing-regulatory-barriers-to-affordable-home-construction/

Treasury opened the 2027 Opportunity Zone designation cycle on July 1, 2026 after the program was made permanent and rural incentives were added.

Primary source: https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0550

Joined with EO 14376 and Public Law 119-101, the policy architecture is:

restrict covered institutional acquisition of existing homes → steer tax-advantaged capital toward construction of additional homes

That is a supported policy join. It is not proof of a particular Qualified Opportunity Fund, grant, loan, construction start, home sale, or affordability result.

Standing return gates

Promote only a material state change supported by a primary record.

International finance

  • Treasury, World Bank, IMF, MDB, G7, or G20 voting instructions, board records, contribution conditions, trust-fund changes, procurement rules, or nuclear/coal project-finance decisions.
  • A named nuclear or coal project with institution, borrower, amount, financing instrument, vote, approval, commitment, disbursement, procurement award, or operating result.
  • Any formal change in U.S. IMF or World Bank membership, quota, voting, contribution, or treaty/statutory status. Speeches about reform are not exit.

Housing

  • Treasury rulemaking, guidance, forms, definitions, compliance systems, or enforcement preparation under Public Law 119-101.
  • January 7, 2027 effective-state evidence, covered-investor notifications, investigations, complaints, penalties, or HUD transfers.
  • Opportunity Zone maps, Qualified Opportunity Fund projects, linked grants or financing, New Markets Tax Credit coordination, construction starts, and sales.

Tax and industrial eligibility

  • Final prohibited-foreign-entity regulations and safe-harbor tables.
  • Project-level tax rulings, examinations, denied or allowed credits, financing changes, litigation, or supply-chain substitutions attributable to the rules.

Gold Card fund

  • Exact Treasury fund symbol and account title.
  • Cash receipts rather than commitments, SF-133 or Treasury account reporting, obligations, expenditures, procurement, audits, refunds, or litigation.

Payment and AI control plane

  • Enterprise Data Platform contracts, architecture, interfaces, identity/provenance controls, model use, evaluation, error, appeal, privacy, or audit records.
  • Attributable Do Not Pay and pre-certification results, including false positives, returns, agency reversals, recovery, and measured improper-payment reduction.
  • A demonstrated join to Reserve Banks, payment FMIs, settlement banks, card networks, stablecoins, or Treasury securities. Shared vocabulary or parallel timing is not an operating join.

Monetary-regime hypothesis

  • A Treasury-issued or expressly Treasury-sponsored stablecoin, reserve vehicle, redemption system, issuer designation, or settlement rule.
  • A statutory or operative transfer of currency issuance, monetary-policy, Reserve Bank, master-account, or settlement authority.
  • A formal IMF/World Bank withdrawal or contribution termination.
  • A gold valuation, title, custody, convertibility, collateral, reserve, or settlement change.
  • A new Treasury buyback authority or executed operation must remain separate from debt cancellation, QE, monetary issuance, or Fed asset purchases.
