Wave 5E — Information Risk and the Fraud-Control Graph

Research window: Exact 2025 Capricorn ingress through exact 2026 Aries ingress.
Evidence cutoff: June 20, 2026, 11:59 p.m. EDT. Later facts are quarantined as handoffs.
Question: Did the winter's criminal-record, cybercrime and benefits-fraud orders create one surveillance machine, or three bounded information-and-enforcement routes that later began to connect?
Finding: The three orders describe distinct risk graphs. EO 14385 directs lawful Justice criminal-history access for DHS and reciprocal foreign conviction exchange, but no named new agreement, data transfer or resulting border decision was isolated. EO 14390 directs a transnational scam-center strategy; Treasury's April 23 sanctions expressly matured it into a named blocking action before the cutoff, correcting the earlier belief that the first explicit descendant was June 23. EO 14395 creates the most extensive domestic benefits-fraud control plane. After the Aries ingress it acquired a convened task force, DOJ division, program war room, data routine uses, large SBA referral, fast-track False Claims Act process and Treasury/Fraud.gov intake system. Those are real bodies and records. They still do not merge into one universal citizen database or convert criminal history, risk indicator, tip, referral, allegation, improper payment and proved fraud into the same fact. The packet's thesis is the regime makes risk portable across institutions by building routes for records, indicators and referrals; decision and remedy remain legally distributed.

The shared ladder is:

identity / event record → lawful access → match or risk indicator → triage → program or enforcement referral → investigation / eligibility decision → hold / exclusion / charge / recovery → notice and remedy

5E.1 — February 6: conviction records are made portable for screening

EO 14385 directs the Attorney General to give DHS lawful access to Justice criminal-history record information for screening and vetting. It also authorizes DHS to exchange felony-conviction records with Visa Waiver Program countries, serious-crime-agreement countries and other trusted allies when:

  • the exchange is reciprocal;
  • a bilateral or multilateral agreement or arrangement exists;
  • the foreign use is limited to traveler and immigrant screening; and
  • privacy safeguards comply with law.

The order changes the requested information route, not the underlying legal character of the record. DOJ remains custodian of its criminal-justice information; DHS remains the screening holder; foreign authorities receive only through a governed exchange.

No newly executed partner agreement, dataset transfer, query volume, named match, visa decision, admission denial or removal case attributable to the EO was isolated by the cutoff.

Maturity. Effective executive direction and conditions for access/exchange.

Counterevidence and limit. A conviction record may be relevant to a legal screening rule but does not itself decide immigration status. “Maximum extent permitted by law” preserves statutory, privacy, accuracy and redress constraints. Reciprocity is not unrestricted international sharing.

5E.2 — March 6 and April 23: scam-center policy reaches named blocking

EO 14390 directs a 60-day framework review and a 120-day action plan for foreign transnational cybercrime. The plan is to identify responsible organizations and create an operational cell within the National Coordination Center. It also calls for private cybersecurity support, State/local resilience, prosecution priority, a 90-day victim-restoration recommendation and diplomatic or sanctions consequences.

The first direct public descendant found is earlier than the prior wave audit believed. On April 23, Treasury sanctioned Cambodian senator Kok An and 28 people and entities in a scam-center network, while State offered rewards for seizure/recovery information and an associated fugitive. Treasury explicitly states that the action was taken in furtherance of EO 14390.

The designations used EO 13694 as amended—not a new blocking authority created by EO 14390. That distinction shows how a new coordinating doctrine recruits an older legal tool. The action named nodes, blocked property interests subject to U.S. jurisdiction and created sanctions-compliance consequences. It did not prove that the network was dismantled, victims repaid, compounds closed or trafficked workers freed.

The publicly due 60-day review, 90-day restoration recommendation and 120-day action plan/NCC-cell material were not isolated as complete documents by June 20. Treasury's next explicitly EO-linked action on June 23 falls after the cutoff.

Maturity. Named sanctions and reward action under inherited legal authority; broader plan outputs unresolved.

Counterevidence and limit. Designation is an administrative legal act, not a criminal conviction. Blocking protects and constrains the U.S. transaction perimeter but does not equal seizure, forfeiture, recovery or restitution. Existing 2025 strike-force and sanctions activity cannot be retroactively credited to the March 2026 order.

5E.3 — March 16: benefits fraud receives a timed control-plane blueprint

EO 14395 creates a White House Task Force chaired by the Vice President, with the FTC chair as vice chair and Treasury, Justice, Agriculture, Labor, HHS, HUD, Education, VA, DHS, SBA, OMB and other bodies represented.

The operative object is not one “fraud database.” It is a graph of at least four node families:

  • person: applicant, beneficiary, owner, caregiver or employee;
  • provider/organization: clinic, retailer, grantee, contractor or intermediary;
  • account/instrument: identity account, device, contact, bank account or payment destination; and
  • program/payment: enrollment, claim, award, invoice, voucher or disbursement.

The order asks the task force to improve proof of identity and eligibility, pre-obligation and pre-disbursement controls, cross-program indicators, information sharing, provider/retailer revalidation and remedies. Each member agency keeps its legal decision rights.

The task force acquires organs after the Aries handoff

EO 14395 is signed four days before the Aries ingress. Most of its visible body belongs to the next seasonal wave and must retain those dates.

March 27–April 24: body, prosecutor, program operation and debt referral

  • The task force's first meeting was publicly convened March 27.
  • On April 7, Justice created the National Fraud Enforcement Division, a real departmental carrier for coordination, investigation and prosecution.
  • In April, CMS launched a Medicaid Fraud War Room with the task force. It used analytics to develop provider leads and coordinate existing State/federal exclusion, penalty, recovery and suspension authorities. The June fact sheet describes an identified lab case; later aggregate results were not public until after the cutoff.
  • On April 24, SBA referred 562,000 suspected-fraud delinquent PPP/EIDL loans tied to $22.2 billion to Treasury collections and transmitted borrowers to Justice.

The SBA action is a major operating receipt, but the language must stay exact. The loans were delinquent and flagged for suspected fraud. Referral starts collection/investigation pathways; it is not adjudication that 562,000 borrowers committed fraud or recovery of $22.2 billion.

April 22–June 17: data plumbing and public intake

  • GSA's April 22 Privacy Act notice added a routine use allowing enumerated GSA and government-wide payment-connected systems to disclose records to Treasury for lawful fraud/improper-payment purposes. The direct cited authorities are EO 14249 and OMB M-25-32; the notice is adjacent implementation in the same control stack, not exclusively caused by EO 14395.
  • Justice's May 27 fast-track policy prioritizes initial review of benefits-fraud False Claims Act complaints within 60–120 days and routes new matters to criminal and administrative holders.
  • Treasury's June 17 Treasury .032 system of records created Fraud.gov tip-intake and referral architecture. It can receive, deduplicate, categorize, triage and refer tips, subjects, witnesses, providers, account/payment identifiers, supporting material and risk indicators. The system was effective on publication; routine uses were scheduled for July 17, after the cutoff, absent change.

This is the clearest “nervous system” in the wave. It moves an allegation to the office that can lawfully review it. It does not decide the allegation.

Maturity. White House body, departmental enforcement carrier, program analytics operation, Privacy Act routes, large debt/referral package, expedited case-review process and public intake/referral system.

Counterevidence and limit. Complete 30/60/90-day products remained nonpublic. The June House bills were not enacted. A routine use does not prove a transfer; a risk signal does not prove fraud; a tip is not evidence tested in an adjudication; a referral is not recovery; an indictment is an allegation; an improper payment may be error rather than fraud. Each adverse action still needs its legal holder, evidence, notice and remedy.

The portable-risk theorem

The winter progression is not centralization into one legal person. It is portability:

  1. a conviction record becomes available to another screener;
  2. a transnational network becomes legible across sanctions, diplomacy, law enforcement and private cyber infrastructure; and
  3. a benefits-fraud signal can move across program, Treasury, inspector-general and Justice routes.

The operating control plane migrates while the underlying decision rights remain distributed. Justice supplies CHRI and prosecutes; DHS screens; foreign partners act under agreements; Treasury blocks or routes payments and tips; agencies and States decide eligibility; CMS and OIG act on provider authority; courts decide liability.

This is the institutional-conformation thesis in a precise form: the old entities remain, but shared risk objects begin to circulate through them under a common executive grammar.

Both poles

Constructive pole — shared signals interrupt adaptive fraud

  • trusted conviction data can improve lawful border screening;
  • government, allies and private cyber firms can see transnational infrastructure as a network;
  • pre-payment and provider controls can prevent losses rather than recover them later;
  • program agencies retain legal decisions while shared services improve observation;
  • a public tip can reach the correct inspector, agency or prosecutor; and
  • named notice, verification and appeal controls can separate innocent anomalies from fraud.

Shadow pole — the graph becomes an exclusion machine

  • criminal history can travel farther than the legal context that made it accurate or relevant;
  • sanctions and security framing can substitute designation for adjudication;
  • commercial and government risk signals can silently fuse across programs;
  • continuous verification can turn ordinary services into identity checkpoints;
  • false positives can follow a person, provider or household through multiple systems; and
  • fraud rhetoric can justify withholding benefits or money before usable notice and remedy exist.

Both poles remain live. Access logs, agreements, data dictionaries, procurement, error audits, holds, referrals, cases, recoveries, notices, appeals and reinstatements—not the astrological rhyme—show which matured.
