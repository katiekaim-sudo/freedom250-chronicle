Dollar tokens in decentralized finance (DeFi): who owes the dollar, who pays interest, what the GENIUS Act covers

Sixteen systems showing who owes the dollar, what keeps its value stable and who can change the rules

A one-dollar target does not make every dollar token the same legal thing. Protocol-issued dollars, hedged (synthetic) dollars, payment coins, vault receipts and tokenized securities can sit in one wallet. Each can have a different issuer, collateral, controller and set of rules.

GENIUS §2(22)Legal classification questionStatute (enacted law)law enacted · how it applies to these tokens is not settled

Whether a token is a payment stablecoin depends on how it is redeemed, not on its $1 price

Who owes the dollar?A payment stablecoin must have an issuer obligated to convert, redeem or buy it back for a fixed amount of money.
What stabilizes it?The law separately requires the issuer to promise, or create a reasonable expectation of, a stable value.
Who can change it?Who exactly the legal issuer is, and what the holder receives on redemption
open the dollar constitution
Legal object

A token qualifies only if it is built for payment or settlement, an issuer must redeem it, and redemption is for a fixed amount of money. Deposits and securities are excluded.

Collateral

What backs a token does not, by itself, decide whether it qualifies.

Custody / venue

Redeeming for cash or a bank deposit differs from receiving USDC or one dollar's worth of crypto.

Mint / redeem

A software swap, a peg stability module (PSM, a contract that swaps the token for another stablecoin) or a collateral redemption is not automatically a legal person's promise to pay money.

Yield layer

A permitted or recognized issuer may not pay yield just for holding, using or keeping the coin.

Administrator

Issuer supervision, and the technical ability to obey lawful orders such as freezes, apply only once the product counts as a payment stablecoin.

GENIUS read

Each DeFi token must be tested against every part of the definition. Calling it a 'stablecoin' does not make it one.

Distribution

Falling outside the definition may avoid some GENIUS rules. It does not give the token the approval a permitted payment stablecoin issuer (PPSI) holds.

Merchant path

Payment processors will likely prefer the coin whose issuer and redemption rights are easiest to certify.

Failure mode

Treating a $1 price-feed (oracle) target or a USDC swap as dollar redemption under the law.

Do not overclaim

Falling outside GENIUS does not mean unregulated. Securities, commodities, lending, sanctions and other laws still apply.

Who owes the dollar?The study covers tokens that may have no ordinary payment-stablecoin issuer.
What stabilizes it?The law requires the study to cover reserves, algorithms, governance and decentralization.

Legal object

A report to the Senate Banking and House Financial Services committees on stablecoins that are not payment stablecoins.

Collateral

The report must analyze what reserves hold and designs backed by the system's own tokens (endogenous designs).

Custody / venue

Not specified. The product types and participants covered should show where institutional custody begins and ends.

Mint / redeem

Expected to separate a software conversion from a legal right to redeem.

Yield layer

The study covers products whose economics do not fit payment coins, which pay no yield.

Administrator

The law expressly requires the study to cover governance structure and decentralization.

GENIUS read

Through 2026-07-28, we found no public copy and no public confirmation that it was sent.

Distribution

How the study classifies these tokens can affect wallets, banks, processors and market utilities.

Merchant path

It determines whether protocol-issued dollars can gain institutional recognition or remain assets that must be converted before use.

Failure mode

Saying Treasury definitely failed, when it may have delivered the report privately to Congress.

Do not overclaim

The law requires delivery to the committees, not public release.

Who owes the dollar?The base payment coin's issuer still owes holders redemption at face value (par).
What stabilizes it?The base coin's reserve stays separate from any lending, fund, hedging or rewards income.
Who can change it?Whoever issues the separate token or account that holds the coin, or a non-issuer that pays rewards
open the dollar constitution
Legal object

An asset split swaps the coin for a new vault, security or fund claim. An actor split keeps the same coin but changes who pays.

Collateral

The underlying payment coin may be lent out, locked in a vault or swapped for an investment.

Custody / venue

The user may hand control of the base coin to a smart contract or platform.

Mint / redeem

Withdrawal rights run against the new receipt or account, not automatically against the base coin's issuer.

Yield layer

The return comes from borrower interest, Treasury income, hedging, staking or incentives, not the issuer's reserve.

Administrator

Protocol votes, vault managers and reward-program terms now decide how the product works.

GENIUS read

The separate token and the rewards program each need their own legal classification. Neither gets automatic protection.

Distribution

Institutional wallets can offer a no-yield coin and a separate earning product side by side.

Merchant path

The earning position is usually converted back to a permitted base coin before payment.

Failure mode

Calling sUSDe, USYC or an Aave receipt 'yield-bearing USDC.'

Do not overclaim

Even if the money feels the same, a new legal or smart-contract claim has been issued.

Protocol-native
DAI / USDS / sUSDSDollar created by DeFi softwareLive product documentslive · unclear who the issuer is

Sky (formerly Maker) runs DAI and USDS without a company that owes holders the dollar

Who owes the dollar?None of the Sky legal entities we reviewed says it owes holders cash on redemption. Whether a DAO (a group of token holders who vote) counts as a legal person is unresolved.
What stabilizes it?Collateral in vaults, swaps through the peg stability module (PSM), interest rates, liquidations and holder votes.
Who can change it?SKY token holders vote, and the USDS contracts can be upgraded
open the dollar constitution
Legal object

Sky describes USDS as issued by the protocol and as a redeemable 'Protocol Obligation.' sUSDS is a separate savings share.

Collateral

Outside stablecoins, crypto, credit, and real-world assets including Treasuries.

Custody / venue

Depends on the collateral and on how each Sky Agent is set up.

Mint / redeem

Holders can convert DAI to USDS and back, and swap into USDC through the PSM. No named issuer redeems for dollars or deposits.

Yield layer

Plain USDS earns nothing automatically. sUSDS and the DAI Savings Rate (DSR) are separate savings positions.

Administrator

Holder votes change collateral, rates and the PSM, and put approved changes into effect.

GENIUS read

Likely not a payment stablecoin. Sky's own claim wording leaves open who the issuer is.

Distribution

Widely used in DeFi and on exchanges. Companies that run the apps can add their own restrictions.

Merchant path

Merchants can already choose DAI payouts. A processor's support does not prove permitted payment stablecoin issuer (PPSI) status.

Failure mode

Treating a protocol claim or a swap into USDC as a legal person's promise to pay dollars.

Do not overclaim

Sky's documents are authoritative on how the system works. They are not a U.S. regulator's classification.

GHO / sGHODollar created by DeFi softwareLive product documentslive · argued to fall outside the law

Who owes the dollar?No named legal person owes holders cash or deposit redemption.
What stabilizes it?Collateral on Aave, minting limits for each Facilitator (a party approved to create GHO), the GHO Stability Module (GSM), interest rates and arbitrage.
Who can change it?Aave DAO (Aave token holders who vote), Facilitators, and GHO Stewards acting under delegated power
open the dollar constitution
Legal object

A dollar-like token created by the protocol. sGHO is a separate savings receipt.

Collateral

Outside assets deposited on Aave. Being crypto-backed does not by itself mean it is backed by its own system's tokens (endogenous).

Custody / venue

Aave smart contracts and arrangements specific to each Facilitator.

Mint / redeem

Users borrow (create) GHO against collateral. The GSM swaps it with USDC; that is not cash redemption under the law.

Yield layer

sGHO earns a savings return set by DAO vote. Plain GHO earns nothing automatically.

Administrator

Holder votes can change minting limits and rates, and freeze modules. We found no blocklist for ordinary holders.

GENIUS read

Strong case that it is not a payment stablecoin, but no regulator has ruled.

Distribution

Aave promotes links to payment services. Apps and institutions keep their own access controls.

Merchant path

Most likely converted to a permitted payment coin before checkout or settlement.

Failure mode

Assuming that because holders cannot be frozen, no person controls supply or modules.

Do not overclaim

A GENIUS analysis commissioned by Aave is not agency approval.

Legal object

Collateral

Outside crypto assets posted as collateral in its lending markets.

Custody / venue

Smart contracts hold the collateral. The app and developer roles are separate.

Mint / redeem

Yield layer

Administrator

GENIUS read

Likely not a payment stablecoin. If the DAO or factory were treated as issuer, it would still fall short on permitted reserves and obeying lawful orders.

Merchant path

Who owes the dollar?Frax Inc. PBC is the most likely issuer. Binding terms naming who owes holders are still needed.
What stabilizes it?Custodians written into the protocol, cash-equivalent and real-world assets (RWA), protocol liquidity and bank payment networks.
Who can change it?Frax Inc. PBC, under authority the DAO (Frax token holders who vote) can take back
open the dollar constitution
Legal object

A payment-style base token, plus a separate sfrxUSD token that holds it and branded or white-label versions.

Collateral

Outside cash equivalents, tokenized Treasuries, assets held by custodians and protocol strategies.

Custody / venue

A network of protocol-approved custodians and RWA providers.

Mint / redeem

Verified people and businesses (KYC/KYB) can create and redeem by ACH or wire. Custodians can also create and destroy tokens.

Yield layer

sfrxUSD is a separate token. FraxNet also advertises rewards for eligible holders of frxUSD itself.

Administrator

Frax Inc. manages compliance and reserves. The DAO can take back or override that authority.

GENIUS read

Of the DeFi-born tokens, the clearest candidate for payment-stablecoin and permitted payment stablecoin issuer (PPSI) status. Its license and yield treatment are unresolved.

Distribution

Bank payment networks, Coinbase Pay, cross-chain and white-label products.

Merchant path

Could reach existing processors if the exact issuer and reserve rules are approved.

Failure mode

Calling FIP-432 a completed PPSI approval, or assuming rewards on frxUSD itself are lawful.

Do not overclaim

The yield question is under watch; it is not a finding of a violation.

Liquity's code cannot be changed, which also means it cannot be brought under the law

Who owes the dollar?No named legal issuer, and no one owes holders cash or deposit redemption.
What stabilizes it?Loans backed by more collateral than they borrow, redemptions for crypto, the Stability Pool and liquidations.
Who can change it?Core contract rules that cannot be changed, plus actions by users and keepers
open the dollar constitution
Legal object

Debt tokens created by the protocol. Stability Pool positions are separate claims with their own risk.

Collateral

ETH, WETH and outside liquid-staking tokens.

Custody / venue

Unchangeable contracts; no central custodian holds reserves.

Mint / redeem

Redeems for one dollar's worth of crypto collateral, not for money.

Yield layer

The base coin earns nothing automatically. The Stability Pool receives interest and liquidation gains.

Administrator

The core code we reviewed has no way to upgrade, pause, block holders or move to a new system by vote.

GENIUS read

Strong case that it falls outside the payment-stablecoin definition. It cannot meet permitted-issuer controls without a new system.

Distribution

Merchant path

Failure mode

Do not overclaim

Ethena's USDe is held at $1 by hedging, and its interest-paying version is a separate token

Who owes the dollar?Ethena BVI Limited, under current non-EEA terms. Only approved Mint Users have direct rights against it.
What stabilizes it?Crypto and liquid-staking tokens (LSTs), offset by derivatives positions, plus funding and basis income and stable reserve assets.
Who can change it?Ethena's issuer and operating-company roles, custodians, the venues where it hedges, and special contract permissions
open the dollar constitution
Legal object

USDe is the synthetic base coin. sUSDe is a separate vault receipt (ERC-4626 token standard).

Collateral

BTC, ETH, SOL, LSTs, stablecoins and derivatives positions.

Custody / venue

Held off exchange through firms including Copper and Ceffu, plus on trading venues.

Mint / redeem

Only approved Mint Users can create or redeem. Ordinary holders must sell on the market.

Yield layer

sUSDe earns staking, funding and basis, and reserve income. USDe itself pays nothing.

Administrator

Gatekeeper and minting roles can turn off functions. sUSDe can block addresses and restrict who stakes.

GENIUS read

Fits poorly as a permitted payment stablecoin. Its reserve and redemption are synthetic and pro rata, not a narrow reserve paying face value.

Distribution

Widely used in DeFi, but U.S. persons cannot be Mint Users.

Merchant path

Likely unwrapped or swapped into a permitted payment coin before purchases.

Failure mode

Treating sUSDe's yield as if plain USDe earned it.

Do not overclaim

A stable price does not turn derivatives collateral into a reserve GENIUS allows.

Who owes the dollar?No single public legal person owes every ordinary M holder redemption for a fixed amount of money.
What stabilizes it?Approved Minters (parties allowed to create M), collateral in special purpose vehicles (SPVs), Validators and an interest-rate model.
Who can change it?Lists approved by holder vote, Validators and the MinterGateway contract
open the dollar constitution
Legal object

One M contract supports both fixed balances and balances that grow automatically (rebasing) to pay earnings.

Collateral

Eligible collateral differs by product and Minter, held through separate SPV and custody arrangements.

Custody / venue

Depends on the Minter. Disclosed examples include European institutional structures.

Mint / redeem

MinterGateway is the only contract that can create or destroy M. Whether ordinary holders can redeem for cash is unresolved.

Yield layer

Approved addresses turn on earning (startEarning) on the same M token.

Administrator

Holder votes approve or remove Minters and Earners. Validators can freeze Minters and cancel token creation.

GENIUS read

Someone controls M but no one owes holders the dollar, and the token itself pays earnings. Both make it a poor payment-stablecoin fit.

Distribution

Builders can wrap M in their own tokens or brand it, through many different legal issuers.

Merchant path

A licensed, product-specific token holding M can reach merchants. Generic M's status is not enough.

Failure mode

Assuming one shared protocol design means one issuer for every coin built on M^0.

Do not overclaim

The technical power to create tokens and the legal duty to redeem them are separate.

Who owes the dollar?Mountain and Paxos International ended their duties to create, reward and redeem under their wind-downs.
What stabilizes it?Leftover market trading or a move to another token replaced ongoing issuer support.
Who can change it?The issuer's wind-down terms, the migration process and remaining trading pools

Legal object

The token still exists technically, but its commercial and legal promise ended or moved.

Collateral

What happens to reserves follows the wind-down terms, not old marketing.

Custody / venue

Some holders, exchanges and pools may keep tokens after the issuer stops service.

Mint / redeem

The issuer stopped redeeming USDM. Eligible USDL balances moved to USDG, which pays no yield.

Yield layer

Rewards and automatic balance increases (rebasing) on the token ended.

Administrator

The issuer can end services. Sanctioned or frozen balances may fall under separate controls.

GENIUS read

Strong proof that a token can keep existing technically after its business ends.

Distribution

Trading pools can continue after official support ends.

Merchant path

Processors should drop an unsupported token, even if its contract still allows transfers.

Failure mode

Claiming GENIUS caused either shutdown without direct evidence.

Do not overclaim

A market price near one dollar does not bring back the issuer's duty to redeem.
