OBBB — Eligibility and Fiscal-Federalism Companion

Updated: 2026-08-15
Status: factual legal map
Rule: A work condition is not an error rate. A hold-harmless percent is not repeal of a provider tax. Expansion still exists. This note does not rewrite OBBB — Fraud, Improper-Payment and Error-Rate Legal Map.

Short answer

P.L. 119-21 is enacted law. It does three load-bearing things the fraud map does not carry.

  1. It writes new eligibility conditions, not just error rates. Medicaid expansion adults must show community engagement as a condition of eligibility (new 42 U.S.C. § 1396a(xx), added by §71119). SNAP able-bodied-adult exceptions are rewritten (§10102). Alien eligibility for SNAP, Medicaid/CHIP, and the premium tax credit is narrowed to a short list (§§10108, 71109, 71301). Those are door rules.

  2. It rewrites the State financing formula around those doors. The old 6 percent provider-tax hold-harmless is replaced by an “applicable percent” that treats expansion and non-expansion States differently (§71115). State-directed payments are capped to Medicare (§71116). The increased-FMAP expansion incentive is closed to late expanders (§71114). Section 1115 Medicaid demonstrations need CMS Chief Actuary budget-neutrality certification from 2027-01-01 (§71118). A $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program (FY2026–FY2030) is a separate pot, walled off from Medicaid match (§71401).

  3. The federal parent keeps the program charters. The State runs the case file. Title to the State treasury never moves. CMS published the §71119 interim final rule on 2026-06-03 (CMS-2454-IFC, 91 FR 33348; effective 2026-07-31). The rule implements the statute. It does not replace it.

The Act’s tax-permanence and energy-credit clocks are a different object. They live in the Code, not in cooperative federalism. Named at the foot. Not mapped here.

2. What this note is not

Leave on OBBB — Fraud, Improper-Payment and Error-Rate Legal Map, and do not re-litigate here:

  • SNAP §10105 payment-error-rate cost share (FY2028; 0 / 5 / 10 / 15 percent State share).
  • SNAP §10106 administrative-cost shift (federal share 50 percent through FY2026, 25 percent from FY2027).
  • Medicaid §71106 3 percent erroneous-excess-payment / waiver squeeze (FY2030).
  • Medicaid/CHIP §§71103–71105, 71107 address, Death Master File, six-month redetermination machinery.
  • ACA §§71303–71305 Exchange verification, SEP lock, full APTC recapture.
  • FEHB §90101 and the other targeted fraud/penalty lines.

Those notes already say: neither SNAP nor Medicaid rate is a fraud rate; the House zero-tolerance SNAP line did not become law; the Act does not order a unified profile or a blockchain.

This companion takes the other half: who may be on the roll, who must work, how States finance the remaining caseload.

3. Eligibility clocks

3.1 SNAP work — §10102 (7 U.S.C. § 2015(o))

The Act strikes and replaces the exception paragraph. It does not rewrite the underlying paragraph (2) three-month / work-hours rule. It changes who is excepted and which States may waive.

New 7 U.S.C. § 2015(o)(3):

Paragraph (2) shall not apply to an individual if the individual is— (A) under 18, or over 65, years of age; (B) medically certified as physically or mentally unfit for employment; (C) a parent or other member of a household with responsibility for a dependent child under 14 years of age; (D) otherwise exempt under subsection (d)(2); (E) a pregnant woman; (F) an Indian or an Urban Indian …; or (G) a California Indian described in section 809(a) of the Indian Health Care Improvement Act.

Object What the Act writes Clock
Age band Exception is under 18 or over 65. Ages 18–64 are inside the ABAWD pool unless another exception applies. No delayed date written. Live as of enactment (2025-07-04) unless a later statute or a court stay says otherwise.
Parents Exception is a dependent child under 14. A parent of a 14–17-year-old is not excepted by (C). Same.
Indian carve-out New (F) and (G). Same.
Geographic waiver Remaining special clause: a noncontiguous State with unemployment at or above 1.5 times the national rate. “Noncontiguous State” = not one of the contiguous 48 or D.C.; excludes Guam and the Virgin Islands. Same.
Time-limited State exemption Secretary may exempt individuals in a noncontiguous State if the State shows a good-faith effort. Exemption expires not later than 2028-12-31 and may not be renewed. Request → USDA determination → hard stop 2028-12-31.

2028-12-31 is the death date of the noncontiguous good-faith exemption. It is not the effective date of the new exceptions.

3.3 Medicaid community engagement — §71119 (42 U.S.C. § 1396a(xx))

Heading: “Requirement for States to establish Medicaid community engagement requirements for certain individuals.”

Condition of eligibility. Beginning “not later than the first day of the first quarter that begins after December 31, 2026,” or earlier at State option, “a State shall provide, as a condition of eligibility for medical assistance for an applicable individual, that such individual is required to demonstrate community engagement.”

The first quarter that begins after 2026-12-31 starts 2027-01-01. That is the outside date. A State may go earlier.

Who. “Applicable individual” = a person eligible under the expansion group, 42 U.S.C. § 1396a(a)(10)(A)(i)(VIII), or under a waiver that is equivalent to minimum essential coverage, age 19–64, not pregnant, not on Medicare, and not otherwise eligible under the plan — other than a “specified excluded individual.”

What counts as community engagement for a month (Secretary sets criteria by regulation; one or more):

  • work ≥ 80 hours;
  • community service ≥ 80 hours;
  • a “work program” (cross-cite to SNAP 7 U.S.C. § 2015(o)(1)) ≥ 80 hours;
  • enrolled in an educational program at least half-time;
  • any combination of the four totaling ≥ 80 hours;
  • monthly income ≥ FLSA minimum wage × 80 hours;
  • seasonal worker with 6-month average income ≥ that same product.

Lookback. Applicants: 1 to 3 consecutive months (State specifies) immediately before the application month. Enrollees: 1 or more months (State specifies) between determinations.

Specified excluded individual includes, as the State determines under Secretarial standards: former foster youth; Indian / Urban Indian / California Indian / IHS-eligible; parent, guardian, caretaker relative, or RAISE family caregiver of a dependent child 13 and under or a disabled individual; veteran with a total disability rating; medically frail / special medical needs; TANF-compliant or SNAP household member not exempt from a SNAP work requirement; in a SNAP-defined drug/alcohol treatment program; inmate; pregnant or postpartum.

Process. Ex parte first. If unverified: notice, 30 calendar days to show compliance or show the person is not applicable, benefits continue during those 30 days if already enrolled, then deny or disenroll after checking any other eligibility basis, with written notice and a fair hearing.

Lock between doors. For IRC § 36B(c)(2)(B), a person “shall be deemed to be eligible for minimum essential coverage” for a month if the person would have had Medicaid but for community-engagement failure. A work-rule drop does not open the premium-tax-credit door.

No 1115 escape. House OLRC prints, text in effect August 14, 2026: “Prohibiting waiver of community engagement requirements. Notwithstanding section 1315(a) of this title, the provisions of this subsection may not be waived.”

Good-faith State delay. Secretary may exempt a State that shows a good-faith build. Hard stop: exemption “shall expire not later than December 31, 2028, and may not be renewed.”

Agency clock — retrieved. CMS-2454-IFC, 91 FR 33348 (June 3, 2026). Interim final rule with comment. Effective 2026-07-31. Comment close 2026-07-31. Implements § 1902(xx). States “are required to implement the new requirement no later than January 1, 2027.” Territories out. The preamble’s policy language is not the statute.

3.4 Expansion cost share — §71120

Beginning 2028-10-01, for a “specified individual” (expansion adult, or equivalent-waiver adult, with family income above the poverty line):

  • no premium / enrollment fee;
  • the State plan shall impose deductions, cost sharing, or similar charges greater than $0 on State-chosen items;
  • cannot hit primary care, mental health, SUD, FQHC, CCBHC, RHC, or the statutory protected services;
  • item cap $35;
  • family aggregate cap 5 percent of family income;
  • a participating provider may require the cost share as a condition of furnishing the service.

Clock is a fiscal-year trigger (FY2029 start), three years after the community-engagement outside date.

CHIP cross-applies. FY2027.

3.6 Retroactive coverage cut — §71112

Replaces 42 U.S.C. § 1396a(a)(34). Expansion adults: assistance only for care in or after the month before the application month. All others: in or after the second month before. (Prior law’s third-month lookback is gone.)

Applies to applications on or after 2027-01-01. Same outside date as community engagement.

3.7 Premium-tax-credit eligibility (not the fraud-map verification block) — §§71301–71302

§71301. IRC § 36B(e): a lawfully present alien is an “eligible alien” only if, for the whole enrollment period, the person is an LPR, a Cuban/Haitian entrant, or a COFA resident.

  • Tax-credit amendments: taxable years beginning after 2026-12-31.
  • Exchange/plan-year amendments: plan years beginning on or after 2027-01-01.

§71302. Strikes § 36B(c)(1)(B) (the old special rule for Medicaid-ineligible aliens). Taxable years beginning after 2025-12-31.

§§71303–71305 stay on the fraud map.

4. Fiscal-federalism clocks

4.1 Provider tax / hold-harmless — §71115

The Act does not repeal provider taxes. It rewrites the hold-harmless safe harbor.

For fiscal years beginning on or after 2026-10-01, an “applicable percent” is substituted for “6 percent.”

State class Tax on that class enacted and imposed on the date of enactment and then inside the hold-harmless threshold No such tax on that class on enactment
Non-expansion Frozen at the percent HHS has already determined 0 percent
Expansion Lower of (that determined percent) or the phase-down schedule 0 percent

Expansion-State phase-down:

Fiscal year Applicable percent
2028 5.5
2029 5
2030 4.5
2031 4
2032 and after 3.5

Nursing-facility / ICF-IID exception. If an expansion State’s tax on those classes was in effect on enactment and inside the threshold, that determined percent applies instead of the phase-down for that class.

“Expansion State” = a State that, beginning 2014-01-01 or later, elects to cover the (i)(VIII) group. Territories excluded.

Two clocks: determination as of enactment (2025-07-04) freezes the baseline; FY2028 is the first year the 5.5 percent cap bites expansion States.

CMS published a follow-on hold-harmless rule on 2026-07-23 (91 FR; “Amending the Indirect Hold Harmless Threshold”). That rule implements §71115. It does not rewrite the schedule.

§71117. Tightens “generally redistributive.” Effective upon enactment, subject to a Secretarial transition not to exceed 3 fiscal years.

4.2 State-directed payments — §71116

HHS shall revise 42 C.F.R. § 438.6 so that, for a service furnished in a rating period beginning on or after enactment, the total payment rate is limited to:

  • 100 percent of the published Medicare rate (or, if none, the State-plan rate) — expansion States with MEC-equivalent (i)(VIII) coverage;
  • 110 percent — all other States.

Grandfather. Payments with written prior approval (or a good-faith effort) before 2025-05-01, or rural-hospital payments with such approval by enactment, or a completed preprint submitted before enactment: beginning with the rating period on or after 2028-01-01, cut 10 percentage points each year until they hit the new cap.

A State that expands on or after enactment takes the 100 percent cap immediately. “State” = 50 + D.C.

4.3 Late-expansion FMAP and 1115 neutrality — §§71114, 71118

§71114. The increased-FMAP incentive now also requires that the State “begins to expend amounts for all such individuals prior to January 1, 2026.” A State that had not started expansion spend by that date does not get the incentive. That date is already past at this cutoff. The expansion group remains.

§71118. Beginning 2027-01-01, the Secretary may not approve, renew, or amend a Medicaid 1115 unless the CMS Chief Actuary certifies the project is not expected to increase federal expenditures against the absence of the project.

4.4 Rural Health Transformation — §71401

Appropriates to CMS, to allot to States: $10,000,000,000 for each of FY2026–FY2030 ($50 billion). Unexpended/unobligated as of 2032-10-01 returns to Treasury. Application window ended not later than 2025-12-31. Only the 50 States (D.C. out).

Allotment: 50 percent equal among approved States; 50 percent by CMS using rural-population and facility factors.

Hard wall against recycling into Medicaid match: the State must certify that none of the money will be used for an intergovernmental transfer, certified public expenditure, or any other spend that finances the non-federal share of CHIP, Medicaid, or a waiver.

This is a separate pot. It is not FMAP. It is not a provider-tax replacement. Official CMS allotment table not retrieved at cutoff.

6. Entity Theory read

Read the federal group the way the chart already does: control, not ownership.

Congress is the parent that amended the charters. HHS/CMS, USDA/FNS, and Treasury/IRS are operating subsidiaries. They received implementation money and rule deadlines. They did not receive ownership of State treasuries.

States are not subsidiaries. They are counterparties under cooperative-federalism statutes. The Act’s move is the accountant’s tell: keep the program person at the federal level (Food and Nutrition Act; Social Security Act titles XIX/XXI; Internal Revenue Code) and rewrite the control terms — eligibility condition, FMAP edge, hold-harmless percent, directed-payment cap, 1115 budget test — so that cash risk and casework sit on the State. That is a consolidation of control over the gate, not a merger of the State into HHS.

Three control instruments, three legal persons:

  1. Condition of eligibility (§71119, §10102, alien lists). The federal parent tells the State who may be on the roll. The State still opens the case. A managed-care vendor is barred from making the §71119 call if it has a money interest in the plan.
  2. Financing formula (§71115–71118, and on the other note §10105–10106 / §71106). Provider tax is a State-law instrument that manufactures federal match. The Act shrinks the safe harbor and the directed-payment wrap. The Rural Health pot is a new federal allotment inside CMS, walled off from becoming Medicaid match.
  3. The cross-door lock. §1396a(xx)’s MEC deeming means the Medicaid work gate and the 36B gate are not allowed to open for the same failure. That is group-level control of two subsidiaries (CMS and IRS) from one statute.

What this is not: a dissolution of CMS, FNS, or IRS; a new government corporation; a statutory unified identity utility. Those claims belong nowhere until a later instrument creates the person.

8. Open clocks

  1. State §71119 good-faith exemption filings and any Secretarial grants or early terminations. Hard stop 2028-12-31.
  2. Which States elect an earlier than 2027-01-01 §71119 start.
  3. USDA implementation of §10102 (handbooks, QC instructions, noncontiguous exemption grants). The Act itself is live as of enactment.
  4. HHS determinations under §71115 of which State/local taxes, by class, were inside the hold-harmless threshold on 2025-07-04. Those determinations are the frozen percents.
  5. §71117 transition length (0 to 3 fiscal years) as HHS actually writes it.
  6. §71116 regulation revision of 42 C.F.R. § 438.6 and the grandfather 10-point annual cut from rating periods on/after 2028-01-01.
  7. Rural Health §71401: official CMS allotment table not retrieved.
  8. Any later public law that amends these sections. This map is P.L. 119-21 plus the retrieved implementing rules.
  9. Court stays. None retrieved against these sections at cutoff. A complaint is not a stay.
