# Title 10 Guard and the Insurrection Act — Force Authorization Map

Updated: 2026-08-15  
Status: factual legal map  
Rule: A Title 10 call is not an Insurrection Act call. A denied stay is not a green light. The unused statute stays unused until a proclamation names it.

## Short answer

The President federalized Guard under **10 U.S.C. § 12406**. He did **not** invoke the Insurrection Act, **10 U.S.C. §§ 251–255**. No § 254 proclamation to disperse was retrieved.

*Trump v. Illinois*, No. 25A443 (Dec. 23, 2025), **denied** the Government’s stay. The unsigned order held that “regular forces” in § 12406(3) “likely refers to the regular forces of the United States military.” Because the President had not invoked a statute that excepts the Posse Comitatus Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1385, the Government had not carried its burden to show that § 12406(3) lets him federalize the Guard to protect federal personnel and property in Illinois. The Court did **not** decide the Insurrection Act. Justice Kavanaugh said so in footnote 2 of his concurrence.

Vault notes that say “SCOTUS greenlit the Insurrection Act” name a different object. The order leaves that statute unused and the Illinois deployment barred at the stay stage.

## 3. What 25A443 held

**Object:** *Trump v. Illinois*, No. 25A443 (Dec. 23, 2025), 607 U.S. ___. Stay application denied. No later merits entry on this docket was retrieved.

**Facts the Court recited.** Federal immigration enforcement met resistance in Chicago. On Oct. 4, 2025, the President called 300 members of the Illinois National Guard into active federal service to protect federal personnel and property. The next day, Texas National Guard members “were also federalized and sent to Chicago.” The statutory hook was **§ 12406(3)**. N.D. Ill. entered a TRO. The Seventh Circuit left them federalized but barred from deployment.

**Holding of the unsigned order:**

> We conclude that the term “regular forces” in §12406(3) likely refers to the regular forces of the United States military. … So before the President can federalize the Guard under §12406(3), he likely must have statutory or constitutional authority to execute the laws with the regular military and must be “unable” with those forces to perform that function.

> At this preliminary stage, the Government has failed to identify a source of authority that would allow the military to execute the laws in Illinois. The President has not invoked a statute that provides an exception to the Posse Comitatus Act.

If protective functions are *not* “executing the laws” under the PCA, “it is hard to see how performing those functions could constitute ‘execut[ing] the laws’ under §12406(3).” Same words, same meaning.

> We need not and do not address the reviewability of findings made by the President under §12406(3) or any other statute.

> The application for stay is denied.

That is the holding. Preliminary. Not a merits judgment. Not a ruling on chapter 13.

**Kavanaugh, J., concurring in the judgment.** Footnote 2:

> As I read it, the Court’s opinion does not address the President’s authority under the Insurrection Act. See 10 U. S. C. §§252, 253. Moreover, the Court’s opinion does not address or purport to disturb the President’s long-asserted Article II authority to use the U. S. military (as distinct from the National Guard) to protect federal personnel and property…

That is a concurrence, not a dissent. A footnote ramification is not a holding.

## 6. Entity Theory read

The Guard is one body with two charters. On Title 32 the governor holds the leash and the United States may pay. On Title 10 the President holds the leash and the members become federal soldiers under § 12405. Consolidation follows the leash, not the patch on the sleeve.

§ 12406 is the call-up. Chapter 13 is the PCA door. 25A443 refuses to let the call-up impersonate the door. The January 20 border proclamation even writes the difference: report “whether to invoke.” That sentence would be surplus if the Act were already on.

Four clocks: statutory existence (all three statutes still on the books) · command (who gives the order) · function (protecting ICE is not the same as executing the immigration laws — the Court’s same-words reading) · pay-and-control pot (Title 32 vs Title 10). A press reading of “green light” is not a rule. The rule is the last line of the unsigned order.
