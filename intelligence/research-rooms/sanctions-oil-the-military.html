Treasury moves to cut a UAE bank off from U.S. accounts over Iran

Under Operation Economic Outcast, FinCEN proposed, but has not finalized, a rule that would bar U.S. banks from correspondent accounts for Banque Misr's UAE branches, and OFAC named new sanctions targets. Iran's domestic banking system has not been shown to be shut down.

What we found

  • On August 28, 2026 FinCEN proposed Section 311 Special Measure Five against Banque Misr's UAE branches. If finalized, covered U.S. institutions could not open or keep correspondent accounts for that bank.
  • The same day OFAC separately designated one person and one company. Those designations are in effect; the FinCEN action is only a proposal.
  • On August 24 Treasury widened the sectoral designation perimeter, made new immediate designations and suspended five authorization channels.
  • The pressure targets Iran's outside money links: foreign correspondent accounts, shell firms, exchange houses, oil-revenue conversion, and crypto exchanges and wallets. Iran's domestic card, clearing and payment systems were not shown to fail.
  • Not established: a final ban, any closed account or failed payment, a new blanket SWIFT cutoff, a named major Iranian bank failing, asset seizures, or any Federal Reserve or IMF change.

Why it matters

Financial pressure is being applied through existing U.S. tools (Section 311, OFAC listings, licenses) that work by cutting access to dollar plumbing, not by passing new law.

What to watch

  • pending · A final Section 311 rule on Banque Misr UAE.
  • pending · Hard evidence of closed accounts, rejected payments or bank failures inside Iran.

State's Cuba report builds a case but creates no new power

The State Department's 100-page Cuba report of July 20, 2026 pulls Cuban spying, a Cuban state body (ICAP), U.S. activist groups and foreign ties into one official threat story. It adds no new legal authority and orders no military action, but gives a public reason for sanctions and enforcement under orders already in place.

What we found

  • The report is unsigned, from the Bureau of Western Hemisphere Affairs, with no stated method or evidence standard. It has 497 links to 339 web pages but only 25 numbered notes.
  • Its strongest support is past Cuban spy cases, such as Victor Manuel Rocha and Ana Belén Montes, and ICAP's status as a Cuban state body. The U.S. designated ICAP on June 4, 2026, and its president was convicted in the Wasp Network case.
  • The claim that about 90% of ICAP staff are tied to intelligence rests on defector accounts and is not shown in the report.
  • It names U.S. groups including the National Lawyers Guild, Code Pink, DSA and Pastors for Peace, and documents real travel, meetings and money ties, but does not show each is controlled by Cuba. The report itself says DSA is not controlled by Cuban agents 'per se.'
  • It gives a public rationale for powers already created under EO 14380 and EO 14404 and already used against Cuban military, energy and economic targets.

Why it matters

This shows enforcement being widened through an official report that recasts domestic groups as part of a foreign threat, using sanctions and investigative powers that already exist. Any next step still needs its own legal action and evidence.

What to watch

  • pending · Any follow-on step aimed at named U.S. groups (sanctions, visa actions, foreign-agent cases or funding reviews), each of which needs its own document.
  • pending · Any formal military order on Cuba, as distinct from reviews of options or speeches.

Oil and gas tightness is selective, and many deals are not yet flows

The Middle East war has made Hormuz, the Red Sea and bypass routes the main supply limit. Diesel is truly tight, but U.S. crude is not, and many announced deals (like the Alaska LNG-Korea plan) have not yet become real shipments.

What we found

  • In June 2026 the U.S. exported 4.735 million barrels a day of crude and 7.805 million of petroleum products; first-half LNG exports averaged 17.4 Bcf a day. The U.S. is a major exporter with or without Venezuela.
  • Ten U.S. diesel export lanes made up about 0.953 million barrels a day in June, 66.5% of distillate exports, led by Mexico, Brazil and the Netherlands. Most go out from the Gulf Coast.
  • Venezuela produces about 1.15-1.20 million barrels a day, and the latest early U.S. receipt figure was about 0.60 million. OFAC permissions, U.S.-controlled payment accounts and Chevron and Eni operating agreements now exist.
  • The roughly $54 billion Alaska LNG-Korea announcement is at project-selection stage only. Korea said participation was not final, and there is no final investment decision, construction or gas flow.
  • Lifting Russia sanctions would first cut shipping and payment friction and reroute crude already selling. It would not repair damaged Russian refineries, and the production-to-capacity gap is about 0.94 million barrels a day.

Why it matters

It shows that energy policy changes travel through permits, licenses, payment accounts and contracts before oil actually moves. Announcements and permissions are steps in that machinery, not delivered supply.

What to watch

  • pending · Korea's formal decision on Alaska LNG participation, financing and a final investment decision.
  • pending · Named PetroChina-Sabine Pass LNG cargos tied to the China trade framework.
  • pending · Recovery of Gulf crude and product trade toward pre-war levels in IEA and EIA data.

Tracing Iraqi oil: what is proven and what is only alleged

Iraqi oil moves through five separate records (physical, title, identity, permission and payment). Public records prove the lawful routes and the spot where they meet the alleged Iran-blending network, but not the cargo-by-cargo chain Treasury describes.

What we found

  • SOMO, Iraq's sole authorized oil marketer, reports Qayarah crude loaded from floating storage (VS87, ST1) near Khor al-Zubayr, for example 495,645 barrels on a July 29, 2025 bill of lading and 971,130 barrels on Feb 4, 2026.
  • OFAC lists sanctioned VS Oil Terminal FZE at Khor al-Zubayr, Jetty 41, and Treasury alleges its tanks and nearby ship-to-ship transfers mixed Iranian and Iraqi oil. In May 2026 Treasury alleged Ali Maarij authorized trucking Qayarah oil to VS Oil. These are official allegations; the manifests, tests and bank records have not been published.
  • At the same Berth 41, SOMO tendered Iraqi-origin fuel oil (posted June 14, 2026) and announced on June 18 a transaction-specific OFAC license to sell to non-sanctioned buyers. The same site carries both lawful and allegedly illicit traffic.
  • Lawful oil payments go to the Iraq 2 account at the New York Fed, managed by Iraq's central bank for the Finance Ministry, but no public record ties any single cargo to it. The 2021 $9.59 billion gap between SOMO and central-bank figures is an unreconciled accounting gap, not proof of theft.
  • The sanctioned tanker DIJILAH still has a current Gard P&I insurance entry (Feb 2026 to Feb 2027), and Treasury does not name which vessel met CASINOVA four times in April 2024.

Why it matters

It shows U.S. control over oil money working through existing tools (OFAC designations and licenses and the New York Fed account) rather than new institutions. It also shows how far sanctions claims can run ahead of the documents.

What to watch

  • pending · The full terms of OFAC license IRAN-EO13902-2026-1448903-1, plus the winning bidder and actual loadings for the June 2026 Berth 41 sale.
  • pending · Qayarah road-tanker awards and truck manifests that could confirm or rule out the alleged diversion.
  • pending · Any cargo-level link from SOMO invoice to buyer payment to an Iraq 2 credit.

The military is reorganizing around shared data, with named vendors at the center

The Pentagon is rebuilding around one chain: sensors, networks, shared data, decisions, action and supply. The strongest evidence is new organizations and contracts, not new weapons, and several big September 2026 moves are still only studies or reviews.

What we found

  • On June 22, 2026 the Army announced a common data baseline for its Next Generation Command and Control program, led by Anduril, with Palantir and Raft. The Navy created a central integrator office called PAE Mission Systems.
  • The Space Development Agency awarded 36 more tracking satellites on July 13, 2026, linking Golden Dome missile defense to its satellite network. The 2026 AI strategy aims AI agents at battle management and 'kill chain execution', and wants commercial models deployable within 30 days of public release.
  • Not everything moves forward: the Space Force accepted the GPS OCX ground system in 2025, then cancelled it in April 2026 after testing found operational risk.
  • A review team (FAST) was set up by a memo signed August 4, 2026, released September 25, to recommend cutting or merging programs. On September 30 the Secretary created an Office of Religious Affairs with 60- and 90-day budget and charter clocks. No director, target list or savings has been named for either.
  • Also on September 30, Project Meridian was commissioned as a 120-day future-warfare study, with Elon Musk, Palmer Luckey and Newt Gingrich named as co-directors. No partner organization, contract, program or fielded system exists yet.

Why it matters

Defense is being rebuilt through its contracts, data rules and reporting lines, with private firms inside the system. The study notes vendors supply parts but do not own the mission, the data or targeting authority.

What to watch

  • 2027-01-28 · Project Meridian's report gate; a public unclassified report and a classified annex are intended.
  • pending · The Office of Religious Affairs' 60-day budget plan and 90-day charter and staffing steps, counted from September 30, 2026.
  • pending · The first FAST recommendation or named program cut.

A new religious-affairs office reporting directly to the Secretary

The September 30 memorandum creates a Secretary-level Office of Religious Affairs with policy, budget-advocacy, research and Armed Forces Chaplains Board powers, but its Director, transferred authorities, budget, staff, programs and practical effects remain separate future receipts.

What we found

  • The office and Director position are effective immediately. The Director is a Principal Staff Assistant, principal adviser to the Secretary and Deputy Secretary, and reports to them without intervening authority.
  • The Director may identify and assume necessary duties and authorities, issue religious-affairs policy on the Secretary's behalf, represent the Secretary externally and control the Armed Forces Chaplains Board and its Executive Director.
  • The memorandum starts a 60-day initial-budget and FY2027 funding-plan clock and 90-day clocks for a chartering directive, organization, initial billets, permanent manning requests and comprehensive military-department requirements reports.
  • The resource mandate reaches beyond traditional chapel support into faith-based homeschooling, youth programs, suicide-reduction and resilience initiatives, contracted faith-based services, and research or analytical tools for spiritual fitness and readiness.
  • The memorandum bars compelled religious participation and diminished free-exercise rights for people of any faith or no faith; the Department release also promises no faith preference. Those safeguards still have to be tested against actual staffing, policy, funding, data collection and command practice.

Why it matters

This is a live example of government being reconstructed through internal organization, delegated authority, budget machinery and policy control. The important question is not merely that a new office exists, but what powers, money, personnel and standards move into it and how those choices affect service members and families.

What to watch

  • 2026-11-29 calculated 60-day gate · The initial baseline budget and FY2027 funding plan, plus any separate Secretary approval, transfer or execution record.
  • 2026-12-29 calculated 90-day gate · The ORA chartering directive, organizational placement, initial billets, permanent manning request and each military department's requirements report.
  • source-triggered · A named Director, exact transfer of duties, authorities or funding, first ORA-issued policy, research or assessment tool, family-support program, contract, complaint, audit or court record.

The military moves on commercial ships, rail and fuel, and now aims for off-grid bases

U.S. wartime movement relies on private carriers, ports, rail and fuel companies held ready by contracts and activation deals. On September 30, 2026 the Department set up FORTRESS America, aiming for independent power at every major base, but which sites, how much power, the funding and the tests are still undefined.

What we found

  • USTRANSCOM calls commercial air carriers, sealift carriers and logistics firms its 'Fourth Component.' The Civil Reserve Air Fleet, Maritime Security Program and Tanker Security Program keep commercial capacity on retainer for a surge. This is not nationalization.
  • The Army's June 2026 energy request for proposals asks private partners to finance, build and run power plants on seven installations. They sell to the grid normally but must power the base for at least 14 days after an outage. The September 18 selections are conditional lease awards, not built plants.
  • FORTRESS America is a five-pillar program. The announcement sets no covered sites, load, endurance, funding, test standard or completion date. The existing legal standard is 10 U.S.C. § 2920's FY2030 critical-mission availability goal.
  • The Army's April 2026 industrial-base strategy treats its 23 depots, arsenals and ammunition plants as one system. The public record does not confirm the goal of 100,000 complete 155 mm shots per month has been met.
  • USTRANSCOM's Joint Transportation Management System aims to join movement and payment data for more than 100,000 users, with full capability targeted for FY2030. The February 2026 Maritime Action Plan proposes a Strategic Commercial Fleet and a Maritime Security Trust Fund; these are proposals, not law.

Why it matters

The military is gaining capacity by contracting, leasing its land and writing priority rights into private networks rather than owning everything. That fits a rebuild done through existing law, leases and payment systems, though most pieces are still solicitations or plans.

What to watch

  • FY2030 · 10 U.S.C. § 2920 critical-mission energy availability standard and full JTMS user capability.
  • pending · FORTRESS America naming covered sites, load and endurance standards, funding and tests.
  • pending · Army energy leases closing, plants built and islanding tests passed.

Independent power for major military installations

FORTRESS America makes installation power a central military program, but it does not yet say which sites count, what loads must run, how long they must run or who will pay and operate the systems.

What we found

  • The September 30 announcement creates a five-pillar program and sets independent power for every major U.S. military installation as the energy objective; it does not establish that every base is covered or already self-sufficient.
  • The biggest legal and technical mismatch is scope: FORTRESS says major installations with an undefined load, while 10 U.S.C. §2920 covers each installation but only the energy required for critical missions.
  • Existing delivery tools are already substantial: installation energy plans, ERCIP military construction, PPAs, enhanced-use leases, performance contracts, utility agreements, microgrids and black-start exercises.
  • Army SCI, Janus, Eielson and Project Pele are still at solicitation, conditional selection, negotiation, licensing or prototype stages. Operating microgrids at Yuma, Miramar, New London, Fort McCoy and Sigonella have different load and duration boundaries and cannot be counted as one uniform capability.
  • The program becomes operationally meaningful when it publishes a covered-site list, load and duration standards, resources and comparable black-start, islanding and endurance results.

Why it matters

The military is trying to turn its domestic basing network from a collection of utility customers and local resilience projects into a coordinated power-and-survival portfolio. If implemented, FORTRESS could join mission priorities, private capital, microgrids, reactors, cyber controls and recurring readiness tests under one command layer.

What to watch

  • 2026-10-30 or earlier on publication · Program-office establishment, named leadership, charter, first implementation guidance and assignment of existing installation-energy programs.
  • source-triggered · A binding definition of major installation and independent power, covered-site list, critical/full-load denominator, outage duration, fuel and test standard.
  • budget or transaction trigger · FYDP/budget line, appropriation, transfer, obligation, executed lease or PPA, financing close, licence, interconnection or construction award.
  • 2028-09-30 and end FY2030 · First directed domestic-installation reactor operation and evidence that §2920 critical-mission availability requirements are actually met.

Who can watch, use force, freeze and spend: a map of the legal levers

A set of maps that keeps apart a legal power, an issued order, a court ruling and what is actually being done, across surveillance, domestic force, war powers, emergency economic powers and how far court orders reach.

What we found

  • Surveillance: the study records FISA Title VII (which holds Section 702) as repealed on June 12, 2026, and treats leftover approvals as a separate matter. The 2026 surveillance court certification was not retrieved.
  • Domestic force: the Guard was called up under 10 U.S.C. section 12406; the Insurrection Act has not been used; the Supreme Court denied a stay in case 25A443.
  • War powers on Iran: there is no enacted authorization for use of force. H.Con.Res.86 is a concurrent resolution, not a law, and the 60-day War Powers clock was not computed.
  • Emergency economic powers: IEEPA does not authorize tariffs, but its power to block property remains. EO 14389 ended the duties, not the emergencies, and Proclamation 11012 rests on a different law, Trade Act section 122.
  • Courts and officers: Trump v. CASA limited nationwide relief as a procedure question. A related map finds FTC commissioners' for-cause protection falling while a Fed governor's protection stands on a stay. Work paused after the sixth batch on August 15, 2026.

Why it matters

These maps track who holds control by reading the binding documents. Much of the change runs through existing statutes, executive orders and court remedies rather than new laws, which is the procedural route the thesis describes.

What to watch

  • pending · Retrieval of the 2026 surveillance court certification and the status of any leftover Section 702 approvals.
  • pending · Whether a 60-day War Powers clock is triggered for Iran actions, and any vote on an authorization.
  • pending · Any use of the Insurrection Act.
