Freedom 250 · Banking and the dollar · Insurance

Who insures exporters and overseas investors against nonpayment or seizure?

Four kinds of risk bearer, in the study's map. For exporters: a private trade-credit insurer, which insures commercial or political nonpayment within limits and conditions, and EXIM export-credit insurance, which covers a stated share of eligible foreign-receivable nonpayment. For overseas investors: DFC political-risk insurance, which covers selected inconvertibility, expropriation, violence and contract risks, and the World Bank Group's MIGA guarantee and risk-sharing facility, which covers qualifying cross-border financing and may share first loss with donor or IDA resources. Export-credit insurance protects an exporter or lender against defined nonpayment. Political-risk insurance protects a qualifying investment or financing against named government or conflict risks. Neither makes the buyer's debt disappear, guarantees a country's currency value, covers physical cargo loss by default, or authorizes a transaction prohibited by sanctions.

What's still uncertain

  • A country-open determination is not transaction approval.
  • A policy is not proof of shipment, investment or default.
  • Political-risk coverage is not a general guarantee of profitability or exchange value.
  • Sanctions can prevent issue or payment even when commercial policy language otherwise responds.
  • Public authorization, claim determination, payment and recovery remain separate sovereign-accounting events.
