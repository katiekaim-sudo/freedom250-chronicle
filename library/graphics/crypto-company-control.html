Freedom 250 · Digital money · Companies · Who controls what

What does each crypto company control, where can it stop a payment, and how does it make money?

Each controls a different step around the coin, not just the coin. Issuers (Circle, Ripple's Standard Custody, Tether, Paxos, Agora) decide who can mint and redeem, and Tether, Standard Custody and Agora can also freeze tokens; Coinbase and Stripe control listings, accounts and which payments qualify; Anchorage's bank can refuse minting or redemption and freeze or seize tokens under its product terms, while Fireblocks' transfer rules are set by its customers. Issuers earn mostly reserve income (Circle: $652.5 million in the first quarter of 2026, with $405.4 million of distribution and transaction costs); distributors and service firms earn fees.

What's still uncertain

  • The stablecoin competition is not one coin against another. Companies are racing to control the steps around the coin: legal liability to holders, reserves, ledgers, keys, risk, conversion to regular money and reaching customers.
  • Several statuses are the companies' own figures or plans ('figures are self-reported', 'some only announced'); each card says which.
  • The cards date from July 28, 2026; newer charter and product steps for some of the same firms are on the charters, issuers and freeze pages.
