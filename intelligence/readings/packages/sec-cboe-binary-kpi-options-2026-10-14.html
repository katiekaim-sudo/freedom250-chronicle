# SEC approves Cboe options on company performance figures

**Answer.** On October 13, 2026, the SEC approved Cboe Exchange's amended rule (SR-CBOE-2026-061) to list standardized binary options on specified company key performance indicators. The SEC also designated these contracts as standardized options under Rule 9b-1. This is an exchange-rule approval, not a first listing or trading receipt. Cboe says it will not list the contracts until the necessary filings for Cboe Clear U.S. (CCUS) are approved or effective. CCUS's clearing-agency application remained a separate proceeding in the official record checked on October 14.

## What a contract would do

A contract tests a named issuer-reported financial or operating measure against a stated strike. The ordinary payout is **$1 or $0**, regardless of how far the measure beats or misses the strike. A call pays $1 if the reported figure is **at or above** the strike; a put pays $1 if it is **below**. The option is European-style and cash settled. Cboe uses the figure in the issuer's earnings-related **Form 8-K, 10-Q or 10-K submitted to the SEC** for the relevant period, rather than a share price or an earnings press release. If that figure is unavailable at expiration, CCUS's alternative settlement rules would apply. The amended position limit is **25,000 standard-option equivalents per KPI class and expiration**: 100 binary contracts count as one standard contract, so the raw limit can equal **2.5 million $1 binary contracts**. Registered market-makers have a narrow offsetting short-call/short-put exemption. See [SEC order pp. 30–32, 96–100](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2026/34-106659.pdf).

The SEC found these specific contracts could be listed as securities options because their issuer KPIs bear on the value of the issuer's stock. The order applies the securities-exchange and standardized-options framework, including account, margin, halt, market-maker, trading and disclosure rules. It **does not** decide that every contract on a company metric is an SEC product. The SEC expressly left room for differently structured contracts on the same KPI to fall under CFTC jurisdiction.

## Why it matters to the existing story

This adds a **securities-exchange route for company-event exposure** beside the CFTC event-contract route. The same reported fact can be an underlying for products with different legal forms, venues, clearers and customer rules. The distinction tests the existing SEC–CFTC authority map rather than resolving the whole prediction-market jurisdiction dispute. It also shows why an exchange approval must be separated from clearing permission and actual operation.

The prior SEC–CFTC authority ledger maps separate present-law powers; the CFTC passive-software reading concerns distribution of CFTC-regulated event contracts through outside apps, not permission for this Cboe security option. The October 3–12 financial-control sweep ended before this SEC order. The October 13 action advances the SEC side from filing to approval, while clearing and first-use remain open.

## Deep read — October 14 return

### The issuer's filing becomes a settlement source

The approved Rule 4.81 names **23 issuers and their individual eligible KPIs**. The list crosses technology, banks, auto, retail, media and crypto-facing firms. Examples include Apple's iPhone and Greater China sales, Nvidia's data-center revenue, JPMorgan's provision for credit losses, Coinbase's trading volume, Marathon Digital's bitcoin holdings and production, Tesla's Model 3/Y production, and Robinhood's Gold subscribers. These are *eligible underliers*, not a statement that every named series has been listed. SpaceX appears as a listed-stock issuer in the 2026 filing; the SEC order says its stock began trading in June 2026. [SEC order pp. 7–11 and 53](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2026/34-106659.pdf).

This is an official-record design choice. The issuer controls its reported measure and disclosure timing; Cboe chooses which eligible KPI, reporting period, strike and series to list; the issuer's SEC filing supplies the final settlement figure; CCUS would turn that figure into cash obligations. A press release may set the announced earnings date and first public information, but the submitted 8-K/10-Q/10-K controls the settlement number. The *disclosure date*, *SEC-filing date*, *last trading day* and *cash settlement* are distinct clocks. [SEC order pp. 5–6, 13–16 and 25–28](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2026/34-106659.pdf).

The expiration date can start as a placeholder and be finalized after the issuer announces an earnings date. Cboe can list up to two reporting-period expirations at once and as far as 12 months ahead, with the first series opened at least one business week before expiration. The permitted trading session is 9:30 a.m.–4:00 p.m. Eastern. For a company that reports before the open, the last trading day is the prior trading day; for one that reports after the close, it is the expiration day. A confirmed early report stops trading and can accelerate expiration. A late report ends trading on schedule but delays settlement; an unofficial leak can prompt a halt. A halt in the issuer's **stock** does not automatically halt its KPI option. [SEC order pp. 13–16, 26–28 and 33](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2026/34-106659.pdf).

The failure rule is consequential. A **late but expected filing** can delay settlement until the KPI is available. If the KPI ultimately will not be reported, CCUS's proposed alternative process may settle at the option's last reliable closing price, such as $0.76, instead of $0 or $1. If CCUS finds no reliable closing price, the exercise criterion is deemed unmet and the contract can expire worthless. If the issuer later restates a reported KPI **after settlement**, the contract's payout is not reopened. This gives the first official filing finality for the option even when the company's accounting record is later corrected. [SEC order pp. 5–7 and 27–28](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2026/34-106659.pdf).

### Trading rules and risk

The contracts would trade electronically as standardized securities options, including complex orders and specified auctions. They would feed quotes and trades into OPRA and report required order data to CAT. Cboe and FINRA would apply options surveillance, including insider-trading checks adapted to the KPI product. Customer positions must be fully funded under the exchange margin rule. The exchange's ordinary obvious-error cancellation rule **does not apply**; Cboe substitutes a class-level maximum execution-price protection. The example cap is $1.03, so a buyer could pay more than the $1 maximum ordinary payout once costs are included. [SEC order pp. 28–38 and 84–101](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2026/34-106659.pdf).

The 25,000 standard-contract-equivalent limit is per KPI class and expiration, with **no aggregation** across different KPIs for the same issuer, different expirations of a KPI, or ordinary options on that issuer's stock. The SEC accepted this because the exposures differ, but it means one firm can carry separate large positions on several correlated measures of one company. The registered-market-maker exemption covers paired short puts and calls and reflects Cboe's expectation that retail customers will mainly buy these options. Position reports and CAT/FINRA surveillance matter more when the raw unit is only $1. [SEC order pp. 30–33 and 96–101](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2026/34-106659.pdf).

Cboe also chooses the **strike ladder** before the result is known. The amended rule lets it define the at-the-money reference using the last reported KPI, the issuer's estimate for the coming period, or analysts' consensus reported by major data vendors, as it deems appropriate. These forecasts can shape which thresholds are offered; they **do not replace the issuer's SEC filing as the settlement source**. This is a separate place where Cboe and data vendors influence the market even though the issuer controls the final reported result. [SEC order pp. 22–24 and 93–95](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2026/34-106659.pdf).

### The clearinghouse is the decisive second proceeding

CCUS is a CFTC-registered DCO inside the Cboe group, but securities-option clearing needs **SEC Section 17A registration**. Cboe says it will not list until the applicable CCUS filings are approved or effective, and it would then file a further exchange rule change naming CCUS as the initial clearer. CCUS requested temporary registration; the original July notice says such relief can last 18 months. The October amendment adds $25 million of contributed equity, parent-control constraints, appeal procedures, direct institutional clearing-member details and a withdrawal of its requested Regulation SCI exemption. Read the **October amendment** before relying on the July application for current membership or governance terms. [SEC exchange order pp. 38–40, 103–06](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2026/34-106659.pdf); [CCUS July application notice](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/other/2026/34-105960.pdf); [October amendment notice](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/other/2026/34-106610.pdf).

The product may be *fully margined after initial settlement* without every margin dollar being **prefunded before novation**. CCUS acknowledged an intraday exposure between accepting a trade and its daily settlement cycle. Its proposed controls include intraday calls, a guaranty fund, contributed capital, member assessments and possible tear-ups. Thus bounded $1 payoff reduces market exposure, but does not erase clearing, timing, member, operational or customer-property risk. [CCUS July application notice pp. 7–11](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/other/2026/34-105960.pdf).

A second application, **File 4-930**, asks the SEC to let CCUS broker-dealer members count customer margin held at CCUS in the Rule 15c3-3 reserve formula the way margin at OCC is counted. The existing text specifically names OCC, which has been the only SEC-registered securities-options clearer. The request is a **notice, not an exemption order**; comments are due October 20. It is a concrete test of whether a second private securities-options clearinghouse can fit the customer-protection rules. [SEC Release 34-106493](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/other/2026/34-106493.pdf).

### The SEC–CFTC line is product-specific and contested

The SEC reasons that these are options with standardized puts, calls, strikes, expirations and premium-paid buyer rights, and that their issuer KPIs affect the value of the issuer's listed stock. It also reasons that a KPI can be tied to the shareholder's pecuniary interest in that stock. **Kalshi disputed** using the 2007 credit-default-options precedent for this post-Dodd-Frank product and urged the SEC to wait for its joint product-definition consultation with the CFTC and for the clearing work. The SEC rejected delay but said its ruling is **nonexclusive**: the same KPI can support a differently structured product under CFTC jurisdiction. This is a case-specific securities route, not a transfer of the whole prediction-market field. [SEC order pp. 105–13](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2026/34-106659.pdf); [Kalshi comment, August 5](https://www.sec.gov/comments/SR-CBOE-2026-061/srcboe2026061-993079-3105906.pdf).

NYSE American filed a **separate proposed** binary-KPI-options rule in September; the Cboe order does not approve it. Robinhood was announced as the first retail broker for Cboe's planned October rollout. Robinhood is also among the issuers whose own KPI could underlie contracts (including Gold subscribers); that dual role is an entity-map and conflict-review question, not evidence of misconduct. [NYSE American notice](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/self-regulatory-organization-rulemaking/sr-nyseamer-2026-82); [Cboe/Robinhood announcement](https://ir.cboe.com/news/news-details/2026/Cboe-and-Robinhood-Announce-Planned-Launch-of-New-KPI-Contracts-Further-Expanding-Prediction-Market-Offerings/default.aspx); [SEC order pp. 7–11](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2026/34-106659.pdf).

### Observatory placement and exact return gates

1. **SEC & CFTC regulatory turn:** one approved SEC exchange rule beside the CFTC event-contract lane; no general jurisdiction victory and no new spot-crypto authority. The saved authority ledger is the closest constitutional owner.
2. **Clearing-House Transition:** CCUS seeks to become a second SEC securities-options clearer, with a separate customer-margin reserve request and inherited OPRA/OLPP language built around OCC. The factual timeline and Coinbase DCO reading supply comparisons; no common clearing permission follows from either one.
3. **Official-record authority:** a company-reported KPI in an SEC filing, not stock price or a social claim, becomes the cash-settlement input. Delays, missing fields and restatements expose who can freeze a value and when.
4. **Retail access and market structure:** Cboe venue, CCUS clearing, Robinhood brokerage, issuer reporting, OPRA distribution and CAT/FINRA surveillance are different legal and operating seats. A planned bundle is not one merged institution.

The next decisive objects are: **CCUS registration**, **File 4-930 disposition**, **Cboe's post-clearer rule**, **accepted options disclosure document**, **actual eligible series and broker screen**, **first trade/open-interest record**, and **first settlement**, especially a delayed or restated KPI case. A Cboe day-one list is a product plan until these gates are independently shown.

## Further read — the market design behind the order

### The clearing fight is about the future of listed options

OCC already received SEC approval in May 2026 to modify its risk model to accept binary options. Cboe nevertheless proposes to use its affiliate CCUS initially for these KPI options. The current options market's shared plans and broker reserve formula repeatedly name OCC, so adding CCUS is a **market-structure change**, not merely choosing a back-office vendor. The separate CCUS registration and customer-margin proceedings still have to be decided. [OCC binary-risk-model approval](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/occ/2026/34-105537.pdf); [Cboe order pp. 38–40](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2026/34-106659.pdf); [CCUS margin request](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/other/2026/34-106493.pdf).

PTG, a principal-trading-firm association, **supports temporary CCUS registration as a bridge** but objects to making exchange-owned clearing permanent. Its stated concern is that separately cleared versions of similar products could split liquidity and prevent firms from netting offsetting risks across OCC and CCUS, increasing collateral needs. This is a stakeholder argument, not an SEC finding or proof that fragmentation has occurred. Cboe's order anticipates possible later OCC clearing and commits to work on listing-plan changes if OCC files to clear these contracts. The decisive later evidence would be OCC product admission, cross-venue fungibility, clearing-member costs, spreads and actual volume. [PTG comment, September 8](https://www.sec.gov/comments/600-47/60047-1031599-3428812.pdf); [SEC order pp. 39–40, 103–05](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2026/34-106659.pdf).

The default design deserves its own lens. CCUS calls the options fully margined **once trades initially settle**, but says it does not require every margin amount to be prefunded before accepting a trade, leaving an intraday timing gap. Its proposed waterfall uses defaulting-member margin and guaranty contribution, Cboe-contributed capital, nondefaulting members' guaranty resources and possible assessments. CCUS also describes partial or full contract tear-ups if ordinary default management fails. These are proposed recovery powers and possible loss-allocation paths, not evidence of a loss or a live securities-clearing service. The October amended application cites **$25 million of contributed equity** and withdrew a requested Regulation SCI exemption. [CCUS July application notice pp. 7–11](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/other/2026/34-105960.pdf); [October amendment](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/other/2026/34-106610.pdf); [CCUS PFMI disclosure filed with the SEC](https://www.sec.gov/files/ccus-ca-1-exhibit-j-2-amend-1.pdf).

### This is already a race among product designs

MEMX filed a separate **proposed** securities-event-contract rule, which the SEC extended for further review on October 7. It uses paired, fully paid **yes/no** contracts: a 60-cent yes bid and 40-cent no bid meet to fund one $1 payout. MEMX expressly distinguishes this from Cboe's separately bought and sold binary calls and puts, arguing that one paired market would make the two outcomes easier to compare. NYSE American separately proposed binary KPI options and could designate OCC or another registered clearer. Neither proposal was approved by the Cboe order. This is evidence of competing legal and market designs, not a single SEC permission for every event contract. [MEMX notice and design](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/memx/2026/34-106182.pdf); [MEMX extension](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/memx/2026/34-106626.pdf); [NYSE American notice](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nyseamer/2026/34-106367.pdf).

### Where Cboe says it wants to go

Cboe and Robinhood said they intend to waive their own fees on these contracts through 2026, subject to regulatory review. That does not remove spreads or every clearing cost; CCUS's application listed a **$0.0002 per-contract** clearing fee for its members. Cboe also said it may later seek approval to clear **tokenized binary security options**. This is a corporate aspiration following the initial registration, not a function approved by the October 13 order, a chosen chain, a tokenized claim or an operating market. The present product is an ordinary exchange-listed, cash-settled securities option. [Cboe/Robinhood September 30 announcement](https://ir.cboe.com/news/news-details/2026/Cboe-and-Robinhood-Announce-Planned-Launch-of-New-KPI-Contracts-Further-Expanding-Prediction-Market-Offerings/default.aspx); [CCUS application notice p. 12](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/other/2026/34-105960.pdf).
