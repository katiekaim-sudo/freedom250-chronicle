The War Footing — Lead Story

Locked and Loaded — Then Held

The President says the United States was ready to strike Iran 'at levels not seen since World War II,' then cancels the attack for a deal: the Strait of Hormuz reopened, the nuclear program ended. Treasury says an agreement could come within a day.

Over a single weekend the Iran confrontation swung from the brink of force to the edge of a bargain. The U.S.A. is 'locked and loaded and ready to go against the Islamic Republic of Iran,' the President wrote, 'at levels of Military Terror, Strength, and Power not seen since World War II' — only to add that Iran and other Middle Eastern countries had asked him to hold off, the perimeters of a deal having been agreed. He would cancel the attack, he said, subject to a rapid agreement that delivered the 'Immediate, Complete, and Total OPENING OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT, and an end to Iran's nuclear threat,' with Israel joining the commitment. By Monday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was telling markets a deal 'could be announced as soon as tomorrow' — and Dow futures jumped more than 600 points on the word.
