How insurers invest trillions, move risk and share losses with government

U.S. insurance runs through many separate regulators, very large insurer balance sheets, reinsurance and public loss programs that together work as one system. Each authority, company, contract and payment still keeps its own legal identity and timing.

What we found

  • The United States has no single insurance ministry. State commissioners are the main supervisors, NAIC coordinates model laws and accounting, federal bodies such as FIO, FSOC and the Federal Reserve have limited roles, and IAIS adds international standards.
  • FIO's year-end 2025 tables put life-sector assets at $9.902 trillion and property/casualty assets at $3.223 trillion. Insurers are major buyers of bonds, mortgages, structured assets and private credit, so their investment choices reach governments, companies and housing.
  • At year-end 2025 the life sector was profitable and cash-flow positive alongside $580.5 billion of ceded premium, an estimated $1.210 trillion of insurer private credit and about $322 billion of life affiliated investments. These are different measures and do not show a present solvency break.
  • Public loss programs such as TRIP, NFIP, state residual plans and catastrophe funds, guaranty systems and PBGC each pay only after their own named triggers; none is a generic bailout. GAO reported in April 2026 that Treasury and CISA had not completed the requested joint assessment of a broader federal response to catastrophic cyber losses.
  • Several 2026 changes are in effect: revised cash-flow ASOPs took effect June 1, VM-22 began for covered new non-variable annuities, and AG 55 entered its first review cycle. Catastrophe-model and life-reinsurance-pricing revisions were still proposals at the October 1 cutoff.

Why it matters

Insurers turn long-term promises into large demand for bonds, mortgages and private credit, and they move defined risks across companies and borders. Public money enters only through specific programs with named triggers, so the retail policy is the legal starting point, not the whole system.

What to watch

  • 2026-10-09 · What survives the NAIC comment process for year-end 2026 AG 55 templates and guidance; the comment close is not a treaty finding or a reserve change.
  • 2026-10-26 · The NAIC white paper on homeowners rates and cross-state subsidization; a white paper is not a state rate order.
  • 2027-01-29 through 2027-01-30 · Transposition deadline and application of the EU Insurance Recovery and Resolution Directive, and the first national or group implementation records; a plan on paper is not an activated authority action.
