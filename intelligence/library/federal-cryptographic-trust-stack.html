Federal Cryptographic Trust Stack

How do authoritative source records, identity proofing, signed claims, key and issuer trust, authorization, audit and post-quantum migration form a durable federal trust stack?

Research through 2026-10-01

Home room: InnovationAlso in: Government Changes
The short version (draft, not yet reviewed by Katie)

NSA made the post-quantum migration public, but the real proof is still downstream

NSA's new hub gives national-security and defense-industry operators one public entrance, while the actual migration still has to move from policy and approved algorithms through validated products, registered solutions and observed system use.

What we found

  • The October 1 hub is an education and routing surface, not a new rule. The 2027 acquisition/support gate and 2030 phase-out gate come from earlier CNSS Policy 15 and CNSA 2.0 transition material.
  • The live hub showed three recommended articles at the cutoff: a primer, a threat-and-migration explainer, and a reaffirmation that NSA does not recommend quantum key distribution or quantum cryptography for NSS unless their implementation limitations are overcome.
  • NSA now makes two risks explicit: Harvest Now, Decrypt Later threatens confidentiality, while Trust Now, Forge Later threatens authentication, signatures and certificate-based attribution. The second branch reaches federal identity, signed claims, software provenance and payment authorization directly.
  • Approved March 2026 CSfC packages already include ML-KEM-1024 and ML-DSA-87, but generally as objective requirements that can still map to classical threshold alternatives where the objective setting is infeasible. Approved algorithm, product support, validation, solution registration and deployed use are different receipts.
  • The launch page compresses the clock and contains visible defects: it says 'Executive Order XX,' omits the separate 2031 use mandate and 2035 end state, and uses inconsistent wording for the Trust Now threat. Those are publication-quality issues, not proof that the underlying policy changed.

Why it matters

This is the missing public bridge between abstract federal PQC policy and the systems that must preserve identity, authority and durable signatures. It gives the Observatory a clean migration ladder and keeps support, compliance and operation from being collapsed into one claim.

What to watch

  • source-triggered · NSA correction or expansion of the hub, including a named EO, consistent threat terminology, technical profiles, white papers, migration tools or retirement of stale legacy text.
  • 2027-01-01 and the 2027 acquisition cycle · A named NSS solicitation, acquisition, waiver or rejection and the first applicable CNSA 2.0-supporting components on the CSfC list; product support, FIPS validation, NIAP evaluation and procurement remain separate.
  • 2028 capability-package cycle · Whether Mobile Access, Campus WLAN, Multi-Site Connectivity, Data at Rest and Key Management actually convert the March 2026 objective settings into required threshold settings.
  • 2030-12-31 through 2031-12-31 · Evidence of incapable-equipment phase-out, registered-solution mitigation and then mandatory CNSA 2.0 use, including any exception or waiver.
  • source-triggered · A named FPKI, PIV/CAC, Login.gov, Treasury SPS, software-signing or certificate/revocation cutover with integration, authorization, operation and recovery evidence.

Research summary

The federal trust stack is a federation of authoritative records, scoped identity proofing, signed claims, issuer-key registries, local authorization, accountable signatures, audit, correction and revocation. PQC preserves cryptographic durability; it does not make a claim true or authorize an action. NSA's October 1 hub is a new public NSS/DIB migration route, not a new rule or completed cutover.
