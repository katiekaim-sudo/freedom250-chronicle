## Maintained finding

The July 30–31 episode is a dated application of the IMF currency-pricing
map. The July reports, August official records and remaining transaction gaps
now belong in one answer.

At the July 31 research cutoff, Reuters reported, citing market sources, that Japan and South Korea sold dollars
and bought their own currencies. USD/JPY fell to 159.22 before partially
reversing to about 160.07 in Asia. The won strengthened about 2 percent to
KRW 1,418 per dollar, its strongest level in nine months. Japanese and Korean
finance officials declined to confirm the transactions at the first report
clock, and no official operation amount was available to that pass. Japan
subsequently confirmed coordination with the U.S. Treasury and published an
aggregate window total, as distinguished below. Korea's amount and a formal
Japan–Korea joint operation remain unresolved in this saved evidence set.

The institutional model being tested is:

> domestic authority decision and reserve use → execution in the FX market →
> dealer repricing → later national disclosure → IMF classification and
> surveillance

It does **not** support this chain:

> IMF instruction → IMF trade → IMF-set yen or won price

At the July source cutoff neither Japan nor Korea was using an IMF financing
program. No program review, performance criterion or Fund disbursement gate
was identified in this episode. The authority map and reported price movements
do not independently establish every transaction, instruction or causal link
in the model above.

separate dollar-versus-domestic-currency transaction reportedly executed by
authorities. Co-occurrence is not causation.

## 2. Who controlled what

### Japan

The Bank of Japan's own explanation states that:

- FX intervention is carried out under the authority of the Minister of
  Finance;
- the BOJ acts on behalf of and at the instruction of the minister; and
- the Foreign Exchange Fund Special Account supplies the funds.

For yen-support intervention, dollar funds held in that account are sold to
buy yen. The BOJ's execution role is distinct from the BOJ Policy Board's
monetary-policy decision.

The correct entity statement is:

> Japan's Ministry of Finance owns the intervention authority; the BOJ executes
> as the minister's agent.

“The BOJ independently decided to intervene” is not supported by the official
authority map.

### South Korea

The statute used by the July study, the Foreign Exchange Transactions Act,
establishes the Foreign Exchange
Equalization Fund, authorizes foreign-currency purchases and sales, and assigns
the Fund's management and operation to the Minister of Finance and Economy. The
implementing decree places the Fund account at the Bank of Korea. The BOK's
organization page used by that study assigns its International Department operation and
management of the FX Stabilization Fund and operation of FX markets.

An older BOK English institutional page uses the then-current “Ministry of
Strategy and Finance” name and describes the ministry–BOK cooperation
framework. It remains useful for functions, but not for the ministry's current
English name.

The first live report attributes the trade to “foreign exchange authorities,”
not to a named executing desk. The bounded entity statement is therefore:

> Korea's FX authorities reportedly sold dollars; the Minister of Finance and
> Economy–BOK system owns the Fund and operational perimeter, while the public
> source available at cutoff does not isolate the final instruction, resource
> account or executing desk.

The BOK's official quarterly series publishes the net transaction of U.S.
dollars by the FX authorities, not a daily trade ledger. Its June 30 release
reports a net purchase of `-$13.628 billion` in 2026Q1—that is, net dollar
sales. The page saved in July scheduled the Q2 release for September 30. A Q3 release
around December 31 was expected from historical cadence, without an official
schedule recovered by that pass. These are dated source states, not a refreshed
calendar. A net quarter cannot identify or exclude a gross daily sale when
other purchases and sales offset it.

### Market

Dealers and venues supplied the reported spot quotes. The reported official
orders would change currency supply and demand; the saved record does not
isolate their contribution from other order flow. No new administered USD/JPY
or USD/KRW parity was identified. The quote, intervention amount and measured
price effect are separate objects.

### IMF

The IMF's role is downstream and institutional:

1. classify the de jure and de facto exchange arrangements;
2. assess intervention and external-sector conditions in Article IV
   surveillance;
3. assess the external position and REER using model inputs and staff judgment;
4. examine Article VIII restrictions and multiple currency practices where
   applicable; and
5. advise that intervention be limited to disorderly or exceptional
   circumstances.

No source in this saved record identifies a Fund execution, approval or
price-setting role in the reported trades. Institutional remit and absence of
a located instruction do not constitute access to every private transaction
record; the claim that the IMF made these quotes is unsupported.

## 3. What the IMF's own country documents say

| Country | IMF arrangement at last country-report cutoff | IMF external assessment | IMF intervention advice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Japan | De jure and de facto free floating; informational annex said no intervention since July 2024 at that report cutoff | 2025 external position preliminarily broadly in line with fundamentals and desired policies | FX intervention limited to exceptional circumstances, including vulnerability from unhedged exposure, shallow markets or de-anchoring inflation expectations |
| Korea | De jure and de facto floating in the controlling source row | 2024 external position broadly in line; 2025 ESR staff-assessed REER gap -2.4% | FX intervention limited to preventing disorderly market conditions |

These assessments are dated objects. They do not pre-approve the July 30
operations, and the July 30 operations do not automatically reverse them.

Most importantly, an IMF statement that an external position is broadly in line
is not a nominal spot-rate target. The IMF can simultaneously assess the
external position as broadly in line and accept that short-lived disorderly
conditions justify limited intervention.

## 4. The “three interventions” classification claim

The IMF's AREAER methodology says a floating rate can be classified as
**free floating** if intervention is exceptional, addresses disorderly market
conditions, and is limited to at most three instances in the previous six
months, each lasting no more than three business days. If sufficient
information is unavailable, the arrangement is classified as **floating**.

This is a descriptive de facto classification criterion. It is not:

- a legal ban on a fourth intervention;
- an IMF order to wait until a calendar date;
- a loss of the sovereign authority to transact;
- a finding of manipulation;
- an Article VIII violation; or
- a penalty that causes the IMF to set a lower yen price.

“Free floating” and “floating” are both floating-arrangement categories. A
classification change may matter analytically and reputationally, but the IMF
document does not create the market effects claimed in viral posts.

Japan's prior 2026 interventions also require the official operation-day
ledger before counting “instances.” Neither the approximately ¥11.7 trillion
April–May figure retained in the claim audit nor the later ¥15.3993 trillion
window total supplies an instance count. The July 30 report falls outside the
July 31 MOF release covering June 29–July 29. Japan's August confirmation does
not reconcile the July 30 New York versus July 31 U.S. Eastern date labels.

## 5. What changes in the Korea crash story

### What remains

- The July 28–29 KOSPI collapse remains centered in a global AI/memory
  repricing, exceptional Samsung/SK hynix concentration, foreign selling and
  still-unquantified leverage amplification.
- July 28 won strength and falling sovereign yields remain evidence against a
  same-day “flight from every Korean asset” claim.
- The July 28 BOK KRW 28 trillion repo operation remains a regular
  open-market-operation rollover unless a later BOK object says otherwise.
- No digital-money, stablecoin or DeFi migration bridge has been established.

### What changes

- From the July 30 New York operation onward, won strength can no longer be
  treated as purely organic price discovery. Reported official dollar selling is a
  confounder; a quantified contribution is not established.
- A stronger won after intervention does not prove that depreciation pressure
  was absent; it may show that authorities offset it.
- The reported July 31 intraday KOSPI surge is consistent with a reversible
  global/chip repricing component; that is our interpretation of a provisional
  market observation, not a completed causal test. It does not, without account-level data,
  settle the role of leverage, forced sales or settlement.
- The T+2 finality questions for July 28–29 remain separate. The July 29 trade
  settlement deadline was July 31 at 16:00 KST, after the original 14:15 KST
  research cutoff. A rebound does not prove that every earlier trade settled
  normally. The later saved August 27 access check still did not recover the
  exact settlement and leverage records.

The necessary bridge is now:

> timed equity flows + spot/NDF and FX-swap data + official intervention
> disclosure + reserve/forward changes + settlement evidence

Without that bridge, “the crash forced the intervention” remains open.

## 6. Japan's policy and official disclosure clocks

Japan's pre-registered test said the cleanest monetary-confidence result would
be a stronger yen, higher front-end JGB yields, an orderly long end and zero
intervention.

That clean result is no longer available for the initial market window.
Reported intervention is an operative confounder. Even a hawkish BOJ hold or a
rate increase cannot retroactively make the earlier yen jump a purely
monetary-policy signal.

The BOJ decision must be read as a separate object:

- policy rate and vote;
- forecast and risk language;
- JGB-purchase decision;
- Ueda's explanation of yen, import prices and inflation expectations; and
- persistence of the yen move after the press conference.

The July 31 MOF release recorded ¥0 for June 29–July 29. It cannot adjudicate
the later reported operation. Japan's August 28 release covers July 30–August 26;
the amount and its limits are recorded below.

## 7. U.S. Treasury is a separate referee and coordinator

The U.S. Treasury's July 2026 FX report:

- placed both Japan and Korea on the Monitoring List;
- found no major trading partner manipulated its currency during the official
  four-quarter period through December 2025;
- described the yen as substantially undervalued after large real and nominal
  depreciation;
- said Japan did not intervene during that report period;
- said Korea sold about $28.0 billion of reserves in 2025 to smooth excess
  volatility amid won depreciation pressure; and
- recorded bilateral joint-statement commitments that intervention should be
  reserved for excess volatility and disorderly depreciation or appreciation.

The report predates the July 30 trades and does not adjudicate them.

Reuters also reported that U.S. authorities performed a rate check and that
Secretary Bessent said the yen appeared undervalued. The New York Fed declined
comment. A rate check—requesting dealer quotes—can signal readiness and alter
expectations, but it is not proof that the United States bought yen. The
institutional precedent is U.S. Treasury direction with the New York Fed Desk
acting as Treasury's fiscal agent; a rate check should not be described as a
Federal Reserve monetary-policy decision.

The August 1 FT report, preserved in the U.S. operation specialist, separately
described a July 31 New York Fed euro sale and yen purchase for Treasury. Its
canonical FT page was not recovered at that cutoff. That report, the earlier
rate check and Japan's subsequent coordination statement are three different
evidence objects. Treasury-directed ESF execution differs from FOMC-directed
SOMA execution; the account allocation is not established. A euro/yen trade is
not direct dollar selling or a sale of U.S. Treasury securities. Pre-event
reserve market values show capacity, not the transaction or its lasting effect.

This adds a distinct control layer:

> U.S. Treasury bilateral consultation and public valuation language can alter
> the market environment without an IMF decision.

## 8. Legitimacy storyline

The event does not show the IMF secretly controlling currency prices. It shows
something more institutionally precise.

The IMF publishes a multilateral rule and assessment layer. National
shareholders retain the balance sheet and execution authority. The United
States, Japan and Korea can coordinate bilaterally and signal through the New
York market before the Fund publishes any new classification or surveillance
assessment.

That creates three factual legitimacy tests:

1. **Symmetry:** are “exceptional” and “disorderly” applied consistently across
   systemic non-borrowers and program countries?
2. **Transparency:** can the public reconstruct the decision, execution,
   amount and rationale before the market effect fades?
3. **Traction:** does IMF surveillance constrain policy, or mainly describe it
   after major shareholders have already acted?

These are open tests. The episode supports an institutional comparison; it does not establish every
link in the sequence, measured causation or a final legitimacy verdict.
