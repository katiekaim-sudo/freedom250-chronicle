USAID — Statutory Person and Operating Death

Updated: 2026-08-15
Status: factual legal map
Rule: A Secretary’s remark that the agency “will no longer implement” is a control sentence. It is not a deed. A $125 million OE rescission is a money sentence. It is not a charter sentence.

Short answer

USAID still exists as a legal person. House OLRC prints 22 U.S.C. § 6563 as law in effect on August 14, 2026: unless abolished under the 1998 reorganization plan in § 6601, “there is within the Executive branch of Government the United States Agency for International Development as an entity described in section 104 of title 5.” No 2025–26 statute abolishing that person was retrieved.

What died is the operating body. EO 14169 paused new obligations and told agencies to continue, modify, or cease programs. State said, on March 28, 2025, that it had notified Congress of a reorganization: realign some functions to State by July 1, 2025; discontinue the rest. That is operating death. It is not repeal.

P.L. 119-28 ¶15 cuts $125,000,000 of unobligated USAID Operating Expenses from P.L. 119-4. A balance cut. Not a charter amendment. Bind that dollar to Impoundment and Rescission — Legal and Entity Map. Do not merge the maps.

2. Four clocks

Clock 1 — statutory existence. Live. § 6563(a) still names USAID as a 5 U.S.C. § 104 independent establishment, “unless abolished pursuant to the reorganization plan submitted under section 6601.” Section 6601 is the 1998 path. The 1998–99 plan kept AID. No 2025–26 statute, and no new § 6601 plan printed in the Federal Register, abolishing the person was retrieved.

Clock 2 — personnel. Hollowed. Official 2026 USAID headcount: not retrieved. Do not import the July 10, 2026 entity-chart staff fraction as a 2026 primary. State and USAID told Congress they would discontinue functions that do not match Administration priorities (March 28, 2025). Operating staff can vanish while the person remains.

Clock 3 — function transfer / implementation cease. Operating death, claimed by State, not enacted by Congress. EO 14169 § 3(c) lets agency heads, with OMB and the Secretary’s concurrence, “continue, modify, or cease” each program after review. State, January 26, 2025: pause all State/USAID foreign assistance for review. State, March 28, 2025: “notified Congress on their intent to undertake a reorganization that would involve realigning certain USAID functions to the Department by July 1, 2025, and discontinuing the remaining USAID functions.” Notification, not a statute.

A State briefing-index snippet for July 2, 2025 says Secretary Rubio “remarked that the U.S. Agency for International Development will no longer implement foreign assistance programs going forward.” Full July 2 briefing HTML: not retrieved at cutoff. Treat the official snippet as a State notice of operating cease. It is not a charter repeal.

Clock 4 — appropriations. Bind to the Impoundment map. P.L. 119-28 ¶15: “Of the unobligated balances under the heading ‘United States Agency for International Development--Funds Appropriated to the President--Operating Expenses’ made available by [P.L. 119-4], $125,000,000 are rescinded.” That cancels $125 million of unobligated OE. It leaves the heading. It leaves § 6563.

3. EO 14169 — what it orders and what it cannot

Signed January 20, 2025. Published January 30, 2025. 90 FR 8619–8620.

Orders. Sec. 2: no further U.S. foreign assistance disbursed unless aligned with the President’s foreign policy. Sec. 3(a): 90-day pause on new obligations and disbursements of development assistance; OMB enforces by apportionment. Sec. 3(b)–(c): review each program; determine continue, modify, or cease. Sec. 3(e): the Secretary may waive the pause.

Cannot. Sec. 4(a): does not impair authority granted by law. Sec. 4(b): implement “consistent with applicable law and subject to the availability of appropriations.” An EO cannot repeal § 6563. Apportionment under § 3(a) is a withhold device. It is not a rescission bill. Put that fight on the Impoundment map.

4. Operating death vs the statutory person

Object A — the person. 22 U.S.C. § 6563. Still printed. Abolition, if it ever comes, runs through § 6601 — a reorganization plan to the named committees. That 1998 machine is how Congress said AID could die. It is not how State’s 2025 notices work.

Object B — the operating body. Pause. Review. Cease. Congressional notification of a reorganization. Those are control facts. They can empty the building. They cannot amend the Code.

P.L. 119-28 ¶15 sits on a third rail: money. An unobligated OE cut can starve the remnant. Starvation is not repeal.

6. Entity Theory read

The group still has a USAID person on the books. Control of the assistance function moved to State. That is the same move as Education: empty the body, keep the name, spend through a neighbor.

An independent establishment under 5 U.S.C. § 104 is a legal person until Congress, or the one plan Congress wrote in 1998, kills it. The entity-chart flag “remnant / dissolving” is the right intent word. It is the wrong existence word. Leave the remnant node up until Clock 1 flips.
