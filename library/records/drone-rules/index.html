Normalizing Unmanned Aircraft Systems Beyond Visual Line of Sight Operations (Part 108 NPRM)

Federal Aviation Administration · August 7, 2025

Proposed; no final rule on record

The long-awaited FAA/TSA 'Part 108' proposed rule laid out performance-based regulations to enable routine low-altitude BVLOS drone operations without visual observers, plus third-party UAS Traffic Management services. Issued under EO 14307's mandate, it is the foundational rulemaking for normalizing commercial drone flight in U.S. airspace.

Maintained reading (reconciled September 7, 2026)
The August 7, 2025 FAA/TSA NPRM proposed a scalable BVLOS framework: Part 108 permits or operating certificates, aircraft airworthiness acceptance, and Part 146 oversight of automated data-service providers. Statutory authority includes the FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024 as well as the policy direction in EO 14307. The January 28, 2026 reopening was a limited further consultation, not final adoption.

Part 108 BVLOS — Denial of Extension of Comment Period

Federal Aviation Administration · September 29, 2025

Proposed; no final rule on record

The FAA and TSA denied requests to extend the Part 108 BVLOS comment period, citing EO 14307's 240-day final-rule deadline and the extraordinary measures needed to meet it. The denial signals the administration's push to fast-track the drone rule rather than slow the rulemaking.

Maintained reading (reconciled September 7, 2026)
The September 29, 2025 procedural notice denied two requests for more comment time and retained the October 6 deadline. It cited statutory and executive scheduling pressures. The original “fast-track” reading describes that decision’s policy context; it is not evidence that the final rule arrived. FAA subsequently reopened limited electronic-conspicuity questions in January 2026.

Part 108 BVLOS — Reopening of Comment Period

Federal Aviation Administration · January 28, 2026

Proposed; no final rule on record

The FAA reopened the Part 108 BVLOS comment period to take additional input on electronic conspicuity and right-of-way provisions. The reopening shows the agency refining the highest-friction technical pieces of the drone rule as it moves toward a final rule.

The January 28, 2026 notice reopened comments for fourteen days on alternative electronic conspicuity. FAA described seven questions following January 6 listening sessions and a January 15 docket memorandum; over half of roughly 3,100 earlier comments concerned right-of-way. Earlier comments remained in the record. This is evidence of a specific unresolved design issue and additional consultation, not an adopted right-of-way entitlement.

Designation — Restrict the Operation of Unmanned Aircraft in Close Proximity to a Fixed Site Facility

Federal Aviation Administration · May 6, 2026

Proposed

The FAA proposed to implement Section 2209, creating a process for operators of critical fixed-site facilities to request unmanned-aircraft flight restrictions on safety or security grounds. It is the security-side complement to BVLOS expansion — the airspace-protection layer the broader drone-integration push depends on.

Tags

Maintained reading (reconciled September 7, 2026)
The May 6, 2026 NPRM proposed Part 74 procedures for standard and special unmanned-aircraft restrictions around eligible fixed sites, implementing statutory §2209 and EO 14305. A restriction would be a legal airspace designation, not permission for a proprietor to jam, seize or destroy a drone. The June 30 extension moved comments from July 6 to August 5, 2026. This remains a proposal on the reviewed instruments, not proof that a particular site received protection.

Electric Vertical Takeoff and Landing and Advanced Air Mobility Integration Pilot Program — Establishment and Request for Proposals

Federal Aviation Administration · September 16, 2025

Notice, not a rule

The FAA formally established the eVTOL/AAM Integration Pilot Program directed by EO 14307, pairing state, local, tribal, and territorial governments with private eVTOL operators to develop frameworks for safe air-taxi operations. The notice opened a request for proposals to select participating projects.

Maintained reading (reconciled September 7, 2026)
The September 16, 2025 notice established eIPP and solicited government/private partnerships for eVTOL and other advanced aircraft, including fixed-wing and short-takeoff designs. Participation agreements did not transfer FAA airspace authority. FAA’s August 6 and August 18, 2026 releases report eight projects across 26 states selected in March and completed hybrid-electric demonstrations in Utah and the PA/NJ corridor. These later receipts move the program beyond solicitation while preserving aircraft and service limits.
