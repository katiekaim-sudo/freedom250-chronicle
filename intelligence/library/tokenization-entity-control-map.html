Who actually controls the crypto infrastructure

Of 20 crypto networks and companies we track, only one is used at scale and none is proven under stress; many brands rest on a handful of legal providers.

What we found

  • Only Securitize reaches “used at scale” on our seven-step ladder, and nothing is proven under stress.
  • The brand is rarely the issuer: Paxos issues PayPal's coin and holds its freeze button; a handful of providers (Paxos, Bridge, M0, Cross River, Lead Bank, Fireblocks, zerohash, Circle) sit under many brands.
  • A holder-to-holder USDC transfer changes only who holds Circle's IOU; it leaves no entry on Circle's, the Fed's or any bank's books.
  • J.P. Morgan's Kinexys is the most complete in-house money machine; Ripple runs five of seven parts itself but its national trust bank is not open.
  • The SEC custody proposal now has its own entry: SEC Crypto Custody Proposal — 2026.

Why it matters

The new crypto infrastructure is being built by and attached to existing institutions such as big banks, DTC and licensed trust companies, rather than replacing them. Money and markets are being rebuilt inside the existing system.

What to watch

  • After Federal Register publication · S7-2026-35 publication date and 60-day comment deadline.
  • October 2026 · DTC's expected general launch of its tokenization service (after July's limited production).
  • 2028-07-18 · GENIUS Act cutoff: U.S. crypto platforms may offer only approved (permitted) stablecoins after this date.
