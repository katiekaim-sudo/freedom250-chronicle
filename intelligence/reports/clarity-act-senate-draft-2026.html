CLARITY Act EHF26724 — Structure, Draft Delta and Crypto Hub Overlay

In plain English

Question: What did the September 14, 2026 Senate draft of the CLARITY Act change, and what happened when it reached the floor?

Answer: The Senate failed to end debate 49–50 on September 15; the CFTC sent its own crypto rule to the White House two days later.

Evidence checked through: 2026-09-14

Legal state: public negotiating draft. The cover still leaves the amendment number and proposer blank. It is not a filed substitute, Senate-passed text, enrolled bill or law at this cutoff.

Bottom line

The four-division architecture remains recognizable, but the September 14 text is not merely a wording clean-up. It adds a more explicit operating constitution for vertically integrated digital-commodity firms, a CFTC-side controlled-DeFi perimeter, a stablecoin deposit-flight circuit breaker, credit-union parity, and a revised blockchain-developer protection. Division C is rebuilt: the July 2029 sunset and extinguishment of liability are gone, and the latest draft creates a permanent divest-or-blind-trust regime for significant crypto-company interests with federal and state-attorney-general enforcement.

For the Crypto Hub, these provisions belong in a proposed legal overlay. They change the questions and future qualification routes for existing Hub actors; they do not confer a present registration, exemption, charter power, asset classification or operating permission.

Legislative clock

  1. Published: EHF26724 was released by sponsors on September 14.
  2. Motion to proceed: the announced September 15 vote is cloture on the motion to proceed, not final passage.
  3. Offer/file substitute: the sponsors say EHF26724 would be offered only if cloture is invoked.
  4. Senate consideration: amendments, cloture on the measure if required and final passage remain separate clocks.
  5. Bicameral agreement: the House and Senate must pass identical text.
  6. Enactment: presidential signature or another constitutional enactment route is still required.
  7. Effective/rulemaking: the general effective date is 360 days after enactment; rule-dependent provisions generally wait until the later of that date or 60 days after the relevant final rule.

No later clock should be inferred from an earlier one.

Material delta from the July 22 baseline

1. The ethics architecture is now permanent

  • Removed: July sections 30105 and 30106, including the January 20, 2029 sunset and the clause extinguishing liability after the sunset.
  • Added/rebuilt: a significant financial interest test, generally an equity interest worth at least $15,000 in a business whose plurality of revenue in any of the preceding three years came from issuing or sponsoring digital assets.
  • Required: divestiture or placement in a qualified blind trust by the effective date; the relevant ethics office receives notice within three days and publishes it within three days.
  • Penalties: issue/sponsor violations require disgorgement plus 20 percent of consideration or $500,000, whichever is greater; significant-interest violations carry 20 percent of the interest's value or $500,000, whichever is greater; prohibited-listing violations can reach $250,000 per violation per day.
  • Enforcement: the United States Attorney General receives a civil route. State attorneys general receive specified routes for injunctive relief and, for prohibited listings, civil penalties.
  • Drafting tension: the intermediary prohibition in the operative text refers to assets issued in violation of the ban; the sponsors' one-page summary describes issued or sponsored assets. Do not silently extend the operative verb.

This supersedes the July characterization of Division C as a temporary ethics rider.

2. Vertically integrated intermediaries receive an explicit control regime

  • Section 20103 directs the CFTC to identify, mitigate or resolve material conflicts across registered persons, including vertically integrated structures.
  • Common ownership or multiple registration is not banned by itself.
  • The required controls include governance, personnel and financial-resource separation where necessary; self-dealing and unfair-pricing prohibitions; customer-asset safeguards; disclosure and management of conflicts; independent oversight; and graduated sanctions.
  • Section 20204 generally bars a registered digital-commodity exchange and its affiliates from proprietary trading. Narrow exceptions cover customer facilitation or hedging, default management, necessary liquidity when otherwise unavailable, regulatory compliance and other CFTC-permitted transactions. Excepted trades may not be principally for profit and carry notice, reporting, information-barrier and governance requirements.
  • Section 20206 adds conflict procedures for broker/dealer affiliate arrangements and multiple registrations.

3. Controlled DeFi gets activity-based SEC/Treasury and CFTC routes

  • Section 10301 uses control and material alterability, rather than the DeFi label alone, to distinguish a non-decentralized protocol. The analysis asks whether a person can materially alter operation outside transparent pre-established code or restrict, censor or prohibit users.
  • Decentralized governance alone is not enough to establish control. Constrained, disclosed incident-response or security councils do not alone establish control.
  • SEC/Treasury and CFTC rules are activity-based: securities or digital-commodity brokerage, dealing, trading, execution, clearing and custody functions are the relevant perimeter.
  • A protocol, code or system does not itself register. A person that controls covered functions may have to register and comply with applicable BSA rules after the required rulemaking.
  • The CFTC-side developer safe harbor is limited to cash or spot digital-commodity provisions and does not create a derivatives exemption.

4. Stablecoin rewards now carry a deposit-flight circuit breaker

  • Section 10404 still distinguishes prohibited issuer-funded interest or yield from specified rewards or benefits.
  • New subsection 10404(c)(3)(C) defines a community bank as a depository institution or holding company with less than $10 billion in consolidated assets.
  • If Treasury makes a written determination within 18 months after enactment that movement from interest-bearing community-bank deposits into payment stablecoins caused a substantial detrimental impact because of activities regulated by the section, Treasury must use notice-and-comment rulemaking, consult the OCC, FDIC and Federal Reserve, and prohibit comparable interest or yield by covered parties.
  • The text places the 18-month limit on Treasury's determination. It does not plainly say that a resulting rule automatically expires after 18 months; this is a drafting question, not a resolved sunset.

5. Federal and insured credit unions receive express parity language

  • Section 10401 expressly includes federal and insured credit unions in the permission to use distributed-ledger technology or digital assets for activities otherwise authorized by law.
  • GENIUS Act corrections add Federal Credit Union Act accounts to specified reserve-asset treatment and make related state-credit-union-supervisor adjustments.
  • The provision preserves activity-specific limits. It is not a blanket authority for every derivative, brokerage, principal, dealer or market-making function.

6. The blockchain-developer protection is rewritten

  • Section 10604 now uses blockchain developer and non-controlling blockchain developer or provider terminology.
  • The shield expressly addresses money-transmitting-business registration under 31 U.S.C. 5330, the money-transmitter definition under 31 C.F.R. 1010.100(ff)(5), and specified financial institution categories under 31 U.S.C. 5312.
  • It extends the protected activity list to transaction compilation/relay/search/sequencing/validation, computational work, nodes, oracle services, bandwidth and user-controlled wallet or custody software.
  • It removes the earlier separate express reference to 18 U.S.C. 1960 and its specific-intent transfer carveout. Other AML, sanctions and terrorism-finance law still applies to conduct outside the shield.

7. State consumer-protection authority is preserved more expressly

  • The CFTC retains exclusive jurisdiction over a registered person's covered activities.
  • States may use the Commodity Exchange Act route against registrants, generally applicable state or local law against unregistered persons, and non-preempted unfair-or-deceptive-practice investigation and enforcement authority.

8. A quieter AI-lab change narrows automaticity

  • Section 10509 changes agency approval from shall approve when standards are met to may approve.
  • It removes automatic approval after an additional 120-day extension.
  • It adds a two-year project limit and a maximum of five active AI test projects per agency.

Crypto Hub overlay

Existing Hub layer Proposed change if enacted and implemented What remains true now
Claim / asset classification Network-token and ancillary-asset treatment would change the first classification branch and disclosure route. Each product's current securities, commodities, contract and state-law analysis still controls.
Actor perimeter SEC, Treasury and CFTC activity tests would put controlling persons, registered spot intermediaries and affiliates into more explicit functional lanes. Brands, affiliates, protocols, developers and legal entities remain separate.
Permission Exchanges, brokers, dealers, custodians and controlled-DeFi operators could receive new or revised registration routes after enactment and rulemaking. No Hub actor gains permission from EHF26724. Existing charters, registrations, orders and contracts remain controlling.
Authoritative record / customer property Customer-property, segregation, custody and intermediary requirements would create new qualification questions. A chain event still does not automatically become the authoritative holder record or legal title.
Instructions / technical operators Non-controlling developers, miners, validators, node/oracle operators and user-controlled wallet software receive a more explicit money-transmission shield. The shield is proposed, function-specific and not a general immunity or derivatives exemption.
Clearing and resources Stablecoin rewards acquire a contingent Treasury circuit breaker; banks and credit unions receive clarified otherwise-authorized DLT/digital-asset activity. Reserve assets, deposit liabilities, cash legs, balance-sheet exposure and redemption obligations remain separate.
Settlement and finality The bill creates intermediary and customer-protection rules but does not make technical confirmation a universal legal-finality event. Each payment, security, commodity and custody arrangement keeps its own finality and insolvency rules.
Books and servicing Registration, disclosure, customer-property and reporting rules would add books-and-records duties by function. Issuer, transfer-agent, custodian, exchange, broker, bank and network books are still distinct.

Hub implementation rule

The Hub should carry EHF26724 as a worked proposed case in the Legal Process room. It may change future qualification questions and expose affected role families. It must not:

  • rewrite a current edge, operating clock or company permission as if the bill had passed;
  • assign proposed registration to a named actor;
  • collapse enactment and agency rulemaking into one date;
  • infer actual vertical-integration compliance, DeFi control, stablecoin deposit flight or ethics violations for a named party;
  • replace the current rulebook, charter, registration, product terms or state-law analysis.
