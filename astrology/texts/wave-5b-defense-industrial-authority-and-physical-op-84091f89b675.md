# Wave 5B — Defense-Industrial Authority and Physical Operation

**Research window:** Exact 2025 Capricorn ingress through exact 2026 Aries ingress.  
**Evidence cutoff:** June 20, 2026, 11:59 p.m. EDT. Later facts are quarantined as handoffs.  
**Question:** When winter policy tried to discipline defense capital, recruit foreign purchases, secure coal and critical material, and loosen Defense Production Act execution, which commands acquired a carrier—and which reached physical operation?  
**Finding:** The five orders form a graduated authority chain rather than one completed industrial program. EO 14372 made delivery and capacity the proposed condition for contractor distributions and compensation, but no named notice or enforcement case was public by the cutoff. EO 14383 reorganized the arms-transfer decision path around domestic capacity and foreign capital, while its task force, catalogs, cases and performance metrics remained nonpublic. EO 14386 reached two identifiable DLA coal-power solicitations, not an award or delivered electricity. EO 14387 reached an Agriculture delegation rule, but no public rated order, allocation, contract or production receipt. EO 14391 removed a presidential-return gate from delegated Energy and Commerce DPA action; Energy used the amended authority the same day to order Sable Offshore's pipeline and oil-production system restored. Oil flowed, and courts immediately became a competing institutional carrier. The packet's thesis is **winter authority becomes materially consequential only when a named holder can issue a callable order into an existing physical system—and even then, operation does not settle jurisdiction, sale or public benefit**.

The shared ladder is:

`industrial diagnosis → decision holder → review / catalog / solicitation → contract or rated order → physical operation → transaction / output → contest → durable settlement`

## 5B.1 — January 7: defense capital is made conditional on delivery

[EO 14372](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/13/2026-00554/prioritizing-the-warfighter-in-defense-contracting) directs the Secretary of War to identify contractors that combine underperformance or inadequate capacity investment with stock buybacks or corporate distributions. An identified contractor can receive notice, a 15-day opportunity for a board-approved remediation plan, and then possible DPA, FAR, DFARS or contract remedies. Future contracts are to contain distribution, compensation and salary-control terms tied to performance and investment.

The order therefore does not enact a freestanding economy-wide dividend ban. Its operative chain requires:

1. a Secretary finding against a particular contractor;
2. notice describing the deficiency;
3. attempted remediation or failed negotiation;
4. an available legal or contractual remedy; and
5. for future contracts, an actual clause in an executed instrument.

The [May 2026 Navy Shipbuilding Plan](https://media.defense.gov/2026/May/11/2003928909/-1/-1/1/NAVY%20SHIPBUILDING%20PLAN%20MAY%202026.PDF) is the clearest public descendant found by the cutoff. It says shipbuilders are expected to invest in workforce and infrastructure while eschewing buybacks and dividends as directed by EO 14372. That is a real planning and policy receipt. It is not a named contractor finding, contract amendment or enforcement action.

**Maturity.** Directed review and contractual redesign; publicly echoed in a service industrial plan.

**Counterevidence and limit.** No public contractor notice, board remediation plan, Secretary decision, stopped dividend, compensation change, contract clause or DPA/FAR enforcement record was isolated. A planning sentence cannot prove that the capital-allocation gate was exercised.

## 5B.2 — February 6: foreign purchases are recruited into domestic capacity

[EO 14383](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/11/2026-02814/establishing-an-america-first-arms-transfer-strategy) reframes arms transfer as an industrial-capacity mechanism. The intended circulation is explicit:

`foreign purchase and capital → prioritized U.S. platform → domestic capacity / new entrants → improved supply and readiness → partner delivery`

The order assigns a sales catalog, industry-engagement plan, named FMS and Direct Commercial Sales opportunities, enhanced end-use-monitoring criteria, a Third-Party Transfer review, advanced contracting notice, a Promoting American Military Sales Task Force and quarterly public performance metrics. It also splits congressional-notification functions between War and State while preserving consultation.

This is a decision-right architecture, not an arms shipment. The public record searched through the cutoff did not yield the task-force charter, priority catalog, industry plan, named opportunity list or first quarterly metrics. Nor did it identify a Letter of Offer and Acceptance, export license, production investment, delivery or foreign payment attributable to the order.

**Maturity.** Effective delegation and mandated interdepartmental machinery.

**Counterevidence and limit.** Administrative outputs may exist without publication; absence from the public record is not proof of nonperformance. But a strategic intent cannot be promoted to a sale, financed production line or delivered weapon without the relevant case record.

## 5B.3 — February 11: coal doctrine reaches procurement intake

[EO 14386](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/17/2026-03156/strengthening-united-states-national-defense-with-americas-beautiful-clean-coal-power-generation) instructs War, coordinated with Energy, to seek long-term coal power purchase agreements or similar contracts for defense and mission-critical facilities.

Two direct procurement descendants were public before the cutoff:

- On March 10, DLA Energy issued [SPE60426R0400](https://sam.gov/opp/d06ba0de283a404b8e2032cc7a2a1baa/view) for an estimated 221,631,740 kWh of coal-generated electricity to Illinois installations in the MISO market, with a May 2027–May 2029 delivery period.
- On March 11, DLA Energy issued [SP060426R0409](https://sam.gov/workspace/contract/opp/50ac46a1835d4105aa8ca8bbda8f43a0/view) for construction and operation of coal-based energy-production facilities on Department of War installations across the continental United States.

These are much more than rhetoric: solicitation numbers, contracting office, offer deadlines, responsibility screens, delivery objects and future terms existed. They are still procurement intake. Neither record, as of the cutoff, proved an award, executed PPA, plant, delivered electron, invoice or payment.

**Maturity.** Requirement and market solicitation.

**Counterevidence and limit.** A government request for offers does not establish that a responsible offer arrived, an award was made, a site was selected, power was delivered or coal supplied new capacity rather than existing generation.

## 5B.4 — February 18: material scarcity receives a delegated allocation holder

[EO 14387](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/23/2026-03628/promoting-the-national-defense-by-ensuring-an-adequate-supply-of-elemental-phosphorus-and) finds elemental phosphorus and glyphosate-based herbicides critical to defense and food security. It delegates DPA Title I priority and allocation authority to Agriculture, requires consultation with War, incorporates the Agriculture Priorities and Allocations System, and instructs the Secretary not to endanger domestic-producer viability.

USDA's [April 13 delegation rule](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-07088.pdf) is the first administrative carrier found. It assigns administration, coordination and implementation of 7 CFR part 789 and expressly includes EO 14387 in the Department's delegated functions.

This corrects the shallow statement that the order had no implementation at all. The holder was formally seated inside Agriculture. The remaining distinction is decisive: assigning the official who may run the system is not issuing a rated order through it.

**Maturity.** Effective DPA authority and departmental administration assignment.

**Counterevidence and limit.** No public priority rating, allocation, producer direction, purchase agreement, loan, grant, capacity expansion, output receipt or measurable reduction in import dependence was isolated by June 20.

## 5B.5 — March 13: a delegation enters a physical system

[EO 14391](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/18/2026-05382/adjusting-certain-delegations-under-the-defense-production-act) makes two compact but consequential changes. It allows the Secretaries of Commerce and Energy to exercise the relevant EO 13603 delegation independently, and clarifies that an agency head need not return to the President when authority is already delegated.

The same day, the [Energy Secretary directed Sable Offshore](https://www.energy.gov/articles/secretary-wright-directs-sable-offshore-restore-santa-ynez-unit-and-pipeline) to restore the Santa Ynez Unit and pipeline system, expressly citing EO 14391's amendment. The order called for prioritization and allocation of pipeline transportation and the resumption of production and transport. This is the wave's clearest transition from a rewritten decision right to an exercised command.

By April 17, [California acknowledged roughly one month of oil flow](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/04/17/governor-newsom-exposes-trumps-sable-offshore-pipeline-lie-one-month-of-oil-prices-have-only-gone-up/). That proves physical operation. It does not prove a customer sale, settled title, lower price or federal expenditure.

The same operation immediately entered competing jurisdictions. California's [March complaint](https://oag.ca.gov/system/files/attachments/press-docs/Complaint%20for%20Declaratory%20and%20Injunctive%20and%20Other%20Relief%20%5B1%5D.pdf) argued that DPA priority/allocation authority could not erase State safety law or federal and State court orders. On April 17 the State court held that the federal DPA order did not nullify the existing injunction requiring State approval. Litigation remained live at the evidence cutoff.

Energy also announced on June 4 that [13 coal and export-infrastructure projects had been selected for up to $500 million in DPA Title III support](https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/funding-defense-production-act-title-iii). The announcement is a selection and ceiling, not proof of obligations or outlays; the detailed July project list falls after the cutoff.

**Maturity.** Delegation, exercised order and physical flow, followed by active judicial and federalism contest.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The oil flowed, but legal supremacy was not settled. California disputed the authority, causal price claims and safety posture. No public sales contract, buyer, payment, federal purchase or durable operating judgment was isolated by the cutoff.

## The industrial-carrier theorem

These orders reveal three different meanings of “the state acted”:

1. **The state named a standard.** Contractor capital should serve delivery; foreign arms demand should serve U.S. capacity.
2. **The state opened a route.** DLA solicited coal power; Agriculture seated an allocations administrator.
3. **The state issued a callable command into existing machinery.** Energy's Sable order entered a physical oil and pipeline system.

Only the third immediately produced observable material movement, and that movement activated another carrier: courts. Institutional structure did not simply crumble. Executive routing became more direct while procurement law, corporate boards, State sovereignty, environmental rules and judicial orders continued to determine what the command could become.

## Both poles

### Constructive pole — authority is tied to capacity and delivery

- contractor incentives are redirected toward production, workforce and timeliness;
- foreign demand can finance interoperable domestic capacity rather than deepen backlog;
- military-energy requirements are exposed to competitive procurement;
- scarce materials receive a named allocation holder; and
- delegated officials can respond quickly when a physical bottleneck threatens supply.

### Shadow pole — emergency purpose outruns adjudicated limits

- executive findings can pressure private governance without transparent case records;
- arms sales can make domestic industrial goals subordinate partner need and oversight;
- coal mission-assurance language can predetermine procurement before comparative system analysis;
- a single producer can receive protected viability without public terms; and
- DPA command can be used to declare State law and court orders subordinate before jurisdiction is settled.

Both poles remain live. Named notices, clauses, task-force outputs, catalogs, offers, awards, rated orders, production records, sales, payments, safety records and final judgments—not astrological rhyme—show which matured.
