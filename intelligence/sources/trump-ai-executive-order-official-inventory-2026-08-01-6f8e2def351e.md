# Trump AI Executive Order Official Inventory

## Bottom line

Through **August 1, 2026 (ET)**, the official record supports a core inventory of
**13 Trump executive orders** that directly concern artificial intelligence or
materially govern Federal AI development, use, acquisition, education, export,
science deployment, cybersecurity, or data-center infrastructure:

- **2 first-term AI orders:** EO 13859 and EO 13960;
- **11 second-term orders:** EO 14148, 14177, 14179, 14277, 14318, 14319,
  14320, 14355, 14363, 14365, and 14409.

This is an instrument census, not a keyword count. It therefore:

1. counts an instrument as an executive order only when the numbered Federal
   Register/GovInfo record says it is one;
2. keeps White House plans, presidential memoranda or determinations, OMB
   memoranda, budget guidance, fact sheets, and agency programs in separate
   rows;
3. separates signature, Federal Register publication, deadline, implementation,
   funding, award, and operating clocks; and
4. admits energy, cyber, quantum, or science orders only in a bounded adjacent
   ring when the text materially connects to AI.

The central architecture is not one omnibus AI order. It is a stack:

`policy reset -> White House science advice -> Federal use/acquisition rules ->
education/workforce -> compute and data-center buildout -> export packaging ->
AI-enabled science -> State-law strategy -> frontier-model cybersecurity`.

## AI governance from policy reset to agency practice

**Targeted reconciliation: September 7, 2026.** The seven original Tech records ask how the AI leadership agenda becomes reviews, public input, purchasing conditions, security work and scientific collaboration. Their common policy-change lens remains visible, while each instrument answers a different question. A change in emphasis is not, by itself, evidence that every safeguard disappeared or that implementation succeeded.

This section joins those original questions to this inventory's existing order and implementation ledger. It corrects the revocation carrier, distinguishes selective cyber amendments from wholesale repeal, and follows the saved procurement-guidance and agent-consultation returns. The broader **August 1 instrument census remains dated August 1**. September 7 is a review of these seven records and identified source returns, not a new whole-government census.

### The policy reset and its separate implementing documents

**Research question:** How the administration resets AI policy and turns a leadership objective into reviews, replacement guidance and a national plan.

EO 14179 was signed January 23, 2025 and published January 31. EO 14148, signed January 20, had already revoked EO 14110; this order directs review of actions taken under that revoked order, lawful revision or rescission of inconsistent actions, an Action Plan within 180 days and OMB memorandum revisions within sixty days. The saved official inventory records April 3 replacement OMB memoranda, and the White House released the Action Plan on July 23. Those are distinct returned documents, not proof every agency action changed or all safeguards disappeared. The original deregulation lens remains useful, with revocation, policy review, guidance, planning and operating results kept separate.

**Clocks:** EO 14179 signed January 23, 2025; filed January 30 at 11:15 a.m.; published January 31, 90 FR 8741–8742. EO 14148 signed January 20, filed January 27 at 8:45 a.m., published January 28. Nominal 60-/180-day dates from January 23 are March 24/July 22. Saved OMB replacements dated April 3; Action Plan public release July 23. Internal completion/submission instants remain unverified.

**Primary evidence:** [EO 14179 policy and implementation direction](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/31/2025-02172/removing-barriers-to-american-leadership-in-artificial-intelligence); [EO 14148 revocation carrier](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/28/2025-01901/initial-rescissions-of-harmful-executive-orders-and-actions); [July 23 public Action Plan release](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2025/07/white-house-unveils-americas-ai-action-plan/).

**Evidence limits:**

- EO 14148 section 2(ggg) is the actual EO 14110 revocation carrier. EO 14179 does not automatically undo every action taken under EO 14110 or independent statutory authority.
- The 180-day command is to develop and submit the plan. July 23 public release, one day after the nominal July 22 calendar derivation, does not establish when internal submission occurred.
- The April 3 OMB memoranda are confirmed in the saved official inventory; this lane did not freshly audit their full texts or agency compliance. Claims about ideological bias are the administration's stated policy framing, not an independent finding.

### Selective changes to cybersecurity duties

**Research question:** How the administration reshapes AI cybersecurity work, software controls and cryptographic obligations while directing technical defensive capacity. Preserve the original build axis and policy-change question; attribute the censorship rationale to the White House fact sheet rather than declaring it proved by the predecessor text.

EO 14306 was signed June 6, 2025; June 11 is publication. It selectively amends the earlier cybersecurity order, retaining and rewriting software-security, cryptographic and AI-security duties while removing others. Its replacement AI section requires access to existing cyber-defense datasets and incorporation of AI vulnerabilities into incident processes. The prior AIxCC-dependent energy pilot, separate Defense AI-defense program and research-priority directions are absent from that replacement. The White House described this as a move away from censorship; that is the administration's stated rationale, not a finding established by the deleted technical provisions. Actual security implementation remains a separate question.

**Clocks:** EO 14306: signed June 6, 2025; filed June 10 at 11:15 a.m.; published June 11, 2025 (90 FR 24723–24726). Predecessor EO 14144: signed January 16, published January 17, 2025. Replacement AI duties carry November 1, 2025 deadlines. The NIST responsibility page was updated January 13, 2026, but its task list is not a completion ledger. Targeted primary review September 7, 2026 preserves saved owners' separate August cutoffs.

**Primary evidence:** [EO 14306 amendment](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/06/11/2025-10804/sustaining-select-efforts-to-strengthen-the-nations-cybersecurity-and-amending-executive-order-13694); [EO 14144 predecessor text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-01-17/html/2025-01470.htm); [White House rationale](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/06/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-reprioritizes-cybersecurity-efforts-to-protect-america/); [NIST responsibility list](https://www.nist.gov/itl/executive-orders/strengthening-and-promoting-innovation-nations-cybersecurity).

**Evidence limits:**

- The amendment is operative legal action, not merely a planned review, but it is not wholesale revocation of EO 14144 or EO 13694. Unamended text and other authorities require their own analysis.
- Removing duties from this order does not prove that related research or a pilot ceased under every other authority. Conversely, elapsed deadlines do not prove datasets, vulnerability processes, standards or deployments were completed.
- The order narrows two specified EO 13694 clauses from any person to any foreign person. That is not evidence of a particular designation, block or enforcement result.
- The replacement PQC subsection retains the product-category list and TLS requirements but omits the predecessor's automatic category-list-to-solicitation and immediate PQC implementation clauses. Do not treat every January 2025 procurement requirement as retained.
- The original offense/defense and pro-innovation lens is an interpretation. The operative replacement AI tasks concern cyber defense and vulnerability management; this record does not establish an offensive AI deployment.

### Federal LLM purchasing conditions and issued guidance

**Research question:** How federal purchasing conditions shape the AI models and vendor information agencies will accept. Preserve the original procurement-side thesis and deregulate axis as historical framing, while separating policy principles, guidance, contractual remedies and measured operation.

EO 14319 was signed July 23, 2025; July 28 is publication. It establishes the administration's truth-seeking and ideological-neutrality principles for federal procurement of large language models, with disclosure options and scope limits. It does not impose one mandatory architecture on all private AI. OMB issued implementing memorandum M-26-04 on December 11, 2025, requiring terms in covered new solicitations or orders, practicable existing-contract changes and agency procedures by March 11, 2026. That is a documented guidance return. Whether a specific model complies, an agency changed its contract, or a remedy was used still requires separate evidence.

**Clocks:** EO 14319: signed July 23, 2025; filed July 25 at 8:45 a.m.; published July 28 (90 FR 35389–35391). Its 120-day guidance target was November 20, 2025. M-26-04 is dated December 11, 2025 and explicitly requires updated agency procedures by March 11, 2026; those are guidance and deadline clocks, not proof of completed procedures. Its two-year sunset runs from issuance unless OMB provides otherwise. September 7 review used the currently served official PDF; the original Tech record is preserved separately.

**Primary evidence:** [EO 14319](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/07/28/2025-14217/preventing-woke-ai-in-the-federal-government); [Authenticated GovInfo text](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-07-28/html/2025-14217.htm); [OMB M-26-04, December 11](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/M-26-04-Increasing-Public-Trust-in-Artificial-Intelligence-Through-Unbiased-AI-Principles-1.pdf).

**Evidence limits:**

- The order's descriptions of DEI and ideological bias are the administration's policy claims, not this research's independent technical findings about a named model.
- The neutrality principle permits ideological judgments when prompted by or otherwise readily accessible to the end user. The guidance allows documentation and disclosure paths, technical latitude and protection of sensitive model information; it is not a categorical ban on every ideological output.
- M-26-04 excludes national security systems, with voluntary application encouraged. Its footnote does not require application to LLMs acquired under a free, open-source license, while recommending due diligence. Agency-built and non-LLM models have additional applicability factors rather than an automatic universal scope.
- The memo requires minimum transparency requests and permits enhanced requests depending on use. Neither the requested documents nor the policy labels establish that a vendor/model has passed a specific evaluation.
- The memo provides a two-year sunset unless OMB directs otherwise. This bounded review does not establish later extension, every agency's compliance, vendor-paid decommissioning or observed model behavior.

### Advanced computing: a conditional licensing pathway

**Research question:** Did the administration concretely ease access to advanced U.S. AI chips for China and Macau, and which licensing conditions constrain that opening?

The January 15, 2026 final rule opened conditional case-by-case review for direct U.S. exports of qualifying advanced-computing commodities to end users in China or Macau. Its thresholds are TPP below 21,000 and total DRAM bandwidth below 6,500 GB/s; H200 and MI325X are examples, not the legal test. Applicants must supply the required certifications, including U.S. supply protection, an aggregate-TPP export ratio, end-user and remote-access safeguards, and shipment testing. This changes the licensing gate without granting a license or proving delivery.

**Clocks:** Filed January 13, 2026 at 4:15 pm; published and effective January 15, 2026. The rule's preamble identifies chips commercially available in the United States at publication; its certifications and testing apply at application and before export as specified. Exact instrument reviewed September 7, 2026; this is not a refreshed approval or shipment count.

**Primary evidence:** [January advanced-computing final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/15/2026-00789/revision-to-license-review-policy-for-advanced-computing-commodities); [Authenticated rule PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-01-15/pdf/2026-00789.pdf).

**Evidence limits:**

- The pathway does not generally extend to reexports or in-country transfers. Other applications retain the rule's presumption-of-denial treatment; all applicable controls and transaction-specific requirements still matter.
- The codified 50% condition compares aggregate TPP exported to China or Macau with aggregate TPP shipped for U.S. end use for the same commodities, from first commercial U.S. shipment to application. It is not a 50% cap on dollar revenue or all national chip exports.
- A qualified lab reviews every shipment before export on an approved license; the preamble permits representative batch sampling, not necessarily testing every chip. A rule, application, approval, test certificate and shipment are distinct evidence states. No licensing or shipment census was performed.

The original policy-change lens is useful precisely because these instruments can pull in different directions. An opening for one commodity and transaction can coexist with restrictions on a named entity. Keep the exact actor, item, destination, end use, instrument and evidence date attached to every conclusion.

**Coverage:** complete BIS announcement and GAO decision, complete September Entity List and January licensing-rule texts; the original Diffusion rule was checked only for its first-page legal clocks. No substantive review of the three separate chip-guidance documents or later approval/shipment census is claimed.

## Scope, method, and confidence

### Admission test

A numbered Trump order enters the **core** only if its operative text does at
least one of the following:

- establishes or resets national AI policy;
- directs Federal AI research, development, deployment, governance, inventory,
  acquisition, or procurement;
- creates an AI education, workforce, export, scientific-computing, health, or
  frontier-model program;
- directly governs infrastructure expressly defined around AI training,
  inference, simulation, or synthetic-data generation; or
- changes the operative legal baseline by expressly revoking an AI order.

An order enters the **adjacent ring** only if its operative text materially
connects energy, cyber, quantum, workforce, or Federal scientific-integrity
requirements to AI. Mere technological relevance is insufficient.

### Official-source ladder

1. Federal Register HTML record and GovInfo PDF for exact instrument text,
   number, signature date, publication date, citation, and document number.
2. White House presidential-actions page for same-day signing/release context.
3. White House, OMB, OSTP, Commerce, DOE, NIH, NSF, CISA, NIST, and other agency
   records for observed implementation.
4. Official government domain census for bounded discovery negatives.

No press article, vendor release, think-tank summary, or secondary legal
database is used as evidence here.

The **existing-corpus** comparison is delegated to
`TRUMP_AI_CRYPTO_EO_EXISTING_CORPUS_INVENTORY_2026-08-01.md`; this lane controls
the primary-official instrument and implementation-state findings.

### Clock rule

Unless an order expressly provides otherwise, its commands take effect when
the President signs it. Later Federal Register publication authenticates and
publishes the order; it does not silently move the signature-date deadline
clock. Derived dates below are calendar-day calculations from signature and
are labeled as such. Where text uses a relative relationship such as “within 90
days after guidance,” the second clock cannot start until the carrier appears.

## Detailed command and deadline ledger

### EO 13859 — American AI Initiative

**Operative commands.** EO 13859 established the American AI Initiative and
directed:

- NSTC’s Select Committee on AI to coordinate the initiative;
- agencies to treat AI R&D as a budget priority and report plans annually;
- agencies to improve access to Federal data and models while protecting
  security, privacy, and confidentiality;
- OMB to solicit input on data/model access, identify barriers, and issue
  regulatory guidance;
- NIST to prepare a Federal technical-standards engagement plan;
- agencies to prioritize AI access to high-performance computing;
- Education and NSF to develop AI-education and workforce recommendations; and
- national-security agencies to prepare an action plan protecting the United
  States AI advantage.

**Main clocks from signature.** 90 days for the OMB data/model request and
workforce recommendations; 120 days for data/source-code guidance and the
national-security action plan; 180 days for agency data improvement plans, the
cloud-computing report, OMB regulatory guidance, and the NIST standards plan;
plus annual budget reporting.

**Implementation/legal limit.** OMB’s later AI-regulation memorandum and
NIST’s AI standards work are implementation carriers, not amendments to the
order. The National AI Initiative Act later supplied a statutory program layer;
statutory codification should not be back-described as another executive order.

### EO 13960 — trustworthy AI in Federal Government

**Operative commands.** For covered non-defense and non-intelligence agency
uses, EO 13960 applied nine principles to the design, development, acquisition,
and use of AI; directed OMB to prepare a policy roadmap; required public AI
use-case inventories and remediation or retirement of inconsistent uses; and
directed CIO Council/OPM workforce and rotational mechanisms.

**Main clocks.** 30 days for agency responsible officials; 45 days for CIO
coordination and OPM inventory work; 60 days for use-case inventory criteria;
90 days for an AI Presidential Innovation Fellows track; 180 days for the OMB
roadmap; annual inventories after the CIO criteria; and 120 days after an
inventory for remediation or retirement.

**Revocation clock and limit.** EO 14110 expressly revoked EO 13960. EO 14148
later expressly revoked EO 14110. The 2025 revocation does not say EO 13960 is
revived. Current Federal governance should therefore be sourced to current OMB
memoranda and active orders, not to presumed common-law revival.

### EO 14148 — AI legal-baseline rescission carrier

The AI-relevant operation is the express revocation of EO 14110. That action
took effect on signature, January 20, 2025. EO 14148 did not replace EO 14110’s
many agency instructions one-for-one. EO 14179 separately created the review,
plan, and OMB-revision process.

### EO 14177 — PCAST

The order establishes PCAST with no more than 24 members, places the Assistant
to the President for Science and Technology and the Special Advisor for AI and
Crypto among its presidentially selected leadership/membership structure,
authorizes advice on science, technology, education, and innovation, and makes
DOE the support agency. It revokes EO 14007 as amended by EO 14109. The order
creates an advisory body; PCAST advice is not an agency rule or appropriation.

### EO 14179 — policy reset and plan

**Operative commands.** The order states a policy of sustaining and enhancing
United States AI dominance, requires a national AI Action Plan within 180 days,
orders review of agency actions taken under revoked EO 14110, authorizes
suspension, revision, or rescission of inconsistent actions, and requires OMB
to revise M-24-10 and M-24-18 within 60 days.

**Observed carriers.** OMB issued [M-25-21, Accelerating Federal Use of AI
through Innovation, Governance, and Public Trust](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-21-Accelerating-Federal-Use-of-AI-through-Innovation-Governance-and-Public-Trust.pdf)
and [M-25-22, Driving Efficient Acquisition of Artificial Intelligence in
Government](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/M-25-22-Driving-Efficient-Acquisition-of-Artificial-Intelligence-in-Government.pdf)
on April 3, 2025. The White House released [Winning the Race: America’s AI
Action Plan](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf)
on July 23, 2025. Those are implementation documents, not executive orders.

### EO 14277 — AI education

**Operative commands and clocks.** The order creates the White House AI
Education Task Force, chaired through OSTP, and directs:

- a Presidential AI Challenge plan within 90 days, with the challenge to occur
  within 12 months after the plan;
- public-private K-12 AI education resources and a readiness review 180 days
  after the first partnerships;
- Education guidance and research measures within 90 days;
- Education, NSF, and USDA teacher-training/research action within 120 days;
- Labor apprenticeship, WIOA, certification, and secondary-school credential
  measures within 120 days; and
- agencies with education grants to treat AI education as a priority within
  120 days.

**Claim limit.** A task force, challenge plan, partnership, grant priority, or
training eligibility rule is not evidence that a particular school received
money or that a national curriculum was imposed.

**Observed challenge operation.** The White House [launched the nationwide
Presidential AI Challenge](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2025/08/first-lady-melania-trump-launches-nationwide-presidential-ai-challenge/)
on August 26, 2025. On June 9, 2026, it [recognized six National Champion
teams](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2026/06/first-lady-melania-trump-honors-americas-next-generation-of-top-ai-talent-at-historic-presidential-ai-challenge-awards-ceremony/)
and reported participation by more than 20,000 students across all 50 States,
the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and 49 Department of War Education
Activity schools in 10 countries. That establishes program launch,
participation, judging, and national awards—not universal school participation,
a mandate, or a grant to every participant.

### EO 14318 — AI data-center infrastructure

**Operative architecture.** The order expressly defines data-center use around
AI inference, training, simulation, or synthetic-data generation and covers
supporting power, chips, networking, data storage, and cloud infrastructure. A
“qualifying project” meets an order-defined threshold such as at least $500
million in committed capital expenditure, more than 100 MW of new load,
national-security designation, or Secretarial designation.

**Commands and clocks.** Commerce is to establish a support initiative and
agencies are to identify existing financial-support tools. Relevant agencies
have 10 days to identify available categorical exclusions to CEQ; after a
relevant agency identifies a project to FPISC, the FPISC Executive Director may
designate and publish it as a transparency project within 30 days; EPA has 180
days for reuse-site guidance; and the Army has 180 days to review nationwide
permits. Interior, Energy, and Defense receive site/authorization/lease
directions.

**Revocation and limit.** The order revokes EO 14141. It accelerates and
coordinates lawful pathways; it does not itself issue a site permit, interconnect
a facility, secure water, finance construction, or commence operations.

### EO 14319 — Federal LLM procurement

The order directs OMB guidance within 120 days on “truth-seeking” and
“ideological neutrality” principles for agency procurement of large language
models. Contracts entered after the guidance must include compliance terms and
a vendor-paid decommissioning remedy after an opportunity to cure. Agencies are
to revise existing contracts when practicable and establish implementation
procedures within 90 days after guidance. National-security exceptions remain.

**Dependency clock.** The agency-procedure clock starts only when the required
OMB guidance issues. The order text is not itself the procurement guidance or a
finding that a vendor/model complies.

**Observed guidance.** OMB issued [M-26-04, *Increasing Public Trust in
Artificial Intelligence Through Unbiased AI
Principles*](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/M-26-04-Increasing-Public-Trust-in-Artificial-Intelligence-Through-Unbiased-AI-Principles-1.pdf)
on December 11, 2025. That starts the order’s 90-day agency-procedure clock;
the memorandum explicitly requires updated procedures by March 11, 2026. The official PDF’s first footnote appears to print “EO
14139”; the authenticated Federal Register order is EO **14319**, so the footnote
is preserved as a source defect rather than propagated as a new instrument.

### EO 14320 — American AI Exports Program

Commerce must establish and implement the American AI Exports Program within
90 days and issue a public call for full-stack proposals. Proposals are due 90
days after that call. Commerce, State, Defense, Energy, and OSTP are to select
priority packages; the Economic Diplomacy Action Group may then mobilize
Export-Import Bank, DFC, technical-assistance, and diplomatic tools.

**Observed program clocks.** Commerce/ITA announced program implementation on
[October 21, 2025](https://www.trade.gov/press-release/department-commerce-announces-american-ai-exports-program-implementation),
beginning with an RFI. It opened the formal [call for proposals on April 1,
2026](https://www.trade.gov/press-release/department-commerce-begins-inaugural-call-proposals-american-ai-exports-program),
with submissions through June 30. On [July 6,
2026](https://www.trade.gov/press-release/commerce-statement-application-conclusion-next-steps-american-ai-exports-program),
ITA reported **78 applications** and said each remained subject to review and
interagency consultation before final designation. The Federal domain census
also identifies [`aiexports.gov`](https://aiexports.gov/) as a live
Commerce/ITA surface. Application is not designation; designation is not an
export-control authorization, financing commitment, disbursement, shipment, or
foreign deployment.

### EO 14355 — pediatric cancer AI

The MAHA Commission, HHS, APST, and Special Advisor for AI and Crypto are to use
AI through the Childhood Cancer Data Initiative, prioritize existing Federal
funds, seek private engagement, improve interoperability, and finalize patient-
data standards capable of handling structured and unstructured information in
a safe and privacy-compliant way.

The order contains no new appropriation and no fixed completion deadline. NIH
Bio Genesis and the broader Genesis Mission may supply relevant infrastructure,
but a broader AI-science portfolio is not automatically implementation of every
pediatric-cancer command.

**September 7 bounded source clarification:** The order was signed September 30, 2025 and published October 7. [HHS separately announced on September 30](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-doubles-ai-backed-childhood-cancer-research-funding.html) that CCDI funding would rise from $50 million to $100 million. This announced budget response is distinct from a new appropriation in the order, award-level spending, deployed models or clinical outcomes. The [order](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/10/07/2025-19495/unlocking-cures-for-pediatric-cancer-with-artificial-intelligence) retains patient/parent control and privacy requirements. For the different question of regulatory evidence and manufactured-product acceptance, read [the existing Wave 3 biotech map](../sources/nssts-wave-3-biotech-quantum-space-map-2026-08-18-79c84751a42e.html)’s “Biomedical mission, regulatory evidence and manufacturing acceptance” section. This addition does not extend the thirteen-order census into a new government-wide survey.

### EO 14363 — Genesis Mission

**Operative architecture.** DOE must establish the American Science and
Security Platform integrating high-performance and cloud computing, AI agents
and models, Federal data, and autonomous laboratories. APST/NSTC coordinate the
cross-agency mission.

**Clocks.** From November 24, 2025: 60 days for at least 20 national science and
technology challenges; 90 days for the compute/resource inventory; 120 days for
data/model assets and an integration plan; 240 days for robotic-facility review;
270 days to seek initial operating capability; and one year for the first
report, annually thereafter.

**Observed July 22, 2026 implementation.** Official White House and agency
records describe a whole-of-government challenge portfolio and more than $5
billion in heterogeneous Federal commitments. The component clocks remain
separate:

- DOE selected 278 projects for **award negotiations**, not 278 completed
  awards or operating projects;
- NIH described more than $1.2 billion as a mixture of **obligated FY2026** and
  **planned FY2027** Bio Genesis support;
- NSF described $380 million for 20 teams over four years, plus up to $20
  million in philanthropy;
- DOE described more than $800 million in private consortium commitments mixing
  compute credits, models, cloud services, expertise, funding, and other value;
  and
- Department of War amounts were described as planned contributions, which
  should not be converted into appropriations, obligations, or completed data
  integration.

These records show a live portfolio and resource mobilization. They do not yet
prove every EO milestone, every award, or full platform initial operating
capability.

### EO 14365 — national AI policy framework

**Commands and clocks.** The order directs the Attorney General to create an AI
Litigation Task Force within 30 days; Commerce to evaluate State AI laws within
90 days; NTIA to address certain BEAD conditions within 90 days; agencies to
assess discretionary-grant conditions; FCC to consider a proceeding within 90
days after Commerce’s publication; FTC to issue an AI policy statement within
90 days; and APST/Special Advisor to recommend Federal legislation.

**Legal limit.** The President directs executive litigation, grant, regulatory,
and legislative-recommendation strategies. The order does not by itself erase
a State law or confer jurisdiction that a statute withholds. Each lawsuit,
grant condition, agency action, and congressional enactment has its own legal
clock.

**Observed implementation.** The Attorney General’s [January 9, 2026
memorandum](https://www.justice.gov/ag/media/1422986/dl) established the AI
Litigation Task Force one day before the nominal 30-day date and named the
Attorney General or designee as chair and Associate Attorney General as vice
chair. The White House published [national AI legislative
recommendations](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/03.20.26-National-Policy-Framework-for-Artificial-Intelligence-Legislative-Recommendations.pdf)
in March 2026. DOJ then [filed a complaint in
intervention](https://www.justice.gov/crt/media/1437846/dl) in *United States v.
Philip Weiser and Colorado* on April 24, 2026 challenging Colorado SB 24-205.
Those objects establish task-force creation, legislative recommendations, and
one litigation action; they do not establish a judgment, congressional
enactment, or blanket invalidation of State AI law.

### EO 14409 — frontier-model innovation and cybersecurity

**Thirty-day lane.** CNSS/Department of War and CISA are to advance AI-enabled
cyber defense for national-security and civilian systems; Treasury is to lead a
cybersecurity clearinghouse with NSA, CISA, industry, and critical-
infrastructure participants; OMB is to address grant funding; and OPM is to
support technology-force cyber hiring.

**Sixty-day lane.** Treasury, NSA, CISA, the White House, OSTP, and NIST are to
develop classified benchmarking and a threshold for “covered frontier models,”
and establish a voluntary framework for up to 30 days of pre-release Federal
access with developers and trusted partners. The order expressly disclaims
mandatory model licensing or preclearance. DOJ is to prioritize applicable
cybercrime statutes.

**Cutoff status.** The principal public deadline dates derive to July 2 and
August 1, 2026. On July 14, the White House [launched GOLD
EAGLE](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/07/white-house-launches-gold-eagle-initiative-for-unprecedented-cybersecurity-vulnerability-coordination/),
the EO-linked vulnerability-coordination clearinghouse built with Treasury,
DHS/CISA, Department of War, industry, open-source, and critical-infrastructure
partners. That establishes a named operational coordination object, not a
complete audit of every 30-day command. No separately titled public voluntary
pre-release frontier-model framework was isolated by cutoff. That remaining
public-record negative is not proof of missed classified, internal, or
nonpublic work; the benchmark lane is expressly classified.

## Claim corrections this inventory supports

1. **“Trump signed three AI executive orders in July 2025”** is true only for
   the July 23 trio. It is not the full two-term Trump AI-order inventory.
2. **A White House plan is not an executive order.** The AI Action Plan is an
   EO 14179 implementation report; OMB memoranda are agency-management
   directives; neither should be assigned invented EO numbers.
3. **Revoking EO 14110 is not the same as reviving EO 13960.** Revival requires
   text or an independently operative carrier, not assumption.
4. **EO 14318 does not “approve AI data centers.”** It creates and accelerates
   support/permitting pathways; every site retains distinct money, land, grid,
   water, environmental, construction, and operating clocks.
5. **EO 14319 is bounded to Federal procurement of LLMs.** It does not directly
   regulate all private AI expression or certify that any model is neutral.
6. **EO 14320 creates an export-program pipeline, not completed exports.** A
   proposal, priority designation, finance eligibility, export license,
   shipment, and foreign operating deployment are different objects.
7. **Genesis commitments are heterogeneous.** Selections for negotiation,
   appropriated funds, obligations, planned future funds, philanthropy, compute
   credits, expertise, datasets, integrations, and operating capability cannot
   be summed as if they are the same state.
8. **EO 14365 does not itself preempt State AI law.** It directs executive
   strategies that remain subject to statutes, agency jurisdiction, procedure,
   litigation, and judicial adjudication.
9. **GOLD EAGLE is implementation evidence, not the whole of EO 14409.** Its
   launch establishes the named clearinghouse, while classified benchmarks,
   voluntary frontier-model access, CISA directives, and workforce/grant
   carriers retain separate evidence clocks.
