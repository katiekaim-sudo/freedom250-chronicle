Arsenal policy and the path to delivery

Targeted review: September 7, 2026. These ten original Arsenal filings ask complementary questions about mission demand, acquisition, export access, maritime capacity, contractor eligibility and material supply. They share a defense-industrial thesis, but repetition of a source does not make their questions interchangeable. This section joins their legal sequence to the existing command-and-control and logistics studies. The original timeline below retains its July 15 implementation cutoff.

The sequence runs from a stated capability through procurement and supplier conditions to an award, qualified output, delivery and sustained use. An acquisition preference can change immediately while its implementation plan is still due. A proposal can define a future requirement without making it operative. A contract can commit capacity before equipment is accepted. Each filing therefore keeps its own question and clocks.

Homeland missile-defense demand

Research question: How homeland missile-defense demand connects sensing, interception, supply chains and future delivered capability.

EO 14186 was signed January 27, 2025 and published February 3. It directs a sixty-day reference architecture, requirements, implementation and funding-plan process for a layered homeland missile shield, including space sensors, interceptors and non-kinetic capabilities. It does not expressly specify directed-energy tools, and its text does not substantiate the saved largest-program or biggest-demand rankings. The later Military Modernization study records July 13, 2026 awards for thirty-six AMDT3 tracking satellites supporting Golden Dome, with launch availability expected by end-2028. That dated award is a concrete implementation return, separate from an operating shield or demonstrated nationwide defensive effectiveness.

Source and clocks: Executive Order 14186 — The Iron Dome for America. Signed 2025-01-27; filed 2025-01-31 08:45; published 2025-02-03, 90 FR 8767–8769. Sixty-day instructions run from signing. Separately saved AMDT3 award 2026-07-13; end 2028 launch-availability expectation, not launch or operation.

Defense acquisition pathways

Research question: How commercial preference, acquisition authorities, workforce and program review change the path from innovation to fielding.

EO 14265 was signed April 9, 2025 and published April 15. Its sixty-day acquisition plan favors commercial solutions and existing Other Transactions and rapid-capability pathways; section 3 also directs their lawful use during plan preparation. Separate instructions cover internal regulations, a 120-day workforce plan, ninety-day major-program review and a 180-day requirements-system review. Programs over specified cost or schedule thresholds are considered for possible cancellation, not automatically terminated. The order changes policy and begins implementation work; a completed DFARS overhaul, delivered capability or measured acceleration requires its own later record.

Source and clocks: Executive Order 14265 — Modernizing Defense Acquisitions and Spurring Innovation in the Defense Industrial Base. Signed 2025-04-09; filed 2025-04-14 08:45; published 2025-04-15, 90 FR 15621–15624. Plan/review periods 60, 90, 120, 180 days run from signing; section 3 interim prioritization begins upon issuance.

Government-wide procurement

Research question: How government-wide buying rules and agency supplements are meant to change access and procurement friction.

EO 14275 was signed April 15, 2025 and published April 18. It directs FAR reform within 180 days, retaining statutory provisions and other provisions needed for usable, effective procurement or economic and national security. It does not command deletion of every nonstatutory rule. The order directs alignment of agency supplements, ten-for-one treatment of specified new proposals by reference to EO 14192, and appropriate interim/deviation guidance before final rules. Its four-year sunset for nonstatutory provisions is an instruction to consider rule changes, not an automatic expiration. The original order establishes a reform mandate, not a completed rewrite or demonstrated savings.

Source and clocks: Executive Order 14275 — Restoring Common Sense to Federal Procurement. Signed 2025-04-15; filed 2025-04-17 08:45; published 2025-04-18, 90 FR 16447–16449. Agency designation 15 days, OMB guidance 20 days, FAR action 180 days; potential sunset would run from a later final rule's effective date.

Foreign defense sales

Research question: Whether export permissions and procurement demand reinforce domestic industrial capacity; preserve the original deregulation lens as a question about actual access and output.

EO 14268 was signed April 9, 2025; April 15 is its Federal Register publication date. It directs faster, more accountable foreign defense sales through priority partner and end-item lists, exportability design, parallel reviews and process plans. It also orders a proposal to Congress on statutory notification thresholds; the order itself does not enact that statutory change. The original export-reform lens is useful as a policy-to-industrial-capacity question. The February 2026 arms-transfer order continues this policy and makes a specific delegation amendment, while actual licensing changes, completed sales and increased production remain separate evidence.

Source and clocks: Reforming Foreign Defense Sales To Improve Speed and Accountability. Signed April 9, 2025; filed April 14 at 08:45; published April 15, 90 FR 15631–15633. Ordered tasks have separate 60-, 90- and 120-day clocks; elapsed deadlines do not establish completion.

Maritime capacity

Research question: How maritime acquisition, yards, workforce and fleet arrangements build capacity that can deliver and sustain a force.

EO 14269 was signed April 9, 2025; April 15 is publication. It orders a Maritime Action Plan and reviews and proposals covering shipbuilding, finance, workforce and federal vessel procurement. Section 15 calls for an acquisition proposal using commercial and modular design practices; section 19 covers surface, subsurface and unmanned programs. These are specified reform tasks, not evidence that approval layers were already removed or naval platforms delivered. The saved February 2026 Maritime Action Plan provides a later strategy return and is read in Contested Logistics beside its distinction between proposed programs, funded projects and measured capacity.

Source and clocks: Restoring America's Maritime Dominance. Signed April 9, 2025; filed April 14 at 08:45; published April 15, 90 FR 15635–15641. Section 3 orders the Maritime Action Plan within 210 days. The saved public plan identifies February 2026, without a specific release day or proof of timely internal submission.

Arms-transfer strategy and delegations

Research question: How foreign purchases and capital are intended to support domestic defense production, competition and new entrants.

EO 14383 was signed February 6, 2026; February 11 is publication. It establishes an arms-transfer strategy intended to use foreign purchases to expand domestic production and competition, including nontraditional firms and new entrants. It also directly amends EO 13637's delegation of specified Arms Export Control Act section 36 functions. That legal amendment is distinct from its directed sales catalog, industry engagement plan, transfer and monitoring reforms, task force and quarterly metrics. The original export-to-industrial-base thesis is preserved as the order's policy rationale; the order alone does not establish completed sales, capital received or production expanded.

Source and clocks: Establishing an America First Arms Transfer Strategy. Signed February 6, 2026; filed February 10 at 11:15; published February 11, 91 FR 6497–6500. Delegation amendment is in section 4(a)(v); ordered tasks carry distinct 30-, 60-, 90- and 120-day clocks, with quarterly metrics separately directed.

Small-business acquisition consultation

Research question: How small businesses and other participants can shape acquisition reform through a specific public input channel.

The July 9, 2025 notice, dated July 7, opened DoD-2025-OS-0018 for comments through August 8. Issued by Defense's Office of Small Business Programs, it particularly seeks small-business input on four EO 14265 topics: acquisition processes, regulations, workforce incentives and the joint requirements system. It is a delivered consultation step, not a rule adopting DFARS revisions. The saved reference to two-phase changes and February 1, 2026 class deviations requires a separately dated implementation source; that later effective state is not established by this notice. Solicited input also does not prove which recommendations were received, adopted or effective.

Source and clocks: Notice of Request for Comments on Executive Order 14265, Modernizing Defense Acquisitions. Dated 2025-07-07; filed 2025-07-08 08:45; published 2025-07-09, 90 FR 30219–30220; comments due 2025-08-08. No time stated for comment cutoff. This notice has no February 2026 effective provision.

Foreign ownership, control or influence

Research question: Who may enter and remain in a defense supply chain when ownership or foreign influence could compromise security or performance; the original guardrail-versus-deregulation comparison is research framing.

The May 7, 2026 DFARS proposal would add beneficial-ownership and foreign ownership, control or influence disclosures and risk mitigation for covered contracts and subcontracts exceeding $5 million. Commercial products and services are generally excepted unless a designated senior official finds the specified national-security risk; the proposal still uses a placeholder for that official. It would use DCSA review, SF 328 disclosures and National Industrial Security System eligibility for award, modification and options. Mitigable risks can be addressed by agreed strategies implemented within 90 calendar days; foreign ownership is not an automatic universal exclusion. Relevant postaward changes trigger updated disclosures, with distinct three-business-day reporting and ten-business-day response steps under the proposed clause.

Source and clocks: DFARS: Mitigating Risks Related to Foreign Ownership, Control, or Influence (DFARS Case 2021-D011). Filed 2026-05-06 at 08:45 (filing line does not specify timezone); published 2026-05-07, 91 FR 24783–24788; comments due 2026-07-06. No separate signature date located in this document. Proposal, not an effective final DFARS requirement. The 90-calendar-day mitigation clock runs from award, option exercise, modification or identification of performance risks; reporting clocks have their own triggers.

Contractor cybersecurity requirements

Research question: How supplier cybersecurity becomes an enforceable acquisition condition; preserve the original software/AI/microelectronics relevance without treating this sector-neutral information rule as proof of AI adoption.

The September 10, 2025 final DFARS rule supplies the contracting mechanism for the separately established CMMC program and became effective November 10. It requires the specified CMMC status and affirmation for relevant contractor systems handling Federal Contract Information or Controlled Unclassified Information when a contract carries the requirement. Solely COTS acquisitions are excluded; other commercial purchases and subcontracts can be covered. Implementation phases begin November 10 in 2025, 2026, 2027 and 2028, progressing from self-assessment requirements toward applicable Level 2 third-party and Level 3 government assessments, with stated discretion during transition. Contracting officers check SPRS before covered awards, options and extensions. Level 1 needs final status; Levels 2 and 3 may qualify conditionally for up to 180 days with a valid remediation plan. Certification is not proof of operational cybersecurity effectiveness.

Source and clocks: DFARS: Assessing Contractor Implementation of Cybersecurity Requirements (DFARS Case 2019-D041). Filed September 9, 2025 at 08:45 (no inferred timezone); published September 10, 2025, 90 FR 43560–43577; effective November 10, 2025. Separate program rule published October 15, 2024, 89 FR 83092, effective December 16, 2024. Original 32 CFR 170.3(e) yields phase 1 on November 10, 2025; phase 2 on November 10, 2026; phase 3 on November 10, 2027; phase 4 on November 10, 2028. DFARS 204.7504 states transition until November 9, 2028 and broader applicable prescription on or after November 10, 2028. No independent rollout-performance survey.

Critical materials and supply-chain mapping

Research question: How physical materials, supplier ownership, solvency, manufacturing concentration and public credit become defense procurement controls; retain the thesis of detailed supply-chain visibility without claiming achieved domestic resilience.

Signed July 20 and published July 23, EO 14415 tightens covered-material waiver policy from January 1, 2027, while retaining specified exceptions and accepted mitigation-plan routes. Referral to the Attorney General is discretionary. Its supply-chain mapping provisions direct policy and guidance within 180 days, followed by implementing regulations within 90 days of completion. Those proposed regulations are to require indentured bills of materials, supplier vetting, mitigation tracking, significant-risk notification within 15 days after completed vetting, a corrective-action plan within 45 days and closeout reporting. The original summary overstates these as immediately universal contractor duties. Acquisitions and critical supply chains remain subject to the Secretary’s scope determinations. Project Vault protection extends beyond the reserve to specified EXIM/DFC-supported foreign sources and other named agency-supported projects. AI-assisted mapping is directed; a deployed mapping system or completed material substitution is not established.

Source and clocks: EO 14415 — Securing America's Defense Supply Chains and Ensuring Domestic Acquisition of Critical Materials. Signed July 20, 2026; filed July 22 at 11:15 am (no inferred timezone); published July 23, 91 FR 46693–46697. Section 2(a) waiver restriction: January 1, 2027. Ninety days from the order is October 18, 2026 for section 4(c) strategy; 180 days is January 16, 2027 for section 2(e) remedies list, section 3(a) guidance and section 4(a) initiation of regulatory action. Section 3(a) regulations are due within 90 days of guidance completion, not on a fixed date independent of that completion. Section 5(a) reports recur every six months from the order until January 1, 2028 and may include a classified annex. The 15/45-day contractor reporting clocks run from completed vetting, not signature or publication.

Defense innovation from entry route to demonstrated result

Targeted review: September 7, 2026. Six original Tech filings ask how software, quantum computing, cyber defense and autonomy move from an idea into usable capability. Their shared question connects acquisition routes, security review, research selection, measured tests and institutional transition. These are distinct steps with different evidence, rather than interchangeable signs of a completed modernization program.

The original software-acquisition and Replicator entries link to journalism despite their saved Defense-release labels. Those captures retain their identities and original wording. The official memos and Army reporting below provide the factual anchors. The DARPA directory is a changing discovery surface; individual solicitation documents and amendments identify the opportunities and dates.

A route for buying software

Research question: How acquisition pathways and eligible contracting mechanisms change the route from software development to fielded capability.

The saved URL is a Breaking Defense article, despite its Department of Defense/Press Release labels. The controlling primary object is Hegseth's March 6, 2025 memorandum, linked from a separate March 10 DoD News explanation. It directs use of the Software Acquisition Pathway as the preferred path for software development components of business and weapon-system programs. Commercial Solutions Openings and Other Transactions become default solicitation and award approaches only where their statutory thresholds are met, for software-pathway programs still in planning before execution. The instruction is immediate and calls for an implementation plan within thirty calendar days; it does not establish measured department-wide adoption or faster delivery.

Clocks: Memorandum dated March 6, 2025; instruction effective immediately; implementation plan due within 30 calendar days. Linked official media path contains March 7, which is not proof of a public release instant. Separate DoD News article published March 10, 2025. Original seed March 6 date is the memo's date; the Breaking Defense article's publication clock was not separately reviewed.

Primary evidence: March 6 software-acquisition memorandum; March 10 official explanation.

Original capture: Directing Modern Software Acquisition To Maximize Lethality (Software Acquisition Pathway memo). Its saved metadata is historical context; the official instruments above establish the reviewed facts.

A security decision for using software

Research question: How cybersecurity and software supply-chain assurance can reduce authorization delays while preserving government risk decisions.

The linked object is Arrington's April 24, 2025 memorandum, not a press release. It directs development of SWFT and a framework and implementation plan within ninety days: cybersecurity and supply-chain requirements, rigorous verification, secure information sharing and Federal Government-led risk determinations. A May 2 publication-clearance stamp is separate from signing; the May 5 public release announces three RFIs covering tools, external assessment and automation/AI. The memo does not itself prescribe AI-assisted verification or replace all ATO processes. The RFI establishes exploration of those capabilities, not a deployed authorization service, completed framework or measured improvement.

Clocks: Memorandum stamped April 24, 2025, corroborated by its visible digital-signature date and the official release. Official mirror carries a May 2, 2025 clearance-for-open-publication stamp. Public release dated May 5, 2025 announces three RFIs and describes the 90-day framework sprint. No exact public release time or completion date established.

Primary evidence: CIO memorandum, official successor host; May 5 SWFT and RFI announcement.

A quantum concept enters independent evaluation

Research question: Which quantum concepts are plausible enough for independent government evaluation, and what would distinguish useful fault-tolerant computing from hype? Government-supported verification as a route from scientific promise to defense-relevant capability.

The April 3, 2025 release announces QBI Stage A selection for a six-month examination of proposed quantum-computing concepts. DARPA's question is whether any approach can reach utility-scale operation by 2033, defined as computational value exceeding cost; this is an evaluation target, not promised delivery or a passed benchmark. The live release includes later roster edits. Its linked Stage B page gives a subsequent, explicit November 6, 2025 state: 11 companies selected for yearlong R&D-plan and risk-reduction work. The saved August 19 company research explains the next distinction: independent evaluation, buildability, delivered hardware and government acceptance each require different evidence.

Clocks: Stage A announcement April 3, 2025; editor updates April 29 and September 9, 2025. The Stage B page explicitly dates its eleven-company roster November 6, 2025. Six-month Stage A, yearlong Stage B and the 2033 utility-scale target are distinct. Current pages retrieved September 7, 2026; saved company analysis retains its August 18/19 evidence window.

Primary evidence: Stage A announcement and editor updates; Stage B roster and evaluation steps; Separate US2QC final-phase announcement.

Cyber systems produce measured competition results

Research question: Can autonomous cyber reasoning systems find and patch software flaws at useful speed and cost, and how do the demonstrated tools reach software supporting critical infrastructure? An automated defensive capability emerging from a government challenge and moving toward wider use.

The August 8, 2025 results show a successful bounded competition: across seven finalists, systems found 54 and patched 43 of 63 synthetic vulnerabilities, plus 18 real vulnerabilities and 11 submitted patches for real vulnerabilities. DARPA corrected the synthetic denominator from 70 to 63. The 54-million-line corpus and approximately $152 average per competition task describe the challenge, not a universal cost or coverage guarantee. Four finalist systems were open source that day; the directly linked archive now provides final-release links for all seven. The result supports the original question about automated cyber defense, while critical-infrastructure integration and sustained operational outcomes remain separate evidence.

Clocks: Results announced August 8, 2025; the editor note does not date the denominator correction. The March 19 procedural announcement scheduled the DEF CON culmination for August 7–10, without establishing the exact scored-round date. Four systems were released at the results announcement; seven final-release routes were visible in the archive on September 7, 2026, without individual release dates verified.

Primary evidence: AIxCC results and correction; Finalist software-release archive; Competition procedure announcement.

Research solicitations open and change

Research question: How do standing DARPA office-wide solicitations invite new defense biotechnology, AI, cyber and information research? Each office has its own scope, and solicitation, invitation, selection, award and operational outcome are distinct steps.

This record combines two distinct FY2026 solicitations. BTO HR001126S0003 was posted October 1, 2025, with original rolling abstract and full-proposal deadlines of September 30, 2026 at 4 p.m. ET. I2O HR001126S0001 was posted November 28, 2025; its original deadlines were November 1, 2026 at 5 p.m. ET for abstracts and November 30 at 5 p.m. ET for proposals. Both require an abstract and a subsequent invitation/notification before a full proposal can be reviewed. I2O Amendment 03 later closed abstract intake effective June 23, 2026, allowing previously encouraged proposers until July 23 at 2 p.m. ET and directing other submissions to IPTO HR001126S0011. The Grants.gov listing says June 24 closure, a one-day discrepancy with the attached amendment that remains visible. The exact amendment controls this reading of solicitation terms; the generic DARPA directory cannot establish the original opening or continuing intake.

Clocks: BTO original cover/overview: October 1, 2025 posting; September 30, 2026 at 4 p.m. ET abstract and proposal cutoff. I2O original cover/overview: November 28, 2025 posting; November 1 and November 30, 2026 at 5 p.m. ET original abstract/proposal cutoffs. I2O Amendment 03 dated and effective June 23, 2026, versus June 24 closure on the Grants.gov listing; invited full proposals allowed until July 23, 2026 at 2 p.m. ET. September 7: exact-document reconciliation, no broad solicitation or award census.

Primary evidence: BTO original solicitation; I2O original solicitation; I2O Amendment 03; I2O opportunity listing and date discrepancy.

Original capture: DARPA FY2026 Office-Wide Broad Agency Announcements (Biological Technologies & Information Innovation). Its saved metadata is historical context; the official instruments above establish the reviewed facts.

Read the mechanisms together

The acquisition memo addresses how an eligible software effort is bought. SWFT addresses how security evidence should inform a government risk decision. QBI evaluates competing technical concepts against a demanding utility question. AIxCC reports measured results in a defined competition. Replicator 2 supplies a particular counter-small-UAS procurement and organizational record. DARPA's office-wide announcements invite research proposals; their amendments can close one route and point applicants to another. None of these steps substitutes for the others.

The military study follows the further integration question through the Army's common data baseline, owned interfaces, fielding and sustainment. Its saved July 15 implementation cutoff remains unchanged. The existing science/company research follows program selection, supplier roles and acceptance; its August evidence windows likewise remain separate from the original Tech dates. This shared section lets the early questions lead into those studies without copying their full explanations.

Review scope: the six original records, exact primary counterparts and directly identified corrections, amendments or later program returns. Competition measurements retain their denominators and setting; an evaluation stage is not validated utility-scale operation. A solicitation or contract is not completed research or delivered equipment. Current link accessibility and a saved analytical cutoff are not publication dates. This is not a whole-defense implementation, contractor performance or current technology-sector census.

Timeline finding

The transition accelerated in three steps:

  1. 2022–2024: strategies and common enterprise vehicles;
  2. 2025: acquisition directives, programme offices and prototype awards;
  3. 2026: common baselines, portfolio reorganizations, cancellations and production/delivery awards.

That is the point at which the story becomes vault-worthy: the architecture is beginning to acquire owners, vendors, field users and failure records.
