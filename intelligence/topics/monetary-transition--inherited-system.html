The inherited money system: claims, access, clearing and loss

Who issues money, who may hold central-bank or settlement accounts, how do claims clear, and who supplies liquidity when payment chains fail?

The inherited system is a layered constitution rather than one ledger. The Federal Reserve keeps the dollar unit and crisis backstop; Treasury is the sovereign issuer-side and fiscal authority; and Reserve Banks act as Treasury's bank and fiscal agents in designated operations. Banks, market utilities, card networks and private intermediaries create, route, record and absorb different claims. New instruments enter this structure; they do not erase the older issuer, access, collateral, liquidity or loss boundaries.

Public money and the clearing core

The Fed, Treasury and Reserve Banks perform different jobs. Fed accounts, Fedwire and central-bank settlement are access-controlled services; Treasury is the sovereign issuer-side and fiscal authority, while Reserve Banks act as Treasury's bank and fiscal agents in designated operations; DTC, NSCC and FICC maintain distinct securities, clearing and collateral functions. The current Fed and clearing work supports a narrower finding than abolition or replacement: private clearing can move into the middle while the Fed retains the currency unit, backstop and selected settlement powers.

The proposed Payment Account and related access debates show why access is a legal and supervisory question. National ex ante constraints coexist with individual Reserve-Bank adjudication; an application, eligibility determination, operating access and completed settlement are separate clocks. A proposed centrally cleared repo arrangement remains a proposal until the named membership, sponsorship, collateral, settlement and default records exist.

The Kraken example makes these distinctions concrete. Kansas City Fed’s May 8 disclosure describes Fedwire Funds-only access for Kraken Financial under existing authority, with restricted balances and no intraday or discount-window credit or interest. It does not grant access to the exchange or all Payward companies. The account, the earlier Payment Account RFI and the later May proposal have different decision clocks. Private claims and ordinary account holders

A commercial-bank deposit is a bank liability; a stablecoin, tokenized deposit, card credential and custody balance are different claims with different issuers, redemption rules and legal remedies. The private monetary stack maps issuers, custodians, networks, merchant acceptance and adapters without finding one unified ledger. A public transfer record can improve visibility while reserves, beneficial ownership, direct redemption, finality and remedy remain institutionally governed.

The foundational bank-versus-credit-union map adds the legal constitution beneath those accounts. It separates national and state bank charters, federal and state credit-union charters, stock and mutual ownership, member fields, FDIC and NCUSIF insurance, holding companies and CUSOs, public-deposit authority, tax treatment and failure resolution. Florida and Texas operate active state-CU systems with public-deposit access; New York has an active state-CU system but retains a bank/trust-company municipal-deposit rule; Wyoming has public-deposit access for federally chartered CUs but no current state-CU law.

A September 15 state-credit-union audit finds a narrower state-level transition than a private proposal for fifty state-owned credit unions. Florida and Wyoming recently admitted ordinary member-owned credit unions to public-deposit programs; Wyoming's Stable Token Commission said that change may facilitate credit-union participation, while Florida's stablecoin pilot ordinarily converts receipts to dollars held at a qualified public depository. Neither state has created an operating state-owned credit union or demonstrated a credit-union role in the digital-money chain. Public-depository approval, an actual deposit, a resulting loan and a digital-money role remain separate clocks.

Retail account agreements make the inherited system tangible. Holds, setoff, intermediaries, outages, arbitration and user-control limits are contract and operating facts. Retail debt adds the lender–merchant–servicer–buyer–regulator chain and keeps origination, servicing, sale, charge-off and loss ownership separate.

Credit approval: evidence, judgment and responsibility

The mortgage standards investigation distinguishes accepted scoring models from the risk a lender will accept. Fannie’s DU gate removal, GSE score competition, rent/cash-flow evidence and electronic verification change different parts of approval. Debt capacity, collateral, product eligibility and lender requirements remain. Fannie reports more than 21,000 applications with improved recommendations from permissioned rent data during September 2021–May 2026; that is neither additional closed loans nor a September 2026 score-reform result. FHA has announced model expansion but current inspected instructions retain existing policy pending implementation. The broader March 2026 mortgage-credit order directs agencies to consider further changes, which need their own implementing instruments.

The operating follow-up locates judgment in income classification, investigation of contradictions, validation boundaries and lender management of quality control. Fannie’s 2026 changes reduce specified document and repeat-verification requirements, introduce employer-level validation and replace fixed prefunding sampling with lender-designed risk assessment. Conditional enforcement relief changes contractual exposure. These are changes to the distribution of work and responsibility; their net effect on cost, staffing and processing time has not been established.

Retail and business lending change through different mechanisms. Named retail deployments use current account data and automated processing within lender policies. SBA’s March 2026 7(a) Small rules replace agency SBSS screening with documented repayment coverage and bank-activity corroboration. OCC/FDIC commercial guidance and the interagency model-risk framework emphasize management judgment. Separate CFPB final rules change fair-lending governance and narrow future business-data reporting; those changes do not share the mortgage initiative’s authority or implementation clock.

One mortgage through legal books and consolidated accounts

The mortgage accounting investigation follows balanced journals across the originator, warehouse lender, Fannie, trust, investor and Fed. A consolidated securitization can restore Fannie’s cash while retaining the mortgage asset and adding external trust debt. Repo introduces a separate institutional borrowing; a Fed purchase replaces the seller’s MBS with a bank deposit and creates reserves at its bank. Internal claims eliminate only within the stated reporting boundary. Selected gross claims, household debt, net assets and income are different measures. The repayment example follows principal through the same layers in reverse.

Liquidity, collateral and failure

Clearing is not settlement, collateral is not cash, and a backstop is not an ordinary operating account. The transition research therefore tracks representation, authoritative record, money, collateral, liquidity, accounting and failure as separate states. Technical atomicity cannot by itself supply legal finality, loss allocation or an exit route.

The inherited system's persistence is clearest under stress: access, liquidity and emergency authority are controlled by named institutions and legal instruments. Emergency monetary-policy research maps DPA, Stafford, IEEPA, NEA and adjacent authorities as possible activation routes, while the judicial money map tests the perimeter through master-account, preemption, charter, sovereign-asset and bank-failure cases.

The September 10 financial-market-infrastructure crosswalk identifies the international FMI/PFMI framework, U.S. Title VIII financial-market-utility category, Fed policy and Regulation HH, and SEC/CFTC clearing rules as distinct layers. Our liquidity research already cites PFMI; the follow-through maps named clearing, payment and digital arrangements to their respective authorities and connects transfer-agent record control to distinct settlement and risk functions. A standard used as an analytical benchmark is not by itself an applicability determination.
