# December 2024 Beneficial Ownership Reporting Perimeter

## Answer first

The December 3, 2024 nationwide preliminary injunction in *Texas Top Cop Shop*
did not itself permanently repeal the Corporate Transparency Act or finally
settle its constitutionality. It interrupted an approaching January 1, 2025
reporting deadline while the case proceeded. The judicial state then changed
several times: the Fifth Circuit temporarily stayed the injunction, a merits
panel restored it, and the Supreme Court stayed it pending appeal on January
23, 2025. A separate Eastern District of Texas order continued to interrupt
implementation until that court stayed its own order on February 17.

The durable change came from a distinct executive and regulatory route.
Treasury suspended enforcement against U.S. citizens and domestic reporting
companies on March 2, 2025. FinCEN's March 26 interim final rule then removed
domestic entities from the regulatory definition of reporting company,
retaining a narrower reporting perimeter for qualifying foreign entities while
exempting U.S.-person beneficial owners. On August 14, 2026, FinCEN adopted
that narrowed structure as a final rule, with additional U.S.-person
exemptions.

The lifecycle is therefore:

`broad domestic-and-foreign duty → judicial interruption → deadlines reopen →
enforcement suspension → foreign-entity-only interim perimeter → final narrowed
perimeter`

The court orders and the agency's later exemption rule are separate authority
objects. A stay did not decide the merits, and the final regulatory narrowing
was not simply the automatic legal consequence of the December injunction.

## Research question

What happened to the federal beneficial-ownership reporting obligation after
the December 3, 2024 *Texas Top Cop Shop* injunction, and what operative
reporting perimeter existed by September 25, 2026?

## Scope and method

This is a one-object lifecycle hearing. The object is the BOI reporting
perimeter under 31 CFR 1010.380, not every constitutional challenge to the CTA,
every access rule for the BOI database, or every financial institution's
separate customer-due-diligence duty.

The controlling sources are the Supreme Court docket and order, FinCEN's 2025
interim final rule and current BOI guidance, and FinCEN's August 14, 2026 final
rule. The 2026 final rule provides the agency's official procedural history of
the late-2024 and early-2025 injunctions. Litigation, enforcement discretion,
regulatory scope and filing deadlines remain separate states.

## Lifecycle matrix

| Date | Authority object | State change | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dec. 3, 2024 | E.D. Tex. preliminary injunction in *Texas Top Cop Shop* | Nationwide injunction against CTA enforcement and stay of reporting-rule deadlines | Preliminary relief; not a final merits judgment or statutory repeal |
| Dec. 23, 2024 | Fifth Circuit motions-panel order | Injunction stayed pending appeal | Temporary appellate posture |
| Dec. 26, 2024 | Fifth Circuit merits-panel order | Motions-panel stay vacated; injunction restored | Reporting remained interrupted through this case |
| Jan. 7, 2025 | E.D. Tex. order in *Smith* | Separate nationwide implementation/enforcement interruption | Independent order; later procedural change mattered even after Supreme Court acted in *Texas Top Cop Shop* |
| Jan. 23, 2025 | Supreme Court order in *McHenry v. Texas Top Cop Shop* | Stayed the *Texas Top Cop Shop* injunction pending appeal and possible certiorari | Stay addressed preliminary relief, not ultimate CTA constitutionality |
| Feb. 17–18, 2025 | *Smith* order stayed; FinCEN notice | BOI reporting again became required; most deadlines extended to Mar. 21 | Reopened duty was short-lived before Treasury changed enforcement policy |
| Mar. 2, 2025 | Treasury announcement | Enforcement suspended against U.S. citizens, domestic reporting companies and their beneficial owners | Enforcement policy preceded the regulatory amendment |
| Mar. 26, 2025 | FinCEN interim final rule, 90 FR 13688 | Domestic entities excluded from reporting-company definition; foreign reporting companies retained with U.S.-person exemptions | Immediately effective interim rule; foreign-only perimeter remained operative |
| Aug. 14, 2026 | FinCEN final rule, 91 FR 52508 | Narrowed perimeter adopted as final; U.S.-person company-applicant and FinCEN-ID-update exemptions added | Final regulatory state at cutoff; qualifying foreign entities still report non-U.S.-person BOI |

## What changed substantively

The original Reporting Rule required many domestic and foreign entities to
identify themselves, their beneficial owners and, for newer entities, company
applicants. The December injunction interrupted the compliance clock just
before the January 1, 2025 deadline for many pre-2024 companies.

The final 2026 rule does not merely postpone that broad system. It permanently
narrows the regulatory class. Domestic entities are exempt from BOI reporting.
The remaining reporting-company definition principally reaches entities formed
under foreign law and registered to do business in a U.S. jurisdiction.
Reporting companies do not report U.S.-person beneficial owners or company
applicants, and U.S. persons are not required to supply that information.

FinCEN's current guidance states the resulting deadlines:

- a qualifying foreign entity registered before March 26, 2025 was required to
  report by April 25, 2025; and
- a qualifying foreign entity registered on or after March 26, 2025 generally
  receives 30 calendar days after notice that its registration is effective.

## Authority handoff

The most important factual distinction is between interruption and redesign.

1. **Court orders interrupted the rule.** They changed whether FinCEN could
   enforce the broad reporting architecture while litigation continued.
2. **The Supreme Court changed the injunction posture.** It did not itself
   rewrite the reporting-company definition.
3. **Treasury changed enforcement policy.** That policy relieved domestic
   parties before rule text changed.
4. **FinCEN changed the regulatory perimeter.** The interim and final rules
   used statutory exemption authority to convert a broad domestic-and-foreign
   filing system into a foreign-entity-focused one.

The mature result is therefore not “the court killed BOI reporting.” The
federal obligation migrated: from a broad domestic ownership registry toward
a much smaller foreign-entity reporting perimeter, with explicit U.S.-person
exemptions.

## Non-findings and boundaries

- The December 3 injunction was not a final Supreme Court merits ruling.
- A stay of an injunction did not establish that every CTA provision was
  constitutional.
- Treasury's non-enforcement announcement did not itself amend 31 CFR
  1010.380; the March 26 rule did.
- Domestic BOI exemption does not erase State entity-registration rules,
  financial institutions' customer-due-diligence obligations, sanctions
  screening or other ownership-disclosure regimes.
- “Foreign reporting company” does not mean every foreign entity; registration
  to do business in a U.S. State or Tribal jurisdiction and regulatory
  exemptions still matter.
- Filing, database access, enforcement and observed law-enforcement use are
  distinct objects. This package does not establish the number, quality or
  downstream use of reports.
- No astrology interpretation is part of this factual package.
