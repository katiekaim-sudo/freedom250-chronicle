Freedom 250 · Banks and the dollar · Treasury payments

Has the federal government really stopped sending paper checks?

Mostly, not entirely. Executive Order 14247 (signed March 25, 2025 · published March 28, 2025) ordered an end to paper checks for federal payments, and a customs notice of July 8, 2026 says the government no longer sends them; but the Treasury's own April 29, 2026 proposal still sets out when agencies may send one, and the PBGC was moving its remaining paper-check recipients on June 24, 2026. In FY2026 Treasury screened >1.1 billion payment requests (about $3.7 trillion) before the money went out.

What's still uncertain

  • Two readings in the record: the customs notice (July 8, 2026) says paper checks have stopped, while the Treasury's proposed rule (April 29, 2026) keeps limited exceptions and the PBGC notice (June 24, 2026) refers to remaining paper-check recipients. The library's order-status reading for EO 14247 is: most disbursements electronic · exceptions remain.
  • The library has no record of the Treasury disbursement proposal (April 29, 2026) becoming a final rule.
  • The Federal Reserve Payments Study row carries no numbers in the master; the check-volume figures it reports are not shown here.
  • Screening numbers: A match is not an eligibility decision. A returned request is not an ACH return, final denial, fraud finding, recovered dollar or audited saving. Future-ledger seams describe what a permissioned provenance layer could record; they are not deployed-system claims.
