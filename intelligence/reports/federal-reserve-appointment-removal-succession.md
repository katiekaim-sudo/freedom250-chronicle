## Bottom line

The Federal Reserve has no single personnel chain. Congress built five overlapping ones:

1. **Article II chain:** the President nominates and the Senate confirms the seven Governors and separately confirms the Chair, Vice Chair and Vice Chair for Supervision designations.
2. **Regional corporate chain:** member banks elect Class A and B Reserve Bank directors; the Board of Governors appoints Class C directors; eligible Class B and C directors appoint the Bank president and first vice president subject to Board approval.
3. **FOMC chain:** Governors serve ex officio; Reserve Bank boards elect five representatives and five alternates annually; the Committee elects its own chair, vice chair and staff officers and selects the SOMA manager and deputy manager.
4. **Internal management chain:** the Board governs its staff and delegates powers to specified members and employees; each Bank board and president govern Bank officers and employees, with an independence carveout for the general auditor.
5. **Oversight chain:** the Board Chair appoints the joint Board/CFPB Inspector General, but removal requires written concurrence of two-thirds of the Board plus advance written reasons to Congress under the designated-federal-entity rules.

No actor controls every chain. The President cannot directly select a Reserve Bank president, a Class A or B director, an FOMC Bank representative, the SOMA manager or a Bank general auditor. Member banks cannot select Governors or Class C directors, and since Dodd–Frank their Class A representatives cannot participate in appointing a Bank president. The Board of Governors is the principal junction: it approves Bank presidents and first vice presidents, appoints Class C directors, designates Reserve Bank chairs and deputy chairs, supervises the Banks and may suspend or remove any Reserve Bank officer or director under 12 U.S.C. §248(f).

## 2. The federal Board chain

### Governor office

Sections 241–245 create seven Governor seats, each tied to a staggered fourteen-year term rather than to the individual occupant. The President nominates and the Senate confirms. No two Governors may be selected from the same Federal Reserve district, the President must give due regard to specified geographic and economic representation, and at least one Governor must have demonstrated primary experience working in or supervising smaller community banks.

Three succession details matter:

- A person appointed after a midterm vacancy serves only the predecessor’s unexpired term.
- Service for an unexpired term does not bar a later full fourteen-year appointment; service of a full term does.
- At expiration, a Governor continues until a successor is appointed and qualified. A political failure to confirm a successor therefore does not automatically empty the seat.

Section 245 separately authorizes a recess commission expiring with the next Senate session. That is a statutory restatement of a temporary route, not a way to create a new fourteen-year tenure without Senate confirmation.

### Chair and vice-chair designations are separate offices layered on Governor tenure

The President designates, and the Senate separately confirms, a Chair, Vice Chair and Vice Chair for Supervision from among sitting Governors for four-year terms. Those leadership terms do not create additional Board votes and cannot outlast the underlying Governor service.

The Chair is the Board’s “active executive officer” **subject to the Board’s supervision**. The Chair manages and represents the institution but does not become a unilateral substitute for the seven-member Board. The statute also supplies meeting continuity: Vice Chair in the Chair’s absence; a Board-elected chair pro tempore if both are absent.

The Federal Reserve Act expressly protects the Governor seat with “for cause” language. It does not separately spell out whether or under what standard the President may terminate a four-year leadership designation while leaving the person as a Governor. That designation question should remain marked unresolved unless a controlling case or later statute answers it.

### Governor removal after *Trump v. Cook*

The Supreme Court’s June 29, 2026 stay decision is now part of the operative structure. It denied the Government’s request to displace the injunction protecting Governor Lisa Cook, treated the Fed’s for-cause arrangement as consistent with a distinct historical central-banking tradition, and required notice plus an opportunity to respond before removal. It did not finally decide whether the asserted mortgage-related allegations constitute cause after adequate process.

The safe rule is therefore not “Governors can never be removed” and not “cause means whatever the President states.” It is:

> a Governor has a constitutionally valid statutory for-cause protection, judicial review is available, and lawful process is required; the substantive boundary of cause remains fact- and case-dependent.

## 3. The nine-director Reserve Bank chain

Each Reserve Bank board contains three classes of three directors:

- **Class A:** elected by member banks and representative of stockholding banks.
- **Class B:** elected by member banks but required to represent the public; may not be an officer, director or employee of any bank.
- **Class C:** appointed by the Board to represent the public; may not be an officer, director, employee or stockholder of any bank and must meet district-residency requirements.

The Board divides each district’s member banks into three groups by capitalization. Each group elects one Class A and one Class B director. The route is not “the largest banks own six seats”: each member bank casts the vote assigned by statute within its group, and affiliated member banks under one bank holding company are restricted to one participating bank in the nomination/election process.

Terms are staggered and last three years. Vacancies are filled through the same route as original selection, only for the remaining term. Board policy generally limits directors to two full terms or seven continuous years; that is a policy control, not the text of §308.

The Board annually designates a chair and deputy chair from the three Class C directors. The chair is also the statutory Federal Reserve agent and official Board representative at the Bank. This office is a built-in federal oversight junction inside the regional corporation, even though many historical Reserve-agent functions have changed in practice.

### Dodd–Frank’s division inside the board

All nine directors govern the Bank subject to federal law and Board orders, but they do not all exercise every appointment power. Since Dodd–Frank, Class A directors may not participate in selecting the Bank president or first vice president. The six Class B and C directors supply the local appointment; the Board supplies required federal approval.

That produces an important distinction:

> member banks elect the three Class B public representatives, and those directors may help select the president; member banks’ Class A representatives cannot.

The September 1, 2026 OIG review found that Board controls over Class C and president/FVP selection exist but need stronger conflict checks, documented roles and guidance. Its ten recommendations are evidence of control weaknesses, not evidence that the statutory route ceased to operate.

## 4. President, first vice president and other Bank officers

Section 341 makes the president chief executive officer and all other executive officers and employees directly responsible to the president. The president and first vice president share the same appointment route and five-year term. Their Systemwide terms run concurrently; the current cycle is March 1, 2026 through February 28, 2031. A mid-cycle appointee receives only the remainder, and reappointment is permitted.

The first vice president is not merely a generic deputy. The statute makes that officer acting chief executive during the president’s absence or disability and during a vacancy. Public law does not fully answer simultaneous president/FVP vacancies; the current bylaws and emergency succession instruments of the particular Bank must be consulted.

The removal chain has two routes:

1. the Bank board’s §341 authority to dismiss officers and employees “at pleasure”; and
2. the Board’s §248(f) authority to suspend or remove any Reserve Bank officer or director, with the stated cause communicated in writing.

As Wave 1 explains, DOJ OLC reads §248(f) as plenary rather than substantive for-cause protection and concludes that a local board may dismiss a president who serves as an FOMC officer only with Board approval. That second proposition is a constitutional saving construction, not express wording in §341.

### General auditor independence

The general auditor breaks the ordinary management chain. Federal Reserve policy requires direct reporting to the Bank board through its audit committee so that the auditor is not dependent on the president or another operating officer for job security. The audit committee recommends appointment and termination and concurs in reassignment; the Bank board acts. The Bank chair is expected to consult the Board’s Federal Reserve Bank affairs committee at least two weeks before appointment, termination or reassignment.

This is consultation and oversight, not proof that the Board of Governors itself appoints each Bank general auditor.

## 5. Branch boards

Board policy permits five- or seven-member branch boards. The Reserve Bank appoints a majority; the Board appoints the remainder. Bank-appointed directors may meet Class A or B qualifications, while Board-appointed branch directors must meet the public-representative qualifications. Terms are three years, staggered under policy, and the Reserve Bank selects each branch chair from the Board-appointed directors.

Branch boards provide local intelligence and governance but do not receive a statutory FOMC vote or an independent central-bank charter. Their composition should not be confused with the nine-member board of the separately incorporated Reserve Bank.

## 6. The FOMC is a second appointment layer

### Membership

All sitting Governors are FOMC members ex officio. The five Reserve Bank representatives and five alternates are elected annually by the Bank boards in the five statutory groupings. Each Bank board—not each director—has one vote in a grouped election. If an elected representative or alternate ceases to be a president or first vice president, the relevant boards may hold a special election for the remainder of the annual term. The Committee determines disputed election or eligibility questions before participation, and each member and alternate takes the federal oath.

### Chair and vice chair

The FOMC’s rules provide that the Committee selects a chair and vice chair from among its own members at the first regularly scheduled meeting of each year. The familiar pairing—Board Chair as FOMC Chair and New York Fed president as Vice Chair—is entrenched practice. It is not the wording of §263 and is not a presidential appointment to those Committee offices.

The rules provide a continuity ladder: if both Committee chair and vice chair are absent, the Board Vice Chair acts; if all three are absent, the present Governor with the longest Board service acts.

### Staff officers and SOMA management

The Committee annually selects its secretary, counsel, economists and other staff officers from existing Board and Bank officers and employees. These are FOMC offices layered on an underlying employment relationship; the FOMC office ends if the person leaves that employment or the Committee selects a successor.

The Committee also selects the SOMA manager and deputy manager. They must be satisfactory to the Reserve Bank selected to execute FOMC transactions and serve at the Committee’s pleasure. If one cannot perform, the FOMC Chair may name an interim person satisfactory to the selected-Bank president until the Committee and Bank complete the replacement route.

That is a genuine dual-key arrangement: the FOMC controls the monetary-policy execution office, while the selected Reserve Bank must accept the person operating within its institution.

## 7. Board staff, delegations and the Inspector General

Board staff are not Senate-confirmed merely because they may exercise significant delegated responsibilities. Section 244 places employment and compensation under the Act and Board rules. Part 265 assigns specified decisional powers to Board members, division directors, the Secretary and other staff. The legal principal remains the Board where the authority is delegated; the Board can amend or revoke the delegation.

The Inspector General follows a special statutory route:

- appointment by the Board Chair on merit criteria and without regard to political affiliation;
- joint jurisdiction over the Board and CFPB;
- general supervision by the designated-entity head, with statutory decisional independence; and
- removal only with written concurrence of two-thirds of the Board and reasons transmitted to both Houses of Congress at least thirty days in advance.

This makes the IG more removal-protected than ordinary Board staff, but the office is not currently presidentially appointed or Senate confirmed. Proposals to change that route are proposals unless enacted.

The OIG’s own published scope also preserves the entity seam: it oversees the Reserve Banks’ performance of Board-delegated functions, but ordinary Bank activities are generally audited by Reserve Bank general auditors rather than by the Board/CFPB OIG.

## 8. Appointment power is not operating command

| Power | What it establishes | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| President nominates Governors | Article II democratic selection at the federal Board | Presidential authority to direct an individual monetary-policy vote |
| Senate confirms | Political accountability and qualification gate | Continuing Senate command over the officer |
| Board appoints Class C director | Federal placement of three public directors in each Bank | Board authorship of every local board decision |
| Member banks elect Class B director | Regional input into public-representative seats | Private ownership of the monetary-policy committee |
| Board approves Bank president | Essential national approval and the central link in OLC’s Appointments Clause theory | Senate confirmation or unilateral Board selection of the local nominee |
| FOMC elects its chair | Committee control of its own presiding officer | A second presidential appointment |
| FOMC selects SOMA manager | Committee control over the execution office | FOMC employment of every New York Desk employee or ownership of FRBNY |
| Board may remove Bank officer/director | Powerful federal supervisory backstop | Proof that the power has been used, or that every local decision is a Board act |
| Appointment of an auditor | Selection of the oversight official | Power to dictate audit findings or suppress lawfully protected work |

## 10. Corrected shorthand

| Unsafe shorthand | Correct rule |
|---|---|
| “The President appoints the Fed Chair and therefore the FOMC Chair.” | The President/Senate designate the Board Chair. The FOMC separately elects its chair annually from its members. |
| “Member banks appoint Reserve Bank presidents.” | Eligible Class B and C directors appoint subject to Board approval; Class A directors are excluded. |
| “The Board appoints every Reserve Bank director.” | It appoints Class C directors and a minority of branch directors; member banks elect Class A and B Reserve Bank directors. |
| “A Governor’s fourteen-year term belongs to the person.” | The staggered seat has the term. A midterm successor serves the remainder and may later receive a full term. |
| “Term expiration always creates a vacancy.” | Governors hold over until successors are appointed and qualified; other offices use their own succession rules. |
| “The President may remove any Fed official.” | Presidential for-cause removal reaches Governors. Other System offices use Bank-board, Board, FOMC or inspector-general routes. |
| “The Fed IG is Senate confirmed.” | Under current law the Board Chair appoints the joint Board/CFPB IG; two-thirds Board concurrence is required for removal. |
| “The New York Fed president is FOMC vice chair by statute.” | The New York president is the continuously represented Bank member; FOMC vice-chair selection is an annual Committee choice. |
| “Bank presidents have lifetime tenure.” | They serve renewable five-year terms and are subject to retirement policy and two removal routes. |
| “A general auditor reports to the Bank president.” | Federal Reserve policy places the general auditor in a direct board/audit-committee reporting chain. |

## 11. Wave 3 ruling

The appointment constitution is neither fully national nor fully regional. Congress placed democratic appointment and removal at the Board, regional election and nomination in the Banks, and a second annual selection layer in the FOMC. The Board of Governors is the decisive junction because it both appoints part of every Bank board and must approve the presidents from whom FOMC representatives are drawn, while retaining statutory suspension/removal power over Bank officers and directors.

But junction is not unity. The President, Senate, Board, member banks, Bank boards, FOMC, audit committees and selected execution Bank each possess different keys. A claim of “control” is proved only by naming which key reaches the decision at issue.

## Non-claims

This report does not conclude that appointment equals daily command; that member banks own or control the FOMC; that the President may never remove a Governor; that “cause” is fully defined; that the Board has exercised §248(f) against a president; that every FOMC staff officer is independently subject to Senate confirmation; that a policy rotation limit is a statutory tenure limit; or that an unfilled office suspends every function of the relevant institution.
