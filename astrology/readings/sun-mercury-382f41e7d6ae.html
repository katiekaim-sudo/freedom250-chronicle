Sun–Mercury

The planets

Sun — Authority, illumination, "everything revolves around the Sun." MUNDANE: the head of government (the President); the executive branch; the focus of executive power. The Sun's condition shows how the government will fare. (Greer: "the Sun represents the actual head of government — whoever officially calls the shots in the executive branch.")

Mercury — Communication, intellect, commerce, transport. MUNDANE: the media; the transportation and communications infrastructure; trade and commerce; education and intellectual life; the postal system and telecommunications. Mercury's condition shows how information flows through the nation — freely or obstructed, truthfully or deceptively.

The combination — mundane

Sun and Mercury are never far apart, so their only major aspect is the conjunction. Greer describes this as "favorable for intellectual and scientific activities; important speeches and successful public relations by the head of government." When the Sun and Mercury meet, the President speaks and the nation listens. Major policy addresses, State of the Union moments, press conferences that actually land — these cluster around Sun-Mercury conjunctions. The executive branch's message gets through clearly.

This is the government's best window for public communication. Scientific announcements carry weight, education policy gains traction, and the media amplifies rather than distorts the executive's message. Trade negotiations benefit from clear terms. Because Mercury also rules transport, infrastructure announcements or ribbon-cuttings under this conjunction tend to be well-received and effective.

The absence of hostile aspects between these two planets means Sun-Mercury is almost always a mildly favorable influence. The main risk is overconfidence in messaging — the government may assume its words landed when the audience was already tuned out.

As trigger: The Sun-Mercury conjunction is fast-moving (exact for roughly a day, with an orb of influence lasting about a week) and marks specific moments when executive communication activates whatever broader storylines are in play.
