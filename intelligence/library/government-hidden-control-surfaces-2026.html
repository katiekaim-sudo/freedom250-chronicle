The government rooms hiding between the boxes

Our agency map is strong, but we have not consistently tracked the recurring public rooms where officials hear requests, coordinate methods, choose projects and turn advice into later action.

What we found

  • The Source Monitor has 1,077 official routes across 398 entities, but its route vocabulary has no first-class category for meeting indexes, minutes, recommendations, agency responses, stakeholder contacts, board actions or implementation dashboards.
  • The Federal Reserve quietly publishes a current stakeholder-contact ledger with participants, topics and attachments, while its statutory Federal Advisory Council publishes substantial quarterly records. These show what was requested or reported before later action, but never prove influence or agreement by themselves.
  • ACUS has an active fall 2026 pipeline on permits, regulatory warnings and frontline decisions. Draft, committee approval, plenary adoption, publication and agency use are separate clocks.
  • GSA's Federal Secure Cloud Advisory Committee publishes unusually rich minutes and recommendation records. July minutes reported 3,320 active agency authorizations and incomplete lifecycle reporting; a November vote on adoption and reuse is still future.
  • The Technology Modernization Fund portfolio exposes project selection, transfers, obligations, delay and cost-overrun flags. It is a rare bridge from policy priority to implementation state, though it is not independent proof of outcome.

Why it matters

These are the connective tissues of procedural reconstruction. Tracking a few material surfaces can reveal requests, shared methods, funding choices and implementation bottlenecks before a final rule, deployment or audit appears—without pretending that advice is authority or that a meeting caused the later decision.

What to watch

  • October 1–20, 2026 · ACUS fall committee meetings and any revised drafts, votes or committee dispositions.
  • October 20, 2026 · TMF initial proposal date for the 2026 permitting-technology and AI call.
  • November 2, 2026 · FSCAC meeting and planned vote on FedRAMP adoption-and-reuse recommendations.
  • December 10–11, 2026 · ACUS plenary disposition and TMF's stated Board-consideration boundary.

Research summary

Freedom 250's largest newly identified blind spot is the relationship-and-process layer between entities and final decisions. The completed program holds a 1,025-body closed-FY2025 FACA denominator, a live FY2026 agency overlay, a corrected 15-body executive-council union, a reconciled 41-center FFRDC census and a terminal 114-row control-surface census: 82 complete and 32 held through the cutoff. Waves 0–13 close the bounded research sweep, membership denominator, sparse-council stops, action joins and the complete CFTC advisory family. Wave 14 completed the governed canonical promotion at 650 Federal Group nodes, 397 institutional seed rows, 110 stable relationship endpoints, 99 typed relationships, 35 promoted membership profiles and 30 exact tree bindings, with no hierarchy-tree nodes added. A held sweep state is an evidence-routing stop and does not by itself mean that a body's legal lifecycle is inactive or terminated. A meeting, recommendation, affiliation, support relationship, board selection or approval never substitutes for later instrument, obligation, operation or effect.
