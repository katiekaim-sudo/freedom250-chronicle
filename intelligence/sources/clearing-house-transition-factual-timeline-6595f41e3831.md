## Inclusion rules

- Entries record a dated public rule, filing, approval, launch, operating change, publication, speech, or formally announced deadline.
- Descriptions state what the cited source says. They do not assign a common motive to the institutions or characterize the entries as one coordinated program.
- `Occurred` means the dated action or publication took place.
- `Proposed` means a rule, service, or operating change remains subject to approval or implementation.
- `Scheduled` means a future date or window was announced by a regulator or institution.
- `Public position` records a speech or stated institutional view; it is not treated as adopted policy.
- Dates are stated only to the precision supported by the source. A month, quarter, or half-year target is not converted into an invented day.
- Each event is indexed below with time and place metadata for later astrological work. `Not publicly stated` is preserved as such; no default noon time is presented as historical fact.
- `Place` identifies the best-supported locus of the action: the regulator's seat for a regulatory order, the announced meeting site for a speech or workshop, and the operating institution's principal site when a source describes an institutional launch without a single physical execution point.
- A distributed technical go-live does not literally occur at one place. Those entries are marked `distributed operation`, with the institution's principal site shown only as the proposed charting anchor.
- DTC is a central securities depository rather than a central counterparty. It is included because its custody, book-entry, settlement, and tokenization functions are directly connected to the clearing-house transition.
- DTCC is the parent organization of FICC, NSCC, and DTC. Entries identify the operating subsidiary when the source does so.

### Recommended charting hierarchy

For later analysis, the cleanest event hierarchy is:

1. **Exact operational activation:** stated production go-live time and operating center; strongest example here is NSCC at 8:00 p.m. ET on June 28, 2026.
2. **Regulatory creation or approval:** order date at Washington, D.C.; use the actual Commission vote or release timestamp if retrievable.
3. **Corporate/legal creation:** incorporation acceptance time and jurisdiction, not the later headquarters location.
4. **Board adoption:** actual meeting place and vote time, if disclosed.
5. **Public announcement:** dateline city and release timestamp; keep separate from the underlying approval or launch.
6. **Distributed technical event:** use the documented control/operations center only when known; otherwise mark the chart location as a convention.
7. **Month or quarter target:** not a chartable historical event until a dated action occurs.

## 2019

### 2019-09-17 — Federal Reserve begins overnight repo operations during money-market stress

**Status:** Occurred  
**Entities:** Federal Reserve Bank of New York; primary dealers; triparty clearing banks  
**Track:** Repo liquidity / settlement infrastructure

The New York Fed began overnight repurchase-agreement operations after overnight funding rates rose sharply. The operations supplied reserves against Treasury, agency-debt, and agency mortgage-backed-securities collateral through the Desk's existing repo infrastructure.

**Source:** [New York Fed — Statement Regarding Repurchase Operations](https://www.newyorkfed.org/markets/opolicy/operating_policy_190917)

## 2020

### 2020-03 — Treasury-market disruption produces unusually severe liquidity strains

**Status:** Occurred  
**Entities:** Treasury market participants; primary dealers; Federal Reserve; FICC  
**Track:** Market stress / central clearing policy background

During the March 2020 market disruption, Treasury-market liquidity deteriorated and dealer balance-sheet intermediation came under strain. Subsequent official reviews treated the episode as part of the policy background for changes to Treasury-market structure, including expanded central clearing.

**Sources:** [Financial Stability Oversight Council — 2020 Annual Report](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/261/FSOC2020AnnualReport.pdf); [Inter-Agency Working Group — Recent Disruptions and Potential Reforms in the U.S. Treasury Market](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/136/IAWG-Treasury-Report.pdf)

## 2023

### 2023-12-13 — SEC adopts expanded Treasury central-clearing rules

**Status:** Occurred  
**Entities:** SEC; covered Treasury clearing agencies; direct participants; Treasury-market counterparties  
**Track:** Treasury clearing mandate

The SEC adopted rules requiring covered clearing agencies to require their direct participants to submit specified secondary-market cash Treasury and Treasury repo transactions for central clearing. The rules also required covered clearing agencies to separate the calculation, collection, and holding of margin for direct-participant proprietary transactions from margin for indirect-participant transactions.

**Source:** [SEC — Standards for Covered Clearing Agencies for U.S. Treasury Securities and Application of the Broker-Dealer Customer Protection Rule](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2023/34-99149.pdf)

**Status:** Occurred  
**Entities:** DTC; NSCC; broker-dealers; investment managers; custodians  
**Track:** Settlement-cycle compression

The standard settlement cycle for most U.S. broker-dealer securities transactions moved from two business days after trade date to one business day. DTC and NSCC implemented processing and operational changes supporting the shorter cycle.

**Source:** [SEC — Shortening the Securities Transaction Settlement Cycle](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2023-29)

### 2024-07 — FICC publishes an industry survey estimating mandate effects

**Status:** Occurred / research publication  
**Entities:** FICC/GSD; 83 sell-side Netting Members  
**Track:** Treasury clearing capacity / margin / liquidity

FICC published survey estimates that the Treasury mandate could add more than $4 trillion of daily clearing activity. Respondents estimated approximately $58.4 billion of aggregate incremental value-at-risk margin and a potential maximum daily liquidity need of $84.5 billion under the survey's assumptions.

**Source:** [DTCC — The U.S. Treasury Clearing Mandate: An Industry Pulse Check](https://www.dtcc.com/ustclearing/-/media/WhitePapers/Treasury-Clearing-Mandate.pdf)

### 2024-09 — NSCC begins an earlier daily clearing window

### 2026-09-01 — SEC proposes electronic and DLT transfer-agent rules

**Status:** Proposed  
**Entities:** SEC; registered transfer agents; issuers; service providers  
**Track:** Authoritative securities records / tokenization

The SEC issued proposed Exchange Act Release No. 34-106246 / File No.
S7-2026-30. The proposal would require electronic master securityholder files,
permit them to use multiple linked systems—including a blockchain or DLT as the
file or a component—and keep one registered recordkeeping transfer agent
responsible for and in exclusive control of each fungible issue. It would also

procedures, restrictive-legend controls and modernized T+1/electronic
processing provisions.

No matching Federal Register public-inspection or publication object was
located at the September 1 cutoff. The exact comment deadline therefore
remained 60 days after Federal Register publication rather than a fixed
calendar date. The proposal is not final or effective and does not approve any
chain, company or token.
