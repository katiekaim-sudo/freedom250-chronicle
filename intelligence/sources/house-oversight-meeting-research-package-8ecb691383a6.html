The strongest finding is not simply that the committee discussed fraud often. It is that the record shows a staged movement from broad diagnosis toward a proposed operating architecture:

improper-payment / fraud diagnosis
  → prevention rather than recovery
  → shared analytics and data access
  → identity, eligibility, provider and account validation
  → Treasury pre-payment screening and payment intervention
  → program-specific test beds in state programs, Medicaid and SNAP
  → continuing identity/account risk at the July 15 hearing

That chain is surrounded by parallel lanes—federal IT, AI, procurement, workforce restructuring, DoD audit, debt/tax administration, immigration records, digital investigations and private technology vendors. The meetings are most valuable when read as a linked committee program rather than 107 isolated political events.

What changed across the window

1. The 119th-Congress committee was institutionally reset

The January 14, 2025 organizational meeting established the new committee rules and panel structure. The date window therefore crosses both a Congress boundary and a committee-name boundary: the 118th-Congress tail used Oversight and Accountability; the 119th Congress uses Oversight and Government Reform.

2. Fraud policy moved upstream

The February 12 and March 11, 2025 hearings articulated the policy pivot: from detecting and recovering money after payment toward identity, eligibility, data and analytics controls before payment. The 2026 record then made the architecture more concrete through Treasury, Do Not Pay, PRAC/analytics, state-administered programs, provider integrity and proposed payment intervention.

3. Markups turned hearing themes into bill packages

The key anti-fraud legislative sequence is not a single bill or a single meeting:

  • March 25, 2025: proposed PRAC continuation/transition and related government-reorganization measures.
  • March 18, 2026: program-integrity, exclusion, savings, transparency and fiscal-preparedness measures.
  • April 29, 2026: nine-bill anti-fraud/program-integrity package, including H.R. 8312, H.R. 8463, H.R. 8464 and H.R. 8467.

The May 20, 2026 business meeting belongs to the adjacent government-efficiency/declassification lane—GAO duplication scoring, D.C. congestion charges, CBP retirement corrections and cold-case records—not to the April anti-fraud package.

The April 29 markup is the legal center of gravity. H.R. 8312 proposes a permanent fraud-focused inspector-general function at Treasury; it does not create an Attorney General inside Treasury. H.R. 8463 concerns Treasury data access and pre-payment verification; H.R. 8464 concerns intervention in elevated-risk payment requests. Committee approval, House passage and enactment remain separate states.

4. Technology is not an adjacent topic; it is the proposed control layer

The archive repeatedly joins AI, federal IT, cybersecurity, cryptography, identity proofing, private analytics vendors, background-investigation systems, procurement and data-center power. The strongest technology sequence is:

  • April 1, 2025 — AI/data-center economics and power;
  • April 29, 2025 — federal IT modernization;
  • June 5, 2025 — federal AI adoption, procurement and risk;
  • July 22, 2025 — private-company AI demonstrations;
  • September 16–17, 2025 — DoD identity-system modernization and AI policy;
  • December 10, 2025 — AI/data tools in human-trafficking investigations;
  • February 4 and April 16, 2026 — technology/procurement legislation and sovereign-compute framing;
  • July 14–15, 2026 — China/AI/energy competition followed by identity/fraud-risk controls.

Source and status controls

  • A committee hearing page proves a committee event record, not the truth of every claim made there.
  • An improper payment can be an overpayment, underpayment, missing documentation, timing issue or eligibility error; it is not automatically fraud.
  • A risk score, watchlist hit or data match is an indicator, not an adjudication.
  • A markup action is not House passage, Senate action, enactment or implementation.
  • The Treasury payment rail, the agency's eligibility decision, an inspector-general investigation and a DOJ prosecution are distinct authority layers.
  • A private vendor demonstration is not proof of a federal deployment or procurement.
  • Current roundtable archive HTML contains a seven-day date defect in the hidden datetime field. Use the visible event date and corroborating event materials.
  • Scheduled page times are not verified first-gavel times. Recover the audible gavel from video only when later chart work requires an event time.
