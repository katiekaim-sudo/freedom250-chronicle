# Wave 1D — Pressured Institutions

**Research window:** January 20 through March 19, 2025.  
**Question:** Which institutional bodies could the incoming regime actually eliminate, which congressionally created bodies could only be reduced or starved, and where did law, appropriations, courts or continuing operation force a fourth act after the attempted dissolution?  
**Finding:** The February and March reduction orders did not perform one uniform act called “dismantling government.” They separated the institutional field into two species. Nonstatutory executive creations—Federal Executive Boards, selected advisory committees and eventually the Presidential Management Fellows program—could be terminated through executive and regulatory action. Statutory bodies were instead ordered toward a **minimum lawful presence**, with OMB positioned at the funding-request gate. That produced real staffing, grant and service cuts, but not organic-act repeal. The later record divides again: some bodies were restored, some restructured and continued, some remained in unresolved litigation, and three later absorbed enacted account cuts without losing their charters.

This packet follows the institutional object through later maturity so that a March 2025 direction is not mistaken for a completed March 2025 result. The astrology is an archetypal hearing, not causal proof or a claim that the signing date explains the legal outcome.

## The chain in one view

```text
name an entity “unnecessary”
    ↓
separate nonstatutory functions from statutory minimums
    ↓
require a compliance-and-charter report to OMB
    ↓
cut staff, grants, services or funding requests
    ↓
test the cut against organic law, appropriations and procedure
    ↓
restore, restructure, remain contested, or acquire a later enacted account cut
```

The entity’s name is therefore only the first clock. Legal person, personnel, program operation, money and contest can all move differently.

## 1D.1 — February 10–19: direct executive removal and the statutory-minimum experiment

### February 10 — the Federal Executive Institute is selected for direct elimination

**Mover.** The President and OPM.

**Institutional change.** EO 14207 directed OPM to eliminate the Federal Executive Institute. Unlike a congressionally chartered department or independent agency, FEI was an executive-branch leadership-development institution inside OPM. The order could remove the programmatic carrier without asking Congress to repeal an organic statute.

**Maturity.** Executive direction in this wave; later personnel, property, records and program closure require their own receipts.

**Counterevidence and limit.** Eliminating a training institute is not eliminating the Senior Executive Service, the civil service or executive-development authority across government.

**Plotline.** The regime begins by removing the institutions that reproduce the prior managerial class.

### February 19 — EO 14217 deliberately uses two different legal verbs

**Mover.** The President; OMB; OPM; the heads of the Presidio Trust, Inter-American Foundation, U.S. African Development Foundation and U.S. Institute of Peace; and the officials responsible for the named advisory bodies.

**Institutional change.** [EO 14217](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/02/25/2025-03133/commencing-the-reduction-of-the-federal-bureaucracy) required:

- elimination of **nonstatutory components and functions** of the four named entities;
- reduction of their **statutory functions and personnel** to the minimum presence required by law;
- a 14-day report identifying which functions were statutorily required;
- rejection of inconsistent funding requests, but only to the extent consistent with law;
- withdrawal of the regulations supporting the Federal Executive Boards and Presidential Management Fellows program;
- termination of five named advisory committees; and
- a 30-day White House search for additional entities and committees.

**Maturity.** A live reduction command plus a charter-classification exercise. It did not dissolve the four statutory legal persons on signature.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The order’s own grammar concedes the boundary: statutory functions survive. “Minimum” is not self-defining, and the report to OMB is not a completed closure, transfer or rescission. The order preserved existing legal authority and made implementation subject to law and appropriations.

**Plotline.** The center does not merely attack an institution; it demands that the institution prove which organs the law requires. The charter becomes a survival test.

### The direct-removal contrast — Federal Executive Boards

The Federal Executive Boards were created by presidential memorandum rather than statute. OPM later stated that all FEBs had ceased operations, their records, property and unexpended funds had been transferred, and a RIF had been implemented before OPM removed 5 CFR part 960. The [final rule](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-03-21/pdf/2025-04814.pdf) was published and became effective March 21—one day after the exact 2025 Aries ingress.

That is the clean control case. Where the underlying authority was executive, the carrier could be ended and its rule removed. Where Congress supplied the charter and appropriation, the path became a multi-clock contest.

## 1D.2 — March 14: the second roster exposes the organic-law boundary

### EO 14238 broadens the pressured-entity class

**Mover.** The President; OMB; and the heads of FMCS, USAGM, the Wilson Center, IMLS, USICH, the CDFI Fund and MBDA.

**Institutional change.** [EO 14238](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/03/20/2025-04868/continuing-the-reduction-of-the-federal-bureaucracy) repeated the same structure with a seven-day reporting clock: eliminate nonstatutory components and functions, reduce statutory functions and personnel to the minimum required by law, explain the statutory perimeter to OMB, and reject inconsistent funding requests where law allowed.

**Maturity by March 19.** A reduction order and near-term reporting gate. The signature did not prove that any legal person had disappeared, any appropriation had been rescinded or any later staffing action had survived review.

**Counterevidence and limit.** FMCS, IMLS and MBDA had permanent statutory duties and FY2025 appropriations. The later First Circuit record expressly treated them as congressionally established and still appropriated bodies. The Wilson Center’s institutional form, the CDFI Fund’s statutory functions and the other entities’ distinct legal bases likewise had to be heard separately; one adjective—“unnecessary”—could not decide every charter.

**Plotline.** The administrative state is placed under a presumption of dispensability, but law forces the regime to reveal the exact object it is acting on: legal person, nonstatutory program, staff, grant, service or account.

## The later maturity record

This table is not backdated into the March sky. It follows the March seed through the project’s evidence cutoff so that the institutional story includes contest and return.

| Entity or carrier | What changed | Later maturity | What survived or resisted |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Federal Executive Boards** | Operations ceased; records, property, funds and personnel were transferred or reduced; implementing regulations removed. | **Eliminated** as a nonstatutory executive carrier. | The underlying field agencies and their statutory regional functions remained. |
| **Presidential Management Fellows** | EO 14217 ordered the regulatory termination path. | **Directed toward termination; later-rule clock separate.** | The broader civil-service and Pathways architecture was not abolished on February 19. |
| **FMCS** | Staff was cut from more than 200 to roughly 10–15; public-sector and other mediation services were curtailed. | **Reduced → contested → restored.** The May 13 preliminary injunction ordered restoration; the First Circuit denied a stay on September 11; the reconciled entity audit records the December 30 vacation of the layoffs and restored operation. | The statutory mediation mission, appropriation and legal person survived. |
| **IMLS** | Staff was placed on leave, a broad RIF was prepared and grants were terminated or withheld. | **Reduced → contested → restored.** The May injunction ordered employees and grant processing restored; IMLS publicly reinstated all terminated grants on December 3, 2025; an April 2026 settlement reversed the RIF and returned staff. | The statutory grantmaker continued and is issuing 2026 awards. |
| **MBDA** | Employees were placed on leave or reassigned, grants were terminated and programs curtailed. | **Reduced → contested; not closed at cutoff.** The May preliminary injunction required restoration; the later reconciled audit records a November 2025 permanent injunction with appeal pending in 2026. | Congress continued to treat MBDA as a permanent, appropriated agency and restricted transfer of its funds; the legal person survived. |
| **Wilson Center** | The institution was cut and reorganized. | **Restructured/continuing.** It opened 2026–27 fellowship competitions and operated congressional-staff fellowships in spring 2026. | Core research and fellowship functions remained; this is a wounded-and-recomposed entity, not a completed dissolution. |
| **USIP and USAGM/VOA** | Leadership, staff, property or operating control became subjects of direct intervention and litigation. | **Contested/unresolved at the project cutoff.** Appellate questions remained live or paused behind other removal-power cases. | Statutory identity and judicial claims survived; neither order alone settled ownership or lawful control. |
| **IAF, USADF and USIP accounts** | Initial EO pressure was followed by a later congressional fiscal act. | **Enacted account reduction:** P.L. 119-28 rescinded unobligated balances of $27 million at IAF, $22 million at USADF and $15 million at USIP. | An account rescission is not organic-act repeal. It made the money cut real without erasing the charter. |
| **Presidio Trust, CDFI Fund and USICH** | Ordered toward a statutory minimum and subjected to the OMB funding-request gate. | **Legal persons continued; completed emptying not proved in this packet.** | Absence of a retrieved closure receipt cannot be converted into either proof of normal operation or proof of dissolution. |

The strongest primary legal receipt is the [First Circuit’s September 11, 2025 opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca1-25-01477/pdf/USCOURTS-ca1-25-01477-0.pdf). It denied the requested stay, recorded that FMCS, IMLS and MBDA were created and funded by Congress, and left in place relief requiring restoration of employees and already-awarded funding. The district court did not forbid all efficiency measures; it required reasoned action that did not prevent statutory obligations.

## What had changed by March 19

- A formal class of “unnecessary governmental entities” now existed inside executive policy.
- Each targeted statutory body had to identify its minimum lawful core for OMB.
- OMB was positioned to reject future funding requests inconsistent with the reduction policy where law permitted.
- Several nonstatutory advisory bodies were ordered terminated.
- OPM had been told to unwind the Federal Executive Boards and PMF regulations.
- The second reduction roster had opened a seven-day compliance clock.

## What had not changed merely because the orders existed

- No organic act had been repealed.
- No appropriation had been rescinded by the President alone.
- “Minimum lawful function” had not been uniformly or finally adjudicated.
- A compliance report was not a completed RIF, grant termination, asset transfer or service shutdown.
- OMB review of a funding request was not an enacted rescission.
- Later judicial restoration did not mean no disruption occurred.
- Later operation did not necessarily restore the old entity in its old size or form.

## The institutional theorem recovered from the packet

The crumbling occurs on different clocks:

1. **identity:** the center declares the prior entity unnecessary;
2. **personnel:** staff are removed, reassigned or placed on leave;
3. **operation:** grants and services stop or narrow;
4. **money:** requests are rejected, funds are withheld or Congress later rescinds balances;
5. **contest:** courts and appropriators test the action against the charter; and
6. **recomposition:** the entity returns, survives wounded, remains contested or continues with a smaller account.

This is the “fourth act” missing from a simple alive/dying binary: **dissolve → contest → restore or restructure**.

### Governing hierarchy

The strict governor remains the fixed-rising **2024 Aries ingress**, while **2024 Capricorn** is the nested winter room.

- Strict-governor Sun in Aries in the 6th, joined Neptune, naturally describes executive identity entering the administrative body while the distinction between mandate, function and dissolving boundary becomes the sovereign question.
- Mars, the chart ruler, acts from Aquarius in the 4th: the action is aimed at the state’s underlying architecture, not merely its public image.
- The closed root loop ultimately forces the question back through law, value, relationship and discipline. A root loop is a circle of service, not proof that nobody governs.
- Moon opposite Pluto holds the public/people pole against concentrated restructuring power. The chart supports both restoration through durable civic need and coercive emptying that leaves the legal shell behind.
- In the nested Capricorn frame, Sun in the 3rd makes reports, statutory inventories, regulations and written compliance tests the winter carrier. The institution is first translated into a document that says what must live.

The constructive pole is **constitutional pruning**: nonstatutory duplication is removed, every body states its lawful mission, and obsolete carriers are separated from durable public duties. The shadow pole is **shell governance**: an entity remains legally alive while staff, accounts and operating capacity are stripped until the charter becomes nominal. The later restoration cases show that neither pole was guaranteed at the start.

## Handoffs kept open

- **March 20:** exact 2025 Aries ingress opens a new governing year. EO 14242, signed that day, directs the Secretary of Education to facilitate departmental closure only to the maximum extent permitted by law. It is the next and largest test of the statutory-minimum problem and belongs to Wave 2, with a date-only boundary caveat if signing time is unavailable.
- **March 21:** the Federal Executive Board final rule becomes effective. This is the first clean post-ingress receipt from the February order.
- **March–May:** actual staffing, grant and service reductions at FMCS, IMLS and MBDA acquire operating body, then enter judicial contest.
- **July 24:** P.L. 119-28 turns selected balance reductions into enacted law. This is a money-clock maturation, not a retroactive March dissolution.
- **Late 2025–2026:** restoration, restructuring and unresolved appeals become their own astrological chapters. They should not be compressed into the original EO date.
