Sun–Neptune

The planets

Sun — Authority, illumination, "everything revolves around the Sun." MUNDANE: the head of government (the President); the executive branch; the focus of executive power. The Sun's condition shows how the government will fare. (Greer: "the Sun represents the actual head of government — whoever officially calls the shots in the executive branch.")

Neptune — Dissolution, the Ideal, oceanic depths of the unconscious. MUNDANE: collective movements, mass sentiment, propaganda, popular causes; the unity of the people (from divine to mob). Almost always malefic in mundane charts unless completely unafflicted. (Greer: "the single most dangerous indication in a mundane chart" when in hostile aspect to the Sun.)

The combination — mundane

Under helpful aspects (sextile, trine), the government rides a wave of popular enthusiasm. Greer describes a situation where "government is popular with the masses; success through popular enthusiasm or sheer luck." The President appears charismatic, even inspired. Popular movements align with executive goals rather than opposing them. Propaganda works — but in the helpful version, it works because the message resonates with genuine collective feeling. The nation experiences a sense of unity. Luck plays an outsized role; things simply go the government's way for reasons no one can fully explain.

Under hostile aspects (conjunction, square, opposition), Sun-Neptune becomes what Greer calls "FAR AND AWAY the most unfortunate indication" in mundane astrology — "something in the nature of a downfall or collapse." The executive is engulfed by confusion, disorder, and the failure of legislation. Propaganda backfires or is exposed. The government becomes deeply unpopular, and those in power suffer "unpopularity and misfortune." Mass movements turn against the state. Scandals erupt that dissolve public trust. The President appears lost, deluded, or actively deceptive. This is not a sharp Uranian crisis — it is a slow drowning, where the ground gives way beneath the government's feet and nothing solid can be found to stand on.

The asymmetry here is extreme. Helpful Sun-Neptune is pleasant but vague; hostile Sun-Neptune is one of the most destructive combinations in the mundane astrologer's toolkit.

As trigger: Sun-Neptune aspects are fast-moving (days to about two weeks) and mark the specific moments when mass sentiment, propaganda, confusion, or collective vision activate whatever broader outer-planet storylines are in play.
