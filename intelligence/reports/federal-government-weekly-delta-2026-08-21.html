Controlling ruling

The sharpest new whole-government object is NSPM-17, the National Space Transportation Policy. The White House posted the controlling memorandum at 16:54:58 EDT on August 20, after the prior research cutoff. It revokes the 2013 policy, assigns a new APST-coordinated implementation overlay and opens 90-, 120-, 180- and 240-day deliverable clocks. It is a policy and coordination instrument, not a launch, appropriation, site, contract, construction project, export-control result or proof of the stated 2030 capacity goal.

The rest of the window is a layered implementation day. August 21 publication closed three proposal clocks carried from August 20; DOL/OFCCP published three final rules with different future effective dates; operating agencies opened or approved bounded authorities, guidance, awards and contracts; and one OIG closed recommendations without testing their effectiveness. The record does not support a claim that all of those layers are already operating or that the same actor controls them.

This merge controls Workbench research strictly after August 20 at 12:19:33 EDT through August 21 at 15:00:00 EDT. The live-vault whole-government canon still ends August 13 at 21:25:45 EDT because neither the August 20 whole-government package nor this continuation has been accepted as a full vault merge. Selected later topical landings, including the DTCC notice/testing map, do not move that whole-government clock.

Executive readout

  1. Space transportation now has a replacement policy and clock spine. The APST coordination overlay does not make the directed departments subordinate entities or prove that any deliverable has occurred.
  2. Three financial proposals moved from Public Inspection to publication. SEC crypto and CFTC compute comments close October 20; CFTC CPO/CTA comments close October 5. None is final or operating.
  3. The OFCCP trilogy is final but not yet fully effective. Section 503 and VEVRAA changes generally take effect September 21; the EO 11246 regulation rescission takes effect October 26; one Section 503 amendment waits until December 21.
  4. Education delegated authority and issued guidance. Three Ed-Flex approvals bring the official total to 25 states, but approval is not proof of a state waiver. The education-technology letter is nonbinding guidance.
  5. The semiconductor-finance program crossed from announcement to an open NOFO. OSC's NSFF proposal window closes November 1 at 17:00 EDT. A NOFO is not an application, selection, loan, obligation, disbursement or investment. The source literally labels that clock EDT even though November 1, 2026 is the U.S. daylight-saving transition date; no silent zone correction is made.
  6. Awards and obligations were kept separate. NASA announced four ULI awards totaling about $30 million without per-team allocations. Selected War Department contracts disclose current obligations, but ceilings, unexercised options, later increments, delivery and payment remain separate.
  7. A defense report changed public-data state, not occurrence time. The FY2025 sexual-assault report was delivered to Congress August 12 and released publicly August 21; survey estimates are not adjudicated-case counts.
  8. One oversight record genuinely closed. FLRA OIG accepted policy, training and form changes and closed three recommendations, while expressly declining to test operational effectiveness.
  9. A congressional staff report remains an attributed claim. The House Judiciary Democratic staff's $1.653 billion clemency-related calculation is not a full-committee finding, audit, judgment or new pardon action.
  10. Registry metadata did not move legal authority. CISA's publisher field now assigns passport.gov and passports.gov to State, and 17 domains disappeared from the current registry. Neither change proves a transfer, decommission, redirect or loss of authority.
