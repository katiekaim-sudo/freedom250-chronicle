Ieepa Emergency Powers Tariff And Blocking State

*Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* and *Trump v. V.O.S. Selections, Inc.*, Nos. 24-1287 and 25-250 (Feb. 20, 2026), 607 U.S. ___: > **Held:** IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs. The Federal Circuit judgment against the tariffs (No. 25-250) is **affirmed**. The D.D.C. action (No. 24-1287) is **vacated and remanded with instructions to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction**. The tariff challenges belong on the CIT / Federal Circuit path. Roberts, C.J., announced the judgment. Parts I, II–A–1, and II–B (the statutory holding that “regulate … importation” does not include the power to tax) are the opinion of the Court, joined by Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Barrett, and Jackson. Parts II–A–2 and III (major-questions overlay) are Roberts, Gorsuch, and Barrett only. Kagan, joined by Sotomayor and Jackson, concurred in the judgment on ordinary statutory tools and would not have invoked major questions. Thomas dissented. Kavanaugh dissented, joined by Thomas and Alito. **Judgment 6–3.** The list in 50 U.S.C. § 1702(a)(1)(B) is investigate, block, regulate, direct and compel, nullify, void, prevent or prohibit — acquisition, holding, use, transfer, importation, exportation, dealing in property. **“Absent from this lengthy list of specific powers is any mention of tariffs or duties.”** Had Congress meant the tariff power, it would have said so, as it does in the statutes that actually grant duties (Trade Expansion Act § 232; Trade Act § 301). **Same day, operating paper.** EO 14389 (Feb. 20, 2026; 91 FR 9437): the additional **ad valorem duties imposed pursuant to IEEPA** in the listed 2025–26 emergency orders “shall no longer be in effect and, as soon as practicable, shall no longer be collected.” **The national emergencies remain.** Actions that are not those IEEPA duties remain. Section 232 and section 301 duties are **unaffected**. EO 14388 (de minimis suspension) is **unaffected**. **Same day, different statute.** Proclamation **11012** (Feb. 20, 2026; 91 FR 9339): a 10 percent ad valorem surcharge under Trade Act § 122, 19 U.S.C. § 2132. The statute lets the President proclaim a surcharge **not exceeding 15 percent** for a period **not exceeding 150 days** unless extended by Act of Congress. The proclamation itself: goods entered or withdrawn for consumption on or after 12:01 a.m. EST **February 24, 2026**, through 12:01 a.m. EDT **July 24, 2026**, unless earlier ended or extended by Act of Congress. **No extending Act retrieved at cutoff.** That is not IEEPA. A later CIT judgment on 11012 is a different object, named below. **Claim cut.** “IEEPA is dead” is **not established**. The tariff use is dead. The block/regulate/nullify machine is the statute Congress wrote. OFAC still runs on it.
