How 16 financial giants are going digital

Sixteen financial giants are connecting new digital ledgers to existing bank systems. Citi, Bank of America, Deutsche Bank and HSBC show the broadest work, but none has proved one joined system working at large scale or through a serious failure.

What we found

  • The 16 incumbents split into five camps: banks turning deposits into digital money, custodians adding digital wallets to their existing books, firms issuing digital bonds and funds, firms putting fund shares straight on public blockchains, and firms still in research mode.
  • Citi expanded tokenized deposits to seven markets and added separate Coinbase stablecoin and Swift instant-payment routes; those routes do different jobs and do not share one ledger or finality rule.
  • Bank of America's own product and engineering hiring now proves active stablecoin, tokenized-deposit and post-trade construction, but no public client product or transaction. Deutsche Bank reported one euro Partior transaction while its crypto-custody launch remains conditional.
  • HSBC named its licensed HKD stablecoin RedCoin but said none had yet been issued. Standard Chartered's licensed Anchorpoint joint venture and SG-FORGE's daily reserve page likewise sharpen the issuer and custody map without proving universal settlement or recovery.
  • Fidelity was added late and has a lot live: a national trust-bank digital custodian, its FIDD stablecoin, a limited on-chain record for its FYOXX fund shares, and collateral accounts. Its 401(k) reach is distribution muscle, not control over what plans hold.
  • Retirement money could shift onto blockchain quietly, underneath the same 401(k) screen. But no live Fidelity or Franklin model yet shows a tokenized U.S. workplace target-date fund, a plan-level blockchain record, or benefits paid in digital cash.
  • No institution publicly proves full scale plus public stress-testing, error correction, recovery and retiring the old systems across all seven areas studied.

Why it matters

The same institutions that run today's system are rebuilding its interfaces from the inside. The new evidence shows faster construction and more live routes, but legal claim, official record, reserves, payout and loss still sit with different actors.
