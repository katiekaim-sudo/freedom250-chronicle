White House election files show real risks, not proof of changed results

The July 16, 2026 White House release (58 PDFs, 269 pages) documents four separate things: foreign collection of voter data, internal intelligence disputes, weak state and local election networks, and questionable voter registrations. As released, none of it proves an ineligible or altered vote changed an outcome.

What we found

  • Voter data: reporting describes a 204,822,241-record U.S. voter dataset and Chinese analysis involving at least 18 states. Some later state data was bought or downloaded from commercial sites; the rest of the sourcing is partly redacted.
  • Intelligence: internal emails show a real dispute over how China reporting was described, and also show FBI analysts challenging the minority view's sourcing.
  • Election systems: CISA found serious patching, login and remote-access weaknesses, and authorized testers took full control of some state and local networks within hours or days. No record shows a foreign actor changed cast votes or certified totals.
  • Registration: Michigan records show possibly fake registration applications and a multi-year investigation. DHS match figures on noncitizens and deceased registrants have no published method; reported maxima in DHS letters total 256,463.
  • On June 22, 2026 a D.C. federal court vacated the 2025 DHS and SSA records notices and the modified SAVE system, and denied a stay on July 8; the appeal (No. 26-5243) is pending. The 'SAVE America Act' is not law; the live vehicle is the House amendment to S. 1383, passed February 11, 2026.

Why it matters

Voter-eligibility checks are being pushed through agency databases, grant terms, court fights and bills rather than one sweeping change. Keeping what is documented apart from what is claimed shows how far that push actually reaches.

What to watch

  • pending · D.C. Circuit ruling in No. 26-5243 on the SAVE system and any replacement records notices.
  • pending · Senate action on the House amendment to S. 1383.
  • pending · DHS's matching method, data dates and state-by-state outcomes behind its registration figures.
