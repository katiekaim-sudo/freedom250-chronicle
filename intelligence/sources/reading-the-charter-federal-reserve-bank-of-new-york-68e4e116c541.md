# Reading the Charter — Federal Reserve Bank of New York

Source text: `1913 - Federal Reserve Act §4 (verbatim).md` in this folder — the statutory template every reserve bank, including FRBNY, is organized under. Every claim cites the exact FRA section.

## 1. Immunities & liability shields

FRBNY is a "body corporate" with power "to sue and be sued, complain and defend, in any court of law or equity" (§4 ¶4 "Fourth") — so it is NOT categorically immune from suit like a sovereign or an international organization would be; it can be sued as a corporation. Its shield is narrower and specific: tax exemption (see below) and the funding-independence language that protects the SYSTEM's money from congressional appropriation control (§10 ¶4, captured under the Federal Reserve System entity), which indirectly protects FRBNY's own assessed contributions to the Board.

## 2. Tax exemptions

FRA §7(c) (captured under the Federal Reserve System entity, applies identically to FRBNY as one of the twelve reserve banks): FRBNY's capital stock, surplus, and income are exempt from federal, state, and local taxation, except real estate taxes.

## 3. Who appoints, who removes, and for what cause — the control map

This is where FRBNY gets structurally interesting relative to the Board in Washington. FRBNY's president is NOT a presidential appointee — the president is chosen by FRBNY's OWN board of directors (specifically the Class B and Class C directors, §4 ¶4 "Fifth"), subject to Board of Governors *approval*, for a 5-year term. The nine-member FRBNY board itself is split three ways: Class A directors (3 seats) are "chosen by and representative of the stockholding banks"—member banks holding the district Bank's mandatory, nontransferable statutory subscription stock (§4 ¶10); Class B directors (3 seats) represent "the public" but are barred from being officers/directors/employees of ANY bank (§4 ¶11, ¶14); Class C directors (3 seats) are designated directly by the Board of Governors in Washington and must not be officers, directors, employees, OR STOCKHOLDERS of any bank (§4 ¶12, ¶15). One Class C director is designated "Federal reserve agent" (§4 ¶20). The member-bank election right is real but bounded: one vote per member bank within its class regardless of shares, and no member-bank vote for the three Class C seats. It is not ordinary proportional shareholder control.

## 4. Money: self-funding vs. appropriations

FRBNY is capitalized by its member banks, which are statutorily required to subscribe 6% of their own paid-up capital and surplus to district-Bank stock (§2 ¶3, captured under the Federal Reserve System entity)—this is not voluntary; it is a condition of national-bank membership in the System (§2 ¶1). One-half of the $100 par subscription is paid and one-half remains callable; the stock cannot be transferred or hypothecated, its dividend is capped, and withdrawal/liquidation rights do not create a market-value residual claim. FRBNY funds its operations from its lending/open-market income and applies the Act's dividend, surplus and earnings-remittance rules. A current cash transfer, a deferred-asset increase and statutory surplus treatment are separate accounting clocks. See [Federal Reserve Financial Constitution — Source Document](../sources/federal-reserve-financial-constitution-source-document-203118d0ec33.html). No congressional appropriation touches FRBNY's operating budget at any point in this loop.

## 5. Audit & disclosure carve-outs

Same regime as the System generally (§2B(c), §11(s), captured under the Federal Reserve System entity) — FRBNY's counterparty and collateral details for open-market operations and emergency lending surface only on the delayed disclosure schedule (1-2 years, depending on facility type), and are exempted from ordinary FOIA until that release date.

## 6. Amendment & withdrawal clauses

No FRBNY-specific amendment mechanism — it is amended whenever Congress amends the Federal Reserve Act. A reserve bank CAN be dissolved by Act of Congress or by "forfeiture of franchise for violation of law" (§4 ¶4 "Second") — but this requires affirmative congressional or judicial action; FRBNY has no unilateral exit and member banks cannot simply withdraw FRBNY's charter.

## 7. Emergency powers

FRBNY is the operational hub through which the System's emergency §13(3) lending and routine §14 open-market authority is actually EXECUTED — the New York Fed's trading desk is where Treasury and agency securities purchases are physically transacted on the FOMC's direction (§12A(b), captured under the FOMC entity). This is not a separate emergency-powers grant to FRBNY specifically in the statute — it flows from FRBNY's practical role as the System's designated markets desk, layered on top of the general §13(3)/§14 authority that applies to any reserve bank.

## 8. The definitions section

FRBNY inherits every FRA §1 definition ("bank," "member bank," "district," "reserve bbank," "continental United States") without its own separate definitions section. One quietly load-bearing fact: the organization-certificate provisions (§4 ¶1-3) required FRBNY's founding certificate to specifically state "the territorial extent of the district" — meaning FRBNY's jurisdiction (the Second Federal Reserve District, covering New York State, parts of New Jersey and Connecticut) is a matter of that original certificate's text, which — per the capture note in the verbatim file — was not locatable online this pass.

## 9. Succession/perpetuity & dissolution

FRBNY has corporate "succession... until dissolved by Act of Congress or until forfeiture of franchise for violation of law" (§4 ¶4 "Second") — no natural sunset. On dissolution, any surplus after debts, dividends, and par stock value becomes U.S. property (§7(b), FRS note) — Treasury, not the member-bank stockholders, is the residual claimant.

## 10. Anything strange — the clause you'd never expect

The organization-certificate mechanism itself is strange in hindsight: FRBNY's actual founding legal instrument — the document that specifically brought "Federal Reserve Bank of New York" into corporate existence in 1914 — appears to exist only in physical archival form (National Archives Record Groups 82 and 101), not digitized anywhere public. A 110-year-old, still-operating body with trillion-dollar balance-sheet consequences has a founding certificate that isn't a Google search away. That's the strangest finding of this whole pass — not a hidden clause, but a hidden DOCUMENT.

## Layer map

- **1913-1914 — Federal Reserve Act (Pub. L. 63-43) + Reserve Bank Organization Committee designation.** The Act creates the corporate template (§4); the Committee (Treasury Secretary, Agriculture Secretary, Comptroller of the Currency) designates New York as a Federal Reserve city in April 1914; FRBNY's organization certificate is executed and filed with the Comptroller; FRBNY opens for business November 16, 1914, alongside the other eleven reserve banks.
- **→ Banking Act of 1935 (49 Stat. 703-705).** Amends §4 ¶4 "Fifth" (effective March 1, 1936) to restructure how the FRBNY president is appointed — approval now runs through the newly renamed Board of Governors rather than the old Federal Reserve Board with Treasury/Comptroller seats on it. This is also when FRBNY's president permanently joins the FOMC (§12A, FOMC entity file) with a non-rotating seat — arguably the single most consequential structural fact about FRBNY specific to this bank alone.
- **→ Federal Reserve Reform Act of 1977 (91 Stat. 1387-1388).** Amends §4 ¶10-11 (Class A and B director selection) to add non-discrimination requirements — a civil-rights-era update to the 1913 director-election machinery, not a power shift.
- **→ Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act of 2010 (124 Stat. 2126).** Amends §4 ¶4 "Fifth" again — the president's term is now explicitly tied to Board of Governors "approval," tightening Washington's grip on FRBNY's chief executive selection in the wake of the 2008 crisis, during which FRBNY's president (then Timothy Geithner) was arguably the single most operationally powerful unelected official in the country.

## Entity Theory read

FRBNY is the operational hands of the Federal Reserve System — the Board in Washington sets policy, the FOMC directs strategy, but FRBNY's trading desk is where the money actually moves, which is why its president gets the one permanent (non-rotating) reserve-bank seat on the FOMC while every other regional bank shares a rotating chair. Who really holds the leash: nominally the Class A/B/C director structure splits control three ways among member banks, "the public," and Washington-designated overseers, but the Class C "Federal reserve agent" role is the tell — one director is statutorily required to keep a Board of Governors office ON FRBNY's own premises and report directly to Washington, meaning even inside FRBNY's own boardroom there is a built-in Washington watchdog. Does it survive a change of control? Yes by statute (explicit corporate perpetuity, §4 ¶4 "Second"), but its actual founding instrument sitting unindexed in physical federal archives rather than online is a reminder that "survives" and "is fully documented in public view" are not the same claim — the corporate shell is permanent and legible; the founding paperwork, oddly, is not.
