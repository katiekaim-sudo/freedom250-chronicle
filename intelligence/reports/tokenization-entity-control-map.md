# Crypto Infrastructure Hub — 2026-09-01

## Purpose

This package owns the governed source registry and generated projection for the
Observatory's first-class **Crypto Hub** reference view. Tokenization remains a
major use case, but the governed scope is broader: **who steers, builds,
operates, regulates, connects and settles each crypto infrastructure stack?**

The hub's central finding is that the relationship web is dense while actual
corporate control is sparse. Ownership, minority investment, board influence,
registration, commercial partnership, operating structure, policy advocacy,
technical integration, asset flow, historical origin and **proposed-rule
overlap** are therefore separate edge classes. Proposal overlap marks where a
current capability touches the SEC's proposed architecture without turning
that overlap into approval or a final-rule compliance finding. Ripple,
XRPL, RLUSD and XRP remain separate
objects; Securitize's affiliates remain separate regulated legal entities; and
Wormhole remains an interoperability layer rather than an issuer, transfer
agent, custodian or title authority.

For the FCA's bounded 2026 UK market description, use the
UK cryptoasset actor-and-flow baseline.
It maps the FCA's role taxonomy, consumer access and custody co-location,
settlement clocks, cross-border structure and October 2027 regime without
creating new Hub entity relationships or treating brand-level survey responses
as legal-entity market shares.

The generated Hub currently carries **298 nodes, 372 typed relationships, 370 source
objects, 20 governed ecosystem profiles, fourteen shared stack-expansion system
families, three governed process maps, sixteen
governed asset traces with process-role references, eight analytical lenses and eight operational clocks**.
The profiles cover
**Ripple/XRPL, Securitize, Ethereum, Hedera, Stellar, Canton, Solana, Avalanche,
Chainlink, Bitcoin, DTC/DTCC tokenization, cash legs and tokenized bank money,
Circle/USDC/CPN/Arc/Tazapay, Coinbase/Base/Tokenize, Algorand, XDC, IOTA/TWIN and bounded

The schema-v4 process layer supplies the shared evidence for Process, Trace

Ethereum appears in all three process maps, but its role is not the same in
each. It operates live execution and consensus for tokenized assets and native
USDC state and transport. In the FX map it appears only as a transport layer:
the registry does not assign Ethereum the currency quote, conversion, funding,
clearing, payout, issuer, reserve or redemption functions.

Ripple's tokenization view now makes three different collateral ideas visible
without treating them as one capability. Fubon Bank's 2023 e-HKD pilot is a
historical external dependency in **Underlying & collateral**: the tokens
represented bank charges over property on a private ledger built with XRPL
technology, not title deeds or assets controlled by Ripple on the public XRPL.
Licuido remains admission, issuance and market-access buildout because its
collateral-mobility announcement does not prove custody or control of the
underlying asset. Evernorth and Pathfinder are separately identified XRP
treasury and custody actors, not tokenized-asset controllers; their pending
combination, restricted pre-close XRP and proposed XLS-66 participation do not
fill a tokenization process cell.

Hedera, Solana, Avalanche, Stellar, Algorand and XDC now receive the same
native-USDC treatment in the Stablecoins map: each operates token-state
execution and network transport, while Circle and its named counterparties
remain external dependencies for issuer authority, reserves, liability records,
direct redemption and remedy. Hedera also appears in FX only for one completed
Aberdeen-Lloyds-Archax pilot in which tokenized fund units and gilts moved as
collateral. Canton separately records one completed Treasury-for-USDCx cash-leg
event while leaving USDCx issuer and redemption terms unresolved. Neither
single-event receipt establishes a production corridor or general service.

Circle's September 4 definitive agreement to acquire Tazapay is mapped as
**pending control**, not current ownership. The live operating relationship came
first: Tazapay was already a CPN Beneficiary Financial Institution converting
received stablecoin into local fiat and paying through local bank rails. The map
now separates Circle Internet Group, the actual purchaser Taurus Acquisition
Inc., CTS-operated CPN, Tazapay Pte. Ltd., Tazapay Canada Corp.'s exclusive
stablecoin on/off-ramp role and Tazapay's local payout stack. If the transaction
closes, Circle would internalize a regulated last-mile adapter; it would not
collapse the exact licences, bank books, local payment systems, USDC issuer or
beneficiary-finality obligations into one entity. The $400 million all-stock base
price is adjustable and the expected 2027 close still requires MAS approval and
other conditions. Circle's reported $25 billion annualized volume, 60-plus
partners, 100-plus payout markets and roughly 60% stablecoin-including volume are
aggregate company claims as of July 31, not audited USDC-only or corridor-level
measurements.

The SEC proposal-overlap view makes the proposed
accountability sequence inspectable from issuer and recordkeeping transfer
agent through the authoritative master securityholder file, linked systems,
wallet records, governance, reporting, corrections and downstream services.
The network view separates protocol governance, operators, execution layers,
native-token economics and named product connections rather than collapsing a
company, network and token into one object.

## Boundaries

- Evidence cutoff: **2026-09-17 for the Innovation Exemption, corporate-stack
  and Arc refresh; other evidence retains its existing dates**.
- Circle and Taurus do not control Tazapay before closing. The signed agreement
  remains subject to MAS and other approvals and closing conditions; it does not
  transfer Tazapay licences, bank relationships, customer contracts or local
  payment-system control. Tazapay's existing CPN role remains a separately live
  partnership.
- Tazapay Pte. Ltd.'s Singapore MPI licence does not include Digital Payment
  Token service. Tazapay identifies Tazapay Canada Corp. as the exclusive group
  entity providing stablecoin payment and conversion services. Neither entity
  issues USDC or controls its reserves or redemption terms.
- SEC Release 34-106246 / S7-2026-30 is a **proposal**, not a final rule.
- SEC Release 34-106402 / File 4-927 is an **effective conditional order**, but
  effective relief is not an operating TSV. No tracked Hub ecosystem is named,
  selected or qualified by the order; the venue's 30-day notice and all
  product, issuer, record, liquidity, settlement and operating receipts remain
  separate.
- A registration describes the named legal entity and permitted role; it is not
  approval of every affiliate, product, chain or transaction.
- A minority investment or board seat is not control unless a source proves
  control.
- A technical bridge moves messages or supported representations; it does not
  itself determine beneficial ownership, legal title, custody or finality.
- A chain's native fee, burn, staking or governance economics describe network
  utility; they do not determine the authoritative securityholder record or
  establish value capture from a tokenized product.
- Technical finality, the authoritative legal record and the cash or redemption
  remedy are separate endpoints. A DTC token, Base wallet, trade credential or
  bank-money representation does not inherit legal title merely by settling on
  a chain.
- DTC limited production in July 2026 does not backdate the expected October
  general-service launch; Arc private mainnet does not make the planned
  September 16 public mainnet or its announced founding-validator cohort live;
  Circle National Trust's effective charter does not prove reserve-management
  activation or operating volume; Ripple and Coinbase preliminary charter
  approvals do not make those proposed trust banks open.
- Coinbase's minority Circle stake and USDC distribution/reserve-income
  economics are material company relationships, but they do not make Coinbase
  the USDC issuer, reserve obligor or sole stablecoin-network governor.
- Fubon Bank's 2023 e-HKD pilot proves one historical home-equity-lending test
  in which tokens represented property liens or bank charges on a private
  ledger built with XRPL technology. It did not tokenize title deeds, use the
  public XRPL mainnet, establish a current production service or transfer the
  authoritative property record to Ripple or XRPL.
- Licuido's announced collateral-market mobility supports Ripple's admission,
  issuance and market-access buildout, but it does not prove custody,
  immobilization or duplicate-issuance control for an underlying asset.
- Evernorth and Pathfinder remain legal entities separate from Ripple. Their
  reported pre-close XRP holdings and BitGo custody arrangements describe a
  corporate treasury path; the pending combination and proposed Single Asset
  Vault/XLS-66 participation do not prove a completed listing, operating
  lending volume, collateral control or a tokenization or FX route. XLS-66's
  documented protocol design is uncollateralized lending, not an underlying-
