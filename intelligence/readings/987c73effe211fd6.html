Final answer
The United States is not wired into one international order. It participates
through a constellation of legally distinct objects whose power comes from
different sources:
constitutional seats and blocking rights in the UN Security Council,
IMF, NATO consensus process and specified ITER decisions;
shareholder and appropriations leverage in the World Bank Group, IMF and
other funded bodies;
exceptional assessed-contribution leverage in bodies such as the OAS,
IAEA and ICAO;
national implementation control over ICAO, IMO, ITU, WIPO and BIS-hosted
outputs;
command, hardware and facility access in NATO, CERN and ITER;
agenda and coalition power in the G7, G20, Kyoto Vision and Artemis
Accords; and
exit, withholding and successor-building power exercised through treaty
notices, programme defunding, bilateral agreements and new charter bodies.
The restructuring thesis survives, but only in a bounded form:
The United States is selectively exiting some regimes, cutting funding or
participation at some edges, refitting powerful incumbents from inside,
incubating smaller coalitions and moving a few chosen functions into new
bilateral or bespoke international machinery. It is not replacing the old
order wholesale.
The strongest completed migration is the U.S. bilateral foreign-health aid
route. The strongest new institutional setup is the Board of Peace. Artemis is
the strongest non-binding shared-machinery coalition. Kyoto Vision remains
parallel doctrine. Most other headline exits have no successor owner.

Where U.S. power is strongest
1. Constitutional blocking and agenda gates
The U.S. Security Council veto is a Charter power; it does not control the
General Assembly or Secretariat.
The 16.49-percent IMF vote blocks decisions requiring 85 percent but cannot
pass them alone.
NATO acts by consensus, while forces and use-of-force decisions remain
national.
ITER core decisions require unanimity, giving the United States a genuine
treaty veto in the specified fields.
Board of Peace Members have one vote, but decisions require Chairman
approval; the Chairman controls subsidiary bodies, appointments, veto and
internal interpretation.
2. Money
The most striking U.S. percentages are not one ownership ledger:
OAS 2026 quota: 49.990%;
IAEA 2026 scale: 25.005%;
ICAO 2026–2028 scale: 21.69%;
WHO assessment scale before asserted exit: 22%;
OECD 2026 Part I budget share: 18%;
IMF vote: 16.49%;
World Bank entities: roughly 9.48%–16.91%, depending on the entity;
NATO common-cost share: 14.9039% for 2026–2027; and
ITER construction sharing baseline: 9.09% for the United States.
Some figures are votes, some budget assessments, some project shares and some
quota/shareholder positions. They cannot be added or compared as if all were
equity.
WIPO demonstrates the opposite model: about 96 percent of 2026–2027 income is
expected from user fees, so demand for filing services matters more than the
U.S. assessed contribution. BIS is funded as a central-bank-owned bank, not by
a sovereign assessment. G7/G20 and political accords have no standing
assessment at all.
3. Domestic implementation
The international body often owns the common record while national authorities
own legal effect:
ICAO Council adopts SARPs; FAA implements or publishes differences.
IMO administers conventions and audits; flag, port and coastal States
legislate, certify, inspect and enforce.
ITU maintains the binding international spectrum/orbit framework; FCC and
NTIA allocate and license domestically.
WIPO processes international applications; national or regional offices
grant or refuse rights.
BIS-hosted standards require U.S. legislation, rules, supervision or
operating adoption.
This is not a hidden surrender of sovereignty. It is a negotiated relay whose
international and domestic gates must both be mapped.
4. Facilities and command
NATO combines common command and planning with nationally owned forces.
CERN is Member-owned; the United States is a non-Member LHC/HL-LHC observer
and project partner with no Member vote or assessment.
ITER is a treaty organization in which the United States is a Member,
Domestic Agency operator, in-kind producer and future user.
Facility access, project committees, data/IP terms, appropriations, hardware
acceptance and scientific outcomes are distinct clocks.
The completed bypass ruling
Confirmed migrations or default routes
Bilateral global health: U.S. assistance moved into country MOUs, joint
committees, co-investment and bilateral funding pipelines. WHO's universal
functions did not move.
OECD tax fork: U.S. rejection produced a side-by-side regime that was
incorporated into the incumbent OECD/G20 record and reporting machinery.
WTO MPIA: participating non-U.S. Members use an Article 25 appellate
route while the Appellate Body remains unavailable.
WIPO Madrid e-Filing: the U.S. outbound trademark route moved toward,
not away from, incumbent WIPO machinery.
Shared machinery without completed transfer
Artemis Accords: 76 signatories, Principals and working groups, rotating
chairs, workshops and technical recommendations; no binding rule,
assessment, court or treaty replacement.
Board of Peace: a new legal organization and Gaza execution
constitution with Security Council authorization; September 2026 field
governance had not completed the handoff.
Doctrine or exit without successor
Kyoto Vision is level-2 parallel doctrine.
Paris withdrawal is complete and UNFCCC withdrawal is pending, but no
successor climate institution exists.
UNESCO exit is pending without a general successor.
UNHRC nonparticipation and UNRWA defunding cut U.S. edges without transferring
their incumbent functions.
Internal rebalancing, not bypass
NATO command roles shifted inside NATO.
IMF and World Bank missions are being refit through existing shareholder
machinery.
OAS core membership continues despite peripheral contraction and heavy U.S.
funding leverage.
IAEA, OPCW, INTERPOL, ICAO, IMO, ITU, CERN and ITER remain actively used.
The Board of Peace finding
The Board is the closest international analogue to the domestic restructuring
pattern:
UN Security Council authorization and renewal gate
-> external charter organization
-> Chairman-gated governance
-> operational entities and voluntary funding vehicles
-> proposed field administration and security command
-> six-month report back to the Council
It preserves the incumbent's legitimacy and review gate while placing selected
execution outside the UN Secretariat. That is structurally significant.
It is also bounded. Resolution 2803 concerns Gaza and expires at the end of
2027 absent Council action. UN humanitarian actors remain. The Palestinian
administrative committee had not entered Gaza by September 2026. The Board's
broader Charter mission is not itself a broader Security Council mandate.
What would prove the theory at full strength
A future case should not be called a replacement until the record shows:
a predecessor function and its legal owner;
a new legal or operational owner;
a valid authority or agreement for the handoff;
money, staff, data, infrastructure or programmes moved;
operators and users normally using the new route;
authoritative records and correction/remedy owned by the successor; and
the incumbent reduced to a residual or ceremonial role in that bounded
function.
No case in this project proves wholesale replacement of the UN system, NATO,
the Bretton Woods institutions or the technical/scientific infrastructure.
