## Short answer

On June 30, 2026 the Supreme Court, per the Chief Justice, held:

> Children born in the United States to parents unlawfully or temporarily present are "subject to the jurisdiction" of the United States and are citizens at birth under the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship Clause.

The judgment was **affirmed**. Roberts, C.J., joined by Sotomayor, Kagan, Barrett, and Jackson. Kavanaugh concurred in the judgment and dissented in part. Thomas (joined by Gorsuch) and Alito and Gorsuch filed dissents.

EO 14160 (Jan. 20, 2025) had told agencies not to issue or accept citizenship documents for two classes of U.S.-born children: (1) mother unlawfully present and father not a citizen or LPR; (2) mother's presence lawful but temporary and father not a citizen or LPR. *Barbara* holds those children are citizens. The Order's central rule lost.

EO 14418 (Aug. 6, 2026; 91 FR 51991) cites *Barbara* and then directs agencies not to issue or accept citizenship documents where **neither parent is a citizen** and one of four residual predicates applies (alien-enemy / FTO-SDGT parent; foreign-government employee, drawn wider than ambassadors; commercial birth-tourism or surrogacy; birth in a territory where statute does not confer citizenship). Every department and agency must publish implementation guidance within 30 days — **September 5, 2026**. The order names State, DOJ, DHS, and SSA for enforcement. It does not name Commerce, Census, USPS, or elections.

A guidance document is not a loss of status. A refused passport is not a holding that the Clause does not apply.

## 3. What *Barbara* actually held

**Cite:** *Trump v. Barbara*, No. 25-365 (June 30, 2026), slip op. Certiorari before judgment to the First Circuit.

**Question presented (syllabus):** whether the Constitution guarantees citizenship to children born in the United States of parents who are unlawfully or temporarily present.

**Held (syllabus):** they "are 'subject to the jurisdiction' of the United States and are citizens at birth under the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship Clause."

The Court reads the Clause against *jus soli*, *Wong Kim Ark*, and *Schooner Exchange*: jurisdiction is the nation's full power inside its territory. The historic exceptions are narrow — children of foreign ministers (extraterritorial fiction), children born in territory the sovereign does not control, and the old Indian-nations exception. People who come here for "business or caprice" are "amenable to the jurisdiction of the country." Unlawful or temporary parental presence does not take the child out.

The syllabus is explicit that the words "mother," "father," "lawful," "temporary" — the EO's vocabulary — "are absent from the Clause."

**Kavanaugh, J.,** concurred in the judgment and dissented in part. That separate writing is **not** the holding. Do not promote it into "the Court invited Congress to legislate this away" unless you are quoting Kavanaugh and labeling it as such.

**What *Barbara* did not do:** it did not write a code of residual categories. It did not decide EO 14418 (that order did not exist yet). It did not decide Census, SAVE, or voter rolls.

## 4. What EO 14418 still tries to do

Section 1 quotes *Barbara* as holding that the Clause extends citizenship to children born to parents in the United States "for whom no extraterritorial fiction applie[s]." It then says the order "identifies, non-exhaustively, and prescribes action concerning certain categories of children of aliens who do not fall within the rule of birthright citizenship as announced by the Supreme Court."

Section 2 policy: no executive department or agency shall issue documents recognizing U.S. citizenship, or accept state or local documents purporting to recognize it, when **neither parent is a citizen** and any of the following applies:

**(a)** either parent is an "alien enemy," defined to include any member of a designated FTO (8 U.S.C. § 1189) or a Specially Designated Global Terrorist under IEEPA and EO 13224;

**(b)** either parent is a "foreign government employee," defined to include (i) ambassadors, (ii) embassy/consulate employees who are nationals of that country, (iii) persons employed by a foreign government in an official capacity, and (iv) persons employed by an international organization who possess international-organization immunity;

**(c)** either parent engaged in a commercial transaction to purchase or access birthright citizenship, or in fraud to obtain citizenship, including (i) a commercial transaction to ensure the mother is present to give birth, or (ii) a commercial transaction with a surrogate present to give birth;

**(d)** the person is born in a territory or territorial waters of the United States where citizenship is not conferred by federal statute.

Section 3: State, Attorney General, DHS, and the Commissioner of Social Security "shall take all appropriate measures." All agency heads "shall issue public guidance within 30 days."

Section 5(b): implement "consistent with applicable law and subject to the availability of appropriations." No private right of action.

**What 14418 does not contain:** a prospective-birth cutoff (14160 had a 30-day prospective rule; 14418 does not repeat it). An evidence standard. A hearing, appeal, or correction process. A definition of "parent." A data-sharing or matching specification. Any mention of Commerce, Census, USPS, enumeration, apportionment, or elections.

The Sep 5 guidance is the first operating object. Guidance is not an individual adjudication.

Whether categories (a)–(c) fit inside *Barbara*'s "extraterritorial fiction" is a later lawsuit. This note records the claim. It does not decide it.

## 7. Entity Theory read

Who is inside the group. *Barbara* says the U.S.-born child of an unlawfully or temporarily present parent is a member at birth. EO 14418 tries to keep a documentary gate after that holding, aimed at residual categories the order claims sit outside the "extraterritorial fiction" the Court named. Passports, Numident codes, and accepted state birth records are how the group *recognizes* a member. They are not how the Clause *makes* one. The Sep 5 guidance will show which agencies are willing to treat a document as if it were the Clause.
