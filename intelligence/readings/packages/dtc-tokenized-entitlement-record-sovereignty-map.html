# DTC Tokenized Entitlements — Record-Sovereignty Legal Map

**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-08-02  
**Lane status:** source-bearing factual audit; not legal advice  
**Scope:** DTC's tokenization service and the record, control, corporate-action, insolvency, and exit consequences of representing DTC-held securities entitlements on permissioned ledgers.  
**Source rule:** official SEC, DTC/DTCC, and Investor.gov materials only. Industry submissions hosted by the SEC are included solely as labeled commenter positions.

## Executive ruling

The current DTC tokenization model does **not** make the public or permissioned blockchain the shareholder register, disintermediate Cede & Co., or give a wallet holder a direct legal claim against DTC. It creates a controlled token representation of a DTC participant's Article 8 securities entitlement while preserving the existing indirect-holding chain.

The legally relevant records remain layered:

1. the issuer/transfer agent's master securityholder file remains authoritative for **registered ownership**; it ordinarily shows **Cede & Co.** as registered holder for the fungible bulk deposited at DTC;
2. DTC's books remain authoritative for the **participant-level securities entitlement**;
3. in the token service, DTC's Digital Omnibus Account and **LedgerScan** become the official DTC records for tokenized participant entitlements;
4. the participant's books remain authoritative for a customer's downstream beneficial interest or securities entitlement; and
5. the blockchain records token movements, but DTC observes them, retains root-administrator power to reverse or correct them, and treats LedgerScan—not unaided chain state—as its official record.

The shortest accurate formulation is therefore:

> **The token is a programmable representation inside DTC's entitlement ledger, not an autonomous bearer share and not an issuer-register substitute.**

DTC has the decisive token-administration authorities: mint, burn, pause, freeze, unfreeze, claw back, force transfer, and correct without the affected wallet's private key. A participant—or a customer given operational use of a participant-associated wallet—can initiate allowed transfers, but DTC recognizes only the participant as the entitlement holder. Possession of wallet keys does not itself create issuer-registered ownership, direct DTC participant status, or a direct DRS exit.

The practical competitive question is thus not whether “the blockchain” replaces the recordkeeper. It is **which regulated institution's record the blockchain updates**:

- in DTC's model, DLT updates a participant-entitlement layer beneath Cede's issuer-register position;
- in an issuer-sponsored model, DLT can be integrated into the transfer agent's official master securityholder file and update registered ownership directly.

SEC staff has expressly described both architectures. It has not adopted a Commission rule declaring one model the exclusive or preferred legal record.

## I. The record stack

### 1. Issuer register / transfer-agent master securityholder file

| Question | Current answer |
|---|---|
| Legal object | Registered ownership of the issuer's security. |
| Ordinary registered holder for DTC-deposited fungible bulk | **Cede & Co.**, DTC's nominee. |
| Authoritative record | The issuer/registered transfer agent's master securityholder file. SEC transfer-agent rules require maintenance of securityholder records; SEC staff states that a registered transfer agent may use DLT as the official master securityholder file or a component of it. |
| Mint / issue authority | The issuer and its transfer agent govern issuance and registration. DTC's token mint does **not** issue a new share or change the issuer's outstanding share count; it converts the form in which a participant entitlement is represented. |
| Burn / cancellation | The transfer agent cancels or transfers registered positions under issuer authority and applicable law. A DTC token burn merely returns the participant entitlement from the Digital Omnibus Account to DTC's conventional book-entry ledger. |
| Freeze / stop / compelled transfer / correction | Implemented at this layer by the issuer/transfer agent under governing law, court order, stop-transfer authority, corporate documents, or SEC transfer-agent requirements. DTC may separately freeze or force-transfer the token representation at its own layer. |
| Corporate actions | The issuer/agent acts against the registered-holder list. For DTC-held positions, payment, notice, and voting authority ordinarily flow first through Cede/DTC. |
| Insolvency significance | The issuer's bankruptcy affects the underlying security according to its substantive rights; tokenization does not improve the holder's priority. Transfer-agent failure or replacement does not make the blockchain automatically supersede the issuer's official records. |
| Exit from street name | Achieved when the transfer agent debits the Cede/FAST position and registers the investor directly, usually through DRS. |

**Authorities:** SEC, [Transfer Agents](https://www.sec.gov/about/divisions-offices/division-trading-markets/transfer-agents); SEC Trading and Markets staff, [DLT FAQs, Question 11](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/trading-markets-frequently-asked-questions/frequently-asked-questions-relating-crypto-asset-activities-distributed-ledger-technology); DTC, [Operational Arrangements](https://www.dtcc.com/~/media/Files/Downloads/legal/issue-eligibility/eligibility/operational-arrangements.pdf), FAST and DRS provisions.

### 2. Cede & Co. registered position

| Question | Current answer |
|---|---|
| Legal owner shown to issuer | Cede & Co. is the record owner for securities held by DTC in fungible bulk. |
| Authoritative record | Issuer/transfer-agent register, reconciled against the Cede/FAST balance. |
| Who controls the position | DTC acts through its nominee; the transfer agent maintains the registered balance. Participants do not individually appear on the issuer register merely because their entitlements are tokenized. |
| Corporate actions | Cede receives the issuer-level distribution or voting authority. DTC allocates distributions to participants and transmits voting authority through the Omnibus Proxy. |
| Insolvency | DTC's rules include wind-down machinery for transferring rights relating to Cede and fungible-bulk assets to a transferee. The token pilot does not create a separate registered-owner insolvency regime. |
| Exit | Cede's registered balance is reduced when a participant withdraws the position into direct registration or certificate form through ordinary DTC/transfer-agent facilities. |

**Authorities:** DTC [Rules](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/legal/rules/dtc_rules.pdf), Rules 1, 6, and 32A; DTC, [Proxy Services](https://www.dtcc.com/asset-services/issuer-services/proxy-services); DTC, [Operational Arrangements](https://www.dtcc.com/~/media/Files/Downloads/legal/issue-eligibility/eligibility/operational-arrangements.pdf).

### 3. DTC conventional participant ledger

| Question | Current answer |
|---|---|
| Legal object | A participant's Article 8 securities entitlement against DTC as securities intermediary. |
| Entitlement holder | The DTC participant or pledgee to whose DTC account the security is credited. |
| Authoritative record | DTC's centralized books and records. |
| Transfer / correction | A DTC delivery debits one account and credits another under DTC rules. DTC can decline or reverse activity under its rules and procedures. |
| Corporate actions | DTC allocates distributions and voting authority according to record-date participant positions. |
| Insolvency | DTC's no-action request states that financial assets held to satisfy entitlements are not property of DTC under UCC § 8-503 and characterizes the position as bankruptcy remote. This is not a blanket resolution of every shortfall, operational failure, or insolvency fact pattern. |
| Exit | Conventional DTC withdrawal routes include DRS/FAST/DWAC or, where eligible, certificated withdrawal. |

**Authorities:** DTC [Rules](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/legal/rules/dtc_rules.pdf), Rules 1, 5, 6, and 12; DTC [No-Action Request and SEC Staff Response](https://www.sec.gov/files/tm/no-action/dtc-nal-121125.pdf), Article 8 analysis.

### 4. Digital Omnibus Account

| Question | Current answer |
|---|---|
| Function | A DTC account on the conventional centralized system holding the aggregate amount of all securities entitlements currently represented by tokens. |
| Legal effect of tokenization | DTC debits the participant's conventional account, credits the Digital Omnibus Account, and mints a corresponding token to a registered wallet. Registered ownership remains with Cede. |
| Authoritative balance constraint | The aggregate tokenized position must reconcile to the conventional entitlement balance in the Digital Omnibus Account. |
| Mobility | Securities in the omnibus account cannot be moved through ordinary DTC book-entry channels until the token is burned/detokenized. |
| Settlement / collateral status | DTC assigns no DTC settlement value, collateral value, or default-management use to the token. Cash-side DVP occurs away from DTC. |

**Authorities:** DTC [No-Action Request](https://www.sec.gov/files/tm/no-action/dtc-nal-121125.pdf), service architecture, flow of tokenization, and financial-risk limitations; DTCC, [Tokenization Service FAQ](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/digital-assets/dtc-tokenization-service-faq.pdf).

### 5. LedgerScan official DTC record

| Question | Current answer |
|---|---|
| Function | DTC's off-chain cloud service scans supported ledgers, associates registered wallets with participants, and records tokenized entitlements. |
| Legal/operational status | DTC expressly designates the LedgerScan record as part of its official books and records. |
| What it proves inside DTC | Which DTC participant DTC recognizes as entitlement holder for a tokenized position. |
| Conflict posture | DTC retains root controls and says its centralized systems and ultimate final record support recovery. The public materials do not yet provide a complete conflict-of-record procedure for every chain fork, stale scan, duplicate event, rollback, or irreconcilable mismatch. |
| System tier | LedgerScan and the token factory are described as Tier 2 systems, with a four-hour recovery-time objective and two-minute data-loss objective, rather than the Tier 1 status of DTC's core Regulation SCI systems. |

**Authorities:** DTC [No-Action Request](https://www.sec.gov/files/tm/no-action/dtc-nal-121125.pdf), LedgerScan, Business Continuity Planning, and Systems Compliance and Integrity sections; DTCC, [Tokenization Service FAQ](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/digital-assets/dtc-tokenization-service-faq.pdf), reconciliation and records answers.

### 6. On-chain token and chain state

| Question | Current answer |
|---|---|
| Object | A programmable representation of a DTC participant's securities entitlement, using the same CUSIP as the conventional position. |
| Is chain state the issuer's shareholder register? | **No** under the current DTC service. The issuer register still shows Cede. |
| Is chain state an autonomous DTC master record? | **No.** DTC observes supported-ledger activity through LedgerScan and reserves powers to override or correct token state. |
| Who can administer | DTC's root wallet can mint, burn, pause/unpause, freeze/unfreeze, force-transfer, and claw back. The no-action request says those actions can be taken without the affected wallet's private key. |
| Transfer finality | A permitted wallet-to-wallet transfer changes the participant entitlement DTC records through LedgerScan, but the transfer is administratively reversible and does not itself alter Cede's issuer-register position. |
| Cross-chain movement | DTC burns on the source network and mints on the destination network; there is no free-standing bridge asset that escapes DTC administration. |
| Current supported networks identified by DTC | AppChain (Hyperledger Besu), Canton, and Stellar. Network availability does not change the legal record hierarchy. |

**Authorities:** DTCC, [Tokenization](https://www.dtcc.com/digital-assets/tokenization); DTC [No-Action Request](https://www.sec.gov/files/tm/no-action/dtc-nal-121125.pdf); DTCC, [Tokenization Service FAQ](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/digital-assets/dtc-tokenization-service-faq.pdf).

### 7. Registered wallet and private keys

| Question | Current answer |
|---|---|
| Who may register | DTC participants register wallets with DTC. A participant may establish a wallet for customer benefit and may contractually let the customer use the keys. |
| Whom DTC recognizes | The associated DTC participant—not the customer keyholder—as entitlement holder. |
| Does “non-custodial wallet” change custody or title? | DTC says no. Wallet custody terminology does not change the DTC-participant relationship or the underlying indirect-holding chain. |
| What keys confer | Operational ability to sign permitted token instructions, subject to participant terms, network rules, DTC observation, and DTC root controls. |
| What keys do not confer | Direct registration with the issuer, DTC membership, an independent claim against DTC, immunity from clawback, or an automatic DRS route. |
| Lost/stolen keys | DTC's root powers are expressly designed to address lost/stolen tokens and erroneous or unauthorized activity. Public materials do not establish that every customer can invoke that relief directly; the customer relationship remains with the participant. |

**Authority:** DTC [No-Action Request](https://www.sec.gov/files/tm/no-action/dtc-nal-121125.pdf), Registered Wallets, Customer Relationships, and Root Wallet provisions; DTCC, [Tokenization Service FAQ](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/digital-assets/dtc-tokenization-service-faq.pdf).

### 8. Participant's customer subledger / beneficial interest

| Question | Current answer |
|---|---|
| Legal object | The customer's beneficial interest or Article 8 securities entitlement against its broker/custodian as securities intermediary. |
| Authoritative record | The participant's customer-account records, not DTC's register and not mere possession of a wallet key. |
| DTC relationship | DTC says it has no legal relationship with the participant's customer and recognizes only the participant associated with the wallet. |
| Transfer | A customer may be allowed to operate a wallet under bilateral arrangements, but the participant remains responsible to DTC. A customer-to-customer transfer may require movement between wallets associated with DTC participants and updates in both intermediaries' customer records. |
| Corporate actions | Economic and voting rights flow from DTC to participant and then to customer under the broker/custodian agreement and applicable law. |
| Participant insolvency | Tokenization does not make the customer a direct DTC entitlement holder. The customer's protection continues to depend on Article 8, segregation and recordkeeping, and—where applicable—the broker-dealer/SIPA regime. Wallet-key possession alone does not bypass that intermediary insolvency. |
| Exit | The customer must instruct its broker/custodian to detokenize and process a DRS/FAST/DWAC withdrawal; it cannot alter the issuer register simply by sending the token to an unregistered personal address. |

**Authorities:** DTC [No-Action Request](https://www.sec.gov/files/tm/no-action/dtc-nal-121125.pdf), Customer Relationships and Article 8 analysis; Investor.gov, [Holding Your Securities](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-97).

### 9. DRS / FAST / DWAC direct-registration exit

| Question | Current answer |
|---|---|
| Legal change | The investor ceases to hold only through street name and becomes a registered owner on the issuer/transfer-agent books. |
| FAST role | The FAST transfer agent acts as custodian for DTC's balance and reconciles the Cede position; FAST supports movement between DTC's indirect system and direct registration. |
| DRS role | DRS records the investor directly with the issuer/transfer agent without a physical certificate. |
| Token prerequisite | Under the current DTC service, the token must first be burned/detokenized so the entitlement returns from the Digital Omnibus Account to a conventional participant account. The ordinary DRS/FAST/DWAC instruction then changes the issuer-register position. |
| Direct token-to-register path | No direct token-to-DRS or token-to-issuer-register conversion path is described in the current DTC no-action package or FAQ. |
| Timing / control implication | The blockchain transfer can be 24/7, but exit from DTC street name still depends on participant, DTC, and transfer-agent operating windows and procedures. |

**Authorities:** DTC, [Operational Arrangements](https://www.dtcc.com/~/media/Files/Downloads/legal/issue-eligibility/eligibility/operational-arrangements.pdf), FAST, DRS, and DWAC provisions; Investor.gov, [Holding Your Securities](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/general-resources/news-alerts/alerts-bulletins/investor-bulletins-97); DTC [No-Action Request](https://www.sec.gov/files/tm/no-action/dtc-nal-121125.pdf), detokenization flow.

## II. Control-rights matrix

| Action | Issuer / transfer agent | DTC / Cede | DTC participant | Customer / wallet user | Chain operator / protocol |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Issue underlying security | Authorizes and records issuance. | Receives eligible deposit into Cede position; does not create issuer shares by token mint. | May deposit through eligibility channels. | None directly. | None. |
| Mint token | No direct routine role stated in DTC model; issuer consent remains a contested policy issue. | **Exclusive administrative function in service design.** Debits participant account, credits Digital Omnibus, mints token. | Instructs DTC to tokenize eligible position. | May request through participant. | Executes contract transaction subject to DTC permissions. |
| Burn / detokenize | No change to issuer register. | Burns token, debits Digital Omnibus, credits participant conventional account. | Instructs DTC. | Requests through participant. | Executes contract transaction. |
| Ordinary token transfer | Issuer register unchanged. | Observes and records through LedgerScan. | Associated participant remains the legally recognized DTC actor. | May sign if participant permits and supplies key access. | Validates allowed transaction. |
| Freeze / pause | May impose registered-layer restrictions under substantive authority. | **Can freeze/unfreeze and pause/unpause token activity.** | May restrict its customer's access; cannot defeat DTC root control. | No sovereign right against DTC freeze. | Implements configured control. |
| Clawback / force transfer | Can act at registered layer when legally authorized. | **Can claw back or force-transfer without wallet private key.** | May request correction but DTC controls official token remedy. | Cannot make token irreversible against DTC. | Implements root instruction. |
| Correct erroneous record | Corrects issuer master file. | Corrects DTC/LedgerScan and token state; reconciles aggregate to omnibus. | Corrects customer subledger and submits DTC instruction. | Raises dispute through participant. | Chain history may remain technically visible, but effective ownership state can be overwritten by permitted administrative transaction. |
| Cash dividend | Issuer/paying agent pays Cede/DTC. | Allocates to participants through ordinary distribution system; current token has no DTC cash-settlement value. | Credits customer. | Receives through intermediary. | Future tokenized deposit/stablecoin functionality is outside current base relief. |
| Vote / consent | Solicits registered holder. | Cede/DTC issues Omnibus Proxy to record-date participants. | Sends voting materials/authority downstream and submits instruction. | Gives voting instruction through intermediary. | No independent voting sovereignty. |
| Appraisal / dissent / inspect stock ledger | Issuer and state law govern. | Cede must act under participant instruction; DTC describes a separate proxy/legal-document process. | Must instruct DTC and establish record-date position. | Acts through participant unless directly registered. | None. |

**Bottom line:** the chain offers continuous movement; DTC retains legal recognition and administrative override; the issuer/transfer agent retains the registered-owner record. These are three different forms of control.

## III. Corporate-action path

### Cash and securities distributions

The base service preserves the conventional path:

1. the issuer or paying agent recognizes Cede as registered holder and remits the distribution to DTC;
2. DTC allocates the distribution to participants according to record-date positions;
3. participants allocate it to customers according to their own records;
4. for a tokenized position, DTC may require detokenization or use its root authority to convert or reposition tokens when necessary to process the event.

DTC's current relief does **not** assign the token DTC cash-settlement value. Proposed future payment using stablecoins or tokenized deposits is expressly outside the current preliminary service. A same-CUSIP token therefore does not make the corporate-action cash leg atomic inside DTC.

### Voting and governance

Cede remains the shareholder of record. DTC's Omnibus Proxy transfers voting authority to participants holding positions on the record date; participants then solicit or transmit instructions from ultimate beneficial owners. A token wallet does not itself receive issuer-record voting authority.

For appraisal, dissent, or stock-ledger rights, DTC states that the participant must instruct DTC and have Cede execute the relevant authority. That legal-document path remains separate from token movement and may take multiple business days.

### Reorganizations and mandatory events

DTC says the base token should carry the same economic rights as the conventional entitlement, but its no-action request also preserves the power to require participant detokenization or to force-convert a token to conventional book entry for distributions, replacements, security incidents, and operational instructions. The public FAQ does not yet provide a complete event-by-event matrix identifying which elections, tenders, splits, mergers, redemptions, conversions, bankruptcies, or tax events remain natively tokenized and which force re-entry into the conventional system.

**Authorities:** DTCC, [Distributions](https://www.dtcc.com/asset-services/corporate-actions-processing/distributions); DTC, [Proxy Services](https://www.dtcc.com/asset-services/issuer-services/proxy-services); DTC [Rules](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/legal/rules/dtc_rules.pdf), Rule 6; DTC [No-Action Request](https://www.sec.gov/files/tm/no-action/dtc-nal-121125.pdf); DTCC, [Tokenization Service FAQ](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/digital-assets/dtc-tokenization-service-faq.pdf).

## IV. Insolvency and failure-state map

| Failure state | What the current architecture preserves | What it does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|
| DTC insolvency | DTC relies on UCC § 8-503: participant property interests in financial assets held to satisfy entitlements are not DTC property. DTC rules also provide wind-down transfer machinery. | The no-action package is not a judicial opinion resolving shortfalls, competing claimants, cybercorruption, or every receivership scenario. |
| Participant / broker insolvency | The customer still holds through the participant; ordinary Article 8, customer-protection, segregation, and any applicable SIPA mechanisms remain relevant. | A customer's wallet key does not turn the customer into DTC's direct entitlement holder or an issuer-registered owner. The materials do not specify a customer-facing DTC recovery procedure for every failed-participant wallet. |
| Issuer insolvency | The token carries the same underlying security economics as the conventional entitlement. | Tokenization does not raise priority, change debt/equity rank, or insulate the security from issuer bankruptcy. |
| Transfer-agent failure | Registered ownership remains an issuer/transfer-agent record function subject to the transfer-agent regime and replacement/continuity arrangements. | Chain state does not automatically become the replacement master securityholder file in DTC's model. |
| Supported-chain outage or fork | LedgerScan, DTC's centralized systems, reconciliation, and root controls are designed to preserve or reconstruct the participant entitlement record. | The public materials do not disclose a complete fork-choice, timestamp-precedence, or contested-finality playbook. |
| LedgerScan outage / stale scan | DTC describes a Tier 2 recovery objective and retains a centralized Digital Omnibus constraint. | The exact legal priority among last-observed chain state, recovered LedgerScan state, signed but unobserved transactions, and participant customer books is not fully public. |
| Lost or compromised wallet key | DTC can freeze, claw back, force-transfer, burn, or remint without the compromised key. | A customer has no stated direct contractual remedy against DTC; remediation runs through the participant relationship. |
| DTC root-key or administrator compromise | Permissioning, surveillance, and centralized reconciliation provide control points. | Public material reviewed does not disclose root-key quorum, segregation of duties, emergency authorization, key-rotation, or independent attestation in enough detail to audit the control environment. |

## V. Exit from street name: the actual state transition

```text
token in participant-associated wallet
        │
        │ participant instruction / customer request through participant
        ▼
DTC burns token and LedgerScan records detokenization
        │
        ▼
Digital Omnibus Account debited
participant conventional DTC account credited
        │
        │ DRS / FAST / DWAC withdrawal instruction
        ▼
Cede / FAST registered balance reduced
        │
        ▼
issuer-transfer-agent master file credits investor directly
        │
        ▼
investor is registered owner in DRS
```

This sequence separates two legally different actions:

- **detokenization** changes the form of the participant's DTC entitlement; and
- **DRS withdrawal** changes the issuer's registered-owner record.

Only the second exits street name. An on-chain transfer—even to a wallet operationally controlled by the ultimate customer—does not perform that legal state change.

### C. Why SR-DTC-2026-007 belongs in this map—but not in the token pilot

The filing does not create tokenization rights and should not be cited as if it does. It is relevant because it exposes the same institutional boundary in a conventional corporate-action context:

- DTC seeks standardized participant/agent procedures and operational discretion;
- transfer agents argue that they are issuer agents and cannot bind the issuer beyond delegated authority;
- the Commission has paused the filing and requested a fuller record rather than accepting either side's characterization.

That dispute is a live analogue for future tokenized corporate actions: a programmable token cannot itself answer who has legal power to bind the issuer, set an election cutoff, reverse an instruction, or allocate loss. Those powers still arise from issuer authority, DTC rules, transfer-agent agency law, contracts, and Commission oversight.

## VII. Legal classification of the DTC token

SEC staff's January 2026 taxonomy provides the cleanest analytical lens:

- an **issuer-sponsored tokenized security** integrates DLT into the issuer or transfer agent's master securityholder file, so an on-chain transfer can update registered ownership; and
- a **third-party custodial tokenized security** represents a direct or indirect interest in a security held by an intermediary, including through a securities entitlement.

On the disclosed facts, the DTC token operationally fits the second pattern: Cede remains registered owner, DTC recognizes a participant entitlement, and the participant may maintain a customer entitlement below it. This is a factual classification **inference** from DTC's architecture and the staff taxonomy, not an SEC adjudication that DTC's product carries a particular statutory label in every transaction.

The inference matters because it prevents three category errors:

1. **same CUSIP** does not mean same record layer;
2. **same economic rights** does not mean the wallet holder is the issuer's registered shareholder; and
3. **self-controlled keys** do not mean self-custodied legal title outside the intermediary chain.

## VIII. Record-conflict priority

The available official materials support the following practical priority, subject to governing law and a court's ultimate authority:

| Conflict | Presumptive controlling record / process |
|---|---|
| Chain shows token but LedgerScan/Digital Omnibus does not reconcile | DTC can freeze/correct/burn/force-transfer; its official LedgerScan and centralized omnibus records control DTC recognition. |
| LedgerScan shows participant A but participant's customer books show customer B | DTC recognizes participant A. Customer B's claim is against participant A under the downstream relationship. |
| DTC shows participant entitlement but issuer register does not support Cede balance | Issuer/transfer-agent register and DTC/FAST reconciliation process must be corrected; token state cannot create additional issued securities. |
| Customer holds wallet key but participant disputes authority | Key possession proves operational control of the address, not direct legal status at DTC or the issuer. Contract, account records, and DTC's registered-wallet association govern recognition. |
| DRS register shows investor directly while stale token remains | There must not be duplicative ownership. DTC/participant/transfer-agent reconciliation and DTC root correction should eliminate the stale representation; public pilot materials do not publish the complete exception workflow. |

This table is a reasoned architecture inference, not a judicial conflicts rule. The current no-action package does not supply a comprehensive choice-of-record statute.

## IX. What changed—and what did not

### Changed

- a participant entitlement can move among registered wallets 24/7 on supported ledgers;
- DTC can use programmable controls at token level;
- LedgerScan becomes a new official DTC record for the tokenized subledger;
- the same CUSIP can have conventional and tokenized entitlement forms;
- DTC gains continuous chain visibility and an explicit token-administration layer.

### Did not change

- Cede remains registered owner at the issuer;
- DTC remains securities intermediary to the participant;
- the participant remains intermediary to its customer;
- voting, dividends, appraisal, and direct-registration rights still traverse the conventional legal chain;
- tokenized positions receive no DTC collateral, settlement, or default-management value;
- DRS/FAST/DWAC remains the route to direct registration;
- token transfers remain reversible under DTC root authority;
- the no-action letter does not amend the DTC Rules or settle state-law title questions by Commission adjudication.

## X. Unresolved questions that warrant the next document pull

1. **Participant legal terms.** Obtain the final tokenization service supplement, participant agreement, registered-wallet terms, Technology Standards, and any chain-specific addenda. The no-action narrative describes them, but the reviewed public package does not disclose every operative clause.
2. **Root-administrator governance.** Identify key custody, signing quorum, privileged-access controls, dual authorization, emergency powers, audit logs, key rotation, and independent assurance over mint/burn/freeze/clawback/force-transfer.
3. **Conflict-of-record procedure.** Obtain the formal hierarchy and reconciliation playbook for chain forks, LedgerScan latency, reverted blocks, simultaneous cross-chain instructions, stale customer books, and omnibus shortfalls.
4. **Failed-participant wallet treatment.** Identify whether DTC can map and transfer customer-benefit wallets directly to a successor participant, what evidence it requires, and how that interacts with SIPA administration and customer account records.
5. **Corporate-action matrix.** Obtain the event-by-event rules for dividends, splits, mergers, tenders, elections, conversions, calls, redemptions, bankruptcies, tax withholding, proxy record dates, and events requiring forced detokenization.
6. **Direct-registration bridge.** Determine whether DTCC and transfer agents are designing an API or atomic handoff from token burn to FAST/DRS registration, and whether service-level or issuer-consent terms exist.
7. **Issuer consent and eligibility.** DTC eligibility is presently discretionary. Determine whether final service terms require issuer or transfer-agent consent for tokenization and whether either can opt out or impose smart-contract restrictions.
8. **No-action clock.** Pin the legally operative “launch of the preliminary version” date that begins the stated three-year withdrawal period. DTCC's July 15 production announcement alone should not be assumed to resolve that contract/regulatory clock without a formal launch notice.
9. **Rule-filing path.** Track whether DTC submits a Rule 19b-4 filing before expanding beyond highly liquid assets, adding DTC collateral/settlement value, or approaching expiration of staff relief.
10. **SR-DTC-2026-007 disposition.** Review comments filed after the Aug. 2 cutoff, any DTC response, and the Commission's eventual approve/disapprove order for statements about agent authority, issuer assent, cutoff control, and liability.

## Final assessment

DTC's service is legally conservative and operationally ambitious. It moves the **participant-entitlement transfer mechanism** onto controlled DLT while preserving the issuer register, Cede position, Article 8 intermediary chain, corporate-action path, and direct-registration exit. The chain gains speed and programmability; DTC gains a new administrative control plane; the ultimate customer does not gain a new direct title relationship.

The genuine record-sovereignty contest is therefore between two possible authoritative uses of DLT:

- **DLT beneath Cede**, with DTC/LedgerScan authoritative for participant entitlements; or
- **DLT at the issuer register**, with the transfer agent's master securityholder file authoritative for direct ownership.

Current SEC staff materials accommodate both. DTC has obtained a fact-specific non-enforcement runway for the first. Transfer agents are advocating the second and challenging DTC's ability to expand control without issuer assent. The Commission has not yet resolved that institutional boundary.
