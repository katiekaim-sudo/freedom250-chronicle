SEC Tokenized NMS Stock Innovation Exemption

In effect

Securities and Exchange Commission · latest step: September 17, 2026

Let qualifying venues trade tokenized versions of listed U.S. stocks under conditions, without some of the usual national market system rules.

An SEC exemptive order (Release 34-106402) that runs through September 17, 2031. In effect, but no trading venue was operating under it at the library's cutoff.

The Commission order is effective and supplies two temporary conditional exemptions through September 17, 2031. Still open: No venue may operate until its complete public notice has run for at least 30 calendar days. Existing broker-dealer and other participant obligations are not broadly waived.

What it does not doThe order opens a venue category, not a named venue, chain, token or product. A complete TSV notice must run for at least 30 calendar days, and issuer, issuance, record, custody, clearing, settlement, participant and failure rules remain separate.

Still unknownWhich venue, if any, starts trading under the exemption first.

What right it creates
The pool must contain Tokenized NMS Stock carrying the same company interest, voting, dividend and residual rights as the underlying NMS stock. Still open: An actual issue, tokenizer, holder-rights instrument and Securities Act route must still be identified. Synthetic exposures, rights and warrants are outside the defined stock category.
Who it covers
The order separates the TSV operator, affiliates, tokenizers, liquidity providers, participants, registered activities and service providers and requires public conflict disclosure. Still open: No Hub company or ecosystem is named as a TSV, Covered Firm, tokenizer, participant or service provider. Each legal entity and role must be proved from the eventual notice and contracts.
Which record counts
Token holders must receive equivalent stock rights and the TSV must disclose the tokenization, legal and technical evaluation, wallets, chain, contracts, keys and correction-related controls. Still open: The order does not make the AMM, wallet or chain the issuer's authoritative ownership record or grant transfer-agent relief. The actual record hierarchy remains issue-specific.
Who can give instructions
Participant access is permissioned even though the application must be public and deployed on public, permissionless DLT. The TSV sets admission standards, pool parameters and administrative controls. Still open: The first notice must identify identity checks, differentiated treatment, upgrade, pause, override, privacy, MEV and service-provider controls. Public infrastructure does not mean public market access.
Clearing, and the money behind it
A Covered Firm may provide own-account Tokenized NMS Stock liquidity and disclose agreements, fees, rebates and token incentives. The TSV itself may not finance purchases or lend, borrow or hypothecate assets on the venue. Still open: The order supplies no CCP, custody, external financing, haircut, default waterfall or token-inventory receipt. Persistent net XRP or other token inventory remains unproved.
When a trade is final
The order permits a venue architecture capable of self-custody, extended hours and faster settlement and requires disclosure of actual clearing and settlement arrangements. Still open: Near-instant settlement is a possible benefit, not a required or proven legal-finality result. Each securities, crypto, money-market-fund and cash leg needs its own finality and insolvency analysis.
Books and servicing
The TSV must retain detailed interest, execution, permissioning, wallet, fee, halt, ADV, notice and event records; equivalent voting, dividend and issuer-communication rights must continue. Still open: Trade reporting within ten minutes is not the same as SIP reporting or proof that issuer, transfer-agent, custodian and participant books reconciled. Corporate-action servicing remains product-specific.
Errors, defaults and losses
Underlying-market halts must carry across; significant operational events require participant and SEC notice and remediation; records remain examinable by the Commission. Still open: The order can require disclosure that surveillance, complaint or other protections are absent. It does not itself provide a complete customer-loss, clearing-default, custody-failure or smart-contract remedy.

Fedwire Securities delivery versus payment (Operating Circular 7)

In effect

Federal Reserve Banks · latest step: January 5, 2026

Sets the terms on which a security and its payment move together on Fedwire Securities, which is the benchmark that tokenized settlement is measured against.

The Reserve Banks' operating circular for Fedwire Securities, effective January 5, 2026. In effect. It governs Treasury and agency securities on the Fed's own system, not tokens.

OC 7 and account eligibility govern use. Still open: No authority is inferred for a nonparticipant.

What it does not doIt is not a separately initiated Fedwire Funds payment order and does not establish unlimited credit or universal access.

Click for more: full saved dates, conditions and open questions
What right it creates
The route handles securities interests under issuer-specific book-entry law. Still open: Exact issuer terms differ.
Who it covers
Reserve Banks operate the service for authorized account holders. Still open: Participant and issuer roles must be traced.
Which record counts
Securities-account and master-account entries are named records. Still open: Customer and intermediary books need separate review.
Who can give instructions
OC 7 governs transfer-against-payment instructions. Still open: Authorization facts are participant-specific.
Clearing, and the money behind it
This is settlement service, not automatically CCP clearing. Still open: A separate CCP may add margin/default resources.
When a trade is final
OC 7 sections 9.1–9.2 govern posting and finality. Still open: Trace exact account events for a specific trade.
Books and servicing
Posting joins securities and corresponding cash entries. Still open: Trace holder-level servicing through the issuer and participant arrangements.
Errors, defaults and losses
Service rules allocate operating responsibilities. Still open: No specific outage or dispute outcome is supplied.

SEC crypto custody proposal for advisers and funds

Proposed

Securities and Exchange Commission · latest step: October 1, 2026

Give registered investment advisers and regulated funds two conditional ways to hold crypto for clients, modernize the wider custody rules, allow some required records to be kept on a blockchain, and add public reporting.

A 760-page Commission proposal issued October 1, 2026 (Release IA-7023, file S7-2026-35). Comments run 60 days after Federal Register publication. Not final.

Proposed rules 223-1(b)(7), 17f-8 and 17f-9 would create residual self-custody and conditional State trust company routes; self-custody would require a written no-qualified-custodian determination re-tested at least quarterly. Still open: Federal Register publication, final action, effective and compliance dates, asset eligibility, exact custodian availability and route-specific compliance remain unproved.

What it does not doThe proposal grants no present permission, does not cover every crypto asset or account, supplies no general exchange-custody or DeFi exception, does not make a chain the universal ownership record and does not prove a named adviser, fund, custodian, asset, wallet, protocol or transaction complies or operates.

Still unknownThe Federal Register publication date, and so the comment deadline, was not yet known at the library's cutoff.

Click for more: full saved dates, conditions and open questions
What right it creates
Custody scope depends on the crypto asset's legal classification and account type; self-custody would also require a written Article 8 financial-asset and securities-intermediary election. Still open: Determine the exact asset, account, client right, obligor, governing law and whether the mandatory entitlement frame matches the intended holding relationship.
Who it covers
Adviser, designated supervised key holders, regulated-fund board, independent accountant, qualified custodian, State trust company, technology provider, client and regulator retain distinct functions. Still open: Prove each legal entity, related-person status, delegation, vendor access, board role and contract rather than using a shared brand or wallet label.
Which record counts
Required crypto records could be maintained onchain only when true, accurate, current, retained, promptly produced in human-readable form and supplemented offchain for ownership, identity or other missing information. Still open: Name the authoritative record for ownership, customer positions, accounting, statements, correction and disputes; a public address or explorer is not automatically complete.
Who can give instructions
Possession of any portion of key materials can establish custody; only designated supervised persons may hold keys and at least two people, including management, must jointly authorize movement. Still open: Inspect the actual MPC or multisignature design, recovery shares, limits, client access, vendor access, authentication and accepted-instruction lifecycle.
Clearing, and the money behind it
Per-client address segregation, State trust financial statements, internal control reports, network reconciliation and related-cash limits would create a custody protection layer. Still open: Capital, insurance, collateral, liquidity, indemnity, rehypothecation, platform prefunding and default resources remain arrangement-specific or open for comment.
When a trade is final
The proposal recognizes network records and custody controls but does not propose a general exception for assets held on non-custodian trading platforms and does not create a universal DeFi route. Still open: Prove venue authority, cash leg, delivery-versus-payment, technical confirmation, legal discharge, staking or protocol control and finality for each leg.
Books and servicing
Addresses, balances, transactions, client statements or electronic views, Article 8 agreements, annual reviews, board records and independent reports would form a joined but multi-record custody constitution. Still open: Corporate actions, airdrops, staking, privacy data, accounting entries, tax, long-term access and reconciliation exceptions still require exact operating evidence.
Errors, defaults and losses
The proposal would require cyber response and recovery, board notice for serious events, control-report exceptions, transition to a newly available custodian and an Article 8 creditor-priority frame. Still open: Actual reversibility, insurance payment, insolvency priority, protocol loss, key compromise, custodian failure, client compensation and Commission loss reporting remain unproved or unresolved.

SEC proposal for linked electronic securities records

Proposed

Securities and Exchange Commission · latest step: September 1, 2026

Set rules for securities whose official ownership record is linked to an electronic, possibly blockchain-based, record kept by a transfer agent.

A Commission proposal (Release 34-106246, file S7-2026-30). Not operative.

Release 34-106246/S7-2026-30 was proposed. Still open: No final rule or compliance date at cutoff.

What it does not doIt would not authorize every chain, bridge, wallet, or vendor as a transfer agent, clearing agency, or FMI.

Still unknownThe library records the release but not its issue date or comment deadline.

Click for more: full saved dates, conditions and open questions
What right it creates
A security issue and holder right remain the starting legal object. Still open: Specific issue terms are not supplied by the proposal.
Who it covers
One registered recordkeeping transfer agent would remain accountable. Still open: Each linked-system vendor contract needs review.
Which record counts
A master file could span linked systems while the transfer agent retains exclusive control. Still open: Actual reconciliation is issue-specific.
Who can give instructions
Transfer-agent controls would govern record updates. Still open: No universal wallet or chain instruction rule.
Clearing, and the money behind it
The proposal itself supplies no clearing or funding arrangement. Still open: Identify the resources required by the actual transaction.
When a trade is final
A chain event could form part of recordkeeping. Still open: It would not itself establish DvP or legal title finality for every issue.
Books and servicing
The accountable transfer agent would maintain the master file. Still open: Servicing terms remain issue-specific.
Errors, defaults and losses
Named legal responsibility would remain with the transfer agent. Still open: Existing duties, proposed changes and actual provider remedies require separate review.

CLARITY Act (Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, H.R. 3633)

Bill blocked

Congress · latest step: September 15, 2026

Split crypto oversight among the SEC, the CFTC, Treasury and the banking agencies, give crypto exchanges, brokers, dealers and custodians a way to register, and add rules on conflicts of interest, stablecoin rewards and ethics for officials' crypto holdings.

Passed the House (the Senate's vote was on taking up the House-passed bill, H.R. 3633). On September 15, 2026 the Senate voted 49 to 50 on whether to take the bill up, short of the 60 votes needed, so it is not law. The Senate sponsors' final text (EHF26724, 635 pages) had been released the day before.

Spot exchanges, brokers, dealers, custodians and persons controlling covered DeFi functions would receive proposed registration or compliance routes after the required rules. Still open: EHF26724 is not law, its amendment fields remain blank, and no Hub actor receives a current registration, exemption or charter power from it.

What it does not doThis case changes future qualification questions only. It grants no present permission, classification, registration or compliance status to any Hub actor, asset, protocol or transaction.

Still unknownHouse passage date and vote tally: add from the House Clerk's official roll call (not yet in the library).

What right it creates
Network-token and ancillary-asset provisions would create new federal classification and disclosure branches for some digital-asset transactions. Still open: Enactment, implementing rules and product-specific facts remain necessary; the draft does not classify every existing Hub asset.
Who it covers
The draft separates SEC/Treasury, banking-agency and CFTC functions and adds activity-based controlled-DeFi tests plus explicit vertically integrated affiliate controls. Still open: Each named legal entity, affiliate link and control fact must still be proved; a brand or protocol label is not the regulated person.
Which record counts
Customer-property, segregation, custody, disclosure and books-and-records provisions would add qualification requirements by intermediary function. Still open: The draft does not make a blockchain the authoritative legal record for every asset or replace issue-specific contracts and record hierarchies.
Who can give instructions
Revised developer protections would expressly cover specified non-controlling development, validation, node, oracle, bandwidth and user-controlled wallet activities for named money-transmission categories. Still open: The shield is proposed and function-specific, removes the earlier express 18 U.S.C. 1960 reference, and does not create a derivatives or general-conduct immunity.
Clearing, and the money behind it
Stablecoin rewards would carry a contingent Treasury deposit-flight circuit breaker, while federal and insured credit unions would gain express parity for otherwise-authorized DLT and digital-asset activity. Still open: No deposit flight has been determined, no circuit-breaker rule exists, and asset, liquidity, reserve, credit and balance-sheet exposure remain arrangement-specific.
When a trade is final
The proposal regulates intermediary and customer-protection functions without declaring technical confirmation a universal legal-finality event. Still open: Payment, securities, digital-commodity, custody and insolvency rules still determine finality for each leg and legal relationship.
Books and servicing
Registration, customer-property, disclosure and reporting provisions would add function-specific recordkeeping and servicing duties. Still open: Issuer, transfer-agent, custodian, exchange, broker, bank and network records remain separate and would require implementing rules and contracts.
Errors, defaults and losses
The draft adds affiliate-conflict controls, exchange proprietary-trading limits, preserved state consumer-protection routes and a permanent ethics enforcement regime with specified federal and state remedies. Still open: These are proposed duties, not present findings that a named Hub company or official complied, violated, caused harm or bears a particular loss.

SEC staff FAQs on which crypto assets are securities

Staff guidance only

SEC Division of Corporation Finance · latest step: September 25, 2026

Explain how staff apply the Commission's March 2026 interpretation to questions about when a crypto asset is part of an investment contract.

Nine staff answers issued September 25, 2026. Not binding and not a Commission action.

The FAQs are issued staff guidance applying Release 33-11412/34-105020 and cite the Commission view in the proposed Regulation Crypto Assets release. Still open: They do not amend the interpretation, make Rule 400 or Form TR operative, approve an offering or grant any intermediary permission.

What it does not doThe FAQs refine legal analysis but create no rule, registration, exemption, safe harbor, venue permission, asset approval or operating receipt. The March interpretation remains the Commission-level object, and Regulation Crypto Assets remains proposed.

Click for more: full saved dates, conditions and open questions
What right it creates
Asset classification uses the Commission's definitions of functional and decentralized, while fulfillment of essential-managerial-effort promises uses the functionality or decentralization threshold the issuer itself represented. Still open: An exact asset still needs current system facts, rights and value-source evidence; satisfying an issuer-authored milestone does not itself establish the Commission's classification test.
Who it covers
The relevant actors include the issuer, affiliates, agents, any Rule 405 promoter, a successor assuming promises, a receipt issuer or custodian and a protocol-based liquid-staking provider. Still open: A brand, developer, platform or successor label does not establish the exact legal relationship or promoter status.
Which record counts
A pure receipt evidences the depositor's continuing ownership of a stated deposited asset without changing its rights or adding separate economics. Still open: Verify deposit and ownership records, exact token terms, reward source, liens and whether the issuer can transfer, lend, pledge, rehypothecate or otherwise use the asset.
Who can give instructions
Marketing current utility and indefinite non-profit-focused aspirations likely do not, without more, promise essential managerial efforts; listing alone does not make a platform a promoter absent Rule 405. Still open: Review the full communication channel, authorization, specificity, profit framing and platform relationship rather than relying on labels.
Clearing, and the money behind it
A qualifying receipt leaves the issuer unable to use or expose the deposited asset to third-party claims; post-functional buyback announcements are not managerial promises merely because they alter supply. Still open: Custody, balance-sheet use, insolvency exposure, funding source, pool economics and any pre-functional yield or return framing remain transaction-specific.
When a trade is final
The FAQs classify relationships and promises; they do not identify settlement assets, discharge rules, finality or failed-leg treatment. Still open: Every securities, crypto, receipt and cash leg still requires its own account, custody, finality and insolvency analysis.
Books and servicing
Once a system is functional, securing, maintaining, improving or enhancing it and facilitating network effects do not involve essential managerial efforts under the cited Commission view. Still open: Separate ordinary post-functional service from unfulfilled issuer promises, new profit-linked undertakings and the actual administration of holder rights.
Errors, defaults and losses
Successor assumption preserves the promise relationship; by contrast, issuer statements likely do not create a new investment contract once a functional system has no central party capable of controlling success or failure. Still open: Prove the succession instrument, remaining obligations, control distribution, abandonment or completion facts and any later promise rather than treating a handoff or decentralization slogan as dispositive.
