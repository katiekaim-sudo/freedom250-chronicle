Wave 3G — Special-Purpose Bodies

Research window: Exact 2025 Cancer ingress through the eve of exact 2025 Libra ingress.
Evidence cutoff: June 20, 2026, 11:59 p.m. EDT. Later facts are quarantined as handoffs.
Question: When the Cancer-period regime created an office, commission, council, task force or emergency board, which bodies became functioning carriers and which remained coordination or advice on paper?
Finding: “A body was created” is only the first maturity clock. The Office for Special Peace Missions acquired a named envoy and later appeared as a seat in other White House machinery. The Make America Beautiful Again Commission later convened and launched a policy framework. The sports council had a host, appointment capacity and assigned test administrator, but its public nationwide launch fell after the cutoff. The Olympics task force had named membership, a DHS administrative home and an October report requirement, yet no public report packet or completed operational plan was verified by the cutoff. The LIRR emergency board is the clearest full lifecycle: appointed members, hearings, a report, termination, a public hearing, a second board, a short strike and a negotiated deal. Even there, the board recommended and held the status quo; it did not write the contract.

The shared chain is:

purpose → temporary carrier → named chair / host → information and coordination → report or recommendation → external legal holder → operation or settlement

3G.1 — June 30: the peace office acquires a named human carrier

EO 14311 established the Office for Special Peace Missions inside the White House Office. A presidentially appointed Special Envoy for Peace Missions heads it and coordinates conflict-ending work with State, Defense and other agencies.

The order is unusually short. It does not define a geographic mandate, budget, staff table, negotiation procedure or independent authority. Its institutional content is a new coordination room inside the President's own office.

Appointment proves the office had a carrier

Steve Witkoff's official financial-disclosure record identifies him as Assistant to the President and Special Envoy for Peace Missions, appointed June 30, 2025. The White House annual personnel report likewise lists that title. Later presidential instruments seat that office in other White House initiatives, and January 2026 Board of Peace records identify Witkoff in the peace-mission role.

Maturity. Office established plus a named presidential appointee and later operating presence.

Counterevidence and limit. The office remains a White House Office component. Coordination with State and Defense does not transfer the Secretary of State's foreign-policy stewardship, Title 10 command, treaty-making power or appropriations. A peace agreement, ceasefire, recognition decision, foreign commitment and operating international body each needs its own instrument and parties. The later Board of Peace is a separate legal object, not evidence that EO 14311 itself created it.

3G.2 — July 3: the conservation commission becomes an interagency policy room

EO 14313 created the President's Make America Beautiful Again Commission. The Secretary of the Interior chairs it; the Assistant to the President for Domestic Policy serves as executive director; and Defense, Agriculture, EPA, OMB, CEA, the Chief of Staff, NEC and CEQ are represented.

The commission may monitor implementation, coordinate agencies, make recommendations, develop voluntary/collaborative conservation policy and propose ways to expand water and recreational access. Separately, the order gives five statutory land-management agencies a general policy list: stewardship with growth, greater recreation/hunting/fishing access, voluntary conservation, shorter administrative delays and collaborative species recovery.

February 11, 2026: commission operation becomes visible

Interior's official release says the commission convened at the White House and launched MABA 250, a five-priority strategic framework. It also connects the initiative to an $8 million refocusing of grant programming for big-game winter range and migration corridors.

That is enough to promote the commission from paper stand-up to operating advisory and coordination body. It is not enough to assign every cited administration land action to the commission; the release aggregates agency accomplishments and political claims.

Counterevidence and limit. The commission is not a department, bureau, land-title holder, permitting agency or appropriations account. Its framework is not a withdrawal, lease, permit or payment. The five land agencies retain their organic authorities. The commission's June 30, 2026 midterm report falls ten days after this packet's cutoff and remains a later handoff.

3G.3 — July 31: the fitness council is reconstituted, but test operation matures later

EO 14327 revokes the 2018 council order and rewrites the 2002 council instrument. It creates a President's Council on Sports, Fitness, and Nutrition with up to 30 presidential appointees, two-year terms, an executive director and a two-year FACA sunset unless extended.

HHS provides funding, administration and technical support. HHS administers the reestablished Presidential Fitness Test with Education in support. The council recommends rather than commands: test design, awards, school programs, youth-fitness goals, public/private partnerships and readiness campaigns.

Maturity by cutoff. Operative council instrument, administrative host and program assignment. A verified public member roster, school participation census and completed nationwide test operation were not located inside the cutoff.

Post-cutoff handoff. On June 29, 2026, HHS publicly launched the restored Fitness Test and GetActive.gov/kids, reported state commitments and identified Paul Levesque as council vice chair. That is the first clean public program receipt found, but it belongs to the Cancer 2026 period—not retroactively to this packet's evidence state.

Counterevidence and limit. A council is not a department. HHS support does not make it a permanent HHS bureau. A recommendation to schools is not a federal physical-education mandate, and a readiness rationale is not a Defense or Selective Service order.

3G.4 — August 5: the Olympics task force creates a coordination topology

EO 14328 established a White House task force chaired by the President and vice-chaired by the Vice President. Cabinet, White House, FBI and FCC members sit on it; an executive director manages daily operations through the Deputy Chief of Staff for Strategic Implementation; DHS supplies administrative housing and funding.

The task force's work is inherently cross-jurisdictional:

  • security and counterterrorism;
  • transportation;
  • border entry and exit;
  • visa processing and credentialing;
  • state/local information sharing; and
  • removal of legal, logistical or regulatory barriers.

Member agencies were required to report their planning and activity to the executive director by October 1, 2025. The task force sunsets December 31, 2028 unless extended.

Maturity by cutoff. Established coordination body, named membership and reporting route. DHS and other agencies had preexisting Olympics planning and budget objects, but the reviewed public record did not supply the task force's October 1 report packet, a clearly identified executive director, a complete federal operating plan, issued Olympic credential or EO-attributable visa decision.

Counterevidence and limit. Administrative housing at DHS does not merge State, Treasury, Defense, Justice, Commerce, Transportation, FCC or FBI into DHS. Membership does not transfer each member's statutory decision right. A forecast, budget request or planning procurement is not 2028 operational readiness.

3G.5 — September 16–18: the LIRR board shows what a temporary body can actually do

The LIRR and five unions left statutory mediation on August 18. A 30-day cooling-off period began August 19; absent agreement or a presidential board, the parties could have exercised self-help at 12:01 a.m. on September 18.

EO 14349 created Presidential Emergency Board 253 effective at that exact September 18 threshold. It required a three-member neutral board, a report within 30 days and a 120-day status-quo period. The board terminates when it reports; its files then pass into National Mediation Board physical custody.

This is the packet's clearest example of a body whose design includes its own demise.

The later record completes the lifecycle

  • The President appointed Ira Jaffe as chair and Sidney Moreland IV and Thomas Pontolillo as members.
  • PEB 253 reported on October 17, 2025 and terminated.
  • The parties did not all accept its recommendations; NMB held the required public hearing on November 17.
  • EO 14374 created second board PEB 254 on January 16, 2026.
  • PEB 254 reported on March 16 and selected between the parties' final offers.
  • After the final statutory cooling-off period, workers struck on May 16.
  • New York's official record says a tentative deal was reached on May 19 and service returned.

Maturity. Full report-and-termination lifecycle, second-board sequel, short operating strike and negotiated settlement.

Counterevidence and limit. Neither board imposed a contract. The first board's recommendation did not settle the dispute; even the second final-offer process did not eliminate bargaining or self-help. The state, carrier and unions—not the temporary board alone—carried the final agreement. The National Mediation Board's custody of records is not ownership of LIRR or of the presidential board.

What changed during the Cancer period

  • The White House added a personalized peace-coordination office.
  • Interior received an interagency conservation advice-and-monitoring room.
  • HHS received the administrative carrier for a reconstituted sports council and fitness-test program.
  • DHS received the administrative home for a presidential Olympics coordination network.
  • A statutory rail-dispute clock was interrupted by a purpose-built neutral board at the edge of self-help.
  • Temporary bodies became a repeatable way to connect existing agencies without formally merging them.

What did not change merely because a body was named

  • An office did not become a department.
  • A commission did not acquire land title or permitting authority.
  • A council did not mandate a school program.
  • A task force did not command every member agency.
  • A required report was not proof of a completed plan when the report itself was not public.
  • An emergency board did not become an arbitrator with compulsory contract power.
  • Administrative housing, funding support and records custody did not transfer organic authority.

Institutional theorem

The regime uses thin temporary bodies as coordination membranes.

They are powerful when the task is to:

  1. convene actors who otherwise hold separate authorities;
  2. collect information into one presidential room;
  3. create deadlines and a common agenda;
  4. make recommendations or define a handoff; and
  5. delay irreversible action while the surviving holders bargain or decide.

They are weak when the desired outcome requires title, appropriation, adjudication, contract, permit or operation that the body does not legally hold. The body's maturity should therefore be scored by its native function—not by whether it looks like a miniature agency on an organization chart.

Both poles

Constructive pole — purpose-built coordination preserves distinct authority

  • a small presidential office can coordinate diplomacy without recreating State;
  • a commission can align land agencies while leaving organic powers intact;
  • an advisory council can combine public, private, school and health expertise;
  • an Olympics task force can expose interagency barriers years before the event;
  • a statutory emergency board can prevent abrupt service disruption and give parties a neutral record; and
  • sunsets can prevent temporary coordination bodies from silently becoming permanent agencies.

Shadow pole — the body becomes a theater of completion

  • a prestigious title can obscure an unstaffed or undefined office;
  • a commission can claim agency outcomes it only aggregated;
  • a council can turn health into national-performance symbolism before schools have an implementable program;
  • a mega-event task force can centralize security and entry systems behind unpublished plans;
  • a neutral report can delay but fail to resolve conflict; and
  • a temporary body can multiply executive interfaces without making responsibility easier to locate.

The evidence supports two visibly operating advisory/coordination carriers, one fully completed statutory-board lifecycle, one assigned-but-later fitness program and one Olympics coordination body with unresolved public implementation. It does not support five equally mature institutions.
