Who owns and activates federal emergency communications

Public-safety radio, satellites, fiber, airwaves, tribal rights and continuity plans form one emergency communications layer. The study keeps the assets, the rights, the operators, everyday access and emergency activation as separate things.

What we found

  • The study's scope covers public safety, satellite, fiber, spectrum, tribal sovereignty, continuity of government and government access.
  • Its core rule: owning a network, holding the right to use it, running it, using it day to day and taking it over in an emergency are five separate states.
  • A dated September 29, 2026 map refreshes only one seam: how states and the federal government coordinate emergency communications through NCSWIC. It does not update the rest of the package.

Why it matters

Emergency communications are a quiet place where federal, state, tribal and private control meet. Who can switch a network into emergency use is a procedural power, not a visible one.
