COVID origins files: the paper's wording hardened late, but no proof of a directed cover-up

The Senate reading-room records show the Proximal Origin authors' view shifted in stages, a sweeping "no lab scenario is plausible" sentence was added late after peer review, and press messaging overstated certainty. They do not show government direction, a knowing falsehood, a lab origin or destroyed records.

What we found

  • A January 31, 2020 Fauci email asked for rapid expert review of a furin-site concern and proposed notifying the FBI and MI5 if it held up. The first notes on February 1 treated engineering, lab passage or accident, and natural spillover as separate possibilities.
  • The February 17 manuscript sent to Nature did not say every lab scenario was implausible, and Andersen still favored lab culture passage as the likelier lab route. On February 20 the authors told reviewers the pangolin data did not refute a lab origin.
  • The blanket no-lab sentence first appears in the March 4–5 drafts and stayed in the March 17 Nature Medicine paper, which also kept a line that other theories could not be proved or disproved. The Scripps press release said the analysis firmly determined a natural origin.
  • On February 2 Fauci wrote to Collins, "Please delete this e-mail after you read it." The email survived and was produced; no record shows it was deleted, that federal records were destroyed, or why he asked.
  • At an April 17 briefing Fauci said the mutations were "totally consistent" with an animal-to-human jump. The study grades this accurate as far as it goes but incomplete on accidental lab release, with knowing falsehood not shown. Andersen testified in 2023 that Fauci and Collins did not control the drafting.

Why it matters

This is a test of who holds authority over the scientific record and how institutions turn qualified findings into public certainty. The record supports scrutiny of messaging and record-keeping, not proof of a coordinated plan.

What to watch

  • pending · Native draft history and journal editorial files showing who asked for the March 4–5 no-lab sentence.
  • pending · Evidence on whether DEFUSE-type experiments were actually performed, which the study names as the next priority.
  • pending · Federal retention and mailbox records for the February 2 email.
