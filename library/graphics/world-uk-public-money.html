Freedom 250 · Foreign relations and world money · United Kingdom

Who runs the City of London and UK public money?

HM Treasury runs UK public money: it administers the UK Consolidated Fund, the National Loans Fund and the Debt Management Account and owns the Exchange Equalisation Account, the Bank of England and The Royal Mint; the Bank of England (whose capital is held for Treasury, with statutory independence) operates RTGS / CHAPS and acts as Treasury's agent for the Exchange Equalisation Account. Parliament legislates for 'tax, Supply and financial-services law', and the National Audit Office serves it. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland each keep their own consolidated fund, answerable to their legislature. The City of London is run by the City of London Corporation, through the Court of Common Council and the Court of Aldermen; it keeps the City Fund, the City's Estate and the City Bridge Foundation separate, and it 'does not set UK tax, regulate UK finance or constitute a state within a state.' It is a different body from the Greater London Authority and the Mayor of London. 'The City' as a financial market is not a legal person: the FCA and the PRA regulate the firms in it.
