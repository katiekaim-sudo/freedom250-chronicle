Controlling finding

The less-obvious companies are not merely small suppliers. They frequently sit at one of four more consequential seams:

  1. they render the stimulus against which a system is judged;
  2. they create or custody the raw evidence needed to reproduce a result;
  3. they write the technical record on which capital, permitting or procurement decisions rely; or
  4. they operate the range, shipment, waste, calibration or insurance rail without holding the final public decision.

The most important risk is therefore role compression, not ordinary ownership by itself. A corporate family may help specify, supply, calibrate, test, interpret and later implement. That is a structural concern to control, not proof of bias or misconduct. No public evidence reviewed in this wave shows that any named firm falsified a result or improperly influenced a Science Strategy decision.

The registry adds 38 cases across five waves. Named range task orders are real adjacent assignments. Technical reports are real project records. Current licenses and package certificates are real capabilities. None of those facts, without the target join and final disposition, becomes a Science Strategy award or accepted capability.

What a defensible acceptance chain requires

requirement / official scenario
  -> exact authorized party
  -> calibrated execution environment
  -> raw evidence and transformation custody
  -> independent challenge or witness
  -> named decision authority
  -> signed disposition

The first five questions for any hidden company are:

  • What exact legal entity and facility performed the work on that date?
  • Did it design, supply, install, calibrate, test, analyze or accept?
  • Who owned the source data, models, scripts, uncertainty budget and audit log?
  • Was the result independently reproducible or witnessed?
  • Who had the legal or contractual right to accept, reject, waive or pay?

Three corrections are particularly important:

  • Spirent PNT did not move to VIAVI. The current owner is on the Keysight side of the divestiture boundary.
  • Temasek's link is an ownership/control review object, not proof of state direction. Temasek says Singapore's government does not direct its portfolio companies.
  • Danovo cannot yet be reduced to one clean legal identity for award attribution. The trade-name record and the subsidiary exhibit must be reconciled to the actual contract.

Certification, calibration and insurance families

  • Intertek Testing Services NA, Inc. is the exact U.S. NRTL entity. The Intertek group can also advise, commission and test, so each scope and team must be separated before an ETL mark is treated as independent project evidence.
  • FM Approvals LLC is legally distinct from Factory Mutual's risk engineering and insurance activity. Keep the product approval, the insurer's recommendation, the owner's response and the bound policy as four records.
  • Transcat, Inc. is a calibration roll-up that also distributes and rents instruments and subcontracts some specialist work. The target must receive the exact lab/scope, subcontractor, CMC, decision rule and as-found/as-left data.
  • Schellman Compliance, LLC remains the FedRAMP assessment entity. Goldman Sachs Alternatives announced a March 2026 strategic investment and Lightyear became a minority investor, but the public record does not establish Goldman's percentage or sole control.
  • Lake Shore Cryotronics, LLC entered the DwyerOmega/Arcline family in June 2026. The first post-acquisition check is accreditation continuity and the exact LLC name, not an assumption that the older Inc. certificate silently migrated.

Minimum referee-control package

  1. Exact entity, site, W-9/CAGE/UEI, parent and effective date.
  2. Exact accreditation, method, range, standard version and exclusions.
  3. Role matrix: design, supply, install, calibrate, test, witness and accept.
  4. Affiliate bids, prior advisory work and a written OCI disposition.
  5. Owner access to raw data, models, scripts, uncertainties and audit trail.
  6. Golden reference, split sample, replicate lab, witness or peer review.
  7. Vendor report kept separate from agency, utility, AHJ or owner decision.
  8. Recheck on acquisition, divestiture, trade name, lab move or scope expiry.
  9. A real second source, not a generic list of companies.
  10. Label the state as structural concern, controlled seam or demonstrated conflict; do not collapse them.

Wave 2 — PNT test truth is a constitution, not a vendor

No private company owns PNT test truth. A defensible result is assembled by separate authorities:

Ranked less-obvious companies

  1. Talen-X, Inc. d.b.a. Safran Federal Systems is the strongest public alternative to Spirent for protected-signal, CRPA/wavefront, jamming, spoofing and I/Q scenario rendering. Its DCSA proxy structure permits cleared work; it does not confer authority over the threat, cryptography or result.
  2. Spirent Federal Systems, Inc. / Keysight has the strongest incumbent-tool trail. It can render authorized signals but cannot approve the threat, validate itself as the operational truth source or accept the tested system.
  3. NAVSYS Corporation is the genuinely hidden challenger. It has historical 746th Test Squadron simulator adjacency and current alternate-PNT activity, but the age of the public simulator award must remain visible.
  4. Rohde & Schwarz USA, Inc. is an instrumentation and calibration path. Public M-noise capability must not be described as authorized encrypted operational M-Code.
  5. VIAVI Solutions Inc. / Jackson Labs is more likely to control timing, holdover, transcoding, record/replay or a red effect source. Its GPSG-1000 is explicitly for non-encrypted receivers; a jammer/effect source is not the independent judge.

The actual custody packet

The target must preserve the government-furnished threat parameters; security and encryption-material authority; simulator configuration and seed/state; motion truth; RF-channel settings; generated I/Q; raw receiver observables; calibration, phase and power corrections; event logs; analyst transformations; and the final signed government disposition.

DoDI 4650.08 assigns official threat, cryptographic, operational-test and NAVWAR compliance roles across government authorities. DoDI 5000.89 permits integrated government/contractor evidence but preserves independent evaluation and does not let integrated testing replace IOT&E. NIST's rule is equally important: traceability belongs to a measurement result and its uncertainty-bearing chain, not to the instrument brand or calibration sticker.

Wave 4 — the critical-minerals qualified-person network

A qualified person is generally retained by the issuer. The QP may write or approve technical sections and consent to disclosure; it is not appointed by the SEC, the permitting agency, the lender or the federal funding office, and it does not accept construction or production.

USA Rare Earth's unusually revealing agreement seam

Commerce's Direct Funding Agreement AP-2026-0044 allows the Department to consult unnamed advisers when evaluating disbursement conditions. Public conditions also point to implementation of a Wheat Ridge safety review, third-party validation of nuclear-material licensing and a power-infrastructure plan for Magnet Project 2. The public 8-K describes milestones such as feasibility studies, design, facility completion, equipment installation, technology transfer, production qualification, customer agreements and target volumes.

But the agreement expressly says disbursement is not approval or acceptance of the work. The Department's consultants are unnamed, the future Round Top PFS QP firm is unnamed, and the omitted milestone schedule prevents a public reader from knowing which evidence object controls the next draw. This is one of the best live examples of a missing referee that matters more than the visible award headline.

Wave 5 — commercial fusion after the reactor headline

The current U.S. fusion pathway is still moving through an NRC byproduct- materials framework. The NRC proposed its fusion rule on 2026-02-26; the comment period closed 2026-05-27 and the final rule remains a future object. Agreement States are already material: Massachusetts regulates CFS/SPARC material, Virginia the ARC path, Tennessee Type One, and Washington Helion.

Downstream chain

Agreement State or NRC license
  -> applicant / RSO / material-control program
  -> tritium source and assay
  -> dispensing and custody transfer
  -> authorized package and carrier
  -> receiving survey and accountancy
  -> waste characterization and WAC
  -> manifest, transport and authorized disposal
  -> decommissioning funding assurance
  -> insurer / broker / lender reliance

Companies that become pivotal downstream

  • Ontario Power Generation Inc. is the strongest public commercial tritium source node. Its Darlington system extracts and stores CANDU-produced tritium. A Canadian supply capability is not a U.S. target procurement.
  • Canadian Nuclear Laboratories Ltd. is the custody/dispensing node in the clearest public chain: OPG-owned tritium for JET was dispensed, packaged and shipped by CNL. That is a strong comparator, not a U.S. target contract.
  • EnergySolutions, LLC / Clive and Waste Control Specialists LLC are plausible disposal endpoints only after classification, WAC, contract, manifest and compact/license conditions are met. A license is not a promise to accept novel fusion waste.
  • NAC International, Inc. holds the OPTIMUS-L package certificate. A certificate holder is not automatically the carrier, approved user or target contractor, and the certificate may not cover a proposed content package.
  • Marsh LLC is the most important hidden insurance node found. Its current nuclear-construction practice explicitly addresses fusion, and it participated in Lloyd's fusion-insurance work. No named target placement or binder was found.
  • Beazley, Oak Re, Northcourt/NC Fusion and Newfront are market-capacity or intermediary signals. Event participation and white papers are not bound coverage.
  • American Nuclear Insurers belongs on the legal-pathway watch, but Price-Anderson coverage must not be assumed. Materials-license fusion is not automatically a utilization facility; treatment may differ by license and final rule.

The NRC's draft application guidance makes several hidden commercial roles explicit. Most facilities are expected to need a decommissioning funding plan; financial assurance can involve prepayment, surety, insurance, sinking funds or third-party guarantees. Calibration work must be authorized and traceable. Licensees must control receipt, transfer, use, storage and disposal records. Transport must satisfy NRC/DOT package and carrier requirements. Waste must go to an authorized recipient under its acceptance criteria and manifest system. Novel fusion waste may require site-specific disposal analysis.

The tritium market is also a hard physical disconfirmer. The IAEA says existing CANDU-derived supply is far too small for a commercial-scale fusion economy. DOE's roadmap therefore treats larger U.S. tritium loop/stand capacity and eventual breeding as separate development objectives, not as solved supply.
