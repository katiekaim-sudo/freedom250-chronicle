Hawaiʻi Public Corruption Primary-Source Object Ledger

Control rule

This is the package's controlling factual spine. It admits only:

  • filed court objects or an exact official docket locator;
  • enacted law, bill text, committee reports, testimony, journals and votes;
  • agency procurement, contract, award, invoice, payment and reimbursement records;
  • campaign reports, amendments, Commission minutes, orders and referrals;
  • audits, inspector-general findings and official enforcement records; and
  • official agency descriptions of their own programs or actions.

Media accounts may identify a document to request. They do not establish a fact in this ledger.

Current primary-source finding

The official record supports a consequential but incomplete chain:

DOH public testing partnership
  → FEMA-approved testing project
  → DOH invoices and projected obligations
  → Act 5 bridge appropriation
  → FEMA reimbursement

The official record does not yet support this longer chain:

named donor or intermediary
  → identified campaign transaction
  → identified official agreement
  → identified legislative act
  → identified contract or payment
  → charged quid pro quo

The second chain requires the filed indictment, exact campaign transactions and vendor-level contract/payment records. Those objects remain open.

II. HB 2392 to Act 5: the appropriation object

What the enacted law did

HB 2392, HD1, SD1 became Act 5 when Governor David Ige approved it on April 12, 2022. The introduced bill identifies Representative Ryan Yamane as its introducer. The enacted object:

  • appropriated $99,433,565 from general revenues for FY 2021–2022;
  • appropriated $71,000,000 from general revenues for FY 2022–2023;
  • stated the purpose broadly as COVID-19 mitigation, including vaccinations, distribution of supplies, testing, monitoring, reporting and related staffing;
  • made the Department of Health the expending agency; and
  • took effect on approval.

Total enacted authority: $170,433,565.

The law names no contractor, nonprofit, consortium, individual, testing site, invoice, FEMA project or payment recipient. It therefore cannot prove that any particular entity received the appropriation.

The amount changed during the legislative process

The House and Senate primary objects show two materially different requests:

Stage Date Primary-source amount and stated purpose
DOH testimony to House Pandemic & Disaster Preparedness Feb. 3 $12,000,000 for surge-response vaccination activities through June 30, 2022
House committee report HSCR162-22 Feb. 10 amount left blank; report notes DOH's $12,000,000 vaccination request
DOH testimony to House Health Feb. 15 repeats $12,000,000 vaccination request
DOH testimony to House Finance, chaired by Sylvia Luke Feb. 25 repeats $12,000,000 vaccination request
DOH testimony to Senate Health Mar. 14 requests $99,433,564.58 for FY22 and $71,000,000 for FY23 for FEMA-reimbursable testing and surge staffing
Senate Health report SSCR3238 Mar. 18 inserts DOH's two-year appropriation request
Senate Ways and Means testimony Mar. 23 repeats the expanded request; asks to remove “vaccinations” because the funds would not pay for vaccination services
final Act 5 Apr. 12 rounds FY22 to $99,433,565; retains $71,000,000 for FY23 and the bill's broader purpose language

The critical transition is therefore not “a $171 million testing bill appeared in the House.” The House Finance object contained the earlier $12 million vaccination request. The two-year $170.43 million bridge amount entered the record in the Senate after DOH and Budget and Finance supplied the testing and surge-staffing obligations.

Roll-call and committee clocks

The official bill history shows:

  • Feb. 3: House Pandemic & Disaster Preparedness passed the bill with amendments; Ty Cullen and Justin Woodson were excused.
  • Feb. 15: House Health, Human Services & Homelessness, chaired by Ryan Yamane, passed HD1 unamended.
  • Feb. 25: House Finance, chaired by Sylvia Luke, passed HD1 unamended.
  • Mar. 3: the House passed HD1 on third reading without opposition.
  • Mar. 14: Senate Health passed the bill with amendments after the Governor requested expeditious consideration.
  • Mar. 23: Senate Ways and Means passed SD1 unamended.
  • Mar. 29: the Senate passed SD1, 24–0.
  • Apr. 8: the House reconsidered its disagreement with the Senate amendments.
  • Apr. 12: the House agreed to the Senate amendments; the bill received final reading, was transmitted to the Governor and became Act 5.

Committee membership or a recorded vote proves participation in a legislative act. It does not prove corrupt intent or an agreement.

DOH described the appropriation as a bridge loan to pay invoices, maintain its base operations and await 100-percent FEMA reimbursement. The worksheet does not identify invoice numbers or payees. Its categories are not a vendor ledger.

IV. Program and FEMA objects

DOH's Senate testimony identified four FEMA-approved projects:

Project category Performance period stated by DOH
vaccinations Nov. 1, 2020–Apr. 1, 2022
Delta medical surge staffing Aug. 16–Nov. 14, 2021
Omicron medical surge staffing Jan. 10–Apr. 1, 2022
community testing July 26–Dec. 31, 2021

DOH said it was working with HI-EMA on an additional community-testing project for Jan. 1–Mar. 31, 2022.

HI-EMA announced on Jan. 7, 2022 that FEMA had approved more than $25 million for expenses incurred at 30 testing and community-outreach sites operating from July 26 through Dec. 31, 2021. HI-EMA identified the National Kidney Foundation of Hawaii as the State's testing partner for those sites.

DOH had separately announced on Aug. 18, 2021 that it was expanding free community testing through a partnership with the National Kidney Foundation of Hawaii Consortium and independent pharmacies. Those agency statements prove the program relationship. They do not state the contract value, consortium membership, subcontract split or amount paid to any named entity.

That operational relationship predates HB 2392's introduction by approximately six months. Act 5 therefore did not create the testing partnership. The appropriation record says that at least some FY22 authority would pay or reimburse testing and surge-staffing services already rendered.

Premier Medical Group appears in a different official object: HI-EMA's 2020 Safe Travels Hawaiʻi evaluation-testing program named Premier among multiple testing and data-sharing partners and said the State would fund up to $1.5 million for the overall Oct. 18–Dec. 31, 2020 program. That is a program ceiling, not an identified payment to Premier, and it predates HB 2392.

No primary object recovered here establishes that Premier and the National Kidney Foundation consortium were the same legal entity, that either was controlled by Solidum or that an Act 5 payment reached Premier.

V. Procurement-system audit

Hawaiʻi's HANDS system states that its award pages are award notices only and that the procuring agency maintains the procurement record.

An archived award search produced:

Award date Object Original award amount Awardee
Aug. 11, 2021 Reopening Schools: COVID-19 School Testing Program $4,000,000 National Kidney Foundation of Hawaii

This is a Department of Health award notice covering Hawaiʻi, Kauaʻi, Lānaʻi, Maui and Molokaʻi. It is a distinct school-testing object. The title and amount do not establish that it paid the community-testing invoices in the Act 5 worksheet.

As of the cutoff, HANDS searches returned:

  • no award notice for Premier Medical Group;
  • no additional archived award containing National Kidney Foundation that matched the Act 5 community-testing bridge; and
  • no current CPO request containing National Kidney Foundation.

Because HANDS itself warns that procurement records remain with the agency, these zero-result searches do not prove that no emergency contract, amendment, purchase order, invoice or payment existed.

X. Government Fraud plotline fit

Hawaiʻi is structurally different from a beneficiary-identity or false-claim story. The primary record is pointing upstream:

campaign-finance object
  → public official / committee gate
  → emergency appropriation
  → agency bridge funding
  → emergency procurement / invoice
  → reimbursement
  → campaign and criminal enforcement

The most consequential transparency break is between the enacted appropriation and the vendor-level payment record. The Legislature's objects are unusually clear about the total need, service categories and FEMA credits, while the public procurement surface does not disclose the matching Act 5 contract and invoice chain. That makes Hawaiʻi a useful appropriation-gate and inside-government influence branch of the Government Fraud plotline.

This is an architecture finding, not a guilt finding. It remains falsifiable: the contracts, invoices and FEMA records may show ordinary emergency procurement, reasonable pricing, delivered services and complete reimbursement. The same objects may instead identify control failures or charged recipients. The point is to acquire the objects before selecting the story.

XI. Safe factual statement

The primary record presently supports this:

In 2021 the Hawaiʻi Department of Health operated community testing in partnership with the National Kidney Foundation of Hawaii. In March 2022 DOH told the Legislature it had or expected $79.8 million in community-testing costs and $119.9 million in surge-staffing costs, offset by $29.27 million in FEMA reimbursement and a budget restriction release. The Legislature enacted a $170.43 million two-year bridge appropriation to DOH as Act 5. The enacted law did not name a vendor. A July 24, 2026 State indictment exists at case 1CPC-26-0000936, but its complete official charging text and the vendor-level payment chain remain to be recovered.

Anything beyond that statement must be tied to the missing primary objects above.
