D.V.D. Third-Country Removal Authority — 2025–2026

What decision right changed in D.V.D. between the March 2025 third-country-removal TRO and the September 2026 appellate and emergency-relief return?

Research through 2026-09-25

The short version (draft, not yet reviewed by Katie)

Court requires notice before deportations to third countries; DHS asks Supreme Court to pause it

A federal appeals court on September 18, 2026 largely upheld a ruling that DHS's guidance for deporting people to countries not named in their removal orders was unlawful, because it did not give fair notice and a chance to raise fear claims. DHS asked the Supreme Court for a stay on September 24.

What we found

  • The fight began with a February 18, 2025 ICE directive and DHS guidance of March 30, 2025 that allowed some removals without further process when the U.S. had diplomatic assurances.
  • A district court set the guidance aside on February 25, 2026 under the Administrative Procedure Act. The First Circuit affirmed on September 18, 2026, except for two rulings about the order in which DHS must try countries, which it threw out for lack of standing.
  • On September 23 the First Circuit lifted its March 16 stay, so the judgment took effect. On September 24 DHS filed Supreme Court application No. 26A406; Justice Jackson asked for a response by 4 p.m. EDT on September 28.
  • DHS's filing says it canceled a flight carrying 70 people, pulled nearly 150 people from flights and faced added cost and diplomatic strain. These are claims in a court filing, not checked against agency records.
  • DHS still has the legal power to make third-country removals. What changed is the procedure it must follow: effective notice of the destination and a real chance to raise persecution or torture fears.

Why it matters

It shows how a major enforcement policy is fought out through the system's own machinery: agency guidance, the Administrative Procedure Act, appeals and emergency applications. The power itself stays; the courts are reshaping the process around it.

What to watch

  • 2026-09-28 · The response filed in No. 26A406 and any government reply.
  • pending · Whether the Supreme Court grants an administrative stay or a stay pending certiorari.
  • pending · Any new DHS guidance setting out notice, screening and hearing steps, and records of resumed or canceled flights.

Research summary

The September 18 First Circuit opinion is a same-object legal maturation of the March 2025 D.V.D. dispute. It vacated two sequencing declarations for lack of standing but otherwise affirmed the final judgment setting aside the challenged DHS guidance. The First Circuit dissolved its stay on September 23. On September 24 DHS filed Supreme Court application No. 26A406 for an immediate administrative stay and a stay pending certiorari; Justice Jackson requested a response by September 28. The filing attributes canceled and disrupted flights, added expense and diplomatic engagement to the restored judgment, but those operating claims remain filed government assertions rather than independently reconciled agency records.
