Research summary

The task force is now visible as a federated operating architecture: the White House coordinates, agencies screen and take program actions, DOJ has an administratively operating National Fraud Enforcement Division and multi-agency National Fraud Detection Center, and Florida is an explicit consumer of Center-generated leads. Six OIGs publicly identify themselves as NFDC Charter signatories committing dedicated analysts and investigators, but the Charter text remains unavailable; its strongest proved function is a staffed membership and operating-governance layer, distinct from federal and State data agreements. PRAC is bringing a mature cross-agency analytics capability that already uses machine learning, graph analysis and predictive risk models. H.R. 9576 would make the Division statutory; the live September 10 Rules print keeps permanence and Senate-confirmed leadership but leaves duties to the Attorney General. Ohio's published model MOU proves a recurring data-delivery and permitted-use framework that expressly transfers no funds; the Florida and fifteen-agency texts, Charter clauses, technical annexes, NFDC system/model records, lead-to-case lineage, false-positive measures and collected-recovery chain remain publicly unresolved. Identity, eligibility, payment, allegation, adjudication and recovery remain separate proof states.
