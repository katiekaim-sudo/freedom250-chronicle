# IMF and Currency Pricing — The Referee, the Model, and the Program Gate

> The IMF does not ordinarily make the dealer quote for a national currency.
> Its power sits around the quote: it recognizes the member's regime, classifies
> the actual system, models the external position, polices specified official
> restrictions and multiple rates, and—where Fund money is involved—can make a
> new price-formation architecture a condition of access.

## The finding

The documents separate three roles that are often collapsed.

### The referee

Article IV, Section 2 places exchange-arrangement choice and notification with
member authorities; Section 3 provides firm surveillance over exchange-rate
policies and requires information for it. The 2022 regime-choice handbook
recognizes country-specific cases for fixed and floating arrangements. Advice
on sustainability and capacity development do not transfer regime choice to
IMF staff.

AREAER records de jure and de facto arrangements, FX markets, current-payment
restrictions, multiple currency practices and capital controls. Its observed
classification does not set a rate, endorse the regime, establish a
market-clearing price or turn surveillance advice into an order.

Article VIII, Sections 2–3 reach specified current-payment restrictions and
multiple currency practices created or permitted by official action. The saved
guidance records February 1, 2024 as the revised MCP policy's effective date.
The analysis asks which official act segments the market or taxes/subsidizes
transactions, which effective spread results, and which covered transactions
meet the policy's tests. A parallel premium alone does not establish a breach.
Temporary approval has a jurisdictional purpose and duration; it does not make
the IMF the rate's author or certify a fair price, and does not cancel the
member's applicable obligations when approval ends. The approval and the later
removal, expiry or renewed approval of the measure are separate source states.

This is legal and classificatory authority around the rate. It is not a
standing dealer mandate.

### The model

The External Balance Assessment compares actual external positions with norms
consistent with fundamentals and desirable policies. Its inputs include
current-account, real-effective-exchange-rate, and external-sustainability
models. The IMF expressly combines those models with staff judgment. The saved 2026
global-imbalances paper adjusts observed positions for cyclical factors and
generally gives the current-account model more weight because real exchange
rates are more volatile and harder to explain. A REER is trade-weighted and
inflation-adjusted; staff adjustors and uncertainty remain part of the account.

An assessed REER gap is therefore neither a tradable “fair value” quote nor a
legal manipulation finding. It is a counterfactual policy assessment with
uncertainty.

### The program gate

When a member needs Fund resources, the exchange-rate question can become a
condition, clock, review, and financing decision.

Ethiopia's 2024 ECF request makes FX liberalization and removal of rationing
and priority rules a prior-action object. The saved program account reports
completion of the domestic directive before approval. Its design authorizes
dealers to negotiate rates and specifies a daily transaction-weighted NBE
benchmark. This pass did not recover the separate directive, first benchmark
publication or underlying dealer data. The documented financing condition is
distinct from independent proof of market clearing or daily benchmark operation.

Egypt's February 26, 2026 Board statement calls for market-determined movement
and transparent intervention by the Central Bank of Egypt for disorderly market
conditions. The saved review account makes about US$2.3 billion available; it
does not independently establish borrower receipt or compliance with every
intervention commitment.

Pakistan's saved program account preserves unrestricted interbank discovery
while recording a quarterly net-sales/net-purchases constraint. The saved
wording needs its operative report clause and definitions before conversion to
an arithmetic rule. Gross sales
above US$200 million in a rolling 30-day period trigger IMF consultation.
Monthly intervention publication has a three-month lag; six-month-ahead gross
reserves and government FX-debt-service information is to be announced semiannually.
These are intervention and disclosure commitments, not a ban on every SBP
transaction or evidence that each promised publication occurred.

Argentina's April 15 staff-level statement describes wider bands, quarterly
monetary reporting and 2026 objectives of at least US$8 billion in net-reserve
accumulation and US$10 billion in central-bank FX purchases. These are dated
objectives, not realized totals. The already-saved
[May 21 Board review](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/05/21/pr26165-argentina-imf-completes-2nd-rev-of-extended-arr-under-eff-concludes-2026-aiv-consultation)
subsequently completed the second review, waived the missed end-December 2025
NIR criterion after corrective measures, modified future targets and made about
US$1 billion available. The review does not by itself show that each April
pricing proposal was implemented or that every earlier target remained unchanged.
The program comparison and the maintained symmetry audit retain that chronology.

Beyond these cases, the original control map retains program instruments for
FX rationing and priority lists, channel unification, dealer rate negotiation,
benchmark publication, reserve floors, net/gross intervention limits and
transparency, surrender and repatriation, restrictions and MCPs, auction design,
market data and dealer conduct, and capital-flow liberalization sequencing.
The specific program document determines which are conditions and when they apply.

The common pattern is second-order:

> Fund condition → domestic legal or central-bank act → changed supply,
> access, intervention, or benchmark → market price

This is a relationship to test, not a claim that every arrow occurred in every
case or before every Board decision. Conditions may be prior actions or future
commitments, and changed rules do not establish a measured price effect.
“Second-order pricing power” and the earlier “pricing-perimeter power” are our
inference labels, not IMF terminology.

## Eight distinct IMF functions

1. **Regime right and notification.** The member chooses its exchange
   arrangement and notifies the Fund.
2. **Classification.** IMF staff record de jure and de facto arrangements and
   exchange restrictions.
3. **Surveillance.** Staff assess the regime, policy mix, intervention,
   reserves, capital-flow measures, and external position.
4. **Valuation assessment.** EBA and staff judgment estimate whether the
   external position and real effective exchange rate are broadly consistent
   with fundamentals and desirable policies.
5. **Jurisdiction.** Article VIII reaches specified exchange restrictions and
   multiple currency practices created or permitted by official action.
6. **Program conditionality.** When a member uses Fund resources, agreed prior
   actions, performance criteria, benchmarks, and review conditions can change
   how foreign exchange is supplied, rationed, surrendered, auctioned,
   benchmarked, or defended.
7. **Capacity development.** IMF experts advise authorities on regime
   transition, market design, intervention, data, and reference-rate
   methodology.
8. **IMF operational valuation.** The Fund calculates the SDR's daily currency
   value and operational conversion rates from market and central-bank-supplied
   inputs.

## The pricing objects

Under a float, dealers and venues quote while central banks may transact.
Under a peg or band, the authority announces or defends a parity or range.
An auction or fixing derives its result from bids, quotes or transactions;
rationing or segmentation can leave users facing different official and
parallel prices because access differs.

| Object | Who produces it | What the IMF does |
|---|---|---|
| Executable spot rate | Dealers, venues, auctions, or official windows | Observes, assesses, and sometimes conditions the surrounding mechanism |
| Official reference rate | Usually a central bank or benchmark administrator | Provides methodology/governance advice; applies MCP tests to certain official uses |
| Parallel rate | Informal or unauthorized market | Analyzes the spread, restrictions, scarcity, and incentives |
| REER / external gap | Statistical series plus IMF model and judgment | Produces an assessment, not a dealer quote |
| Program-monitoring rate | Defined by the program's technical memorandum | Uses an accounting conversion to test conditions |
| SDR value | IMF calculation using supplied market rates | Directly calculates the IMF unit-of-account value |

This distinction stops a common analytical error: treating a modeled
real-effective misalignment as though the IMF had commanded a matching nominal
devaluation.

### How a benchmark is made

The 2025 reference-rate handbook distinguishes central-bank fixing sessions,
panels of bank quotes, point-in-time platform quotes and transaction-weighted
rates across a window. A central bank may administer the result, but public or
private administrators are possible. The source, window, executability,
representativeness, outlier controls and governance matter; IMF technical advice
does not mean IMF staff publish the domestic fixing. A TMU conversion is an
accounting convention unless the document separately makes it a market rate.

### Surveillance can recommend a policy mix

External-sector surveillance considers net international investment positions,
current accounts, real rates, capital flows, reserve adequacy and intervention.
The saved Integrated Policy Framework account recognizes circumstances for FX intervention,
macroprudential or capital-flow measures depending on market depth, currency
mismatches, pass-through and other frictions. It supplies no universal
instruction to float and no standing IMF power to execute national FX trades.

## Where the legitimacy crisis actually enters

[2026-07-30 - Who Gets Adjusted — IMF Symmetry and the Price of Access](../sources/2026-07-30-who-gets-adjusted-imf-symmetry-and-the-price-of-access-f8de40a7bcd6.html)
found that systemic members receive candid advice while borrowers face the
additional sequence of conditions, dates, exceptions, and money.

Currency pricing shows exactly where that asymmetry becomes operational.

For a non-borrower, an IMF conclusion that the external position is too strong
or too weak mainly travels through publication, markets, and domestic politics.
For a program member, reserve floors, intervention limits, surrender rules,
market-unification actions, and restriction removal can be tied to a Board
review and purchase.

The legitimacy question is not whether the Fund has secretly chosen every
exchange rate. The primary record does not support that.

It is:

> Who must turn a contested model of “warranted” external balance into a dated,
> finance-linked redesign of the institutions that allocate foreign exchange?

That question is factual and testable. It requires like-with-like program cases,
the signed domestic commitments, observed intervention and volume data, and the
distributional effects of leaving or removing an official rate.

## Price architecture is distributive

Ethiopia's program report makes the distributional mechanism explicit. An
overvalued official rate plus surrender and rationing rules acted as an implicit
tax on exporters and an implicit subsidy for importers with official access.

Changing the rate mechanism therefore reallocates more than currency:

- who must sell FX;
- who may buy it;
- which imports receive priority;
- what subsidy remains hidden in the exchange rate;
- how much inflation passes through; and
- which households receive compensating protection.

This connects currency pricing directly to the social-protection hierarchy in
the symmetry audit. A price reform may be a prior action while mitigating
transfers sit at another legal rank.

## The narrow direct-pricing exception

The IMF directly calculates the SDR's daily currency value and operational
conversion rates. But the underlying component rates are market or
central-bank-reported inputs. The SDR is an IMF reserve asset and unit of
account, not a national currency rate imposed on dealers.

The saved SDR operational account uses mid-market spot inputs supplied by the
Bank of England around noon London time, with the Federal Reserve Bank of New
York and European Central Bank as backup providers. Fixed basket currency
amounts valued at those rates produce the SDR value. Representative currency
rates for other IMF operations are reported by issuing central banks; IMF
publication and conversion are not origination of national market prices.

That operational function is real. It is not evidence of a unified global
currency price authority.

## Institutional responsibilities

| Actor | Documented authority or function | Does it normally make the national spot price? |
|---|---|---|
| Member government / central bank | Chooses regime; operates peg, band, auction, intervention, reserve and capital-control policy; may administer fixing | Sometimes directly or through rules/intervention |
| Authorized dealers / venues | Quote, match, and execute FX transactions | Yes, in market-based systems |
| Private benchmark administrator | Calculates a reference/fixing from quotes or transactions | Produces benchmark, not necessarily executable spot |
| IMF staff | Surveillance, EBA/ESA, AREAER classification, program negotiation/monitoring, capacity development | No, ordinarily |
| IMF Executive Board | Completes surveillance; approves arrangements, reviews, waivers, MCP/restriction approvals, and access | No; can govern legal/financing perimeter |
| Bank of England / issuing central banks | Supply market or representative rates for IMF operational calculations | Supply inputs; do not act as IMF rate setters |
| IMF Finance Department | Calculates/publishes SDR and operational conversion values | No national spot quote; calculates IMF operational values from supplied inputs |

## What is established

- Member authorities choose the exchange arrangement.
- The IMF classifies and surveils arrangements and exchange-rate policies.
- EBA/ESA are model-and-judgment assessments, not spot quotes.
- Article VIII jurisdiction can reach officially created restrictions and
  multiple currency practices.
- IMF-supported programs can condition the price-formation perimeter.
- Reference-rate technical assistance normally advises a domestic
  administrator.
- The Fund directly calculates SDR and operational values from supplied inputs.

## What is not established

- that the IMF secretly fixes ordinary national exchange rates;
- that an EBA gap is a legal order or manipulation ruling;
- that every parallel premium is an MCP;
- that “market-determined” means free of state influence;
- that every program exchange-rate reform was necessary, optimal, or evenly
  applied;
- that temporary MCP approval endorses a price as fair;
- or that SDR valuation activates a replacement reserve or settlement system.

## Evidence needed for a pricing claim

To establish a specific pricing mechanism or effect, identify:

1. the price object;
2. the producing entity;
3. the legal or program instrument;
4. the operative date;
5. the transaction or accounting use;
6. whether the measure is advice, jurisdiction, approval, or condition; and
7. the observed market transmission.

“IMF set the rate” requires evidence that an IMF organ itself fixed the
tradable national-currency rate. An EBA estimate, a program consultation, or a
condition to adopt a market mechanism is insufficient.

These fields govern what the evidence establishes, not whether useful research
may be saved. A missing implementation record remains a named research gap.

## What would move the story

The following are retained research questions, not new watches or completed
follow-ups. The program comparison names the outstanding country-source gaps.

- a program cohort coding every exchange-rate condition by legal rank;
- before/after dealer volume, bid-ask spreads, parallel premia, intervention,
  reserves, import access, and pass-through;
- signed domestic directives matched to IMF prior actions and Board dates;
- the duration and exit path of each approved MCP/restriction;
- social-mitigation promises classified by QPC, indicative target, structural
  benchmark, prior action, or narrative status;
- EBA staff adjustors and uncertainty bands compared across similar countries;
- documented implementation of exchange-rate advice by systemic non-borrowers.
