**Release date:** July 16, 2026  
**Status:** `pending` vault review

**Source of record:** [White House — Election Integrity](https://www.whitehouse.gov/election-integrity/)

## Short answer

The release is consequential, but it does not prove one unitary election-fraud event.
It discloses four different evidence lanes:

1. **Foreign collection and exploitation of voter data.** The reporting identifies
   a 204,822,241-record U.S. voter dataset and PRC analysis involving at least 18
   states. Some later state data was explicitly purchased or publicly downloaded
   from commercial sites; the acquisition lineage of the 18-state and 204.8M-row
   reporting remains partly redacted. The release does not establish 220 million
   distinct nonpublic records, fraudulent ballots or altered results.
2. **Intelligence handling and analytic disagreement.** Internal emails show a
   real dispute over whether some China reporting was being characterized away
   from an election nexus. They also show FBI analysts challenging the minority
   view's sourcing and evidentiary standards. This supports an analytic-integrity
   inquiry; it is not self-proving evidence that a confirmed election operation
   was suppressed.
3. **Election-system and state/local network vulnerability.** CISA documents
   serious patching, segmentation, authentication, logging and remote-access
   weaknesses. Authorized assessors obtained full control of some state/local/
   tribal/territorial networks within hours or days. The corpus does not establish
   that a foreign actor altered cast U.S. votes or certified totals.
4. **Registration and eligibility controls.** The Michigan records contain
   substantial evidence of potentially fictitious or falsified registration
   applications and a multi-year investigation. The DHS one-page summary asserts
   large noncitizen and deceased-registration matches. Neither lane, without more,
   proves that an ineligible ballot was cast, counted, or changed an outcome.

The White House landing page and the President's address repeatedly compress
**capability, source reporting, analytic dissent, database matching, and
investigative allegations** into more certain occurrence language. The package's
central control rule is therefore:

> **Name the object, evidence state, geography, and clock before carrying a claim.**

## Evidence firewalls

| Do not collapse | Into |
|---|---|
| Raw intelligence report | Finished or coordinated intelligence judgment |
| Analytic dissent or coordination dispute | Proof that the dissenting claim is true |
| Possession of voter-registration data | Access to a voting system or ballot |
| Publicly obtainable voter data | Newly stolen private records |
| Capability or vulnerability | Observed exploitation |
| Authorized penetration test | Foreign intrusion |
| Registration application | Accepted registration |
| Registration match | Citizenship adjudication |
| Registered person | Ballot request, cast ballot or counted vote |
| Referral or investigation | Charge, conviction or changed result |
| Release/public-approval stamp | Declassification of the underlying object |
| Original product date | Declassification, posting, implementation or event date |

## Current legal and legislative states

- On June 22, 2026, the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia vacated
  the 2025 DHS and SSA System of Records Notices and the modified SAVE system;
  on July 8 it denied the federal defendants' request for a stay pending appeal.
  The appeal is pending as D.C. Circuit No. 26-5243. The DHS summary's description
  of enhancements as merely “suspended pending appeal” omits the district court's
  operative vacatur and statutory holdings. See the court's
  [merits opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/district-of-columbia/dcdce/1%3A2025cv03501/285454/111/),
  [stay opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/district-of-columbia/dcdce/1%3A2025cv03501/285454/123/),
  and [appellate docket](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/circuit-courts/cadc/26-5243).
- “SAVE America Act” is not enacted. The operative 2026 vehicle is the House
  amendment to S. 1383, passed by the House on February 11 and returned to the
  Senate on February 12. H.R. 7296 and S. 3752 are introduced standalone bills.
  Track the [S. 1383 status page](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1383/all-info),
  not the slogan alone.
