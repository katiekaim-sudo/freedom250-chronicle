House Oversight storyline and reading map
Coverage: 2024-11-01 through 2026-07-15, inclusive
Committee corpus: 107 official public meetings — 77 hearings, 15 markups/business meetings, 15 roundtables, 0 forums
Status: factual-first synthesis and retrieval guide; no live-vault edits; no astrology interpretation
Read this map correctly
This is the cross-event layer over the complete meeting census. It does not replace the chronological registry or the event-level witness records. Its purpose is to show which meetings become more informative when read together and where the committee moved from diagnosis to proposed legislation.
The source boundary is literal: official committee hearings, business meetings/markups, and committee-hosted roundtables with public event pages. It excludes ordinary press releases, letters, subpoenas, transcribed interviews, field appearances and reports issued without a qualifying public meeting. The official archive surfaces are Hearings, Markups, Roundtables and Forums.
Two institutional periods sit inside the window:
November–December 2024 is the tail of the 118th Congress, when the body was named the House Committee on Oversight and Accountability.
January 2025 onward is the 119th Congress, under the name House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform. The January 14, 2025 organizational meeting is therefore a boundary event, not merely another markup.
For roundtables, use the visible date on the official event page and in the detailed registries. The archive's embedded datetime metadata is systematically seven days earlier for multiple roundtable records and appears to reflect publication rather than meeting time.
The four clocks that must stay separate
Clock
What an event can establish
What it cannot establish
Hearing / roundtable clock
Testimony, questions, documents entered, committee framing and policy proposals
A judicial finding, enacted authority, deployed system or verified loss amount
Committee clock
Notice, amendment, vote and committee reporting of a measure
House passage, Senate passage or enactment
Legislative clock
Introduction, chamber action, enrollment, presidential signature and effective date
Operational deployment unless the law itself makes the change self-executing
Implementation clock
Rulemaking, appropriations, contracts, data-sharing agreements, staffing, system launch and adverse-action process
Retroactive proof that the preceding testimony or forecast was correct
The same separation applies to executive action. An executive order may direct work and set deadlines; it does not prove that every deliverable was completed, that every agency connected its data, or that a production control system is operating.

1. Fraud moves from loss rhetoric to a payment-control architecture
This is the clearest developed sequence in the entire corpus:
The War on Waste — February 12, 2025 establishes the majority's scale argument and foregrounds identity verification, unsupported self-attestation and continuing audit.
Shifting Gears — March 11, 2025 states the doctrinal move from post-payment recovery toward prevention, data sharing and modernized systems.
March 25, 2025 business meeting advances H.R. 2277, which proposed extending and renaming PRAC as the Fraud Prevention and Accountability Committee. It is an important but distinct precursor to the later proposed Treasury IGFAR function; neither should be treated as created merely because a committee reported a bill.
Safeguarding Procurement — June 4, 2025 applies fraud-risk management to the contractor/acquisition lane; it is about risk controls and procurement governance, not proof that every flagged contract was fraudulent.
Locking in the DOGE Cuts — June 24, 2025 joins the prevention argument to spending cuts and institutional durability. Waste, fraud, duplication, policy disagreement and workforce reduction remain distinct categories.
Minnesota Part I — January 7, 2026 and Curbing Federal Fraud — January 13, 2026 put a state-administered case record beside PRAC analytics, Treasury screening and GAO data science.
Minnesota Part II — March 4, 2026 tests political and administrative responsibility at the state/federal seam.
March 18, 2026 business meeting advances program-integrity, exclusions, savings, project-overrun, fiscal-shock and spending-transparency measures. This is committee action only.
Fraud Prevention in State-Run Programs — April 15, 2026 defines the split control problem: federal money and rules, state records and administration, Treasury payment capability, and audit/enforcement after disbursement.
April 29, 2026 business meeting assembles the core legislative back end: Treasury pre-payment data access, payment intervention, government-wide analytics, a proposed permanent fraud-focused inspector-general function, continuous risk controls, workforce training and state-program audits. Committee reporting did not enact the bills.
Universal Basic Fraud — June 3, 2026 and Combating Waste, Fraud, and Abuse in SNAP — June 25, 2026 test the doctrine against provider enrollment, beneficiary eligibility, duplicate participation, identifiers, state systems and payment suspension.
Emerging Fraud Threats — July 15, 2026 supplies the proposed front end: synthetic/stolen identity detection, remote proofing and continuing person/account risk, with privacy, false-positive, exclusion and national-ID concerns inside the same record.
Control-chain synthesis:
loss claim / state case
-> prevention doctrine
-> data and analytics
-> identity and eligibility decision
-> Treasury pre-payment screen
-> hold / condition / return / pay
-> referral, exclusion, recovery and appeal
Status boundary: hearings developed the diagnosis; the March and April markups advanced proposals; later House passage of some measures still did not equal enactment; executive deadlines and agency pilots require their own implementation evidence.
2. Identity/data/control is a federation of gates, not one database
Three separate chains should be compared without collapsing their legal authorities:
Public identity and administrative records
Oversight of the U.S. Census Bureau — December 5, 2024 → April 30, 2025 business meeting → September 16, 2025 DoD background-check hearing postponed; no proceeding → December 2, 2025 business meeting → Occurred DoD Background Check System hearing — February 24, 2026 → Emerging Fraud Threats — July 15, 2026.
The April 30 meeting considered resolutions seeking records about DOGE security clearances, AI/personal-data use and federal-records compliance alongside budget-reconciliation text. The Census, personnel-vetting, benefits and fraud-proofing systems involve different statutes, operators, populations, data sources and appeal rules. The recurring architectural question is who may connect which records to which decision.
Sensitive private data crossing into public power
Securing Americans' Genetic Information — June 10, 2025 → Using Modern Tools to Counter Human Trafficking — December 10, 2025 → Crimes Against Children in the Digital Age — January 22, 2026 → Privacy Protections and the Second Amendment — May 14, 2026 → Emerging Fraud Threats — July 15, 2026.
Genetic records, platform/device data, investigative leads, firearms trace data and commercial identity-risk signals are not interchangeable. The connector is the public/private boundary: vendor-held or administratively collected data becoming an input to a government decision.
Identity handoff around unaccompanied children
UAC Part I — July 23, 2025 → Using Modern Tools to Counter Human Trafficking — December 10, 2025 → Crimes Against Children in the Digital Age — January 22, 2026 → UAC Part II — June 30, 2026.
This is a records-handoff and duty-of-care sequence across DHS, HHS, sponsors, inspectors general and law enforcement. It is adjacent to trafficking analytics, but a control failure, risk indicator or lost contact is not automatically proof of trafficking.

7. Energy becomes the physical layer beneath AI and industrial policy
Exposing the Truth on LNG — December 4, 2024 → Leading the Charge — February 26, 2025 → America's AI Moonshot — April 1, 2025 → Made in the USA — April 29, 2025 → The New Atomic Age — July 22, 2025 → Artificial Intelligence and American Power — April 16, 2026 → Winning the Economic Competition with China — July 14, 2026.
The chain moves from fuel/export and grid reliability into load growth, nuclear capacity, permitting, manufacturing, semiconductors, critical minerals and geopolitical competition. A hearing can establish an argued dependency; it cannot license a reactor, interconnect a data center, approve a transmission line or prove that a forecasted load will materialize.
8. Money, debt and tax appear as fiscal-control and household-cost lanes
This committee's money story is mostly fiscal and administrative rather than settlement-rail or central-bank architecture:
Welfare State Part I — February 11, 2025 and Welfare State Part II — May 7, 2025 frame transfer-program size and design.
Housing Affordability — January 22, 2026 links regulation, supply, credit, insurance, subsidy and household balance sheets.
The IRS Under Audit — April 15, 2026 is an operating-entity record for the tax collector.
Reducing America's National Debt — May 14, 2026 groups debt, spending, fraud and regulatory cost politically; it does not establish that fraud explains the debt stock.
The Working Families Tax Cuts in Practice — June 4, 2026 addresses claimed implementation effects of enacted tax legislation. Realized effects and projections require independent economic verification.
The Treasury connector belongs mainly in the fraud-control chain: payment screening, data access, fiscal-contingency analysis and payment intervention are different Treasury functions and should not be collapsed into one “Treasury control” event.
9. D.C. is a compact federal-control and court-design laboratory
September 10, 2025 business meeting advances a fourteen-bill D.C. package touching congressional review, public space, bail, sentencing, policing, schools, judicial nominations and appointment of the D.C. attorney general. Oversight of the District of Columbia — September 18, 2025 supplies the adjacent testimony record. December 2, 2025 business meeting adds a D.C. judicial-deference proposal. March 18, 2026 business meeting includes D.C. traffic-camera authority; May 20, 2026 business meeting includes D.C. congestion tolls.
The correct unit is the individual bill. Committee votes, House floor action, D.C. enactment, congressional disapproval, appointment changes and court rulings all run on different clocks.
The adjacent enforcement/courts thread is From Protection to Persecution: EPA Enforcement — September 16, 2025 → Farming on Trial — April 21, 2026 → Privacy Protections and the Second Amendment — May 14, 2026. Separate enforcement action, litigant claim, settlement, statutory authority and judicial ruling.
10. National security/secrecy has two distinct subchains
Records, classification and public trust
Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena — November 13, 2024 → JFK Files task-force hearing — April 1, 2025 → JFK Files obstruction hearing — May 20, 2025 → UAP Transparency and Whistleblower Protection — September 9, 2025 → Declassified MLK Records — January 22, 2026 → Mind Control and Accountability: MKULTRA — June 30, 2026.
Witness recollection, document release, official authentication, classification decision, archive custody and statutory declassification authority are separate evidence classes. The committee title often states the majority's conclusion and should not be rewritten as a neutral finding.
Strategic systems and supply-chain security
Unauthorized Drone Activity over Military Installations — April 29, 2025 → 23andMe Genetic Information — June 10, 2025 → Preparing for the Quantum Age — June 24, 2025 → Two Sides of a Digital Coin — June 25, 2026 → Winning the Economic Competition with China — July 14, 2026.
This is not the same as the declassification lane. It is a control-of-capability lane: airspace sensing, genetic datasets, cryptography, communications/information access, compute, power and critical inputs.
Non-obvious connectors worth preserving
Fraud and AI meet at adjudication, not merely detection. A model or vendor can produce a risk signal; the consequential question is which public official or system may turn it into a benefit denial, payment hold, provider suspension or referral. Emerging Fraud Threats should therefore be read with Privacy Protections and the Second Amendment, not only with other fraud hearings.
Procurement is the hinge between DOGE rhetoric and state capacity. Bid protests, acquisition security, software assets, defense innovation and military-contract overruns decide whether “efficiency” means fewer contracts, faster awards, different evaluation rules or stronger audit rights. Start with Bid Protest Reform and Military Contracts.
Quantum belongs in both cyber and records governance. “Harvest now, decrypt later” risk makes retention duration and data sensitivity material long before a cryptographically relevant machine exists. Pair Preparing for the Quantum Age with 23andMe Genetic Information.
USPS is also a payments and identity surface. Mail theft implicates checks, identity documents and account takeover, while USPS finance and regulation remain entity-specific. Add An Update on Mail Theft and Crime to the USPS management/regulator chain.
Medicaid/SNAP are implementation laboratories for the federal identity stack. They convert abstract identity and data-sharing proposals into state burden, provider enrollment, recipient eligibility, duplicate detection, notices and appeals. Read Universal Basic Fraud and Combating Waste, Fraud, and Abuse in SNAP before generalizing from Emerging Fraud Threats.
AI buildout is an energy and industrial-base story before it is a software story. America's AI Moonshot, The New Atomic Age and Artificial Intelligence and American Power form the minimum physical-stack set.
D.C. is where appointment design, judicial deference, policing and federal override collide. The September 2025 markup/hearing pair is more revealing than either alone: business meeting plus D.C. oversight hearing.
Secrecy and fraud share an institutional-trust problem but not an evidence standard. Whistleblower protection, archive access and inspector-general analytics all address whether insiders and records can surface misconduct. That does not allow UAP testimony, fraud allegations and audit findings to be treated as equivalent proof.

Transcript and document extraction targets
Universal event template
For every Tier 1 or Tier 2 event, extract:
Object: person, provider, claim, payment, contract, record, model, facility, asset or legal authority.
Actor: committee, agency, state, inspector general, Treasury component, contractor, vendor, court or regulated party.
Action: verify, match, score, hold, pay, deny, refer, suspend, recover, exclude, procure, terminate or disclose.
Authority: current statute/rule, executive direction, agency policy, contract term, proposed bill or witness recommendation.
Clock: occurred, proposed, committee-reported, House-passed, enacted, effective, pilot, awarded, deployed or scheduled.
Evidence class: testimony, GAO/OIG finding, agency record, indictment, conviction, estimate, model output, vendor claim or committee characterization.
Counter-control: notice, appeal, human review, audit trail, privacy limit, retention rule, false-positive measurement and remedy.
Fraud / payment-integrity extractions
Exact definition and denominator behind every dollar, rate and loss claim.
Whether the speaker means fraud, improper payment, unknown payment, administrative error, unsupported documentation, waste or recovery.
Data sources named; who owns them; whether access is current, proposed or technically possible.
Exact pre-payment action available to agency and Treasury; whether it is mandatory, discretionary or proposed.
Match/risk threshold, false-positive rate, identity-resolution method, adverse-action notice and appeal.
Handoff among PRAC, GAO, inspectors general, Treasury, program agencies, states and prosecutors.
Bill number and section for any claimed new authority; then separately track committee, House, Senate, enactment and implementation states.
Priority set: The War on Waste, Shifting Gears, Curbing Federal Fraud, Fraud Prevention in State-Run Programs, April 29 business meeting, SNAP and Emerging Fraud Threats.
AI / cyber / compute extractions
Compute/power quantity, forecast year, geography, grid/interconnection assumption and source.
Whether a described capability is demonstration, pilot, procurement, contract award, production use or policy proposal.
Model/operator, training/inference data, hosting environment, human decision point and audit/logging requirement.
Cyber baseline, migration deadline, supply-chain dependency and residual single point of failure.
Public/private boundary: which vendor retains data, scoring logic, keys or operational control.
Priority set: America's AI Moonshot, Salt Typhoon, Federal Government in the Age of AI, Preparing for the Quantum Age, AI and American Power and Winning the Economic Competition with China.
Federal operations / procurement extractions
Legal entity and responsible office; do not collapse department, component, regulator and contractor.
Baseline headcount, asset, system or contract measure and its source date.
Announced cut versus completed separation, lease termination, property sale, program closure or contract modification.
Bid/protest forum, standard of review, stay effect, timeline and proposed jurisdictional change.
Material weakness, unsupported balance, cost overrun, waste and fraud as separate findings.
Committee bill language versus implemented acquisition rule or workforce action.
Priority set: GAO High Risk List, Unlocking Government Efficiency Through IT Modernization, Bid Protest Reform, December 2 business meeting, February 4 business meeting, DoW Financial Management and Military Contracts.
Identity / immigration / health extractions
Person, sponsor, provider, business and account identity as different record types.
Proofing at enrollment versus authentication at login versus continuous monitoring after access.
Federal/state data handoff, update frequency, legal authority, record winner and correction process.
Eligibility rule versus identity fact versus program-error measurement versus criminal allegation.
Child-safety or trafficking tool output as investigative lead rather than automatic proof.
Medical, genetic and benefits data retention, reuse, secondary disclosure and appeal rights.
Priority set: 23andMe Genetic Information, UAC Part I, Using Modern Tools to Counter Human Trafficking, DoD Background Check System, Universal Basic Fraud, SNAP and UAC Part II.
Claim and status caveats
Committee titles are framing. Many titles state a majority conclusion. Preserve the title verbatim but write the factual summary from testimony, documents, agency records and counter-evidence.
Testimony is not adjudication. A witness allegation is neither a conviction nor an audit finding. A GAO/OIG finding is still not automatically a criminal fraud finding.
Do not convert improper-payment figures into fraud rates. Improper payment, payment error, unsupported documentation, eligibility error, estimated loss, waste and adjudicated fraud are separate objects.
A risk score is not a fact about a person. Record the data, model, threshold, decision maker, false-positive process, notice and appeal before describing an account or identity as fraudulent.
Committee approval is not enactment. Markups create committee records and may report measures. Track later chamber action and implementation bill by bill.
Roundtables are informal policy records. Participant claims and demonstrations can identify questions and private capabilities; they do not create the procedural or evidentiary record of a hearing or markup.
Scheduled time is not verified gavel time. Event-page times are filing keys until audible video establishes convening time. This map contains no chart anchors.
Archive metadata requires QA. For roundtables, the visible event date controls over embedded archive metadata; the latter is seven days early across multiple records.
Agency names and components matter. Treasury/Bureau of the Fiscal Service, PRAC, GAO, an inspector general, a state agency and a private vendor do not become one “fraud system” because they exchange data or appear at one hearing.
The absence of a meeting is not the absence of committee activity. Letters, subpoenas, depositions, reports and negotiations were excluded by design and need a separate investigative-action registry if later required.
Best next build from this corpus
The highest-value next artifact is not another narrative summary. It is a transcript-level control-object table for the Tier 1 set with one row per concrete proposal or claimed operating capability:
event -> speaker -> entity -> object -> data -> authority -> action
-> status -> evidence class -> counter-control -> next legal/operating clock
That table would let the Observatory compare the committee's recurring vocabulary with the actual machinery: who sees the data, who makes the decision, where payment is stopped, what becomes appealable, and which proposal ever moves from hearing to law to production.
