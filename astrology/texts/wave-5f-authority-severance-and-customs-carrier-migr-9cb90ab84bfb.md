# Wave 5F — Authority Severance and Customs-Carrier Migration

**Research window:** Exact 2025 Capricorn ingress through exact 2026 Aries ingress.  
**Evidence cutoff:** June 20, 2026, 11:59 p.m. EDT. Later facts are quarantined as handoffs.  
**Question:** When the winter orders used tariffs to pressure Cuba, Iran and India, what survived after the Supreme Court removed IEEPA as a tariff authority?  
**Finding:** The wave contains a compact institutional death-and-rehousing sequence. EO 14380 and EO 14382 created conditional country-determination ladders, not automatic Cuba- or Iran-linked tariffs. EO 14384 actually removed India's 25-percent Russia-oil duty and opened the ordinary CBP refund path. On February 20 the Supreme Court held that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs; EO 14389 then terminated the listed IEEPA additional duties while preserving the emergencies and non-tariff actions. EO 14388 simultaneously kept the worldwide de minimis suspension and moved postal collection onto the temporary Section 122 surcharge. The policy perimeter therefore did not simply vanish. **A legally failed tariff body was severed while customs identity, entry, carrier-remittance and collection machinery migrated to a narrower carrier.**

The packet's ladder is:

`emergency claim → country/product predicate → presidential tariff decision → HTS/ACE entry treatment → judicial authority test → duty termination → surviving customs process or replacement statute`

## 5F.1 — January 29 and February 6: pressure is designed as a decision ladder

[EO 14380](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/03/2026-02250/addressing-threats-to-the-united-states-by-the-government-of-cuba) declared a Cuba-related national emergency and made a potential additional duty depend on several distinct decisions:

1. Commerce determines that a foreign country directly or indirectly provides oil to Cuba;
2. State, consulting Treasury, Commerce, Homeland Security and USTR, decides whether and to what extent a duty should be recommended; and
3. the President decides whether and at what rate to impose it.

[EO 14382](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/11/2026-02813/addressing-threats-to-the-united-states-by-the-government-of-iran) used the same basic architecture for countries directly or indirectly acquiring covered Iranian goods or services. Its text says that an additional duty—“for example, 25 percent”—**may** be imposed after the Commerce finding, State recommendation and presidential decision.

The two orders therefore created monitoring, classification and recommendation power. They did not themselves place every oil supplier to Cuba or every purchaser from Iran under a fixed tariff. No named affirmative country finding, presidential rate instrument, HTS implementation or collection record was isolated before EO 14389 ended the listed IEEPA tariff actions on February 20.

**Institutional change.** Commerce becomes the predicate finder; State becomes the coordinating recommender; the President retains the rate decision; CBP would become the border executor only after those gates close.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The national emergencies and other sanctions authorities did not disappear with the tariff theory. “No tariff descendant located” does not mean Cuba or Iran policy was inactive; it means this particular conditional tariff carrier did not publicly reach an assessed entry before it was severed.

## 5F.2 — February 6: India relief reaches the executable customs layer

[EO 14384](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/11/2026-02818/modifying-duties-to-address-threats-to-the-united-states-by-the-government-of-the-russian-federation) is materially different. It found that India had taken significant steps involving Russian-oil imports, U.S. energy purchases and defense cooperation, and terminated HTS headings 9903.01.84 through 9903.01.89 effective for entries or warehouse withdrawals at 12:01 a.m. EST on February 7.

The operative chain was:

`presidential alignment finding → specified HTS headings terminated → entry treatment changes → any required refund follows ordinary CBP law and procedure → Commerce monitors possible resumption of Russian-oil imports`

This is a completed U.S. legal-rate change, not proof that every stated Indian commitment was fully performed. Nor does the refund clause disclose claimant, entry, amount, approval or settlement. It creates eligibility and procedure.

**Maturity.** Effective rate removal with a reimposition watch and a lawful refund route.

## 5F.3 — February 20: the court removes the claimed tariff authority

In [*Learning Resources v. Trump*](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1287_4gcj.pdf), the Supreme Court held on February 20 that IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs. The decision did not erase the customs service, the Harmonized Tariff Schedule, country-of-origin records, importer obligations or every other trade authority. It removed the legal basis claimed for this family of additional ad valorem duties.

[EO 14389](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/25/2026-03832/ending-certain-tariff-actions) translated that authority loss into executive administration. It ordered agencies to end, as soon as practicable, additional IEEPA duties imposed under nine named order families, including the Cuba and Iran orders and the earlier Canada, Mexico, China, Venezuela, reciprocal, Brazil and Russia/India stacks.

The order expressly preserved:

- the underlying national emergencies;
- actions not imposing or implementing IEEPA additional duties;
- duties under Section 232 and Section 301;
- the February 20 de minimis order; and
- the February 20 Section 122 proclamation.

The accurate state is therefore **authority severed and listed duties terminated**, not “trade policy ended.” Nor does the termination order itself prove the amount collected, stopped, protested or refunded.

## 5F.4 — February 20 and 24: de minimis survives by changing carriers

[EO 14388](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/25/2026-03829/continuing-the-suspension-of-duty-free-de-minimis-treatment-for-all-countries) says the invalidation contingency anticipated in EO 14324 had occurred and that Commerce had notified the President that adequate postal-duty systems were in place.

It preserved the suspension of the 19 U.S.C. § 1321(a)(2)(C) exemption and split administration by mode:

- nonpostal shipments formerly eligible for de minimis use an appropriate entry type in ACE and remain subject to applicable duties, taxes, fees, exactions and charges;
- international postal carriers or approved qualified parties collect and remit the specified duty to CBP;
- the postal rate is borrowed from the February 20 [Section 122 proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/02/imposing-a-temporary-import-surcharge-to-address-fundamental-international-payments-problems/); and
- country of origin and value become required postal data fields.

The annex modified the HTSUS, and the retuned regime became effective for covered entries on February 24.

This is the wave's clearest example of carrier migration:

`IEEPA rate chassis invalidated → de minimis suspension preserved → postal rate attached to Section 122 → carrier collects/remits → CBP receives and ACE handles nonpostal entry`

The legal purpose, customs liability, carrier remittance, Treasury cash and economic incidence remain separate clocks. No authority-level collection/refund ledger was isolated through the cutoff.

## 5F.5 — The surviving body is the interface

The institutional continuity does not lie in one percentage. It lies in the interface that can be retuned:

| Layer | What survives or changes |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | country of origin, importer, carrier, product and value remain governing facts |
| **Classification** | HTS headings can be terminated, amended or attached to a new authority |
| **Entry** | ACE and postal processes still decide how a shipment enters |
| **Money** | assessment, collection, remittance, drawback and refund continue on distinct clocks |
| **Authority** | IEEPA tariff power dies; Sections 122, 232, 301 and ordinary customs law remain separate possible carriers |
| **Contest** | courts can invalidate a carrier without abolishing the administrative machine that applied it |

This is crumbling institutional structure in a precise sense: the old legal justification cannot hold the action, but the operating system is capable of receiving a replacement instruction.
