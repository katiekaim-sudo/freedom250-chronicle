Federal fusion project, capital and regulatory registry, 2022–2035

Evidence cutoff: 2026-08-15
Status: pending workbench research; object-level implementation registry
Scope: the eight companies in DOE's Milestone-Based Fusion Development Program, their machines and sites, public capital, verified milestones, regulatory and grid objects, and the Genesis-linked implementation layer
Authority boundary: federal, state, utility, laboratory and local-government records control public-program and regulatory state. Company records establish company designs, contracts, experiments and claims only at the verb and boundary the source supports.

Short answer

DOE's eight Milestone companies are one funding cohort, not eight equivalent U.S. fusion power plants. At the cutoff:

  • operating objects include research machines and engineering platforms, not a commercial fusion generator;
  • three companies report DOE approval or certification of a preconceptual power-plant design, which is a design milestone rather than device performance, construction or licensing;
  • Commonwealth Fusion Systems has the most developed U.S. commercial chain: site, local land-use approval, private power-purchase agreements and a PJM interconnection application, but no operating ARC plant;
  • Type One has the deepest public utility and Agreement-State path at Bull Run, but Infinity One is a non-generating test/training platform and Infinity Two remains approval-conditioned;
  • Realta's June 19 direct-energy-conversion experiment produced real electric current at several-hundred-watt scale, while its own technical account says the output was mostly recovered input energy and was neither net electricity nor large-scale conversion of fusion-born power;
  • the public Milestone record reaches executed agreements, obligations, outlays and some verified technical milestones. Other federal fusion programs remain at different and generally earlier rungs.

The durable plot is therefore:

appropriation -> program allocation -> selection -> executed instrument -> obligation -> verified milestone -> outlay -> technical output -> commissioned capability -> licensed operation -> net electricity -> grid delivery

No current object reaches the end of that chain.

Object and state rules

  1. A company, legal recipient, machine, site, program award and power plant are separate objects.
  2. selected, negotiating, executed, obligated, verified, outlaid, completed and closed are different capital states.
  3. A preconceptual-design review does not establish machine performance, site control, construction, licensing, interconnection or electricity.
  4. An operating laser, magnet, plasma or repetitive-pulse platform is not silently promoted to an operating fusion power plant.
  5. A PPA, letter of intent, land-use approval or interconnection application is meaningful commercial execution, but none proves construction or grid delivery.
  6. Company power figures remain design or target values until a measured plant record defines heat, gross electricity, internal/recirculating load, net electricity and export.
  7. Foreign-parent, foreign-machine and foreign-site facts remain visible. A U.S. DOE recipient is not automatically a U.S.-sited power project.

Federal Milestone Program baseline

DOE selected the eight recipients on May 31, 2023 and announced that all eight had signed Technology Investment Agreements on June 6, 2024. Payments are conditional on satisfactory review of negotiated milestones. DOE initially obligated $46 million for the first 18 months; the $415 million program figure is a statutory authorization through FY2027, not an appropriation or an award already paid.

DOE's January 16, 2025 public update independently identified five completed early milestones: two for Focused Energy, one for Realta Fusion and two for Thea Energy. It said CFS, Tokamak Energy, Type One, Xcimer and Zap were still working on early science-and-technology milestones at that time. The June 2026 roadmap lists all eight as participating as of April 2026. Later company releases report additional DOE approvals; those retain the provenance company-reported DOE approval unless a DOE action record is cited.

Sources: DOE award list · DOE June 2024 strategy and agreements · DOE January 2025 milestone update · DOE 2026 roadmap

Type One Energy — Project Infinity / Bull Run

Project Infinity is an umbrella containing several separate objects:

  • Infinity One — subscale stellarator engineering-verification, test and workforce-training platform in reused Bull Run turbine-building space. It is not described as an electricity-generating plant. Current commissioning/startup target: 2029.
  • Infinity Two — prospective grid-scale fusion plant at Bull Run. No final construction decision, TVA Board approval, definitive offtake, public interconnection approval or operating license was located.
  • Bull Run synchronous condenser — separate TVA grid-stability conversion of retired generating equipment; not a fusion generator.
  • High-heat-flux facility — separate ORNL/Type One/University of Tennessee materials-testing object at the campus; ORNL says site conditioning is under way and targets completion at the end of 2027.

The 350/400 MWe seam

Dated 2025 releases, the January 2026 licensing announcement, TVA's current page and NRC's current page retain 350 MWe. Current Type One copy and TDEC's June 2026 release use 400 MWe from the same stated 800 MW fusion design. No primary change-control record explains the increase. Preserve the values as source versions; do not average them or treat them as two plants.

The January package cannot have been a completed application under Chapter 0400-20-14, which was filed in March and effective in June. Type One calls it an initial filing and TDEC says it laid groundwork for subsequent submissions. Its exact scope—Infinity One, Infinity Two, both, or a staged campus safety basis—remains unresolved.

Genesis and AI-Fusion DCP implementation

The March 17, 2026 Genesis RFA anticipated $293.76 million across all subjects, not fusion alone. Phase I was described as $500,000–$750,000 for nine months; Phase II as $6–$15 million over three years. Laboratory work may use Field Work Proposals while non-laboratory awards may use Other Transaction Authority, so a USAspending-only audit is incomplete.

The RFA makes the fusion DCP more concrete than the roadmap alone. It calls for:

  • centralized materials datasets and qualification models;
  • plasma-facing and divertor digital twins;
  • real-time confinement and driver/target control;
  • tritium processing, inventory, accountancy and detritiation models;
  • blanket thermophysical, corrosion and neutronics databases;
  • a standards-based whole-plant repository joined to public and private facilities;
  • remote-maintenance, robotics and construction-lifecycle tools.

The July 22 selection list includes named work on fusion design orchestration, control twins, structural-material creep/fatigue, born-digital interfaces, REBCO and magnet manufacturing, a D-T process-loop replica, a multimodal multi-device foundation model, plant lifecycle engineering, Realta neutral-beam optimization, PPPL gyrotron delivery, target manufacturing, diagnostic edge AI, lithium-isotope separation, stellarator blanket/neutronics optimization and structural-alloy scale-up.

All 278 projects remain selections for award negotiation in the public record. The list gives neither phase nor project amount, and the promise that awardees will gain platform access is prospective. The separate more than $800 million partner figure mixes compute credits, model access, cloud infrastructure, expertise, partnerships and some direct funding; it is neither all cash nor fusion-specific.

Operating promotion test: an authenticated user or system accesses a named DCP resource, executes a logged cross-resource workflow, calls or informs a physical experiment and receives a traceable result with model, data, instrument, identity, time and correction state.
