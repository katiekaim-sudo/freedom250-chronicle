# Super Intelligence Force — authority, membership and report gate

<!-- plain-english-entry:start -->
## In plain English

**Question:** How is federal agency AI and service-delivery infrastructure moving from fragmented systems into shared access, network and triage layers, and which authority, implementation, operation and effect gates remain open?

**Answer:** The Super Intelligence Force adds White House coordination and a reported 120-day review above existing artificial-intelligence programs. It does not yet have a public charter, budget or power transferred from the agencies.

**Evidence checked through:** 2026-10-04

The detailed record below keeps the legal wording, source limits and open questions.

## Answer first

President Trump has announced a real **Super Intelligence Force (SIF)** and
named its leadership, but the public record at this cutoff supports a **White
House coordination and recommendation body**, not a new department, military
service, regulator or government-wide operating authority.

The announcement names Director of National Intelligence **Jay Clayton** as the
lead and **FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson**, **Under Secretary of Defense for
Research and Engineering Emil Michael**, and **OPM Director Scott Kupor** as the
other leaders. It says the group will coordinate the Federal Government's
engagement with consumers, public-interest groups, religious organizations,
critical-infrastructure providers and Super Intelligence companies, and will
report to the President and White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles.

The strongest additional mandate is reported, not yet supported by a published
charter: the group has **120 days** to examine AI/SI risks and opportunities and
recommend what responsibility the Federal Government should assume. A public
charter, memorandum, executive order, Federal Register instrument, staff list,
budget, appropriations line, executive-secretariat owner and reporting template
were not located. Until one appears, the exact start date for the 120-day clock
is unresolved; the approximate return window is **January 31–February 1, 2027**.

The new body changes the political coordination layer. It does **not** by
itself change the underlying legal authorities of the DNI, FTC, Defense, OPM,
OMB, OSTP, NIST or agency Chief AI Officers. The existing Freedom 250 ruling
therefore survives and gains a new specimen: **centralized orchestration over
federated legal authority**.

## Leadership map: what each office can actually carry

### Jay Clayton — coordination and national-security framing

Clayton was sworn in as the ninth Director of National Intelligence on August
3, 2026. Under 50 U.S.C. 3023, the DNI heads the Intelligence Community, advises
the President and national-security councils on intelligence matters and
oversees the National Intelligence Program. Those are substantial coordination,
collection and analytic authorities inside the intelligence domain.

They are not a general power to regulate private AI companies, direct civilian
agency programs, control Federal procurement, rewrite workforce rules or issue
binding consumer-protection standards. The chair choice strongly frames SI as
an intelligence, cybersecurity and strategic-competition problem, but it does
not merge the 18-member Intelligence Community with the rest of government.

### Andrew Ferguson — competition and consumer-protection edge

The FTC can investigate and act against unfair methods of competition and
unfair or deceptive acts or practices under the FTC Act. It also has study and
information-gathering authority and is already examining AI markets, companion
chatbots and AI-accuracy questions.

Ferguson's participation gives the Force a real route into consumer protection,
competition and fact development. It does not turn a voluntary White House
accord into an FTC order, make the Chair the Commission, bypass Commission
votes or supply a new AI statute.

### Emil Michael — Defense research, prototyping and company interface

Michael is the Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering and the
Department's Chief Technology Officer. Under 10 U.S.C. 133a, the office directs
Defense research, engineering, technology development, prototyping and
transition subject to the Secretary of Defense.

That makes Michael a natural bridge to frontier-model companies and national-
security technology. His authority remains Defense-bounded. Participation does
not authorize him to direct civilian regulators, agency records or private-
sector deployment outside an applicable contract, statute or later instrument.

### Scott Kupor — Federal workforce and organizational machinery

OPM is the Federal Government's central civilian personnel-policy and human-
capital manager. Kupor can bring hiring, skills, classification, workforce
planning and government-wide HR machinery to the assignment.

That is a material lane because Federal AI capacity depends on people, skills,
clearances, leadership roles and reorganized work. OPM participation does not
grant control over critical-infrastructure operators or their technical systems;
any such engagement remains consultation until another authority or agreement
acts.

## The Force is an overlay on an existing AI constitution

The Federal Government already has multiple AI coordination and control layers:

| Existing layer | Current function | SIF relationship at cutoff |
|---|---|---|
| White House AI Action Plan | National policy blueprint across innovation, infrastructure and security | SIF may reconcile risks and opportunities, but no amendment or replacement is published. |
| OMB M-25-21 and M-25-22 | Agency CAIOs, governance boards, inventories, high-impact controls and acquisition duties | Still operative. SIF does not replace CAIO accountability or procurement controls. |
| Chief AI Officer Council | Cross-agency implementation, templates and shared practice | No public reporting or membership join to SIF was located. |
| OSTP/APST | Science-and-technology policy; EO 14434 legislative-definition proposal due in 60 days | No public allocation explains whether SIF will review, absorb or separately advise on that proposal. |
| NIST | Measurement, risk-management, interoperability, testing and evaluation language | No announced core seat despite the Force's risk-and-opportunity assignment. Consultation may occur later but is not yet established. |
| Sector regulators and departments | Apply existing mission statutes, enforcement powers and program authorities | Their authorities remain separate; the four named leaders cannot collectively manufacture a new statute. |
| Congress | Legislation, appropriations and oversight | No SIF statute, appropriation or committee reporting requirement was located. |

This creates a coordination question, not yet a conflict: SIF appears to sit
above or beside the existing machinery as a short-deadline presidential review.
The missing charter matters because it should show whether the body is advisory,
whether members vote, which office supplies staff, which records are created,
what information it can request and how it relates to OMB, OSTP, NIST and the
National Security Council.

## The White House Accord is a promise layer, not a regulator

The announcement says the Force follows the September 29 **White House Accord
on Super Intelligence**. The one-page accord asks frontier-model companies to
use four layers:

1. internal controls to monitor model capabilities, alignment and cyber,
   biological, chemical and unintended-system-access risks;
2. an empowered internal team to test those controls and remediate problems;
3. an independent external auditor or evaluator; and
4. an independent board committee receiving reports and overseeing remediation.

It also says participating companies will meet regularly to develop standards
and best practices, and that codification in law or regulation may make sense
later.

At cutoff, the accord does **not** name a Federal recipient for audit results,
define auditor independence, prescribe a test standard or threshold, require
public reports, establish incident-notification clocks, identify enforcement or
penalties, create a remedy for affected people, or transfer a regulator's
authority. It is a voluntary corporate-governance commitment. SIF may become
the government-side receiver or translator, but that role is not yet established
in a public instrument.

## What this builds on and changes in the Library

This development builds directly on the September 29 SI nomenclature order,
the voluntary industry accord and the existing national AI architecture in the
Federal Agency AI Plans and Infrastructure package.

It **confirms** the package's central finding that the administration is adding
high-level routing and coordination layers rather than merging legal authority.
It **changes** the story by adding a named presidential coordinator, a four-
office leadership group and a fixed report window above the agency CAIO,
procurement, standards and sector-enforcement layers.

It **partly answers** the open question created by the September 19 “AI Force”
announcement: who will lead it, which constituencies it will engage and where
it reports. It does **not** answer the harder questions: the charter, legal
basis, executive secretariat, staff, money, records custody, information-
request authority, relationship to OMB/OSTP/NIST, public-hearing process,
decision rule or implementation power.
