Question: Are the G20 monetary systems doing the jobs assigned by the IMF Articles of Agreement, and how do tariffs, trade flows, invoicing, reserves, exchange policy and IMF transactions change that answer?

Short answer

Currency Watch examines the monetary system behind each unit. The Articles bind IMF members and their policies, not a banknote in isolation. Treaty status and the study's performance judgments are different objects: a weak performance grade is not itself a formal IMF finding. The research keeps three outputs separate:

  1. a categorical legal-status record for IMF obligations and any formal Fund finding, approval or exception;
  2. a regime-adjusted performance report card for price stability, exchange order, external adjustment, current-payment usability and resilience; and
  3. a trade-transmission record showing whether tariffs, invoicing, intervention, controls and financing actually move the balance of payments.

This is a cross-cut over the existing TOKEN / STATE-LEDGER / METAL / WALL / PLUMBING taxonomy. It is not a sixth pole. It measures the condition and use of the unit moving through all five.

Strongest early finding

The tariff lane is not merely adjacent to this watch. The July 20 snapshot examined the U.S. surcharge imposed under 19 U.S.C. § 2132, “Balance-of-payments authority.” That statute permits a temporary surcharge or quota to address a large and serious U.S. balance-of-payments deficit, prevent imminent significant dollar depreciation, or cooperate in correcting international disequilibrium. The February 20, 2026 proclamation expressly used the deficit branch for covered entries, subject to listed exemptions and Section 232 non-stacking, and set a conditional July 24, 2026 end clock for goods entered for consumption or withdrawn from warehouse.

The court clock is separate. On May 7, the Court of International Trade held the proclamation unauthorized and granted plaintiff-specific relief. The Federal Circuit stayed that relief pending appeal on June 11, so collection continued in the saved pre-expiry record while the appeal remained unresolved. Neither court event silently changed the scheduled July 24 clock. These are dated source states, not a statement that collection continued after that date. The trade and tariff specialist and later September tariff companions hold the separate mechanics and later research routes.

But legal purpose is not demonstrated effect. The IMF's 2026 U.S. Article IV assessment of the broader U.S. tariff regime—not an isolated causal assessment of the Section 122 surcharge—said tariffs would have a relatively modest effect on the trade deficit while raising goods prices, reducing activity and causing spillovers. Assessing the claimed adjustment effect requires later trade, price, saving-investment, FX and current-account evidence. Legal purpose alone cannot supply that causal result or change a sealed grade.

Scope

Included

  • the 19 country members of the G20;
  • the 17 unique currencies those countries use;
  • the euro as one currency object plus separate France, Germany and Italy policy records and a euro-area shared-policy overlay;
  • EU and African Union institutional context without inventing a separate AU currency;
  • Articles I, IV, V, VI, VIII, XIV, XIX and XXX; the Integrated Surveillance Decision and relevant Selected Decisions;
  • Article IV consultations, External Sector Reports, AREAER, WEO, BOP/IIP, reserves, exchange arrangements and official central-bank records;
  • tariffs, quotas, export controls, invoice/payment/funding currency, customs incidence, export-proceeds rules, intervention, capital controls, swap lines, IMF purchases/repurchases and SDR exchanges when they affect the watch.

Analytical boundaries

  • a prediction that one currency will replace another;
  • a single blended league-table score;
  • treating every intervention as manipulation;
  • treating a tariff announcement as a current-account improvement;
  • treating local-currency payment as de-dollarization of funding, hedging, collateral, insurance or reserves;
  • treating IMF purchases, SDR trades, central-bank swap lines and commercial FX swaps as one instrument.

Current research verdict

The useful question is not “which currency went up?” It is:

Did this monetary system preserve domestic and external order, permit adjustment and current payments, and carry its actual international load without shifting destructive costs into another lane?

That question lets tariffs, trade deals, sanctions, local-currency corridors, reserve shifts, IMF financing and private payment rails enter one watch without becoming one object.
