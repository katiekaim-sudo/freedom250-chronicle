# OPM Structural Change — Coverage and Gap Audit

## Bottom line

Katie's hunch was right, with one important qualification: we did not lack the
OPM story entirely. We had many of its legal and executive-control pieces, but
they were distributed across unrelated packages and were not discoverable as
one OPM process map. The largest substantive omissions were the operating
systems and implementation chains beneath those legal changes.

The change is bigger than workforce reduction. Across the audit window OPM was
being repositioned as:

1. a central hiring, ranking, appraisal, suitability and appeals governor;
2. a governmentwide HR platform and shared-service operator;
3. a digital record, retirement and workforce-data utility; and
4. an eligibility and improper-payment control node joined to Treasury, SSA,
   CMS, carriers and the Postal Service.

That architecture is material to both **Politics / administrative-state
control** and **Government Fraud / how the government is cut, measured and
watched**. It does not prove that every ordered migration, staffing plan,
appeal, match, firing, savings claim or performance distribution occurred.

## Highest-priority missing or underdeveloped structures

### 1. Retirement changed from paper routing to a shared digital operating record

OPM's May 7, 2025 directive moved new retirement intake toward the Online
Retirement Application. NFC- and IBC-serviced agencies became mandatory June 2,
2025, and OPM intended to stop accepting paper in July. The stronger operating
clock is June 30, 2026: OPM reported that more than 95 percent of applications
were electronic and that the system had processed more than 155,000
applications over the prior year.

This is a material process change, not merely a website launch: agency,
payroll-provider and OPM case records now share an electronic path. The issuer's
speed and quality claims still require separate evidence.

- Primary: [May 7, 2025 OPM directive](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/transmittals/2025/SIGNED%20OPM%20Memo%20-%20Paperless%205-7-2025%20FINAL.pdf)
- Operating state: [July 1, 2026 Last Day of Paper release](https://www.opm.gov/news/news-releases/opm-marks-last-day-of-paper-ending-paper-retirement-processing-after-more-than-65-years/)
- Disposition: `promotion_review`; upgrade the existing thin July 1 event rather
  than create a competing event.

### 2. Federal HR 2.0 is a central system-of-record program, not only an Oracle award

The December 10, 2025 OPM–OMB directive told agencies to stop duplicative Core
HCM projects unless OPM/OMB granted an exception and set a phased path toward a
single commercial governmentwide system of record. The June 10, 2026 Oracle
award was already captured. The missing middle is OPM's March 17 HR Shared
Service Center: a voluntary fee-for-service operation offering benefits,
classification, compensation, labor relations, payroll, vetting, performance,
recruitment, retirement and workforce-reshaping services, with a named cohort
expected to begin transitions in FY2027.

- Primary: [Federal HR 2.0 directive](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/creating-federal-hr-20-by-consolidating-core-human-capital-management-across-the-federal-government.pdf)
- Primary: [OPM HR Shared Service Center](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/modernizing-hr-services-by-establishing-the-opm-hr-shared-service-center.pdf)
- Existing award: [Oracle Core HCM announcement](https://www.opm.gov/news/news-releases/opm-awards-contract-for-first-ever-governmentwide-hr-platform/)
- State limit: policy, procurement and service-center creation are proved;
  production migration, signed IAAs, decommissioning, cost savings and service
  outcomes are not.
- Disposition: `promotion_review` as one joined modernization/control object.

### 3. OPM became a larger digital personnel-record and data utility

OPM reported that a cloud eOPF platform replaced its legacy service in May–June
2025 after migration of more than 400 million files and five million accounts.
On January 8, 2026, Federal Workforce Data replaced FedScope with monthly public
data, downloads and new categories. A direct Login.gov document-request route
for separated employees followed in August 2026; that last page had already
been screened as a current-state object.

- Primary: [eOPF launch](https://www.opm.gov/news/opm-launches-new-electronic-official-personnel-folder-platform.pdf)
- Primary: [Federal Workforce Data launch](https://www.opm.gov/news/news-releases/opm-launches-new-workforce-data-site/)
- Operational surface: [Federal Workforce Data](https://data.opm.gov/)
- Disposition: `promotion_review` for the joined infrastructure spine. Preserve
  the issuer-report boundary on migration completeness, data quality and use.

### 4. AI is now explicitly encouraged across the hiring lifecycle

The August 27, 2026 memorandum advises agencies to use AI in hiring while
applying OMB M-25-21's high-impact analysis. It covers job-document drafting,
résumé screening and summarization, eligibility, assessment, ranking and
selection use cases. It also preserves validation, veterans' preference,
privacy, accessibility, auditability, reconsideration and human-review duties.

OPM says AI features will be added across USA Staffing, USAJOBS, USA Hire, USA
Class and USA Performance. That is a forward product direction, not proof that
any feature was deployed or valid in a specific selection.

- Primary: [Use of Artificial Intelligence in the Federal Hiring Process](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/use-of-artificial-intelligence-in-the-federal-hiring-process.pdf)
- Disposition: high-priority `promotion_review`.

### 5. Hiring discretion and staffing control were rebuilt in several layers

The May 2025 Merit Hiring Plan supplied skills-based assessments, shared
certificates, leadership involvement, a two-page résumé gate and reporting.
The Rule of Many final rule then made numerical ranking available: agencies may
predefine a sufficient group and selecting officials may choose anyone on the
resulting certificate, while category rating remains available and veterans'
preference remains applicable. The rule became effective November 7, 2025,
with full compliance due March 9, 2026.

EO 14356 and its existing Workbench owner added Strategic Hiring Committees and
Annual Staffing Plans. OPM's July 30, 2026 implementation memo makes September
30 the FY2027 plan deadline, but the related 5 CFR 250 proposal was not final at
cutoff. Separately, probation changed toward affirmative public-interest
certification instead of passive completion; no individual separation follows
from the rule alone.

- Primary: [Rule of Many final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/08/2025-17125/reinvigorating-merit-based-hiring-through-candidate-ranking-in-the-competitive-and-excepted-service)
- Primary: [Annual Staffing Plans guidance](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/guidance-on-annual-staffing-plans.pdf)
- Primary: [Probationary-period final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/06/24/2025-11576/strengthening-probationary-periods-in-the-federal-service)
- Disposition: Rule of Many `promotion_review`; probation and Annual Staffing
  Plans `historical_repair` into their existing executive-order chains.

### 6. Performance management acquired new distribution, review and certification rules

The September 2025 SES rule removed the regulatory prohibition on prescribed
rating distributions and authorized OPM to impose limits. The July 7, 2026
general-workforce rule removed Level 2, authorized standardized distributions,
ended universal mandatory higher-level review of Level 1, changed the grievance
boundary, required a supervisory critical element and added biennial OPM system
certification. It became effective August 6; the standardized fiscal-year cycle
starts October 1, 2026, and specified compliance is due January 1, 2027.

These rules establish authority and minimum architecture. They do not establish
an actual rating quota, distribution, demotion, removal or agency certification.

- Primary: [SES final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/15/2025-17788/assuring-responsive-and-accountable-federal-executive-management)
- Primary: [General-workforce final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/07/2026-13715/performance-appraisal-for-general-schedule-prevailing-rate-and-certain-other-employees)
- Disposition: `promotion_review` as a joined multi-clock performance object;
  fold the July-sweep mention rather than duplicate it.

### 7. Retirement payment integrity now has an explicit weakness-to-match chain

OPM OIG's June 23, 2026 evaluation found largely manual projects and unresolved
centenarian cases, and left 12 recommendations open at issuance. Its August 5
PIIA audit found OPM met statutory reporting criteria but could not validate
parts of the retirement-control data and identified Do Not Pay scorecard errors.

On August 26 OPM published a new Retirement Services–Treasury Do Not Pay
matching program. The legal start is September 25, 2026, not the notice date;
the agreement is scheduled through September 10, 2029 unless ended or
superseded. Results may not be the sole basis for an adverse action without
independent verification and applicable process.

- Primary: [OPM OIG retirement improper-payment evaluation](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2026-06/2025-OEI-001.pdf)
- Primary: [OPM OIG FY2025 PIIA audit](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2026-08/2025-IAG-022.pdf)
- Primary: [OPM–Treasury Do Not Pay matching notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/26/2026-17369/privacy-act-of-1974-new-matching-program)
- Disposition: OIG/control chain `promotion_review`; September 25 first-operation
  state remains `watch_only`. No match, adverse decision or recovery existed by
  the audit cutoff.

### 8. PSHB is a real new operating benefits system with identified control risks

The Postal Service Health Benefits Program began operation January 1, 2025,
moving postal employees and annuitants from the ordinary FEHB structure into a
separate OPM-administered program. OPM OIG's July and November 2025 reports
identified continuity/funding risk around the Health Benefits Data Platform and
an open eligibility-documentation issue while also recording corrected controls.

- Operating surface: [OPM PSHB](https://www.opm.gov/healthcare-insurance/pshb/)
- Primary oversight: [Critical PSHB Resource Issues](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2025-07/2025-PSHB-091%20-%20Final%20Flash%20Report%20-%20Critical%20PSHBP%20Resource%20Issues_Redacted.pdf)
- Primary oversight: [PSHB Capstone Report](https://www.oversight.gov/sites/default/files/documents/reports/2025-11/PSHB-090.pdf)
- Disposition: `promotion_review` as one operating system plus OIG descendants.
  The reports do not prove a system failure or universal enrollment error.

### 9. A court imposed a material boundary on OPM-directed probationary separations

In *AFGE v. OPM*, the Northern District of California held on September 12,
2025 that OPM lacked authority to order other agencies' probationary
separations through the challenged January/February directives and entered a
permanent injunction plus record-correction requirements. The order did not
bar agencies from taking their own lawful personnel actions and did not order
final reinstatement.

The Ninth Circuit appeal, No. 25-5875, was argued August 28, 2026. No appellate
decision was located by cutoff. The April 2025 Supreme Court emergency stay had
addressed an earlier preliminary injunction and standing posture, not the later
merits order.

- Primary: [authenticated district-court order](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-cand-3_25-cv-01780/pdf/USCOURTS-cand-3_25-cv-01780-8.pdf)
- Appeal clock: [Ninth Circuit oral-argument audio](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/media/2026/08/28/25-5875.mp3)
- Disposition: `promotion_review` with an active appellate watch; court objects,
  not OPM releases, control this boundary.
