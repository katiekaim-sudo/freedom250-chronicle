Annex deep read — money chairs and the gold suite

The parent order is the power. These two annexes are the first object sets that change what we can honestly say about the monetary control plane.

A listing is not a firing. An HTS exception is not a purchase.

1. EO 14410 — the money-agency chairs

Treasury is listed as 223 chairs. That count includes OCC (95). The rest of Treasury is 128. Independent money regulators sit beside it.

OCC is the deepest monetary cluster

Titles that matter for the money story, not the comms/HR ones:

  • Director for Payment Systems Policy (NB2349)
  • Director, Financial Technology (NB3869)
  • Assistant Deputy Comptroller for National Trust Banks (NB2203)
  • Director for Licensing (three chairs: NB1750, NB3193, NB3192) plus Director for Large Bank Licensing and a Licensing Manager
  • Deputy Comptroller for Systemic Risk Identification and Support (NB3387)
  • Director for Treasury and Market Risk (NB1887)
  • Director for Capital Policy (NB1315)
  • Director for International Banking / International Banking Supervision
  • Senior Deputy Comptrollers for Large Bank Supervision and for Supervision Risk and Analysis
  • Six Supervisory National Bank Examiners

That is the national-bank, trust-bank, payments, and licensing surface. A listing is still not a removal and not a charter change at OCC.

What the thin seats say

Fiscal Service = 1. The federal payment and public-debt rail is almost untouched by this census. 14410 does not take the disbursement gate.

Mint / BEP = comms. No coin-design, no note-production, no bullion-program chairs.

SEC = 4 versus CFTC = 38. Listing count is not comparative statutory power. It is where this administration marked policy chairs.

Fed and CFPB = 0. Not proof they sit outside other presidential, statutory, or supervisory controls.

TFI licensing chairs sit on the OFAC license desk without naming OFAC as a listing unit. The appendix uses TFI as the parent unit.

PTAAP does not hold the bullion

The September and November potential-adjustment menus keep platinum-group metals, gold compounds (pharma class), and coins. They do not carry 7108.12.10 bullion or 7108.20.00 monetary gold. Those sit on the live exception book, not the negotiation menu.

2a. Heading 7108 is a fork, not a pile of gold

HTSUS heading 7108 is Gold (including gold plated with platinum), unwrought or in semimanufactured forms, or in powder form. The heading splits on the first digit after the decimal:

  • 7108.1x — nonmonetary
  • 7108.20 — monetary

Silver heading 7106 has no monetary twin. Only gold has this official/commercial split in the Harmonized System. That is why 7108.20.00 appearing on a live exception book is a different kind of fact than 7106.91.10 (silver bullion) staying on the April list.

Jewelry (heading 7113) never enters any of these books. Finished gold jewelry kept the reciprocal add-on. The suite is metal-in-form, not gold-as-ornament.

Two official languages for “monetary”

The tariff book prints Gold, monetary and stops. That silence matters.

IMF / BPM6 / UN trade statistics. Monetary gold is gold to which monetary authorities (or others under their effective control) have title and which is held as a reserve asset. Transactions in monetary gold occur between monetary authorities, or between a monetary authority and an international monetary organization. Everything else — including investment bars held by private parties, and commercial stocks held by an authority that also owns reserve gold — is nonmonetary. Under that language, 7108.20.00 is the reserve-asset line.

CBP NY N300205 (21 September 2018). CBP ruled that “monetary” is not defined in the HTSUS text or the Explanatory Notes to heading 7108, so the word takes its common meaning: “of or relating to coinage or currency.” On that reading, 24k gold coin blanks made to U.S. Mint specifications for legal-tender coins classified in 7108.20.0000, duty-free. That is a minting-blank reading, not a reserve-asset reading.

Both languages are official. Neither is a Treasury purchase, a stockpile credit, or a remonetization statute. Classification is what CBP does at the border. Title as a reserve asset is what a monetary authority’s books do. Those are different clocks.

Why the April hole was a real hole

If only 7108.12.10 is excepted, a 99.5% rectangular investment bar entered as 7108.13.55 takes the add-on. Same metal. Different 8-digit. The April book protected mine/refinery bullion and dore and left the finished commercial bar, the leaf, the powder, the other semis, the official line, and the 7115 article forms on the add-on.

September 5 inserts those remaining 7108 lines plus 7115.90.05 / .30 into U.S. note 2(v)(iii) — the door behind heading 9903.01.32. Effective 12:01 a.m. EDT 8 September 2025 (three days after the order). After that date, every 8-digit line that exists under heading 7108 is on the live exception book.

That is a customs-completeness move as much as a monetary signal. Gold leaf and powder on the same insert weaken a “just remonetization” read. The interesting pair is still 7108.13.55 + 7108.20.00: commercial investment form and official line, added together, kept off PTAAP.

The books next to it

  • Brazil (14323 / 14361). Only 7108.12.10. The April bullion-and-dore line, not the September suite. A different instrument (the 40% Brazil duty), not an amendment of 14257 Annex II.
  • PTAAP (14346 Annex III; 14360 update). Keeps PGM family, gold compounds, and coins. Does not carry 7108 bullion or 7108.20.00. The metal itself is a standing live exception, not a partner-deal chip. Coins and compounds sit on both books — already excepted, and still available as final-agreement language.
  • Canada / Mexico. Not on 14257 Annex I. Gold of those origins rides the separate IEEPA border papers (14193 / 14194 as amended), not this exception book.
  • 14389 (20 February 2026). Ended listed IEEPA ad valorem collection, including 14257 as amended. The emergencies remain. The exception-book history remains as the record of what this administration kept off the add-on while the add-on was live.

What the exception actually does

14257 §3(b) + Annex III heading 9903.01.32 + note 2(v)(iii): goods properly classified in a listed 8-digit subheading do not take the reciprocal IEEPA add-on. Descriptions in Annex II are informational. Classification governs.

It does not zero the underlying Chapter 71 rate (most 7108 lines are already Free at the general rate). It does not buy the metal. It does not move title onto Treasury, the ESF, the Mint, or the Fed. It does not write a reserve-asset election. CBP classifies; Commerce / USTR / DHS hold the modification authority in the parent orders.

What would actually prove a reserve or purchase story

Not on this annex. That proof would be a different primary source: a Treasury / ESF / Fed reserve-asset statement, a Mint purchase or blank-import record tied to 7108.20.00, a Census quantity under 7108.20.00 that is not coin blanks, or a CBP / USITC notice after 5 September 2025 treating 7108.20.00 as live. Those are still open. The annex only proves the classification door was opened.

Hard boundaries

  • 14410 proves placement of a position description. It does not name an incumbent, serve 7-day notice, or transfer a function.
  • 7108.20.00 on the exception book proves a classification treatment for the reciprocal add-on. It does not prove a Treasury purchase, a reserve inflow, a stockpile credit, collateral, or tokenization.
  • Canada / Mexico gold would ride the separate IEEPA border books, not 14257 Annex I.
  • 14389 later ended listed IEEPA ad valorem collection. The exception-book history remains. The emergencies remain.
