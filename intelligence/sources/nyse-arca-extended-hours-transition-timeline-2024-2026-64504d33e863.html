NYSE Arca Extended-Hours Transition — Factual Timeline

Research owner: fed-clearing-treasury Evidence cutoff: September 25, 2026 ET Object: the move from an August 2024 overnight-market failure signal to approved-but-not-yet-operating NYSE Arca 23/5 trading Boundary: this is a factual infrastructure chronology. It supplies no astrology, convergence, causal, or Forecast Ledger credit.

Short ruling

NYSE Arca's extended-hours transition is not one event and was not operating at the February 1, 2026 factual checkpoint.

The sequence is:

market shock and private-venue uncertainty → exchange proposal → SEC permission → clearing and market-data dependency work → revised 23/5 rule architecture → final readiness filing → operation

The reviewed record establishes proposal and permission, then exposes the shared infrastructure required for operation. It does not permit “SEC approval” to be narrated as a live 23/5 market.

The February 2025 permission contained its own operating gate

The SEC approval authorized the temporary rule architecture but preserved a separate commencement condition. Before NYSE Arca could use the longer hours, the Equity Data Plans had to establish an equivalent mechanism for collecting, consolidating, processing and disseminating quotes and trades, notify the exchange that they were ready, and be followed by another exchange filing confirming readiness.

That creates four different states:

  1. exchange design — the operator specifies proposed hours and controls;
  2. regulatory permission — the SEC approves the rule architecture;
  3. shared-infrastructure readiness — data, clearing, reporting, halt and corporate-action dependencies can support the longer day; and
  4. operation — the exchange actually activates the new sessions.

The February 1, 2026 checkpoint sits between states 2 and 3, not at state 4.

The May 2025 data announcement identifies the real counterparty system

The May 6 SIP announcement is more than a technical footnote. It shows why no single exchange can create a national 23/5 equities market by extending its own matching engine.

The operating stack includes:

exchange session → protected quotes and trades → consolidated processors → off-exchange reporting → central counterparty clearing → listing-market symbols and corporate actions → participant books and controls

The SIP operating committees named DTCC clearing, continuous quote/trade dissemination, off-exchange reporting and listing-market support as preconditions. The May 2026 NYSE Arca filing added order eligibility, erroneous trade review, risk controls, regulatory halts and circuit-breaker work.

Thus “market access” is a multi-party operating constitution, not merely a longer clock on an exchange website.
