# Reading the Charter — Fannie Mae

Fannie Mae started life as a government pawn and got sold off to Wall Street without ever losing the government's smell. It is not one thing. It is three things wearing one name, in sequence: a New Deal relief agency (1938), a private company with a government leash (1968-2008), and now a ward of the state again in all but paperwork (2008-present, via conservatorship). Every clause below has to be read against that shape-shifting.

## 1. Immunities & liability shields

- **Sue-and-be-sued, but no seizure.** 12 U.S.C. §1723a(a): the corporation can "sue and to be sued, and to complain and to defend, in any court of competent jurisdiction, State or Federal, but no attachment, injunction, or other similar process, mesne or final, shall be issued against the property of the Association or against the Association with respect to its property." Plain read: you can win a judgment against Fannie Mae. You cannot touch its property to collect. That is a liability shield with teeth — courts can rule against it, but cannot grab its assets while doing so.
- **No fee/toll on federal touch.** §1719(f): "no fee or charge may be assessed or collected by the United States... on or with regard to the purchase, acquisition, sale, pledge, issuance, guarantee, or redemption of any mortgage, asset, obligation... by the corporation." The federal government cannot toll Fannie Mae's core business, even though it created it.
- **Name-theft is enjoinable with automatic punitive damages.** §1723a(e): anyone else using "Federal National Mortgage Association" or "Government National Mortgage Association" in a business name can be enjoined and owes actual damages plus punitive damages "of not exceeding $100 for each day" of violation — no need to prove actual harm to get the punitive money.

## 2. Tax exemptions

- **The corporation (Fannie Mae) is exempt from state and local tax, NOT federal tax.** §1723a(c)(2): "The corporation, including its franchise, capital, reserves, surplus, mortgages or other security holdings, and income, shall be exempt from all taxation now or hereafter imposed by any State, territory, possession, Commonwealth, or dependency of the United States, or by the District of Columbia, or by any county, municipality, or local taxing authority" — with real property carved back in as taxable.
- **The Association (Ginnie Mae) gets the bigger exemption — federal AND state/local.** §1723a(c)(1): "The Association... shall be exempt from all taxation now or hereafter imposed by the United States, by any territory, dependency, or possession thereof, or by any State, county, municipality, or local taxing authority" — again with real property carved out. This asymmetry is a direct fingerprint of the 1968 split: Ginnie Mae stayed a government agency (full tax immunity, agency-grade); Fannie Mae became a shareholder-owned company (federal corporate income tax applies like any other business — the 1968 Act, Pub. L. 90-448 §802(aa), stripped Fannie's federal exemption specifically because it was no longer the government's).
- **Securities carry SEC-exempt status, not tax exemption.** §1719(d) and (e) and §1717(c)(1): mortgage-backed securities, subordinated obligations, and trust participations issued by Fannie Mae are "deemed to be exempt securities within the meaning of laws administered by the Securities and Exchange Commission" — this is a registration/disclosure exemption (skip SEC registration), a different animal from a tax exemption, and it is easy to conflate the two. Do not conflate them.
- **The 1968-era trusts (interagency mortgage trusts under §1717(c)) are flatly tax-exempt:** "The trust or trusts shall be exempt from all taxation." No carve-back language at all in that one sentence.

## 3. Who appoints, who removes, and for what cause — the control map

- **Pre-1954/pre-1968 lineage:** originally an instrumentality of HUD's predecessor (the Federal Housing Administrator, later HUD Secretary), fully inside the executive branch.
- **1968 partition, §1717(a)(2):** the old single FNMA splits into Ginnie Mae (stays inside HUD, no capital stock, pure government corporation) and Fannie Mae (private, shareholder-owned, D.C.-corporation-for-venue-purposes body).
- **Personnel, post-1968 (§1723a(d)):** Ginnie Mae's officers/employees are appointed by the HUD Secretary, subject to civil service law (d)(1). Fannie Mae's officers/employees are appointed by its own **board of directors**, explicitly "without regard to the Federal civil service and classification laws" (d)(2) — a hard line drawn at the 1968 split between "government workforce" and "private workforce," even though both entities share a birth certificate.
- **Executive compensation control, added 1992 and expanded 2008:** the Director of the Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) must pre-approve any golden-parachute-style severance deal for a Fannie Mae "executive officer" (§1723a(d)(3)(B)), and can outright block payment/disbursement to an executive officer under investigation (§1723a(d)(4)). §1719(g)(5) also gives "the Director" power to "approve, disapprove, or modify the executive compensation of the corporation" outright — this is a post-2008 conservatorship-era power, folded into the emergency-Treasury-purchase subsection.
- **The unstated fact that matters most:** none of this charter text names a "conservator" or describes removal-for-cause of the corporation's own board by a regulator in the way the FHFA's own statute (12 U.S.C. §4617, under the Housing and Economic Recovery Act of 2008 / HERA) does. That power lives in FHFA's charter, not this one — see item 9 below and the note under "current status."

## 4. Money: self-funding vs. appropriations — off-budget status

- **"Fully self-supporting" is baked into the statute twice.** §1719(a)(1) and (a)(2) both require that Fannie Mae's secondary-market pricing, lending ratios, and fees "should be... consistent with the objectives that... the operations of the corporation under this section should be within its income derived from such operations and that such operations should be fully self-supporting." This is the textual basis for calling it "off-budget" — it is designed to run on its own fee and spread income, not appropriations, in ordinary times.
- **But Treasury can backstop it, with a dollar cap that has never been updated for the 2008 crisis authority.** §1719(c): Treasury may buy Fannie Mae's obligations, capped at "an amount greater than $2,250,000,000" outstanding at any one time — a 1957-era number that was tiny by 2008 standards, which is exactly why Congress had to bolt on subsection (g).
- **§1719(g) — the 2008 escape hatch.** "In addition to the authority under subsection (c)," Treasury got open-ended purchase authority ("in such amounts as the Secretary may determine") conditioned on an emergency finding that action is "necessary to... provide stability to the financial markets... prevent disruptions in the availability of mortgage finance... and protect the taxpayer" (§1719(g)(1)(A)-(B)). This is the statutory hook actually used in September 2008 for the Treasury's Senior Preferred Stock Purchase Agreements that put Fannie Mae into conservatorship. **The open-market/general purchase piece of this authority sunset December 31, 2009 (§1719(g)(4))** — but the "rights exercised" and "sale of what Treasury already bought" pieces (§1719(g)(2)-(3)) do NOT sunset. Translation: the 2008-era preferred stock and warrants Treasury already holds are still fully exercisable and saleable by Treasury today, permanently, even though the emergency-purchase button itself is disabled.
- **No fee/charge from the federal government on Fannie's core transactions** (§1719(f), see above) — reinforces the self-funding design; the government isn't supposed to be taking a cut, just backstopping.

## 5. Audit & disclosure carve-outs

- **GAO audit — but Fannie Mae pays for its own audit.** §1723a(j)(1): "The corporation shall reimburse the Government Accountability Office for the full cost of any such audit as billed therefor by the Comptroller General." GAO can look at the books, but the entity being audited foots the bill — an odd little dependency in an otherwise-independent-sounding audit power.
- **Mandatory independent private audit too** (§1723a(l)) — standard generally-accepted-accounting-principles audit, separate from the GAO audit.
- **Public disclosure with a built-in escape valve.** §1723a(n)(3)(B): before Fannie Mae's housing-activity report goes public, the corporation "may exclude from the report information that the Director of the Federal Housing Finance Agency has determined is proprietary information" under the 1992 Safety and Soundness Act. This is the FOIA-adjacent carve-out — not a formal FOIA exemption (Fannie Mae, as a private shareholder corporation, isn't a FOIA-covered agency to begin with), but a statutory permission to withhold "proprietary" data from otherwise-mandatory public reports.

## 6. Amendment & withdrawal clauses

- **Perpetual existence, terminable only by Congress, stated twice — once for each twin.** §1717(a)(1): the original body "shall have succession until dissolved by Act of Congress." After the 1968 split, both the Association (Ginnie Mae, §1717(a)(2)(A)) and the corporation (Fannie Mae, §1717(a)(2)(B)) independently "shall have succession until dissolved by Act of Congress." No sunset clause, no shareholder vote can kill it, no regulator can wind it up on their own signature — only a new Act of Congress ends it.
- **The 1968 partition itself was structured as a tax-free corporate reorganization.** §1717(a)(3): the split "constitutes a reorganization within the meaning of section 368(a)(1)(E) of title 26," meaning no gain or loss was recognized for tax purposes, and stock basis/holding period carried through untouched. Congress engineered the divorce so nobody paid a tax bill on it.

## 7. Emergency powers — what unlocks in "unusual and exigent circumstances" and who decides

- This charter's own emergency lever is §1719(g), described in item 4 above: the Treasury Secretary alone determines the emergency ("the Secretary must determine that such actions are necessary to... provide stability... prevent disruptions... protect the taxpayer," §1719(g)(1)(B)) — no court test, no congressional pre-approval, just a Secretary's determination, reported to Congress only *after* the fact (§1719(g)(1)(D)). This is a strikingly low bar for a nearly-unlimited-dollar-amount purchase power (contrast with the fixed $2.25 billion cap under the ordinary-times §1719(c) authority).
- **The actual conservatorship trigger — "unusual and exigent circumstances" language — lives in the regulator's charter (12 U.S.C. §4617, part of the Federal Housing Finance Agency's authorizing statute under HERA 2008), NOT in this Charter Act.** Flagging this clearly: Fannie Mae's own charter never describes how it gets placed into conservatorship or receivership; that trigger and process belongs to FHFA's statute. This document captures Fannie Mae's charter only, per this assignment's scope — see "current status" note below for why that distinction matters practically.

## 8. The definitions section — scope-expanding definitions

- **There is no single stand-alone "definitions" section for this subchapter.** Definitions are scattered and embedded:
  - **"Mortgages" and "home mortgages"** — defined inline, expansively, in §1717(b)(1): inclusive of "any mortgages or other loans insured under any of the provisions of this chapter or title V of the Housing Act of 1949" — this quietly folds Public Health Service Act medical-facility loans and Farmers Home Administration farm/housing loans into the same statutory basket as ordinary home mortgages (see §1717(c)(2), which authorizes trusts for Agriculture Department farm loans, Education Department student-loan financing, HUD, VA, Export-Import Bank, and Small Business Administration loans — none of which sound like "mortgages" to a plain reader, all of which the statute treats as within scope for trust/financing purposes).
  - **"Conventional mortgages"** — §1717(b)(2): expanded by statute to include "a mortgage, lien, or other security interest on the stock or membership certificate issued to a tenant-stockholder... of a cooperative housing corporation" and the associated proprietary lease — i.e., co-op shares count as "mortgages" for Fannie Mae's purchase authority, even though a co-op share is technically personal property, not real estate.
  - **"Credit score"** — §1717(b)(7)(A)(i): "a numerical value or a categorization created by a third party... to predict the likelihood of certain credit behaviors, including default" — added 2018, notable mainly for how late Congress got around to defining this at all.
  - **"Executive officer"** — §1723a(d)(3)(C): defined not in this Act but by cross-reference out to the 1992 Federal Housing Enterprises Financial Safety and Soundness Act (12 U.S.C. §4502) — a definition imported from the entity's regulator's statute, not self-contained.

## 9. Succession/perpetuity & dissolution

- Covered in item 6 above: perpetual, Congress-only dissolution, for both twins independently. Note also the 1968 transitional provisions (uncodified note under §1716b): existing FNMA stock automatically converted share-for-share into new Fannie Mae voting common stock with no tax event, and existing employees were sorted between the two new bodies by operation of law, with HUD's Secretary obligated to "make every reasonable effort to place in other comparable Federal positions" any Ginnie Mae career employee who ended up on neither payroll. Congress didn't just authorize a corporate split — it wrote a full employee-transition and stock-conversion mechanism directly into the statute.

## 10. Anything strange — the clause you'd never expect

- **The corporation cannot be sued into losing its property, ever, by any court, for any reason — full stop, no carve-out stated.** (§1723a(a), see item 1.) That is an unusually absolute litigation shield for an entity that, since 1968, is supposedly just a private shareholder-owned company competing with banks.
- **Fannie Mae is legally barred from doing two specific things private lenders do routinely: it cannot originate mortgage loans, and it cannot bridge-lend to mortgage sellers using loans as collateral pending a sale** (§1719(a)(2)(A)-(B), added 1989). The statute goes out of its way to keep Fannie Mae one step removed from the borrower — a secondary-market buyer only, never a primary lender. This is precisely the boundary that gets blurred in practice when people call Fannie Mae "the mortgage company," when statutorily it's forbidden from being one.
- **Every Fannie Mae security must carry a disclaimer disowning the government — stated four separate times in nearly identical language** (§1719(b), (d), (e), and by cross-reference elsewhere): "not guaranteed by the United States and do not constitute a debt or obligation of the United States or any agency or instrumentality thereof other than the corporation." Congress made Fannie Mae print its own disclaimer on every bond, over and over, in the statute itself — and then, in 2008, backstopped it anyway via the §1719(g) emergency authority and outright conservatorship. The "not backed by the government" label was legally true and functionally false the moment it mattered.
- **A trustor-guarantee trust structure (§1717(c)) lets six unrelated federal agencies — Agriculture (Farmers Home Administration), Education, HUD, VA, Export-Import Bank, Small Business Administration — route their own loan books through Fannie Mae's structure "notwithstanding any other provision of this chapter or of any other law," with the resulting trust instruments "exempt from all taxation" and treated as federal securities for SEC purposes.** This is a little-known plumbing function: Fannie Mae, or historically Ginnie Mae under this same provision, acts as a universal federal-loan securitization trustee across agencies that have nothing to do with housing.

## Layer map — organic act through today

1. **1934 — National Housing Act (ch. 847, June 27, 1934, 48 Stat. 1246), Title III, §301-302.** Established the framework for a federally chartered "national mortgage association" secondary-market facility — the root authority everything below traces back to.
2. **1938 — Amendment (ch. 13, Feb. 3, 1938, 52 Stat. 8, §§4-6).** This is the amendment under whose authority the Reconstruction Finance Corporation actually chartered "National Mortgage Association of Washington," soon renamed **Federal National Mortgage Association** — i.e., FNMA's real birth, as a federal government agency/instrumentality, not a private company. (Per this session's fetch of Cornell's own Editorial Notes / Source Credit for §1716 and §1717, which record the 1938 amendment by citation and one-line description; clean full verbatim text of the 1938 Act itself was not separately fetched this session — this is noted as a gap, not filled by memory.)
3. **1948 — Act of July 1, 1948 (ch. 784, 62 Stat. 1206).** Per Cornell's amendment note: "amended section generally to create a Federal National Mortgage Association with power to purchase, service, or sell insured or guaranteed mortgage, provide for the powers and succession of the Association, and to eliminate the former national mortgage association." A full re-charter of the RFC-created entity.
4. **1954 — Federal National Mortgage Association Charter Act (Act of Aug. 2, 1954, ch. 649, title II, 68 Stat. 612).** THE reorganization. Rewrote §1716 "substituting entirely new provisions for provisions now covered by section 1717... and other sections." This is what gave the Act now called the "Charter Act" its current shape and section numbering (§§1716-1723c), moving tax-exemption language specifically into what is now §1723a(c) (per the Cornell amendment note under §1719: "amended section generally to substitute new provisions... for provisions which related to exemption from taxation. See section 1723a(c) of this title").
5. **1968 — Housing and Urban Development Act, title VIII (Pub. L. 90-448, Aug. 1, 1968, 82 Stat. 476, §§801-810).** THE partition. Split the single FNMA into two bodies effective September 1, 1968: **Ginnie Mae** (Government National Mortgage Association) — stayed inside HUD, no capital stock, pure government corporation, retained the special-assistance/liquidating functions (old §§1720-1721) — and **Fannie Mae** (Federal National Mortgage Association, retained) — became a shareholder-owned, privately capitalized corporation retaining the secondary-market operations (§1719) and general powers (§1718-1719). Structured as a tax-free reorganization under 26 U.S.C. §368(a)(1)(E). Stock converted 1-for-1; employees sorted by operation of law; HUD Secretary given a statutory duty to try to re-place displaced Ginnie Mae career staff.
6. **1989 — Financial Institutions Reform, Recovery, and Enforcement Act (FIRREA), Pub. L. 101-73.** Barred Fannie Mae from originating loans or interim bridge-lending to sellers (§1719(a)(2)); restructured the purposes section (§1716).
7. **1992 — Federal Housing Enterprises Financial Safety and Soundness Act, Pub. L. 102-550, title XIII.** Created the Office of Federal Housing Enterprise Oversight (OFHEO) as Fannie/Freddie's dedicated safety-and-soundness regulator; added the GAO-audit, independent-audit, financial-reporting, mortgage-data-collection, housing-activity-report, and Affordable Housing Advisory Council provisions now at §1723a(j)-(o); added executive-compensation-approval requirements at §1723a(d)(3).
8. **2008 — Housing and Economic Recovery Act (HERA), Pub. L. 110-289, including the Federal Housing Finance Regulatory Reform Act of 2008.** Abolished OFHEO, created the **Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA)** as successor regulator with expanded powers (this is a DIFFERENT entity's charter — FHFA's own statute, 12 U.S.C. Chapter 46 — not reproduced in this file); added Treasury's temporary emergency-purchase authority at §1719(g). **Days after HERA's enactment, FHFA placed Fannie Mae into conservatorship (September 2008), where it remains as of this writing.** The conservatorship trigger and FHFA's power to act as conservator/receiver live in FHFA's charter (12 U.S.C. §4617), NOT in Fannie Mae's own Charter Act captured in this file — flagging this because it is the single most important current-state fact about the entity and it is easy to miss if you only read the document in this folder.
9. **2010 — Dodd-Frank Act, Pub. L. 111-203.** Minor technical amendment to §1719(g)(2) (added deficit-reduction earmark for proceeds of any Treasury sale of Fannie Mae securities).
10. **2018 — Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act, Pub. L. 115-174.** Added the credit-score-model validation-and-approval regime, §1717(b)(7).

## Entity Theory read

Fannie Mae is a chimera built by Congress to eat two ways at once: private profits when times are fat, public rescue when times are lean. It was born a government office in the New Deal, sold to shareholders in 1968 so its debt could sit off the federal books while still smelling like Treasury paper to every bond buyer on earth, and then swallowed back into government hands in 2008 the instant the bluff got called — through a leash that was never written into its own charter at all, but into a separate regulator's law next door. Who really holds the leash: the Treasury Secretary and the FHFA Director, both political appointees, using powers Congress bolted onto this charter in 2008 almost as an afterthought (§1719(g)), with the actual removal-and-control weapon parked in a different statute entirely so casual readers of Fannie Mae's own charter would never find it. Does it survive a change of control: yes, by design — perpetual, dissolvable only by Congress, its stock and staff engineered in 1968 to convert automatically with zero tax friction, built to outlast any single administration, any single Congress, any single crisis. It cannot be killed by a lawsuit (no attachment or injunction against its property), cannot be killed by a regulator's whim (Congress-only dissolution), and apparently cannot even be killed by insolvency (conservatorship, not liquidation, was the 2008 answer). The disclaimer printed on every bond says the government isn't behind it. The 2008 rescue said otherwise. Both statements are, technically, still true.
