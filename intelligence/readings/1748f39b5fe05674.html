## Method

The package treats “start” as six different clocks:

| Clock | Test |
|---|---|
| **Stress** | Did a market, currency, collateral pool or balance sheet force adaptation? |
| **Law** | Did an enacted or effective rule change what may legally be issued, held or settled? |
| **Pilot** | Did a controlled experiment use real or simulated participants/value? |
| **Production** | Can eligible users move live value under operating service terms beyond a bounded experiment? A closed-account service can be production. |
| **Interoperability** | Does the rail cross systems, currencies or borders? |
| **Control** | Who can admit, freeze, sanction, reverse, collateralize or confer settlement finality? |

Every regional record separates:

- the legal entity from the political brand;
- the tokenized asset from the settlement asset;
- messaging/orchestration from final settlement;
- tokenized central-bank reserves, retail CBDC, tokenized commercial-bank deposits and stablecoins;
- `proposed`, `law passed`, `effective`, `PoC`, `pilot`, `controlled go-live`, `production`, `scheduled` and `unverified`.

## Scope boundary

This is a factual-first research layer for later synthesis. It does not predict a single replacement currency, treat BRICS as a supranational issuer, or infer production from a proof of concept. Market-stress interpretation is kept separate from the legal and operational record.

## The question

Who can change the IMF, by which legal route, with what threshold and effect;
what can the Fund actually change in a member country; and which parts of that
answer were already present—but scattered—across the research library?

## Short answer

The IMF is a treaty-based international organization whose powers are split
among its members, Board of Governors, Executive Board, Managing Director and
staff. It can interpret and administer its charter, make policy within that
charter, allocate resources, condition access to financing, conduct
surveillance, approve specified exchange restrictions, operate the SDR system,
and provide requested legal and technical assistance. It cannot enact a
member's budget, rewrite domestic statutes, compel creditors to exchange bonds,
set most daily exchange rates, issue a national currency, or silently enlarge
its own treaty mandate.

The strongest practical leverage often comes from a financing gate: the member
chooses whether to request and implement a program, while the Executive Board
decides whether Fund resources are made available and whether reviews or
waivers are approved. The deepest formal change requires collective member
action: an amendment normally needs acceptance by three-fifths of the members
holding 85 percent of total voting power.
