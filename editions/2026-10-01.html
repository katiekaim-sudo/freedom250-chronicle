SEC proposes crypto custody rules for advisers and funds

TODAY 🚨: The Commission proposed new rules and amendments to provide a tailored framework for the custody of crypto assets for registered investment advisers and regulated funds, i.e. registered investment companies and business development companies.

@SECPaulSAtkins

Atkins proposes crypto custody framework for advisers

Since the advent of Bitcoin in 2008, the crypto asset market has grown from a niche curiosity into a multi-trillion-dollar asset class to which investors actively seek exposure. Unfortunately, our rules and regulations have not kept pace.

To that end, today’s proposal would provide a clear regulatory framework for the custody of crypto assets, giving investment advisers and funds a compliant pathway where none existed before—and replacing the grey of uncertainty created by custody rules crafted for a bygone era.

@EleanorTerrett

SEC self-custody proposal adds bankruptcy protections

Something else that stands out in the self-custody section is the proposal’s apparent focus on bankruptcy protections.

It would require RIAs to have a written agreement with clients stating that crypto assets held in the adviser’s self-custody will be treated as financial assets under Article 8 of the Uniform Commercial Code. That may be intended to reduce the risk that clients lose their crypto if an adviser becomes insolvent — a concern the Clarity Act also tried to address.
