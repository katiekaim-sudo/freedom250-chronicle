Governing question

Did the Korean equity crash produce a verifiable chain from asset-price losses through leverage, funding, collateral, clearing/finality or alternative digital rails?

Research finding

The saved evidence establishes a severe Korean equity selloff and activated exchange controls. It does not establish a completed chain into systemic funding failure, failed settlement or migration into DeFi.

The follow-on sharpened two boundaries that the broad pass had already left unproved:

  1. KOFIA's July 28 credit-financing balances rose while realized forced sales fell. That supplies counterevidence to a same-day broker-credit liquidation surge; it does not cover every leverage channel. The exact July 29 series was unavailable at the parent cutoff and remained unrecovered in the saved August 27 check.
  2. July 28–29 cash-equity trades were due to settle July 30–31. Completion was not yet due at the parent cutoff. The August 27 check still did not recover the joined completion/fail record, so settlement remains unadjudicated.

The working causal interpretation, with the measured and unmeasured links separated, is:

regional AI/memory-semiconductor repricing → unusually concentrated Korean index and large foreign selling → broad price decline and exchange controls → higher collateral pressure and possible product rebalancing, but no observed July 28 public broker-credit liquidation surge → ordinary BOK repo rollover and a corporate-credit warning → post-trade outcome not yet due → crypto trades risk-off without a demonstrated Korean DeFi flow.

The evidence supports an asset-market stress event with collateral pressure. It does not demonstrate a monetary-system or DeFi-transition event.

Keep the drawdown endpoints distinct: the initial study records June 19's intraday peak to July 29's intraday low as −43.93%, and June 22's closing peak to July 29's close as −37.87%. Foreign cash selling and exchange pauses were observed; their exact price impact and manager hedge execution remain unmeasured. Sidecars, circuit breakers and security-level volatility interruptions have different rules; none is a price floor or a settlement receipt.

What the BOK operation says

The BOK did not simply add KRW28tn to a standing KRW30tn injection. Its July 16 KRW30tn repo purchase matured on July 28, when a new KRW28tn 14-day operation was allotted. Bids of KRW30.20tn exceeded supply, showing demand for the operation: the full KRW28tn offered was allotted, not all KRW30.20tn of bid demand. Terms retained the signature of ordinary OMO at the 2.75% policy rate. The replacement was KRW2tn smaller across these two operations alone; other operations, reserve flows and government balances prevent a systemwide net liquidity conclusion.

That is consistent with routine liquidity management under stress, not proof of either “nothing happened” or “emergency rescue.”

Sources: BOK, July 16 terms; BOK, July 28 result.

Both yields fell across those dates while the spread widened 0.3bp. SBS's 71.6bp market snapshot remains a separate quote series; it is not silently substituted for the KOFIA close. No retrieved primary-issuance, CP-rollover or failed-placement series establishes a corporate-credit freeze.

The initial study's first-day won strengthening and falling sovereign yields remain dated observations. June banking/deposit statistics, Q1 intervention and reserve stocks do not measure crash-day funding or capital flight. KOFR methodology is known, but the saved review did not recover the event-date values, volume or dispersion. Fund NAV loss is not a redemption measure.

BOK Article 65/80 lending, intraday credit, collateral frameworks, stabilization vehicles and D-SIFI resolution arrangements belong to the available/pre-positioned authority map. No episode-specific extraordinary draw or default-resource use was established in these studies. Old emergency programs and their expiry clocks are precedents, not current activation.
