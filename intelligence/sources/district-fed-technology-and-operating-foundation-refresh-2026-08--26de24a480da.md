**Question:** What has changed in the technological and operating foundations of
the twelve Reserve Banks, and what does the current structure imply for a dollar
system whose private clearing and token routes are expanding?

## Short answer

The operating constitution survives the refresh.

The Federal Reserve is becoming **more centralized inside while it faces the
possibility of becoming less central outside**.

Inside the System, the twelve Banks remain separate legal corporations, but
payments, information technology, AI, accounting, procurement, and other shared
operations are moving toward common standards, single-System platforms, and
specialized contractor-Bank control. The public record establishes a
presidents-led FRFS enterprise, a Payments Committee and Chief Payments
Executive, ITOC technology governance, and Richmond-hosted National IT under
Board/RBOPS oversight. It does not disclose the full decision rules, service
agreements, vendor register, application RACI, chargebacks, or incident chain.

Outside the System, stablecoins, tokenized deposits, private clearinghouses,
longer-hour market utilities, and programmable collateral can carry dollar
claims without making the Federal Reserve the visible route for every transfer.
The district Banks are not responding by building twelve separate replacements.
They are consolidating their own machinery into a national operating system.

The fresh August 15–29 window produced **no new constitutional transfer** and
only a few bounded state changes:

1. FRFS moved a planned Fedwire Funds standards release from November 2026 to
   November 2027 after Swift changed its own schedule.
2. The New York Fed added Morgan Stanley Private Bank, N.A. to the reverse-repo
   counterparty list, effective August 21, subject to legal, operational, and
   technical setup before actual trading.
3. Boston installed a new general counsel and Executive Committee member,
   effective August 19.
4. Philadelphia announced a new research director and Executive Leadership Team
   member on August 21, effective November 1.

Jackson Hole, Richmond's new FedNow analysis, Dallas's tokenized-deposit paper,
and the other district AI and payments publications are research or public
position objects. They do not establish a pilot, rail change, or control
transfer.

## 1. The foundation already in place

### A. Twelve legal Banks, one increasingly integrated operating system

The Federal Reserve is not a normal parent-subsidiary group. Each Reserve Bank
remains a separate legal entity with its own board. The Board of Governors is a
federal agency with separate books. The FOMC is a statutory policy committee,
not a holding company. FRFS and National IT are shared Reserve Bank operating
structures, not a thirteenth Bank.

The modern control chain is:

```text
twelve Reserve Bank boards and presidents
        -> Conference of Presidents / Payments Committee
        -> Chief Payments Executive / FRFS
        -> service operations and product management
        -> Richmond-hosted National IT and other Bank operating nodes
        -> Board / RBOPS supervision, examination, budget, price,
           rule, major-initiative and material-change gates
```

The 2025 Fedwire PFMI disclosures say every Reserve Bank president and board
approved the FRFS enterprise arrangement in mid-2022. The public descriptions
establish the existence and broad scope of the **FRFS Enterprise Principles**.
They do not publish the operative text, voting rule, reserved matters,
amendments, or twelve approval records.

Sources: [Fedwire Funds PFMI disclosure](https://www.frbservices.org/binaries/content/assets/crsocms/financial-services/wires/funds-service-disclosure.pdf),
[Fedwire Securities PFMI disclosure](https://www.frbservices.org/binaries/content/assets/crsocms/financial-services/securities/securities-service-disclosure.pdf),
and [FRFS leadership](https://www.frbservices.org/about/leadership).

### B. Richmond is a technology host and control node, not the owner of the Fed

Richmond hosts National IT, the System CIO, national infrastructure, and a local
board committee that oversees the Bank's National IT responsibilities. ITOC
sets national IT direction, standards, and security policy. National IT supplies
shared infrastructure under inter-Bank agreements and performance measures.
FRFS controls the payment-service business requirements; the twelve Banks remain
the legal service providers; the Board retains public oversight gates.

Richmond's published inter-Bank computing and support reimbursement is an
accounting allocation through its books, not proof that Richmond owns every
application, contract, payment rail, or dollar of System technology.

Sources: [Richmond governance and charters](https://www.richmondfed.org/about_us/our_governance/charters_memberships),
[National IT Committee charter](https://www.richmondfed.org/-/media/RichmondFedOrg/about_us/our_governance/charters_memberships/pdf/it.pdf),
and the two Fedwire PFMI disclosures above.

### C. AI has already crossed from experiment to internal production

Governor Waller's February 24 speech provides the strongest public operational
evidence. It says the System has:

- a common internal general-purpose AI platform for Reserve Bank employees;
- Fed-approved AI tools available to every employee;
- coding assistants used by hundreds of developers;
- AI-assisted synthesis of regional qualitative intelligence;
- AI embedded in existing legal, risk, procurement, operations, and other
  enterprise workflows; and
- baseline AI literacy and application incorporated into employee performance
  goals across the System.

That is an occurred internal deployment, not merely a conference theme. The
public record still does not name the complete model/vendor stack, application
inventory, data boundary, validation framework, security standard, performance
measure, or district-by-district implementation ledger.

Source: [Waller, Operationalizing AI at the Federal Reserve, February 24, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/waller20260224a.htm).

### D. Back-office centralization is partly foundation and partly unfinished

Waller's April 21 proposal separated locally necessary work from scale-driven
operations. He put monetary-policy voice, regional research and outreach,
supervision, and discount-window work on the local side. He put payments, IT,
fiscal agency, HR, finance, accounting, procurement, vendor management, and
facilities on the System side.

On May 8, he said the presidents had developed a framework in which contractor
Banks would centrally lead functions for the others under service-level
agreements. Individual Banks would give up some day-to-day decisions, the
contractor Bank would receive operating authority, and the Board would oversee
rather than run the function. He also said details remained unfinished.

The proper ruling is therefore split:

- the **financial-services enterprise** is already institutionalized;
- National IT is already a shared infrastructure arrangement;
- the common AI platform is already in use;
- a wider contractor-Bank model for every back-office function is a confirmed
  framework and direction of travel; but
- the full assignment schedule, authority delegations, and implementation
  clocks are not public.

Sources: [Waller, Modernizing Federal Reserve Operations, April 21, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/waller20260421a.htm)
and [Waller, Update on Federal Reserve Bank Operations, May 8, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/waller20260508a.htm).

## 4. What the refresh changes in the larger reading

### Stronger

- The Fed is not merely discussing AI. It has put a common platform, coding
  assistants, and embedded enterprise use into internal production.
- District operational independence is being converted into collective
  Reserve-Bank control, specialized contractor-Bank authority, and shared
  infrastructure.
- Fedwire's calendar can be constrained by the external standards network even
  though the Reserve Banks remain its legal operators.
- The New York RRP roster change shows that narrow access-state movement still
  occurs through a specialized district operating node.

### Not established

- a merger of the twelve Banks;
- a Board takeover of daily Reserve Bank operations;
- a new FRFS Enterprise Principle, Payments Committee or ITOC charter;
- a public contractor-Bank assignment schedule for HR, finance, procurement,
  vendor management, facilities, or all IT functions;
- a new Fed tokenized-reserve pilot;
- a FedNow, FedACH, NSS, FedLine, or current Fedwire functionality change in the
  window;
- a final Payment Account rule or new master-account decision;
- a central-clearing authorization for Federal Reserve market operations; or
- proof that Jackson Hole's academic proposals were adopted by Kansas City,
  FRFS, the Board, or the FOMC.

## 5. Exact records that still matter

1. FRFS Enterprise Principles, amendments, and twelve approval records.
2. Payments Committee, AFRC, and ITOC charters, voting rules, reserved matters,
   and delegated-authority schedules.
3. Contractor-Bank assignment map for HR, finance, procurement, vendor

8. Fedwire November 2027 release scope, exact date, approval path, and revised
   readiness milestones.

## Disposition

This refresh is complete for the stated window and remains **pending** in the
workbench. Katie admitted it to the Research Desk on August 29 as supporting
evidence under `fed-clearing-treasury`. That visibility does not authorize a
vault, watch-calendar, Observatory, wrapper, or installed-app promotion.

The bounded conclusion is:

> The twelve Reserve Banks are becoming specialized nodes of one national
> operating system. That makes the Fed harder to fragment internally at the
> same moment private rails make it easier to route dollars around the Fed's
> daily clearing machinery.
