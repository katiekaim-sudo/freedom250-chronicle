# AI committee open-item pipeline

## Answer first

The committee-level AI transition is narrower—and more revealing—than the
whole national AI agenda.

The live work is moving through four recurring channels:

1. **public disclosure and visible records** — what companies, agencies and
   benefit systems must reveal about AI and the data used around it;
2. **sector risk translation** — converting general AI principles into the
   vocabulary of securities, banking, derivatives, tax, health and retirement;
3. **shared government procedures** — templates, inventories, acquisition,
   cloud assurance, data standards and milestone funding; and
4. **human authority and recourse** — identifying who still decides, when a
   person can intervene and whether an affected person can see, challenge and
   correct the record.

Most of these items have **not** completed the full chain. The normal path is:

```text
staff or working-group agenda
          ↓
draft / roundtable / request for comment
          ↓
committee vote or adopted recommendation
          ↓
transmittal to parent agency, Secretary, Commission, OMB or Congress
          ↓
rule / guidance / funding / contract / implementation decision
          ↓
operation → evidence → correction → measured effect
```

Different bodies enter at different points. A council may implement an OMB
memo without voting on a public recommendation. A FACA committee generally
must approve advice publicly before sending it upward. A regulator may act on
the same subject without proving that the committee caused the action. That
last distinction is essential: **thematic convergence is not a causal join**.

## What these proposals would actually change

### 1. They would make AI legible inside existing law

The SEC proposal does not invent an AI regulator; it translates AI into
materiality, board oversight and issuer disclosure. FSOC and FBIIC/FSSCC do the
same for financial stability, model risk, third parties, fraud and consumer
protection. CFTC is still deciding what an AI/autonomous-systems “rules of the
road” object should be.

This is **institutional absorption by translation**. The change becomes real
when the new vocabulary is cited in a Commission instrument, supervisory
practice, examination, contract or enforcement record.

### 2. They would move public administration toward visible evidence

ETAAC and SSAB are the most constitutionally interesting open items.

- ETAAC pairs automation and faster data exchange with a proposal that the IRS
  explain its AI uses, risk controls and taxpayer recourse.
- SSAB asks SSA to show recipients the facts the agency holds and give them a
  receipt when a report is received and processed **before** broader automated
  processing expands.

Together, they imply a procedural sequence:

```text
agency-held fact
      ↓ visible to the person
reported change
      ↓ acknowledged and traceable
automated or AI-assisted processing
      ↓ explanation and human authority
challenge
      ↓ correction reflected in the authoritative record
```

Neither proposal is operative. But they identify the missing human-facing
counterpart to machine-speed administration more clearly than most high-level
AI policy documents do.

### 3. They would standardize the machinery beneath agency decisions

The CAIO and CIO Councils, FedRAMP Board and FSCAC are not mainly debating
abstract AI ethics. They are working on the reusable machinery:

- governance templates and inventories;
- architecture and make-versus-buy choices;
- identity, data, cloud and security services;
- machine-readable assurance evidence;
- high-assurance cloud pathways;
- reuse of authorizations and lessons; and
- workforce practices and outcome measurement.

This is the closest committee-level evidence for the thesis that institutional
change is happening through interfaces and gates. It is also where public
visibility is weakest: shared priorities and programs are visible, but the
item-level Council product → agency adoption → operating result chain is often
not.

### 4. They would preserve sector owners rather than replace them

PTAC can recommend; HHS and CMS retain payment-model authority. SSAB can
recommend; SSA retains program and adjudication authority. ETAAC can advise;
IRS and Congress decide. IAC advice does not become an SEC or CFTC rule without
Commission action. FSCAC advice does not replace agency authorization.

The transition therefore looks less like a new AI bureaucracy and more like a
new common language moving through old constitutional and statutory owners.

## The most important open proposals, ranked

### P0 — likely to alter the Observatory thesis if they return

1. **SEC AI disclosure disposition** — the cleanest test of whether committee
   advice becomes a generally applicable market disclosure obligation.
2. **FSOC AI Working Group product** — the cleanest test of whether roundtable
   translation becomes interagency or member-agency policy.
3. **ETAAC AI disclosure and recourse response** — the clearest tax-
   administration proposal joining faster automation to public explanation and
   challenge.
4. **SSAB visible-record decision** — the clearest benefits-administration
   proposal making a reliable, recipient-visible record a prerequisite for
   broader automation.
5. **FedRAMP Class D and FSCAC reuse products** — the clearest shared-
   infrastructure test of automated evidence, reusable assurance and retained
   agency risk acceptance.

### P1 — important translation and implementation returns

6. **CFTC AI/autonomous-systems recommendation** — currently a work program,
   not a policy object.
7. **PTAC final roadmap and HHS response** — separates committee adoption from
   payment-model authority and later effect.
8. **CAIO/CIO Council-owned tools and agency adoption** — tests whether shared
   procedural grammar is actually reused.
9. **FBIIC/FSSCC adoption or examination evidence** — tests whether a voluntary
   public-private framework becomes operating supervisory practice.
10. **NAIAC appointment or meeting record** — tests whether the national
    advisory layer is being reconstituted or whether the transition continues
    through more specialized bodies.

## Thesis ruling

The committee evidence supports a more precise version of the Observatory
thesis:

> AI is being absorbed into government through distributed translators. The
> committees do not usually command the system; they formulate the language,
> evidence requirements, technical interfaces and human safeguards that a
> legally empowered parent can adopt, fund, contract, regulate or ignore.

The exciting part is that the new operating constitution is being argued into
existence in unusually specific terms: issuer disclosure, taxpayer recourse,
recipient-visible facts, agent identity, reusable cloud evidence and retained
human authority.

The discipline is that almost every crucial item is still **between gates**.
Recommendation is not disposition. Disposition is not implementation.
Implementation is not operation. Operation is not measured benefit. And
thematic similarity between committee advice and agency action is not proof
that the committee caused it.
