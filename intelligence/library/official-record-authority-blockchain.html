What makes a record official on a blockchain

A record is not official because it sits on a blockchain; it is official because a law or regulator gives it a specific job, a named institution is responsible for it, and something legally follows from it.

What we found

  • "Official record" is not one legal status. A record can be official for one purpose (say, regulatory compliance) and carry no weight for another (say, ownership or audit). Paper, a database or a blockchain can all qualify if the rules allow it.
  • On September 24, CFTC staff said covered firms can keep required records natively on a blockchain, without a duplicate off-chain copy just because blockchain is used. It is staff guidance, not a Commission rule, and the firm stays fully responsible.
  • Firms using a public blockchain must still be able to produce their records if the network or block explorer goes down. The required record also includes the data needed to find, read and explain it, not just the transaction itself.
  • SEC staff goes further: a registered transfer agent may use a blockchain as its official Master Securityholder File, which decides registered holders. The related September 2026 SEC transfer-agent rule is proposed, not final.
  • An on-chain record does not decide accounting or audit questions: which entity reports the item, when it is recognized, how it is measured, or whether it is enough audit evidence. Official, true, public and final are separate claims.

Why it matters

A federal regulator let a blockchain count as the legal compliance record through a staff FAQ under existing rules, with no new law. The machinery changes from inside, while responsibility, ownership and audit judgment stay with accountable institutions.

What to watch

  • pending · Whether the SEC finalizes, changes or withdraws its September 2026 transfer-agent modernization proposal.
  • pending · Whether the full CFTC Commission adopts a binding rule or interpretation replacing today's staff-only guidance.
  • pending · A named futures or swaps firm announcing a live system that keeps its required CFTC records natively on-chain.
  • pending · A live blockchain-based securityholder file hitting a fork, outage, mistaken transfer, court order or lost key, and showing how it was corrected.
