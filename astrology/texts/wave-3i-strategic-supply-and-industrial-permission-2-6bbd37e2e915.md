# Wave 3I — Strategic Supply and Industrial Permission

**Research window:** Exact 2025 Cancer ingress through the eve of exact 2025 Libra ingress.  
**Evidence cutoff:** June 20, 2026, 11:59 p.m. EDT. Later facts are quarantined as handoffs.  
**Question:** When the Cancer-period regime tried to redirect energy investment, accelerate commercial space, stockpile pharmaceutical ingredients and end a prior competition program, which moves changed real inventory or permission and which changed only the administrative weather?  
**Finding:** The four orders occupy four different maturity states. The Strategic Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients Reserve is the only one that publicly reached procurement and custody: HHS says the repository became operational in late December 2025 and some critical APIs were sourced, procured and secured. Commercial-space policy reached a proposed whole-of-government mission-certification process and an explicit hazardous-materials rulemaking intake, but not a binding novel-mission authorization or a named mission approval by the cutoff. The wind/solar order produced a real Treasury safe-harbor restriction and a Secretary-level Interior review choke point; courts later vacated the tax notice and preliminarily enjoined five agency actions for the plaintiff organizations. The competition order cleanly revoked EO 14036 but did not erase the antitrust statutes, agency rules or cases built on separate authority.

The shared question is:

`what does the state want produced? → who may finance, license, stock or review it? → what legal object carries the preference? → did an operator actually receive permission, inventory or money? → what survived contest?`

## 3I.1 — July 7: energy policy becomes a tax-and-permitting choke point

[EO 14315](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/07/10/2025-12961/ending-market-distorting-subsidies-for-unreliable-foreign-controlled-energy-sources) directed Treasury to strictly enforce the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's termination rules for sections 45Y and 48E clean-electricity credits as applied to wind and solar. It specifically targeted manipulation of “beginning of construction,” broad safe harbors and enhanced Foreign Entity of Concern restrictions. Interior was directed to find and remove policies that preferred wind and solar over dispatchable sources.

The statute remained the OBBBA. The EO could direct implementation but could not itself change the Code.

### July 15–17, 2025: Interior creates an approval funnel

Interior's [review memorandum](https://www.doi.gov/media/document/departmental-review-procedures-decisions-actions-consultations-and-other) required wind- and solar-related decisions to pass through the Executive Secretariat and Regulatory Affairs, the Deputy Secretary and the Secretary. The [Department's public explanation](https://www.doi.gov/node/62736) says the funnel reached leases, rights-of-way, construction and operation plans, grants, consultations and biological opinions.

This is a real decision-right change. A field office or bureau could no longer complete many ordinary actions without top-department review. It did not automatically deny every project, but it changed the path and time cost of permission.

### August 15 / September 2, 2025: Treasury restricts the construction test

[IRS Notice 2025-42](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2025-36_IRB) generally removed the five-percent safe harbor for determining whether affected wind and solar construction began before the OBBBA deadline. Except for a narrow low-output solar exception, the physical-work test became the route. The notice applied to facilities whose construction had not begun before September 2, 2025.

That is an operative rule of tax administration, not a political wish. A developer's evidence of construction now affected whether a project could escape the statutory termination date.

### April 21 and June 6, 2026: two court clocks cut back the body

In **RENEW Northeast v. Interior**, a federal district court preliminarily enjoined five challenged agency actions for the plaintiff organizations. The order was preliminary and plaintiff-bounded, not a final universal judgment on every administration renewable-energy action. But it materially interrupted the Interior review machine.

In **Oregon Environmental Council v. IRS**, the district court held Notice 2025-42 arbitrary and capricious, vacated it in full and remanded to the agency on June 6, 2026. That removed the EO-descendant safe-harbor restriction. It did not restore tax credits that the OBBBA itself terminated or amend the statute's July 4, 2026 construction deadline.

**Maturity.** Operative tax and departmental-review machinery followed by material judicial reversal/constraint.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The case record is evidence that the machinery had consequences, not proof that every wind or solar project stopped. Conversely, vacating one IRS notice and enjoining five agency actions did not revive the prior statutory subsidy regime in full. Tax law, Interior permissions, grid interconnection, state siting and physical construction remain separate clocks.

## 3I.2 — August 13: commercial space receives a proposed permission membrane

[EO 14335](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/19/2025-15822/enabling-competition-in-the-commercial-space-industry) set a 2030 launch-cadence and novel-activity ambition and distributed the work across Transportation/FAA, Commerce, Defense, NASA, Interior, CEQ and Justice.

Its architecture has four parts:

1. reconsider Part 450 launch/reentry requirements and environmental review;
2. align spaceport reviews and consider new categorical exclusions;
3. propose individualized authorization for novel activities not clearly covered by existing frameworks; and
4. elevate or create regulatory leadership seats at Transportation, FAA and Commerce.

The order's verbs matter: **reevaluate, report, evaluate, execute an MOU, consider, propose, establish and elevate**. None of those is itself a launch license or mission authorization.

### January 29, 2026: the terrestrial supply chain enters rulemaking

PHMSA's [advance notice of proposed rulemaking](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/29/2026-01758/hazardous-materials-regulations-modernizing-regulations-for-the-transportation-of-hazardous) explicitly cites EO 14335 and asks how hazardous-materials rules should treat spacecraft payloads and components. PHMSA had reviewed 60 special permits and identified recurring issues involving gases, hazard communication and batteries.

This is a meaningful but early descendant. It translates “space competition” into the ordinary terrestrial problem of moving pressurized, explosive or battery-containing hardware to and from a launch site. It remains an ANPRM: intake and problem definition, not a final rule or shipment permission.

### March 2026: Commerce publishes Space Commerce Certification

The Office of Space Commerce released a proposed **Space Commerce Certification** process for novel in-space activities. The proposal uses one application and coordinated interagency review. Its own public description preserves FAA, FCC and other agencies' statutory obligations and says applicants may still need their separate permissions.

The proposal is therefore a **coordination membrane**, not a super-license. The Office expressly labels it nonbinding. It does not displace spectrum licensing, launch/reentry authority, export controls, national-security review or treaty responsibility.

The later pilot call launched August 20, 2026—after this packet's cutoff—and must remain a future handoff.

### March 17, 2026: the Part 450 transition is real but not born here

FAA reported that all launch and reentry licensing had moved to Part 450 after the five-year transition period, naming Blue Origin, Firefly, SpaceX, Rocket Lab and United Launch Alliance vehicles among the transitioned operators. Fourteen Part 450 licenses had been issued since the rule took effect in 2021.

This is real operating infrastructure. It should not be attributed wholesale to EO 14335: the rule and transition clock predate the 2025 order. By the cutoff, no published rewrite of Part 450 clearly created by EO 14335 was located.

**Maturity.** Proposed whole-of-government novel-mission route plus explicit rulemaking intake; adjacent preexisting license system operating.

**Counterevidence and limit.** A proposal is not legal authorization. An office elevation is not a new department. A Part 450 transition is not proof of greater launch cadence or a specific EO-caused license. A spaceport MOU, CZMA evaluation, Endangered Species Committee consideration or categorical-exclusion idea is not a permit, lease or exemption. No named novel mission was certified through the proposed process by the cutoff.

## 3I.3 — August 13: the API reserve acquires inventory

[EO 14336](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/19/2025-15823/ensuring-american-pharmaceutical-supply-chain-resilience-by-filling-the-strategic-active) directed HHS's Assistant Secretary for Preparedness and Response to:

- identify approximately 26 especially critical drugs;
- account for existing funds and work with OMB on lawful repurposing;
- ready the existing Strategic Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients Reserve repository;
- obtain a six-month API supply for those drugs, with a domestic preference where possible;
- update the wider 86-medicine list; and
- propose and cost a second U.S. repository.

The paper preserves three gates: identified lawful funds, ASPR readiness certification and actual procurement/placement.

### Late December 2025: the repository becomes operational

ASPR's FY 2027 Congressional Justification supplies the crucial later receipt. It says clinicians, manufacturers and interagency/White House partners identified the most critical drugs; the first SAPIR instance became operational in late December 2025; and ASPR **sourced, procured and secured some** of the most critical APIs.

This language is unusually valuable because it crosses the line from plan to custody. It proves that some inventory was acquired and secured. It does not prove the complete six-month target.

The same document anticipated that all required quantities would be secured in spring 2026. Anticipation is not completion. The reviewed public record does not disclose the approximately 26-drug list, API quantities, suppliers, award identifiers, purchase values, repository location, domestic share or a certified full-fill date.

**Maturity.** Operating repository with some procured and secured critical API inventory.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The underlying repository and Phlow contract history predate EO 14336, so prior capacity cannot automatically be counted as new. OMB assistance does not create an appropriation. “Secured” does not disclose title, delivery, lot testing, shelf-life rotation or readiness for finished-dose manufacturing. A second-repository proposal remains a plan until site, legal instrument, funding and operation are proved.

## 3I.4 — August 13: one competition constitution is switched off

[EO 14337](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/19/2025-15824/revocation-of-executive-order-on-competition) contains one substantive act: it revokes EO 14036, the July 2021 competition order.

That is a clean Clock-1 event for the **presidential instrument**. The White House Competition Council and the order's continuing presidential directives no longer rest on EO 14036.

The revocation is not a general antitrust repeal. It does not repeal the Sherman Act, Clayton Act or Federal Trade Commission Act; vacate a judgment; withdraw every agency rule associated with the earlier policy; terminate an investigation; or erase agency authority independently supplied by statute. The surviving state of each rule, case and consent decree must be read separately.

**Maturity.** Prior executive coordination paper revoked.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The order does not identify a replacement competition doctrine or unwind schedule. The administration's separate anti-competitive-regulation review under EO 14267 remains a different instrument. “Deregulation” and “competition” may point in opposite directions on a particular market; the title cannot decide the substantive effect.

## What changed during the Cancer period

- Treasury converted a statutory wind/solar deadline into a stricter evidence test before a court vacated it.
- Interior inserted Secretary-level review over a broad renewable-permission surface before a court constrained five actions.
- Commerce gave novel in-space missions a proposed single-front-door process while preserving separate statutory permissions.
- PHMSA began translating launch ambition into hazardous-material classification and transport questions.
- ASPR moved from a nearly empty reserve narrative to an operating repository with some procured inventory.
- The 2021 White House competition paper ceased to govern, while the underlying antitrust constitution survived.

## What did not change merely because industry was preferred or disfavored

- An EO did not amend the Internal Revenue Code.
- A review funnel did not deny every project.
- A court order did not restore every prior subsidy.
- A commercial-space proposal did not authorize a mission.
- An ANPRM did not amend hazardous-materials rules.
- A Part 450 transition did not prove an EO-caused launch.
- A reserve target did not prove a complete six-month fill.
- A White House competition-order revocation did not repeal antitrust law.

## Institutional theorem

Industrial policy operates through **permission, preference and prepared inventory**, not through one master production switch.

The state can:

1. make a tax classification harder or easier to satisfy;
2. raise a permit into a higher approval room;
3. coordinate multiple regulators behind one intake;
4. buy and hold a strategic intermediate input;
5. terminate a prior cross-agency instruction; and
6. leave the legal persons and most underlying statutes intact while doing all five.

These acts can all redirect markets, but their proof objects differ. A tax notice needs a taxpayer and credit determination. A permit system needs an applicant and decision. A stockpile needs title, quantity and custody. A revoked EO needs no transaction at all. The wave becomes legible only when those clocks remain separate.

## Both poles

### Constructive pole — the state reduces strategic fragility

- tax and permitting policy can align public support with a chosen reliability doctrine;
- a single mission front door can reduce duplicative review while retaining safety and treaty obligations;
- hazardous-materials rules can be modernized around real spacecraft configurations;
- API inventory can buffer shortages and support domestic manufacturing demand;
- terminating an overbroad executive coordination paper can restore agency-specific judgment; and
- courts can correct overreach without erasing the legitimate underlying policy question.

### Shadow pole — preference becomes chokepoint government

- an approval funnel can delay a disfavored industry without a transparent denial;
- tax administration can narrow statutory access through guidance that lacks adequate reasoned basis;
- a “single front door” can centralize surveillance or discretion while leaving applicants responsible to every underlying regulator;
- a strategic reserve can hide vendors, quantities, prices and shelf-life risks behind security language;
- repeal of a competition program can permit concentration while retaining pro-market rhetoric; and
- national-security labels can convert ordinary industrial choices into exceptional executive control.

The evidence supports a mixed result: one real stockpile operation, one proposed permission architecture, one operating-but-partly-reversed energy choke point and one clean paper revocation. They are not four votes for the same thesis.
