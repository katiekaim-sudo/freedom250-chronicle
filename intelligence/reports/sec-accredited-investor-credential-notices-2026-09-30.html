SEC Accredited Investor Credential Notices — 2026

Status: five Commission notices issued September 30, 2026; no final designation order yet
Lead question: Did the SEC make CPAs accredited investors, and what would the proposed CPA route actually do?
Research owner: sec-accredited-investor-credential-notices-2026-09-30
Evidence cutoff: September 30, 2026, 8:55 p.m. EDT

Short answer

Not yet. The Commission issued notices and requests for comment under existing Rule 501(a)(10) authority. It is considering final orders that would recognize five additional knowledge-based routes to accredited-investor status: an open FINRA exam; an active U.S. CPA license; a CFA charter; a U.S. CFP certification; and either a Series 79 license or the combined Series 86 and 87 research-analyst license.

The CPA proposal is the most structurally surprising. The Commission would use an active state or territorial accounting license as a federal private-market access credential. No separate SEC investor examination, minimum portfolio, income or net-worth test would apply to that route. NASBA reported 650,667 active licensees from 53 of 55 jurisdictions, but the SEC does not know how many already qualify under another prong or how many would invest in private offerings.

Legal-state boundary

The notices are real Commission action, but they are not designation orders. No CPA, CFA, CFP, Series 79 holder, research analyst or exam taker becomes an accredited investor from these routes until the Commission issues a final order for that route. Each notice calls for comments 60 days after Federal Register publication; the SEC-issued PDFs still contained publication-date placeholders at this cutoff.
