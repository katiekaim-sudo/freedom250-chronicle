## Bottom line

The company map now has a program-centered mirror. Ten federal demand and
acquisition objects resolve into 37 typed company positions, 13 facility
records and four historical calibration cases. The strongest predictive
signals remain same-office access, a live instrument, an exact facility or
operating environment, and a recognized acceptance path. Public evidence
often bounds the serious field without disclosing enough to rank eventual
winners.

The new view preserves four recurring boundaries:

- market research is not a solicitation;
- parallel awardees are not one winner or architecture;
- a company capability site is not a program site without a direct join; and
- production, launch, installation and prototype delivery remain short of acceptance.

### Calibration ruling

The PTS-G design-to-production case is the cleanest current test. The
public record identified five serious design-pool participants, but the
later two-company production selection depended on technical, price and
evaluation information that was not public. A sound model should preserve
the five-company field rather than manufacture a precise winner probability.

JLWS is a useful parallel-award test: direct HEL source and integration
experience would have surfaced both later awardees. QTIME and SDN remain
partial tests because their full applicant or evaluated fields are not public.

## Cross-program findings

1. **Authorization precedes capacity in secure compute.** AWS has an
   operating NNSA environment, while Argonne remains market research; cloud
   scale cannot substitute for a task, identity, ATO and accepted workload.
2. **Facility uncertainty is itself evidence.** Five of thirteen facility
   records remain unresolved or are capability sites without a direct program
   join. Naming the uncertainty prevents corporate site lists from becoming
   fictional program supply chains.
3. **Parallel portfolios need separate clocks.** FITT, QTIME and protected
   SATCOM each contain multiple projects or subprograms. One award cannot
   promote every company or architecture in the portfolio.
4. **Production is still not the terminus.** GPS IIIF and PTS-G have
   production paths, but delivery, launch, checkout and mission acceptance
   remain downstream gates.
5. **The best open forward field is DBPC.** The program asks for an
   integrated production-representative bioindustrial capability, but no
   applicant, team or site is public. Existing BioMADE and DBIMP performers
   are discovery candidates only.
6. **The sharpest prototype race is JLWS.** nLIGHT and Aculight are confirmed
   parallel awardees; the differentiating objects will be integrated duty
   cycle, live-target performance, scale and production transition.
