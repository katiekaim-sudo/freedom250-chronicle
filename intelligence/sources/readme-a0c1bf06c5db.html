The OCC opened a federal trust-bank lane for crypto, but most applicants are still outside the door

In plain English

Question: Which crypto-facing firms have actually become federally chartered banks or trust banks, what may they do, and which firms are still only approved or waiting?

Answer: Five plainly crypto-facing national trust banks appear on the OCC's August 31 active list: Anchorage Digital Bank, BitGo Bank & Trust, Fidelity Digital Assets, First National Digital Currency Bank and Paxos Trust Company. Circle's First National Digital Currency Bank crossed the missing line: the OCC records it consummated on July 24, and Circle says it opened that day. But it opened as an uninsured, nondeposit custody bank for Circle and its affiliates; it does not issue USDC, take deposits or make loans, and reserve management remains a separate future transfer. A much larger group has conditional approval or a pending application. Those firms are not operating national trust banks merely because the OCC approved an application or published their filing.

Evidence checked through: October 13, 2026 at 6:10 p.m. ET

The detailed record keeps application, conditional approval, final approval, consummation, product operation, stablecoin-issuer migration, Federal Reserve membership and Reserve Bank account access on separate clocks.

The governing legal form

A national trust bank is a national bank supervised by the OCC, but it is not automatically an ordinary deposit-and-loan bank. The OCC's licensing manual says national trust banks generally do not take deposits and need not carry FDIC insurance. A qualifying uninsured trust bank may also sit outside the Bank Holding Company Act definition of “bank” when it does not take demand deposits and make commercial loans.

The OCC's 2026 chartering rule made the operative phrase “operations of a trust company and activities related thereto.” It clarified that a national trust bank may conduct related non-fiduciary activities; it did not give every trust bank every power. Each decision letter and business plan still controls the particular bank.

For digital-asset trust banks, the recurring permitted functions are custody and safekeeping, fiduciary or investment-management work, customer-directed trading or conversion tied to custody, settlement and clearing services, and—in some decisions—stablecoin issuance and reserve maintenance. The OCC separately confirmed that national banks may perform specified crypto custody, stablecoin- reserve and distributed-ledger payment activities. Permission is still not operation.

Five active crypto-facing national trust banks

The OCC's trust-bank list active as of August 31 names these five explicit crypto-facing institutions:

Anchorage Digital Bank National Association 25243 The federal trust-bank legal person is active. A Fed account, FDIC insurance for digital assets or use of every advertised group product by the bank.
BitGo Bank & Trust, National Association 25366 The South Dakota trust conversion was consummated by the active-list cutoff. That every BitGo wallet, trading or settlement service is supplied by this bank.
Fidelity Digital Assets, National Association 25398 The national trust bank is active; Fidelity separately put FIDD issuance/redemption into public operation by February 4. A first customer transaction, FDIC insurance for FIDD or a Fed account.
First National Digital Currency Bank, National Association 25361 Circle's de novo trust bank is active. OCC CAS records consummation on July 24. That the bank issues USDC, has assumed USDC reserve management, takes deposits, makes loans or has Reserve Bank account access.
Paxos Trust Company, National Association 25379 The New York trust conversion was consummated by the active-list cutoff. That every Paxos-issued coin, partner program or old state-trust duty migrated at once.

“Active” is a legal-entity state. It is not a common measure of customer volume, transaction scale, reserve responsibility, balance-sheet exposure or stress performance.

Circle: the October story is true, but narrower than it sounds

The saved X headline said the OCC chartered First National Digital Currency Bank. The controlling sources show a three-step legal clock:

  1. December 12, 2025: the OCC granted preliminary conditional approval.
  2. July 10, 2026: Circle announced final OCC approval.
  3. July 24, 2026: OCC CAS records the charter as Consummated/Effective; Circle's August 5 SEC filing says the bank opened.

That makes the core claim true: Circle now has an operating OCC-chartered national trust-bank subsidiary. The stronger version is false.

  • Circle's SEC filing says the bank opened with fiduciary digital-asset custody for Circle and its affiliates.
  • Circle's current product disclosure says Circle National Trust does not issue USDC, accept deposits or make loans.
  • Digital assets held there are not FDIC-insured.
  • Circle's July announcement described reserve management as a planned future capability. The reviewed sources do not establish that the USDC reserve has transferred to the bank.
  • The U.S. issuer, customer contract, reserve fund, commercial-bank cash accounts, BNY custody, token networks and Circle National Trust therefore remain separate legal and operating nodes.

The charter moved federal supervision and custody inside Circle's group. It did not turn USDC into a bank deposit or central-bank money.

Conditional approval is now a broad lane, not an odd exception

The reviewed OCC decisions establish a large preopening group that includes Ripple, Bridge, National Digital Trust Company, Foris DAX/Crypto.com, Coinbase, Laser Digital, Morgan Stanley Digital Trust, Connectia/Sony Bank, Wise, World Liberty, Bastion Platforms, Catena and Agora.

Their business plans differ, but their decision letters use the same crucial boundary: preliminary or conditional approval does not authorize the proposed bank to open before preopening requirements and final approval are satisfied. The August 31 active list did not contain the pre-August members of this group. The September 18 Agora, Catena and Bastion letters themselves retain the preopening boundary.

Two September decisions show how wide the lane has become:

  • Agora National Trust Bank would be a wholly owned Agora Atlas subsidiary. It proposes AUSD issuance and reserve maintenance, custody, payments, settlement and institutional advisory work. The current AUSD issuer remains Agora Bermuda until a later bank opening and asset/liability cutover.
  • Catena Trust Bank would be a wholly owned Catena Labs subsidiary offering custody, investment management, trust, conversion, clearing and execution services, including GENIUS-compliant payment-stablecoin work once the law is effective. The decision requires later conformity to GENIUS and its rules.
  • Bastion Platforms National Trust Company received conditional approval to convert its New York trust company. It proposes white-label stablecoin issuance, custodial wallets and issuer services, but the conversion and its activity migration remain subject to the letter's conditions.

This is not “crypto firms became banks” as one event. It is an OCC-supervised production line with entity-specific conditions and later opening clocks.

Eleven applications remain on the OCC's public digital-assets table

At the cutoff, the OCC search result for its pending digital-assets page named Rain National Trust Bank, Modern Treasury National Trust Bank, zerohash, Dakota National Trust Bank, The First National Bank of Cooper, CBW Bank, Builders Bank & Trust, Payward National Trust Company, Lorum National Trust Bank, EDX Trust and PAYO Digital Bank.

The table is broader than national trust banks: it includes full-service bank and other licensing applications involving digital-asset products. It also notes that conversion applications are not included because they are not subject to the same public-comment treatment. It is therefore a pending-file list, not a complete denominator for the whole charter cohort.

The zerohash application shows what is at stake. It proposes an uninsured national trust bank for custody of digital and traditional assets, related trading, staking/validation, and fiduciary settlement, clearing and escrow. Those are proposed powers in an application, not OCC approval.

Stablecoins and charters overlap, but they are not the same permission

The charter lane and the stablecoin lane meet in four different ways:

  1. an operating trust bank can be the stablecoin issuer, as Fidelity says for FIDD and Anchorage says for its admitted bank-issued products;
  2. a trust bank can provide custody or reserve administration while another group entity remains the issuer, as Circle currently discloses;
  3. a proposed bank can plan to take over an existing affiliate's coin and reserves after opening, as Agora and World Liberty describe; or
  4. a bank can provide white-label issuance or issuer services for other firms, as Bastion and Bridge propose.

GENIUS does not erase those legal persons. The conditional letters expressly require later conformity, cessation or divestiture if needed to comply with the Act and implementing rules. A charter decision made before the GENIUS regime is effective is not itself a GENIUS permitted-payment-stablecoin-issuer approval.

The Federal Reserve door remains separate

Most conditional letters say the proposed national trust bank will apply for Federal Reserve Bank stock under 12 U.S.C. 222. That is not proof of a Reserve Bank master account or payment-services access.

The Fed access-request database was current through August 31 at this research cutoff. It continued to show Anchorage's August 28, 2025 request as pending, Tier 3 and not federally insured. No charter decision in this package is used as a substitute for a Fed database record.

The clean counterexample is Kraken Financial, a Wyoming SPDI rather than an OCC national trust bank. Kansas City approved a limited-purpose account with Fedwire Funds only, no interest, no intraday or discount-window credit, a closing-balance cap, a limited term and no services for Kraken's exchange or other Payward entities. That account does not migrate to Payward's separate OCC trust-bank applicant.

Custodia remains the legal test. On October 13 the Supreme Court docket posted the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City's brief opposing certiorari in No. 26-62. That is a filed opposition, not a cert denial, merits ruling or account grant. The Court has not yet disposed of the petition at this cutoff.

What changed in the Library's answer

This research confirms the Library's main thesis but sharpens it:

  • Confirmed: the crypto-to-bank movement is real and procedural. OCC charters and conditional approvals are putting custody, reserve, stablecoin and settlement functions inside supervised legal persons one institution at a time.
  • Changed: Circle's bank-opening clock is now resolved—July 24, 2026. The older “opening unknown” language is superseded.
  • Not changed: the charter does not collapse issuer, reserve custodian, token network, customer contract, commercial-bank cash leg, Fed account, discount-window credit or FDIC insurance into one permission.
  • Still unproved: most of the cohort has not opened. No package-wide record shows common Fed access, migration of each proposed stablecoin, public scale, a handled failure or a shared federal rescue perimeter.

The emerging system is not one crypto banking regime. It is a federally supervised trust-bank lane sitting beside ordinary insured banks, state trust companies, money transmitters, broker-dealers and Reserve Bank access rules.
