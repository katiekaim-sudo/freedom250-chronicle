# Federal Reserve regional storylines — transcript audit

**Status:** pending workbench research; not promoted to the vault  
**Updated:** July 25, 2026  
**Evidence cutoff:** July 25, 2026  
**Scope:** Dallas/Eleventh District, the June 12 Oklahoma City field hearing,
Kansas City/Tenth District and New York/SOMA evidence; connected to the existing
payment-stack, software, entity-transaction and uses-of-money maps  
**Dollar convention:** every monetary amount is written in full nominal
dollars. Expanded zeros preserve the source's rounding and do not create
additional precision.

This audit tests existing Observatory storylines against recent spoken records.
It separates:

- **T — transcript evidence:** an identified speaker actually said it;
- **O — operating corroboration:** an official action, rule, service or
  accounting record supports it; and
- **I — interpretation:** a synthesis that may explain the pattern but is not
  itself an institutional admission.

The transcript sources are useful for reconstructing the institution's own
story. They are not substitutes for audited expense records, legal authority or
product-level operating rules.

## I. Bottom line

The three regional storylines survive the transcript test, but in a more
precise form:

| Node | What the recent record shows | Evidence grade |
|---|---|---|
| Dallas | regional intelligence plus monetary-plumbing design: data, surveys, research, reserves, discount-window access, repo, clearing and tokenization questions | T + O |
| Oklahoma City / Kansas City | political and institutional defense of the federated model: local intelligence, bank supervision, discount-window lending, public trust, independent views and operational oversight | T |
| New York | the implementation machine for FOMC-directed market operations: SOMA, reserve-management purchases, standing repo operations, counterparties and experiments involving tokenized monetary operations | T + O |

The connecting sequence is:

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    Region["Regional businesses, banks,<br/>directors and communities"]
    Dallas["Dallas and other Reserve Banks<br/>collect intelligence and develop options"]
    FOMC["FOMC<br/>decides policy and operating framework"]
    NY["New York Desk<br/>implements SOMA market operations"]
    Market["Dealers, banks, MMFs, FICC,<br/>clearing banks and other counterparties"]
    Shared["FRFS, FRIT and other System functions<br/>operate payment and technology infrastructure"]

    Region --> Dallas
    Dallas --> FOMC
    FOMC --> NY
    NY --> Market
    Shared --> Dallas
    Shared --> NY
    Market --> Region
```

This is not an ownership chart. New York implements open-market operations as
agent for the FOMC and all Reserve Banks; it does not thereby own the entire
Federal Reserve payment stack. Federal Reserve Financial Services, Federal
Reserve Information Technology, product offices, host Banks and outside
vendors distribute technology and cost control across the System.

The strongest factual synthesis is:

> **The regional Banks are an intelligence, supervision, lending and
> legitimacy network. Dallas increasingly supplies public design language for
> the liquidity and collateral architecture. The FOMC supplies authority. New
> York supplies market execution. National shared-service organizations supply
> much of the payment and technology machinery.**

The stronger claim that this is a coordinated plan to reduce the Fed to a
rule-bound settlement utility remains an **interpretation**, not a proved
fact.

## II. Entity and event corrections

### Oklahoma is not a thirteenth Reserve Bank

There is no “Federal Reserve Bank of Oklahoma.” Oklahoma is within the Tenth
Federal Reserve District. The Oklahoma City office is one of the Federal
Reserve Bank of Kansas City's branches.

The June 12 event was not a Kansas City Fed policy meeting. It was a
[House Financial Services Committee field hearing](https://financialservices.house.gov/calendar/eventsingle.aspx?EventID=411133)
held in Oklahoma City on June 12, 2026. Its four witnesses were:

- Thomas Hoenig, former Kansas City Fed president;
- Esther George, former Kansas City Fed president;
- Gary Kelly, deputy chair of the Dallas Fed board; and
- Benjamin Keen, a University of Oklahoma economist.

The hearing is evidence of congressional and witness framing. Statements by
members or former officials are not current Board, FOMC or Reserve Bank policy.

### New York's two different roles must not be collapsed

The New York Fed:

1. runs the Open Market Trading Desk as agent for the FOMC and all Reserve
   Banks;
2. operates accounts and services for foreign official and international
   institutions; and
3. performs certain securities and market-infrastructure functions.

That does not make New York the current enterprise manager of Fedwire Funds,
Fedwire Securities, National Settlement Service, FedACH or FedNow. The twelve
Banks are the legal service operators and Federal Reserve Financial Services
is the enterprise business organization. National IT and particular Banks may
host or support the infrastructure.

## III. Dallas — the intelligence and architecture node

### A. What Dallas says its staff spend time doing

At the June 3 UTEP event, Dallas regional executive Arturo Barrio described
five System functions:

1. monetary policy;
2. financial stability;
3. supervision and regulation;
4. a safe and efficient payment system; and
5. consumer protection and community development.

Lorie Logan then described the staff production process at approximately
32:45 in the locally preserved auto-caption transcript:

- collect data;
- conduct surveys;
- host roundtables;
- call business and community leaders;
- perform independent economic research;
- publish what staff learn;
- combine regional evidence with national data; and
- feed the resulting analysis into monetary policy, payment-system,
  supervision and financial-stability decisions.

She identified recurring energy, banking, manufacturing and service-sector
surveys. She also described high-frequency data investments made during the
pandemic and Dallas research on inflation, tariffs, the border, energy and
trade.

The [official prepared remarks](https://www.dallasfed.org/news/speeches/logan/2026/lkl260603)
corroborate the institutional claim: Dallas's regional executives maintain the
relationships that inform policymakers; the System has 12 Reserve Banks and 24
branches; and the El Paso branch now supplies intelligence about trade,
migration and energy. The official page does not contain the entire moderated
conversation, so the detailed staff-work description remains transcript
evidence from an automatic caption track.

**What this answers:** regional research is not described as grant funding
passed to an outside arm. Dallas describes an in-house collection, modeling,
publication and policy-support operation, supplemented by directors, councils,
universities, firms and community contacts.

**What it does not answer:** the transcript provides no project-level cost,
staff-hour ledger, contractor list or proof that each research program changes
a decision.

### B. Dallas's design language is much more specific than “set rates”

On April 2, Logan described the Federal Reserve as a bank with approximately
$6,700,000,000,000 of assets, including liabilities of approximately
$2,400,000,000,000 of currency, $3,000,000,000,000 of bank reserves and a
Treasury General Account approaching $1,000,000,000,000. She argued that the
Fed should reduce banks' *need* for reserves rather than make reserves scarce.

Her proposed operating path included:

- more usable discount-window access;
- more usable intraday credit;
- more usable standing repo operations;
- simplified collateral pledging and valuation across all 12 Banks;
- interoperability with the Federal Home Loan Banks;
- a second daily New York standing repo operation;
- removal of the aggregate standing-repo cap;
- possible central clearing of standing repo operations; and
- possible daily discount-window auctions.

Those are institutional-design questions about access, collateral, settlement,
liquidity and balance-sheet size. They are not merely an interest-rate forecast.
See Logan's [April 2 remarks](https://www.dallasfed.org/news/speeches/logan/2026/lkl260402).

On July 9, Logan said market “plumbing matters.” She noted that the SEC's
Treasury-clearing mandate excludes Federal Reserve transactions and argued that
the FOMC could voluntarily clear its open-market operations centrally. She also
identified blockchain integration, faster collateral movement and cyber risk
as live design questions. See her
[July 9 remarks](https://www.dallasfed.org/news/speeches/logan/2026/lkl260709).

### C. Dallas storyline grade

| Existing storyline claim | Grade | Audit result |
|---|---|---|
| Dallas is the “repo-rails shop” | Supported as shorthand | Logan's public agenda repeatedly centers reserves, collateral, discount-window access, standing repo and central clearing. Dallas does not execute SOMA trades. |
| Dallas funds research performed mainly by outside arms | Not established | The transcript describes substantial in-house research and direct collection. Partnerships exist, but pass-through funding is not the stated model. |
| Dallas supplies policy design while New York supplies execution | Strongly supported | Logan proposes and frames operating changes; Perli and the Desk describe implementation readiness and current operations. |
| Dallas's regional story is mostly ceremonial | Contradicted by its account of work, but efficiency untested | Surveys, calls, roundtables and research feed several functions. The record does not establish their cost-effectiveness. |

## IV. Oklahoma City / Kansas City — the federated-system defense

### A. What the hearing says the regional Banks are for

The hearing's common defense of the 12-Bank structure had five parts:

1. **local information:** aggregate and backward-looking national data do not
   reproduce current regional context;
2. **crisis reach:** regional Banks can respond to community and regional
   institutions while New York and the Board focus on national and global
   markets;
3. **credit knowledge:** supervision and local banking relationships inform
   discount-window lending and credit-condition analysis;
4. **independent judgment:** separate presidents, research staffs and directors
   create debate and reduce a single Washington or New York view; and
5. **public trust:** directors, branches, councils and direct engagement create
   local access and institutional “buy-in.”

Hoenig testified that, as a Reserve Bank chief executive, he worked with
directors and other Bank presidents on operations, payments, information
technology, human resources and consolidation when needed. That is direct
evidence that the Bank network's cost is not only economics staff and FOMC
participation.

George said Reserve Bank directors contribute to operational oversight,
budget discipline and monetary-policy deliberations. She connected the
regional structure to financial services, supervision, discount-window
lending and crisis response.

Kelly described Dallas's core work as cash operations, payments, lending,
credit risk, bank supervision, research and regional intelligence. He also said
there are efficiency opportunities and that the work is underway.

Keen offered the most candid cost concession: a centralized institution could
be simpler and less costly, but would concentrate access, expertise and
perspective. His defense was a national network of research, education and
public engagement.

### B. The hearing identifies its own control risk

George said technology has allowed Reserve Banks to consolidate functions for
efficiency, but that consolidation can blur the line between:

- Board oversight;
- consolidated System operations; and
- the separate operating authority and accountability of each Reserve Bank.

That is one of the most important statements in the entire hearing. It matches
the accounting problem already found in the software and intercompany work:

> a function may be nationally controlled, hosted by one Bank, capitalized by
> another accounting entity, charged to all 12 Banks and supervised by the
> Board without a public application-level ownership and invoice map.

The transcript therefore supports both sides of the current inquiry:

- consolidation can reduce duplication; and
- consolidation can make responsibility harder to see.

### C. What the hearing says about funding

During questioning, George said the Fed publishes annual reports showing
portfolio revenue and cost recovery as a payment operator. She recalled that
it took approximately $6,000,000,000 to run the System, including Reserve Bank
support of the Board.

That is a witness recollection with an unspecified year and perimeter. It
should not replace the audited ledger. The combined Reserve Banks' 2025
statements recognized $9,800,000,000 of operating expenses and assessments.
That larger figure includes objects that may not have been inside George's
recalled definition. The two figures cannot be treated as a clean year-over-year
change.

The hearing also discussed a broader estimate of $6,000,000,000 to
$8,000,000,000 and contrasted it with a balance sheet once near
$9,000,000,000,000. Again, those are hearing statements, not audited
reconciliations.

The useful admission is narrower:

> **The System says portfolio earnings finance ordinary operations, priced
> payment services recover their costs, and reports—not congressional
> appropriations—provide the public operating-cost view.**

Our existing accounting audit adds the missing qualification: monetary
interest, payment-service fees, Treasury reimbursements, assessments,
inter-Bank allocations, capital spending and pension activity are different
objects and must not be added as though every line were new external operating
spending.

### D. The hearing's payment position

The late hearing discussion framed the Fed's payment role as:

- broad access;
- security and safety;
- efficiency; and
- preservation of trust in money.

The witnesses did not provide a rule-level answer for digital-asset finality,
privacy, master-account eligibility or the boundary between FedNow and a
central bank digital currency. Davidson alleged continuing CBDC-related work;
George said she was not aware of such a program. The exchange proves dispute,
not the allegation.

The controlling legal and chronology follow-through is
Oklahoma City Fed Structure Hearing — CBDC and §11(h) Audit.
It establishes that the enacted Federal Reserve Act §16A prohibition postdated
the hearing, while the House text predated it, and that the public project
record does not establish a present §11(h) forfeiture case.

The hearing therefore supplies institutional philosophy but does not resolve
the payment-stack questions in the existing control-and-finality audit.

### E. Oklahoma/Kansas City storyline grade

| Existing storyline claim | Grade | Audit result |
|---|---|---|
| Oklahoma/Kansas City is the “gate” | Partly supported | The Tenth District is a concrete account-access gate through Kansas City's Bank-level decisions; the hearing itself focuses more broadly on regional governance and legitimacy. |
| The hearing was a defense of the 12-Bank model | Strongly supported | All four witnesses defended it, while acknowledging cost and possible efficiency gains. |
| The regional structure exists only to gather data | Contradicted | Witnesses described supervision, lending, payments, IT, cash, operational oversight, research and crisis response. |
| Congress is preparing to abolish the regional Banks | Not supported by this record | This hearing argued for preservation and improvement, not abolition. |
| Consolidation is erasing Reserve Bank independence | Plausible risk, not proved outcome | George expressly warned about blurred boundaries; no complete function-by-function control transfer was produced. |

## V. New York — the implementation and market-technology node

### A. The July 9 operating record

The [July 9 workshop](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/events/markets/2026/0709-2026)
was held at the New York Fed and organized with the University of Chicago
Booth School of Business. Its agenda joined payments, liquidity, leverage,
clearing and settlement. Dallas's Logan moderated the panel containing New
York's SOMA manager, Roberto Perli.

Perli's [prepared remarks](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2026/per260709)
show the division of labor:

- the FOMC determines the operating framework;
- the New York Desk executes reserve-management and market operations;
- the Desk says it is prepared to implement any balance-sheet or rate-control
  framework changes the Committee adopts;
- the Desk monitors the market structure in which those operations occur; and
- staff adapt transaction methods as market structure changes.

This is the clearest current evidence for the “implementation machine”
storyline.

### B. Central clearing is being evaluated for the Fed itself

Perli reported that money-market-fund repo volume cleared through FICC “well
exceeds” $1,000,000,000,000 and makes up more than half of money-market-fund
Treasury repo positions. He said the Fed's standing repo operations are not
currently centrally cleared.

He identified a specific reason to consider changing that: central clearing
can permit more netting and reduce dealer balance-sheet costs, potentially
improving participation and the standing repo operation's ability to contain
funding pressure. He also said Fed staff continue to evaluate the benefits and
costs.

This is an evaluation clock, not an authorization or implementation clock:

| Object | Status on July 9, 2026 |
|---|---|
| private Treasury repo migration to expanded clearing | underway |
| standing repo operations centrally cleared | not current |
| staff evaluation | current |
| FOMC decision to clear standing repo operations | not established |
| production launch | not established |

Logan's position that the FOMC *could* voluntarily clear its operations is an
individual policymaker's proposal. Perli's statement that staff are evaluating
the option is operating evidence. Neither proves adoption.

### C. Tokenization is research plus toolkit development—not production policy

Perli described tokenized repo as nascent and currently associated in part with
intraday and intracompany transactions. He gave two opposed reserve-demand
paths:

- instant collateral and a deeper intraday repo market could reduce frictions
  and banks' reserve demand; but
- continuous instant payments can increase gross flows, reduce netting and
  increase demand for precautionary reserves.

New York Innovation Center and Markets Group staff have also worked on:

- Project Cedar, concerning tokenization and market structure; and
- Project Pine, a smart-contract toolkit for central-bank monetary operations.

These projects are evidence of research, prototypes and institutional
capability. They are not evidence that the Federal Reserve has launched a
tokenized repo facility, a retail central bank digital currency or autonomous
smart-contract monetary policy.

### D. New York storyline grade

| Existing storyline claim | Grade | Audit result |
|---|---|---|
| New York “builds the machine” | Supported for SOMA and market-operation capability | The Desk executes and says it can implement FOMC framework changes. |
| New York owns the payment system | Incorrect | Enterprise payment control is distributed across the 12 Banks, FRFS, national IT, host Banks and vendors. |
| Tokenized repo is already a Fed operating rail | Contradicted | Perli called the market nascent and described research and future implications. |
| Fed central clearing is already decided | Contradicted | Current standing repo operations are uncleared; evaluation is active; adoption is not established. |
| New York's research is detached from operations | Contradicted | Its Innovation Center sits in the Markets Group and its projects explicitly examine monetary-operation tools, but production status remains separate. |

## VI. The actual institutional story these records tell

The System's story is not “we need billions of dollars to decide one interest
rate.” It is:

1. maintain a geographically distributed intelligence and trust network;
2. supervise banks and understand credit conditions;
3. supply cash, accounts, payments, settlement and emergency liquidity;
4. convert regional, market and supervisory information into national policy;
5. implement that policy through the New York Desk and Reserve Bank balance
   sheets;
6. maintain the technology, collateral, counterparty, legal, cyber and
   accounting infrastructure under the services; and
7. experiment with market changes before deciding whether to alter the
   operating framework.

The transcript record explains why the staff and technology footprint is much
larger than the public image of “rates and printing.” It does **not** prove
that the footprint is efficient, that every program is necessary or that the
public can trace every dollar to an output.

## VII. What the transcripts add to the payment-stack audit

### 1. Research can be upstream of control

Regional research is not merely commentary. Dallas explicitly says its
intelligence informs payment-system, supervision and financial-stability
decisions. New York's market research informs possible changes to how the Desk
transacts. A research program can therefore be upstream of:

- account-access policy;
- collateral eligibility and valuation;
- operation timing;
- clearing design;
- liquidity-facility participation;
- reserve demand; and
- software-development priorities.

That makes the research-to-decision chain an audit object.

### 2. Central clearing does not resolve the finality question

The July 9 discussion focuses on netting, balance-sheet cost and participation.
It does not establish:

- the authoritative securities ledger;
- the central-bank cash leg;
- settlement timing outside Fedwire hours;
- default liquidity during the clock gap;
- the clearing bank or backup bank;
- which entity would contract for, capitalize and operate the new interface; or
- how its costs would be allocated.

Those questions remain in the payment-stack control-and-finality audit.

### 3. Tokenization can move the reserve-demand problem rather than eliminate it

Perli's two-sided analysis is important. Faster collateral movement can reduce
liquidity friction, while continuous gross settlement can increase liquidity
needs. Therefore:

> **“Tokenized” and “instant” do not automatically mean a smaller Federal
> Reserve balance sheet or lower operating cost. The result depends on netting,
> collateral, operating hours, legal finality and access to central-bank
> liquidity.**

### 4. Consolidation creates a governance audit

The Oklahoma hearing's warning about consolidated functions matches the
payment audit's application-ownership gap. For every material System
application, the desired ledger remains:

| Control field | Question |
|---|---|
| legal operator | Which Reserve Bank or federal entity legally provides the service? |
| policy controller | Board, FOMC, FRFS, a Reserve Bank or another committee? |
| business owner | Which office sets requirements and approves changes? |
| technical owner | Which Bank, national-IT function or vendor operates the code? |
| asset owner | Which books contain the capitalized software or equipment? |
| contract payer | Which entity signs and pays the external invoice? |
| allocation recipients | Which Banks, principals or customers ultimately bear cost? |
| authoritative record | Which system controls finality and corrections? |

The speeches explain why the systems exist. They do not fill this ledger.

## VIII. Storyline synthesis — supported facts versus interpretation

### Established

- The System is deliberately federated across a Board, 12 Reserve Banks and 24
  branches.
- Regional Banks collect direct intelligence, perform research, supervise,
  lend, operate services and participate in national policy.
- Dallas is publicly developing detailed views about reserves, collateral,
  discount-window access, repo and central clearing.
- The New York Desk implements FOMC market operations and is evaluating
  evolving repo-market structure.
- Standing repo operations were not centrally cleared as of July 9, 2026.
- New York staff have performed tokenization and smart-contract research.
- Technology-enabled consolidation can create efficiency and blur
  responsibility; a former Kansas City president expressly identified that
  tension.

### Supported interpretation

- Dallas is functioning as a visible design shop for liquidity architecture
  while New York is the principal execution node for that architecture.
- The political value of the regional Banks is not only information. It is
  also legitimacy, distributed access and resistance to concentration.
- Research, software and payment infrastructure form one control chain rather
  than three unrelated expense categories.

### Not established

- a formal System plan to strip the Fed down to a settlement utility;
- a decision to clear Federal Reserve open-market or standing repo operations
  centrally;
- a live Federal Reserve tokenized repo facility;
- a production central bank digital currency;
- a public project-level ledger showing which research changed which policy;
- a complete application-level owner, vendor, asset and cost-allocation map;
  or
- proof that a 12-Bank structure is more cost-effective than a centralized
  alternative.

## IX. Questions this pass raises

### Highest-priority control questions

1. Which committee or office owns the current central-clearing evaluation for
   standing repo operations, and what decision gate would place it before the
   FOMC?
2. Which Reserve Bank or shared-service organization would contract for,
   capitalize and operate the FICC interface?
3. Would a centrally cleared standing repo transaction settle over Fedwire
   Funds, National Settlement Service, clearing-bank books or another cash
   route?
4. Which securities and cash clocks would apply when FICC is open but Fedwire
   Securities is not?
5. Would central clearing broaden effective access to Fed liquidity through
   sponsors or agent clearers without granting new master accounts?
6. Which New York Innovation Center or Markets Group code, cloud services,
   consultants and staff costs are capitalized versus expensed, and on whose
   books?

### Highest-priority accountability questions

7. For each Reserve Bank research center, what are the annual staff, vendor,
   data and event costs?
8. Which published policy memo, FOMC briefing, rule, product decision or
   supervisory change cites each center's work?
9. Which operational functions have been consolidated since 2022, which Bank
   hosted them before and after consolidation, and how did the allocation key
   change?
10. What output and service-level measures accompany the System's claimed
    efficiency initiatives?
11. When a Reserve Bank director provides “budget discipline,” what records
    show the challenge, revision or rejection of a budget or capital proposal?
12. Can the System reconcile the witness estimate of approximately
    $6,000,000,000 to the audited $9,800,000,000 of 2025 Reserve Bank operating
    expenses and assessments by entity and expense object?

### Falsifiers for the current storyline

The Dallas-design/New York-execution reading should be revised if:

- the FOMC or Board assigns the architecture lead elsewhere;
- an official record shows Dallas has no material role beyond public
  commentary;
- FRFS or another Reserve Bank—not New York—controls the proposed central-
  clearing implementation;
- tokenized-operation work is discontinued or remains isolated from policy
  and market-operation systems; or
- function-level records show regional research and branch intelligence have
  no identifiable decision or service outputs.

## XI. Compact conclusion

The recent records do not show a simple central bank. They show a chain:

> **regional observation and legitimacy → research and design → FOMC
> authorization → New York market execution → shared payment and technology
> operations → private clearing, bank and market response.**

That chain explains much of what the institution says it spends time on. It
also gives us a better audit target than “Why does setting rates cost so
much?”:

> **Which entity controls each link, what output does it produce, whose books
> hold the software and property, which counterparties receive the money, and
> where does legal and cash finality actually occur?**
