Research summary

The IAC is a Dodd-Frank §911 advice body, not a rulemaker, but its recommendations and the Commission's public engagement with them are useful policy-development signals. The March 12 tokenization record is the strongest current chain: Peirce tested the draft's exact provisions before the September 17 Commission order carried several similar constraints. The order does not cite the IAC, so this is direct engagement plus substantive convergence, not proven formal uptake. March and September discussions also contemplated a less document-bound, more machine-readable EDGAR architecture, but EBO owns the system answer and the full Commission owns any binding disclosure change. The joined EDGAR signal analysis places those discussions beside the Form N-PORT data-boundary failure, current support contract, OIG workflow findings and FDTA standards; together they support a provisional regulatory-data-plane hypothesis, not a disclosed Commission design. Citation, engagement, convergence, legal effect, system deployment and operating implementation remain separate clocks.
