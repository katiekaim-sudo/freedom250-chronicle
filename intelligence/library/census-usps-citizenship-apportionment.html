Postal workers helped run a Census test; no citizenship database shown

The 2026 Census test used postal workers as door-to-door counters in two cities and ran an online form that asks citizenship. The public record does not show postal workers asking citizenship or any joined enforcement database, and results are not yet out.

What we found

  • Census cut the test from six sites to Huntsville and Spartanburg on Feb 2, 2026; GAO says 10 of 19 planned activities were removed or reduced. Online response began May 1, door-to-door follow-up June 1, and Census says the test ended Aug 31.
  • The test covered about 154,600 housing units with about 25 postal and 25 regular Census takers per site. In Huntsville postal workers took a separate Census job after hours at $19.75 an hour; in Spartanburg they counted on their own delivery routes during postal work, on postal pay.
  • The online form uses American Community Survey content, including citizenship. The shorter in-person form (D6-QE-IFE) is not public, so it is not shown that postal workers ask about citizenship.
  • Census and USPS signed an interagency agreement that has not been published; a 2018 postal pilot was canceled because the agencies could not settle the legal conflicts. The governing records notice allows no routine uses, so it gives no path to share responses with DHS, ICE or election offices.
  • The approved cost estimate is $5 million, not counting IT and headquarters. The Commerce Secretary's Aug 11 claim of billions in savings has no numbers behind it in the public record.

Why it matters

It shows a possible change to the 2030 count being tested through normal procedure inside the existing Census system. So far the record supports a new workforce channel, not a merged citizenship-enforcement system.

What to watch

  • pending · Published test results on response, cost, quality, productivity and mail disruption.
  • summer 2027 · Census Operational Plan Baseline 2, the first 2030 design decision after the test.
  • 2028-03-30 · Proposed census questions due to Congress, which will show what the 2030 form asks.
