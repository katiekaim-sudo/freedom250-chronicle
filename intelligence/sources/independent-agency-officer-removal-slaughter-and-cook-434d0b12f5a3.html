Independent-Agency Officer Removal — Slaughter and Cook

Updated: 2026-08-15
Status: factual legal map
Rule: Career conversion is not officer removal. FTC is not the Fed. A stay denial is not a merits judgment on “cause.” Humphrey’s does not save the modern FTC.

This note is the officer leash. Schedule Policy-Career — Personnel and Removal Map is the career leash. Judicial Money Map — Source Document JM-01 and JM-02 are the docket. Do not rewrite them.

Short answer

Two opinions, same day, two creatures.

FTC officers. Trump v. Slaughter, No. 25-332 (June 29, 2026): the for-cause removal sentence in 15 U.S.C. § 41 is contrary to the separation of powers. The President fired Commissioners Slaughter and Bedoya without identifying a statutory cause. The District Court enjoined. The Supreme Court reversed and remanded. Roberts, C.J., joined by Alito, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, and Barrett; Thomas joined all but Part III–B. Sotomayor dissented, joined by Kagan and Jackson. 6–3 on the judgment.

If anything remains of Humphrey’s Executor v. United States, 295 U.S. 602 (1935), “the Court overrules it.” The modern FTC “unquestionably exercises executive power and must therefore be controlled by the Chief Executive.” Officers who fall within the President’s “general administrative control” are removable at will.

Fed Governors. Trump v. Cook, No. 25A312 (June 29, 2026): the Government’s application to stay the preliminary injunction that kept Governor Lisa Cook in her seat is denied. Roberts, C.J., joined by Sotomayor, Kagan, Kavanaugh, and Jackson. Thomas dissented. Alito, joined by Gorsuch, dissented. Barrett dissented. 5–4 on the stay.

12 U.S.C. § 242: Governors serve fourteen-year terms and “may be removed only for cause.” The Court will not read that into at-will employment. “Cause” is reviewable. It must imply unfitness for the Governor’s professional duties, not any pretext. A fixed term removable only for cause carries notice and some opportunity to respond before termination. The President’s August 2025 letter did not provide that. The Court does not decide whether the mortgage allegations are statutory cause after lawful process. The injunction stays. Merits remain below.

Slaughter stays removed. The Court: the President “was entitled to cut her tenure short.” The restoration injunction is reversed. Judgment issued July 31, 2026. She does not return by this judgment. Bedoya was also fired; he is not the respondent.

Do not collapse. Slaughter is the FTC statute. Cook is a stay on a Fed Governor. Slaughter Part III–B (Thomas did not join) leaves the Federal Reserve, “to the extent it follows in the tradition of the First and Second Banks,” as an example that may sit outside Myers’s general rule, and leaves non-Article III court tenure “for another day.” It does not “determine the fate of officials not before us.”

3. What Slaughter held

Cite: Donald J. Trump, President of the United States, et al. v. Rebecca Kelly Slaughter, No. 25-332 (June 29, 2026). Certiorari before judgment to the D.C. Circuit. Cite as 609 U.S. ___ (2026). Official slip (docket link): https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25-332_new_geil.pdf (the 25-322_new_4gcj.pdf filename still served and is the same file).

Facts (syllabus). Soon after January 2025 the President fired Commissioners Slaughter and Alvaro Bedoya. He did not identify a statutory cause. He said their continued service was inconsistent with Administration priorities and that they were removed pursuant to Article II. The District Court granted Slaughter summary judgment, declared the removal ultra vires, and permanently enjoined interference with her right to perform her duties. This Court had stayed that order and granted certiorari before judgment.

Held (syllabus): “The FTC’s for-cause removal provision is contrary to the separation of powers enshrined in the Constitution.” Reversed and remanded.

Load-bearing cuts:

  • Article II vests the executive power in one President. Officers who derive their offices from him and remain subject to his superintendence must be removable by him.
  • Myers v. United States, 272 U.S. 52, is the rule: the President may remove subordinates at will.
  • Humphrey’s distinguished Myers by calling the 1935 FTC “quasi-judicial and quasi-legislative” and “no part of the executive power.” That framework “has not withstood the test of time.” It was tethered to a “highly circumscribed view of the FTC’s role.” Later cases (Morrison, Free Enterprise Fund, Seila Law) put more functions on the executive side. “If anything more is left of Humphrey’s, the Court overrules it.”
  • The present FTC “enforces and administers some 80 statutes,” promulgates rules carrying the force of law, investigates, adjudicates, and sues. It “unquestionably exercises executive power.”
  • The Court “has no occasion today to define the bounds of what such power entails.” Not every office Congress creates necessarily comes with executive power (Buckley). The Federal Reserve is named as an example that may sit outside Myers “to the extent it follows in the tradition of the First and Second Banks.” Tenure of non-Article III courts “is not presented or briefed.” Left for another day.

What the judgment does not do. It does not walk SEC, NLRB, FCC, CFTC, or MSPB. Those chairs appear in the dissent or as implication. A citation is not a holding. It does not decide Cook’s merits. It does not convert career staff. It does not repeal 15 U.S.C. § 41’s existence as an agency — it knocks out the removal restriction. Wiener (1958) is named as the only later Humphrey’s application (a solely adjudicatory war-claims body) and is not reaffirmed as a living exception for today’s commissions.

4. What Cook held

Cite: Donald J. Trump, President of the United States v. Lisa D. Cook, Member of the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, et al., No. 25A312 (June 29, 2026). On application for stay. Cite as 609 U.S. ___ (2026). Slip: https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a312_5468.pdf

Facts (syllabus). Fourteen-year term set to expire in 2038. August 2025: FHFA Director’s public letter accused Cook of mortgage fraud; the President posted that Cook must resign; a later letter purported to fire her for cause (“reason to believe” false statements on mortgage agreements; lack of confidence; “faithfully executing the law requires immediate removal”). District Court preliminary injunction. D.C. Circuit declined to stay. Government applied here.

Held: application denied.

  • The Government has not shown it is likely to prevail. Reading § 242 as at-will would be “an interpretive leap out of step with the statute Congress enacted and our Nation’s tradition of central banking.”
  • Whether a Governor should be “removed for cause” is a decision only the President can make (short of impeachment). That does not mean he may make it for any reason, or no reason. Courts independently interpret the statute.
  • “Cause” is a term of art against the common-law backdrop. It must reflect the Fed’s unique status. The key: whether the cause assigned truly implies unfitness for the office — or is a pretext for a more congenial replacement. The Court rejects both a “very low bar” (Government) and Cook’s “very high bar.”
  • Equity may protect the incumbent pending the contest. The PI does that.
  • Independently: Cook was entitled to notice and some opportunity to respond before termination. Not a “full-blown judicial trial.” “The right to support his allegations by argument however brief, and, if need be, by proof, however informal.” Londoner. The August letter did not do that. Without the statutory process, the Court need not reach Cook’s constitutional due-process claim, and it is unlikely the Government prevails on the procedures used.

What Cook does not do. It does not decide whether the mortgage allegations are cause after a lawful process. “We have not addressed the facts, as they have yet to be found.” It does not say Reserve Banks are federal agencies. Footnote 6: upholding the Fed “as currently structured and with its existing enforcement authorities” does not say Congress may later pile on “additional regulatory powers that are attenuated from monetary policy.” Social-media “Cook must resign, now!!!” is not the required notice. A later removal after lawful process is not forbidden. JM-01 already says that.

7. Entity Theory read

The federal group is a stack of persons. Control is not ownership. Three removal clocks stay apart.

Congress still creates the FTC and the Board of Governors. Creation is not insulation. For the FTC, the Court read Article II to mean the President holds the officer leash because the Commission now does the President’s work: rules, investigations, prosecutions. Humphrey’s had described a different animal. Treating 1935’s words as if they still named 2026’s agency was the error.

For the Fed, the Court read a different charter tradition — First Bank, Second Bank, the 1913/1933/1935 System — and a statute that says “for cause” in a setting where even the appearance of independence is the design. That is not a finding that every Board regulation is extra-constitutional. It is a finding that this office, on this stay record, is not the FTC.

Career Policy/Career is a third clock: the President excepts a position, not a Senate-confirmed officer. Do not let a Schedule F slogan impersonate Slaughter, or Slaughter impersonate a RIF.
