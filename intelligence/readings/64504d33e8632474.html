NYSE Arca Extended-Hours Transition — Factual Timeline
Research owner: fed-clearing-treasury
Evidence cutoff: September 25, 2026 ET
Object: the move from an August 2024 overnight-market failure signal to approved-but-not-yet-operating NYSE Arca 23/5 trading
Boundary: this is a factual infrastructure chronology. It supplies no astrology, convergence, causal, or Forecast Ledger credit.
Short ruling
NYSE Arca's extended-hours transition is not one event and was not operating at
the February 1, 2026 factual checkpoint.
The sequence is:
market shock and private-venue uncertainty → exchange proposal → SEC permission → clearing and market-data dependency work → revised 23/5 rule architecture → final readiness filing → operation
The reviewed record establishes proposal and permission, then exposes the
shared infrastructure required for operation. It does not permit “SEC approval”
to be narrated as a live 23/5 market.

August 5, 2024
motivating shock, retrospectively attributed
At the June 25, 2026 New York Fed Innovation Conference, the NYSE president identified the August 5, 2024 Nikkei shock and uncertainty or invalidation on overnight venues as the reason NYSE moved toward longer regulated hours and clearing support.
The host supplied no formal transcript. The local STT is discovery evidence, not verbatim quotation authority; the operator's retrospective account is not an SEC finding.
October 25, 2024
proposal and filing
NYSE announced a 22-hour design, and NYSE Arca filed SR-NYSEARCA-2024-89 with the SEC the same day.
Announcement and filing were not approval or operation.
February 11, 2025
SEC permission
SEC Release 34-102400 approved temporary Rule 7.34-E(T), allowing a future 22-hour session design.
The order expressly conditioned commencement on Equity Data Plan readiness and a later NYSE Arca rule filing confirming readiness.
March 18, 2025
clearing plan
DTCC announced that NSCC would target 24x5 clearing in Q2 2026, subject to regulatory review and necessary rule changes.
A target and plan were not a production receipt.
May 6, 2025
consolidated-data proposal
Two days after the May 4 factual checkpoint, the SIP operating committees announced a planned amendment for near-24-hour data service. They identified prerequisites including DTCC clearing, technical ability to disseminate on- and off-exchange quotes and trades, and listing-market support.
The announcement was not SEC approval, technical readiness, or activation.
January 28, 2026
public dependency review
SEC Trading and Markets Director Jamie Selway said NYSE Arca had approval, while emphasizing that foundational common-protocol and shared-infrastructure questions still had to be answered. He described SIP end-of-2026 proposals and NSCC's June readiness expectation as future dependencies.
The speech, given in an official capacity but expressly not necessarily representing the Commission, did not activate extended trading or establish that every dependency was complete.
February 1, 2026
factual checkpoint
Permission existed; shared data, clearing, trade-reporting, corporate-action and operating-readiness work remained live.
No reviewed source identifies this date as launch, approval, filing, or operational cutover.
May 12 / May 21, 2026
revised 23/5 architecture
NYSE Arca filed, and the SEC published, an immediately effective revision creating an Overnight Trading Session and changing the design from 22 hours to 23/5. The filing retained the requirement for Equity Data Plan notice and another rule filing before activation.
Immediate effectiveness of the rule revision was not immediate operation.
June 25, 2026
operator retrospective and target
In the New York Fed-hosted discussion, the NYSE president linked the project to the August 2024 shock and described a December 2026 go-live target in traditional infrastructure.
A conference statement about a future target is not a launch receipt. The local transcript requires replay before quotation.
December 6, 2026
projected industry launch
The May filing names December 6 as the industry projected readiness date.
Future as of this evidence cutoff; not treated as achieved.
The February 2025 permission contained its own operating gate
The SEC approval authorized the temporary rule architecture but preserved a
separate commencement condition. Before NYSE Arca could use the longer hours,
the Equity Data Plans had to establish an equivalent mechanism for collecting,
consolidating, processing and disseminating quotes and trades, notify the
exchange that they were ready, and be followed by another exchange filing
confirming readiness.
That creates four different states:
exchange design — the operator specifies proposed hours and controls;
regulatory permission — the SEC approves the rule architecture;
shared-infrastructure readiness — data, clearing, reporting, halt and
corporate-action dependencies can support the longer day; and
operation — the exchange actually activates the new sessions.
The February 1, 2026 checkpoint sits between states 2 and 3, not at state 4.
The May 2025 data announcement identifies the real counterparty system
The May 6 SIP announcement is more than a technical footnote. It shows why no
single exchange can create a national 23/5 equities market by extending its own
matching engine.
The operating stack includes:
exchange session → protected quotes and trades → consolidated processors → off-exchange reporting → central counterparty clearing → listing-market symbols and corporate actions → participant books and controls
The SIP operating committees named DTCC clearing, continuous quote/trade
dissemination, off-exchange reporting and listing-market support as
preconditions. The May 2026 NYSE Arca filing added order eligibility, erroneous
trade review, risk controls, regulatory halts and circuit-breaker work.
Thus “market access” is a multi-party operating constitution, not merely a
longer clock on an exchange website.
