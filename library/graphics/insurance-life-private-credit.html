Freedom 250 · Banking and the dollar · Insurance

How do life insurers' promises turn into private credit and offshore reinsurance?

Policy and annuity money becomes long-dated liabilities that insurers invest: long promises supply investable general-account liabilities, and insurer asset allocation directs trillions into bonds, mortgages, structured assets and alternatives, including through a private credit and structured-asset channel. The issuing life insurer can then cede liabilities and economics to an asset-intensive reinsurer, secured by a trust, funds withheld or collateral. The NAIC's September 2026 analysis estimated $1.210 trillion of private-credit exposure at year-end 2025 after removing identifiable overlaps. The annual report says total ceded premium increased 32.8% to $580.5 billion and assumed premium increased 48.1% to $272.5 billion. Treasury convened state regulators in 2026 specifically around private credit, offshore reserve movement, RBC, private letter ratings and evolving business models. The hub's limit: Offshore location, reciprocal-jurisdiction status or large ceded reserve does not alone prove weakened security or insolvency.

What's still uncertain

  • It does not show an industry-wide solvency break.
  • Private placement, private credit, Schedule BA, ABS and affiliated investment are not identical measures or proof of impairment.
  • Offshore location, reciprocal-jurisdiction status or large ceded reserve does not alone prove weakened security or insolvency.
  • None of those records yet changes a named insurer's designation, capital, liquidity, affiliate transfer, pension obligation, claim, resolution or public payment.
  • That supports monitoring, not a declaration of hidden insolvency.
