# Currency Watch — Six-System Pilot Results

**State:** `SEALED-V1_1`  
**Evidence freeze:** 2026-07-20  
**Systems:** USD, EUR, CNY, JPY, SAR and ARS  
**Method:** fixed-rubric isolated blind cards; no composite

There is deliberately no row total, weighted average, league-table rank or
traffic-light conversion. The currency systems do different international
jobs, and a strong service lane cannot cancel a domestic or external failure.

`EUR L5 = NR` is a jurisdiction and coverage result. Article VIII restriction
and multiple-currency-practice states attach to the 21 sovereign IMF members,
not to a fictional euro-area member. The three frozen member subcards cannot be
averaged or generalized to the currency.

## What the pilot reveals

### The same currency can succeed in one job and strain another

- The USD remains orderly and fully functional in exchange, current payments
  and global service, while its persistent current-account and real-exchange-
  rate deviation produces an L4 `C`.
- The JPY still performs external-adjustment and systemic roles at `A` while
  domestic price-anchor performance is `C`. That difference is information,
  not an inconsistency to average away.
- The SAR's peg, domestic anchor and current-payment usability test strongly,
  but oil dependence and the adjustment burden keep external adjustment at
  `B` and underlying order at `B`. Its L6 `A` rests on documented domestic
  monetary service under the USD anchor; the cross-card audit identifies that
  perimeter as a prospective rubric defect.
- The EUR's common architecture performs well, but its legal/member topology
  prevents a currency-wide L5 call until every member record is covered.
- The CNY remains usable for current international payments in the evidence
  tested, but the domestic, financial, exchange, external and systemic-service
  lanes show material bounded impairments. Capital controls were not silently
  converted into an L5 penalty.
- The ARS card isolates the pilot's sharpest current impairments: weak domestic
  anchoring, severe external-adjustment failure and material current-payment
  friction. The IMF EFF program is a separate financing/program clock, not an
  automatic failing grade or a substitute for performance evidence.

### International role must be graded against the role actually carried

USD, EUR, JPY and SAR earn sealed L6 `A` calls for different reasons and at
different scales. The test is not reserve-share size alone. But the merge audit
found that the frozen rubric did not define consistently whether domestic-only
monetary service belongs in L6: SAR was rated from domestic-use evidence while
ARS received `NR` for missing cross-border-role evidence. CNY receives `C`
because the international service it does carry is materially constrained.
Both SAR and ARS calls remain immutable v1.1 results; the L6 perimeter must be
repaired prospectively before extension.

## Trade and tariff result

The trade-pressure lane worked as intended. The graders linked the frozen
tariff, export-control, customs and trade-agreement events, but did not move a
currency grade merely because an event existed.

Most importantly, `TP-US-2026-SECTION122` remained an unresolved event object
across the cards. Its statute, proclamation, customs implementation, court,
cash, price, trade and scheduled-expiry clocks remain separate. The June U.S.
import-price index was duty-exclusive, so it could not establish the landed
incidence of the surcharge. No card promoted a Section 122 causal transmission
into a lane grade.

Other event links were similarly bounded: the EU-U.S. tariff framework,
EU-Mercosur provisional application, EU CBAM, China-U.S. tariff suspension,
Chinese critical-mineral licensing, China-Japan dual-use controls, the
U.S.-Japan tariff framework, Argentine export-duty reductions and the signed-
but-not-implemented Argentina-U.S. object were not allowed to become automatic
monetary judgments.

## Decision

The six-lane architecture produced meaningful distinctions across a global
free float, a shared currency, a managed system, a free float under domestic
strain, a hard peg and a program-country stress case without requiring a
composite. It did **not** pass cleanly: nine cross-card controls passed and one
failed. L6's role perimeter is not yet cross-regime comparable.

The appropriate next move is not to revise sealed calls. It is to:

1. use the frozen prospective v1.2 L6 role perimeter for every new card while
   preserving all sealed v1.1 calls;
2. run the scheduled 2026-07-24 Section 122 event recheck without reopening
   grades absent a passed transmission test;
3. display v1.1 and v1.2 L6 states separately whenever the pilot is compared
   with later cards; and
4. extend the watch to the remaining G20 currencies using the same source
   freeze, blind-card and seal discipline.
