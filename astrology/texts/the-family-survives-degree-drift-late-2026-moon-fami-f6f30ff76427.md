## Late-2026 Moon-family controls

The Taurus, Gemini and December Cancer Full Moons form the best control group
for a question raised by the thirteen-family triage: does a Moon family stop
being coherent when its First-Quarter and Full-phase degrees are not especially
tight?

The answer is no. These three families have the widest quarter-to-Full degree
gaps in the 2026 set—3.471563°, 3.566885° and 2.916634°—yet all three perform
the same large spatial operation. Between First Quarter and Full phase, the
largest empty arc contracts by 116° to 132° while the ten-body occupied field
expands by the same amount. Each harvest is a registered **splay** chart.

That common operation is not a common storyline. The Moon lands in the tenth,
sixth and twelfth houses; the nodal paths move in three different directions;
the root structures differ; declination is absent in one Full chart and dense
in another; and literal visibility reverses the easy assumption that a hidden
house must mean an invisible Moon. The control therefore protects the method
from two opposite errors:

1. requiring a near-exact degree return before a Moon family can matter; and
2. treating one repeated chart-shape operation as if it gave three charts the
   same meaning.

## What the control changes in the method

These three families establish five operating rules:

1. **Do not require an exact degree return.** Tight coordinates are rich when
   present, but loose degree drift does not dissolve a Moon family.
2. **Name the spatial operation precisely.** Here the largest empty arc
   contracts while the occupied field expands; “distribution contraction” is
   misleading.
3. **Do not promote chart shape alone.** Three splay harvests contain three
   different house stories, root structures, nodal directions and declination
   fields.
4. **Keep Whole Sign stage and literal horizon separate.** A tenth-house Moon
   can be visibly high, a sixth-house Moon below the horizon, and a
   twelfth-house Moon visibly high.
5. **Treat threshold misses honestly.** Cancer's 0.262603° Neptune-to-lights
   relay is close and relevant, but it is not inside a 0.25° rule.

The result also clarifies the proper role of the First Quarter. It is not a
second Full-Moon reading and does not need to become a main character. It tells
us what structural work has already been done: Taurus installs the later
Saturn–Neptune mechanism, Gemini repeats the seed degree before handing
authority toward Venus, and Cancer installs the hidden lunar stage plus the
Neptune pressure that the Full chart later perfects.
