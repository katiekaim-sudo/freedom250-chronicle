The CFTC proposes a federal lane for leveraged retail crypto

The CFTC is testing a federal venue for leveraged retail crypto, while ordinary unleveraged spot trading still sits outside the proposed lane.

What we found

  • This is an Advanced Notice of Proposed Rulemaking, not a final rule or even an NPRM. OIRA cleared the preregulatory object on October 2 as Consistent with Change, and the CFTC announced it on October 5.
  • Regulation CTX would cover crypto retail commodity transactions reached by Commodity Exchange Act section 2(c)(2)(D): transactions offered to retail with leverage, margin or financing.
  • Regulation CAM would create a purpose-built subcategory of designated contract market registration for those transactions. The chairman says it would be an option; the CFTC cannot force ordinary unleveraged spot crypto onto its venues without Congress.
  • The main design fights are whether leverage can bring a whole order book inside the regime, whether a DCO must clear the financing, whether listing, lending, custody and settlement can stay vertically integrated, and which customer-property and insolvency rules apply.
  • There are no CAMs today. The control group is the DCM system: twenty-three core principles, self-regulatory surveillance and discipline, Part 40 rule and product filings, and optional FCM/IB/DCO layers. Bitnomial is the nearest spot-crypto analog—and its rules expressly say cleared spot collateral is not section 4d-segregated futures customer property.
  • The SEC-style audit scaffold is already frozen: source manifest, twelve-layer and thirty-provision stable-ID baseline, and build plan for a page atlas, question atlas, exact legal-text crosswalk, control/clock map, economic ledger, drafting audit, claim audit and proposal-to-final redline once the public text appears.
  • The full ANPRM text, docket, numbered questions, CFR parts and exact comment-close date were not public by the October 5 cutoff. The study freezes thirty fields for comparison with the public ANPRM, later NPRM, final rule and first operating venue.

Why it matters

These are the Commodity Futures Trading Commission's first named proposals to use its current retail-commodity and designated-contract-market powers for crypto after the CLARITY bill stalled. They start a formal rulemaking and show where the agency thinks current law reaches, but they are not final rules or a live market.

What to watch

  • pending Federal Register publication · Full AF80 ANPRM text, docket number, numbered questions, CFR parts and exact 60-day comment deadline
  • after comments · NPRM terms for asset eligibility, leverage, clearing, custody, segregation, vertical integration, surveillance, settlement, insolvency and state-law interaction
  • after any final rule · First CAM application and designation, approved rulebook, listed CTX, clearing and custody chain, customer account, trade, volume, failure and remedy receipt
