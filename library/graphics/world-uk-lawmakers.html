Freedom 250 · Foreign relations and world money · United Kingdom

Who can make law for each part of the UK and its territories?

The UK Parliament can make law for the whole UK ('Parliamentary sovereignty within the UK constitutional order'). It has handed law-making for devolved matters to three legislatures: the Scottish Parliament (Scotland Act 1998 as amended), Senedd Cymru (Government of Wales Act 2006 as amended) and the Northern Ireland Assembly (Northern Ireland Act 1998 and Agreement settlement). England has no legislature of its own on the map: it is 'predominantly governed through UK institutions, local government and devolved local arrangements'. The 3 Crown Dependencies (Bailiwick of Jersey, Bailiwick of Guernsey, Isle of Man) and 14 British Overseas Territories are not part of the UK. The Crown Dependencies are self-governing under island constitutional law (Jersey answers to its States Assembly, the Isle of Man to Tynwald), linked through the Crown, with the Privy Council handling 'Royal Assent and specified Crown functions'. Each Overseas Territory runs under its own constitution or Orders within the UK constitutional framework, with a Governor or Commissioner; the map does not name each territory's legislature.
