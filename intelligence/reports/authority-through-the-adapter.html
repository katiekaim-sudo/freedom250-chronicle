Authority Through the Adapter

A comparative deep dive into the old order's new machinery

Evidence cutoff: 2026-08-31T10:42:24-04:00 America/New_York
Status: current Workbench question-led synthesis; factual claims are source-routed; the cross-case judgment remains interpretive and unreviewed
Coverage: selected high-information monetary, securities, industrial-risk and public-administration cases; not a world census
Boundary: no claim of one coordinated plan, inevitable outcome, vault promotion, Currency Watch regrade or implementation authority

Ruling

The thesis holds across the strongest cases, but in a more exact form than “the old order survives.”

The emerging pattern is constitutional refactoring: transaction execution, interfaces and data movement are becoming more distributed, while the power to constitute the claim, certify the authoritative record, grant collateral value, supply final liquidity, correct failure and allocate loss remains with— or is being pulled more explicitly into—states, regulated issuers, central banks, clearing utilities and courts.

This is not mere digitization. Institutions are surrendering some routine handling and direct customer visibility. In exchange, they are making their root powers more explicit and machine-callable.

The old bundle looked like one institution doing many things. The new system separates them:

user interface / wallet / agent
  -> instruction and orchestration
  -> represented legal claim
  -> authoritative record
  -> eligibility and collateral recognition
  -> settlement asset and finality
  -> correction, suspension and recovery
  -> liquidity backstop and loss allocation

Different firms or systems can own every line. The constitutional question is not who moves the message fastest. It is who can make the movement legally operative—and who can undo it or absorb its failure.

Why Japan made the pattern visible

Japan places several versions of the architecture beside one another without pretending they are the same object.

Project Agorá: a shared platform that preserves the two-tier system

Project Agorá's July real-value test processed 30 transactions across 17 scenarios, totalling approximately CHF 800,000. It used tokenised central-bank reserves and commercial-bank deposits. That is a real movement beyond a pure simulation, but the institutional constitution stayed legible:

  • central banks issued and managed the tokenised reserves;
  • commercial banks issued and managed the tokenised deposits;
  • issuance was triggered through external RTGS or core-banking steps;
  • redemptions returned through those underlying systems;
  • bank and central-bank approvals, a contractual agreement, a runbook and fallback procedures governed the controlled environment.

Atomic execution therefore changed coordination inside the test. It did not erase the issuers, the external accounts, human approvals or the institutions responsible for fallback. BIS Project Agorá

Canton/JGB: move the representation, preserve the legal security

The MUFG-led JGB repo/DvP proof of concept synchronizes blockchain state with a JGB that remains in Japan's statutory book-entry transfer system. The chain can improve lifecycle coordination; it does not become a blockchain-native legal JGB merely because state is synchronized. The cash leg, completed transaction and production finality remain unresolved. MUFG release

DCP and JPYSC: competition occurs inside a regulated perimeter

DCP is comparing a lead-bank tokenised-deposit model, a deposit/stablecoin link and connection to existing settlement. The alternatives prove that the final architecture is not settled; they do not prove that bank liabilities or central-bank settlement have been abandoned. DCP release

JPYSC makes the other side visible. A trust-backed private yen token can gain a new distribution form while reserve custody, direct redemption, issuer duties and distributor access remain legally assigned. Public-chain mobility does not turn every holder into a direct claimant on the Bank of Japan. JPYSC issuer and reserve page

The August creation of dedicated FSA payment-services and crypto/stablecoin offices is consistent with the same move at the administrative layer: as the interface pluralizes, the state builds a more specialized supervisory interior. FSA reorganization

The global pattern is not uniform—but it rhymes precisely

SWIFT: distributed execution, retained bank authority

SWIFT's new blockchain-based ledger is a particularly clean test. The ledger uses an EVM-compatible, Hyperledger Besu-based architecture and validates and synchronizes interbank payment commitments. Yet SWIFT says it will operate the orchestration layer, banks retain control of assets and funding, and settlement remains off-ledger through existing infrastructures. In July, 17 banks were preparing controlled live pilots; final settlement still remained in existing systems.

The new object is therefore shared execution assurance—not a common bank, a new world currency or a transfer of each bank's balance sheet to SWIFT. SWIFT design description · July readiness release

DTC: the chain can move the entitlement, but DTC constitutes the record

DTC's authorized tokenization design is more subtle than “the blockchain is only a copy.” A participant can transfer a tokenized entitlement directly between registered wallets, and that transfer can move the participant-level security entitlement. But DTC's off-chain LedgerScan record is its official books and records; registered ownership remains in Cede & Co.; DTC approves the networks and protocols; supported protocols must permit distribution control and reversal; and tokenized entitlements initially receive no DTC collateral or settlement value. A participant must convert the token back to a traditional book-entry entitlement to use it in DTC's traditional end-of-day settlement.

This is a genuine operational migration inside an unchanged Article 8 and DTC constitution. It is neither “nothing changed” nor “DTC disappeared.” SEC no-action letter · DTC implementation FAQ

Eurosystem: the state is entering the new machine

Europe supplies the clearest correction to the idea that public authority will simply sit behind private token networks. Pontes links market DLT platforms to TARGET Services. At initial launch, finality for the cash leg remains in T2, eligibility is restricted to named regulated participant and operator classes, and the Eurosystem defines eligible assets. The longer path is more ambitious: the Eurosystem plans its own DLT platform, native tokenised central-bank money, smart-contract functionality and longer operating hours.

That is not the old state resisting a new rail. It is the monetary sovereign extending its settlement, collateral and liquidity perimeter into the rail. ECB Pontes design · ECB, “Central banks on-chain”

United Kingdom: tokenise the deposit, retain the deposit constitution

The Great British Tokenised Deposit project defines its object as a digital representation of traditional sterling commercial-bank money that retains the protections of conventional deposits. Seven banks participate, and the Bank of England Synchronisation Lab is testing atomic settlement in central-bank money. The innovation is programmability and cross-bank coordination; the claim remains a bank deposit and the final settlement experiment remains central-bank-facing. UK Finance

The cross-domain version

The pattern is strongest in money and securities because finality and title are explicit. It also appears in industrial risk and public administration, but those are analogues, not interchangeable proof.

Industrial finance: private execution, public permission and tail risk

Japan's August JBIC/NEXI gas-power agreements show how a state can steer a private build without becoming its EPC contractor or operator. Commercial lenders, sponsors and builders execute. JBIC lending and NEXI insurance change which obligations can enter regulated balance sheets and who absorbs defined loss. Signed instruments are real; drawdown, construction and operation remain separate clocks. JBIC Pennsylvania · JBIC Texas · NEXI

The same grammar appears in insurance generally: models, platforms and private underwriters can determine access and price, but regulators define admissible capital, public residual pools appear when private capacity retreats, and government-backed reinsurance can socialize strategic tail risk. The machinery is private-public; the failure constitution remains legally allocated.

Scientific administration: portable evidence, authority-specific gates

The federal Scientific Trust Stack points toward interoperable identity, attestation, provenance, AI-agent delegation and execution logs. But identity truth is not permission truth, and provenance is not scientific truth. Award, export, FOCI, data, facility and security decisions remain attached to their native authorities, with human adjudication, correction and appeal.

This is the administrative equivalent of tokenization: evidence becomes portable without silently transferring the power to decide what the evidence legally permits.

What is really moving

The evidence supports five distinct migrations. Calling all of them “decentralization” obscures more than it reveals.

Migration What moves What usually remains anchored
Interface migration wallet, app, API, agent, public chain account access, issuer duty, sanctions, remedy
Execution migration smart contract, orchestration, atomic workflow legal claim, finality rule, fallback
Record migration token or event becomes part of the authoritative state record-owner governance, correction, legal effect
Liquidity migration collateral moves faster or across venues eligibility, haircut, settlement asset, backstop
Loss migration private insurer, guarantor or protocol takes first loss receivership, public guarantee, court remedy, systemic tail

The strongest current evidence is for interface and execution migration. Record migration is real but bounded. Collateral migration is selective and eligibility-gated. Final liquidity and systemic loss remain the least migrated.

The deeper institutional trade

Incumbents are not keeping everything.

They may lose:

  • the customer-facing route;
  • some fee income;
  • control of operating hours;
  • first possession of transaction data;
  • bilateral privilege;
  • the ability to require every instruction to traverse their legacy system.

They are trying to retain or strengthen:

  • definition of the legal claim;
  • licensing and admission;
  • the conclusive or supervisory record;
  • collateral eligibility and valuation;
  • the final settlement asset;
  • correction, suspension and recovery;
  • emergency liquidity and loss allocation.

The result can be a smaller daily incumbent and a harder crisis incumbent. Private systems carry ordinary flow; public or regulated institutions become more decisive when par convertibility, title, liquidity or confidence breaks.

That is the political risk inside the efficiency story: profitable execution can migrate outward while catastrophic liability stays public.

What the thesis does not prove

It does not prove:

  • one global plan or one controlling actor;
  • that every pilot reaches production;
  • that states will win every contest with private networks;
  • that open protocols are cosmetically decentralized;
  • that legal authority guarantees operational competence;
  • that a backstop can intervene quickly enough in a 24/7 automated run;
  • that the public receives greater accountability merely because rules are machine-readable.

The same architecture can arise because every large system confronts the same questions: who owes, which record controls, what counts as collateral, when the obligation ends and who pays when it fails.

Bottom line

Japan is not an outlier. It is an unusually legible specimen of a broader transition.

The emerging struggle is not simply state versus market or bank versus crypto. It is a negotiation over which layer each actor can afford to lose.

The transaction layer is becoming competitive. The constitutional layer is being rewritten, but not abandoned. The decisive actors will be those that control the adapters between representation and legal effect:

token -> claim
event -> authoritative record
asset -> eligible collateral
instruction -> final settlement
failure -> corrected state
loss -> named balance sheet

The first serious failure—not the next launch—will show whether those adapters really work and whether the old authorities retained power or merely retained liability.
