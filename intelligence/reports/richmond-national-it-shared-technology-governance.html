Question: Is the Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond merely the physical host for shared Federal Reserve technology, or is it one of the Reserve Banks pursuing a distinct structural redesign through National IT and national procurement?

Short answer

Richmond is more than a server host, but less than a unilateral System technology sovereign.

The public record now supports a real Richmond corporate-governance chain for National IT:

System ITOC sets national technology direction and standards -> Richmond's National IT Committee reviews performance, risk, security, controls and budgets -> Richmond's president and System CIO manage the function -> the Richmond board approves the National IT budget and System-CIO personnel actions -> the Board of Governors and RBOPS retain supervisory, examination, budget and material-initiative gates.

Richmond also houses the National Procurement Office, which develops and executes certain contracts sourced collectively for multiple Reserve Banks and supports National IT. That makes Richmond a corporate, executive, procurement, security, infrastructure and accounting locus for the shared technology layer.

But different principals govern different objects. The Information Technology and Operations Committee (ITOC) sets System IT policy and standards. Federal Reserve Financial Services (FRFS) and the Chief Payments Executive define payment-service business requirements and oversee product/software development. Each Reserve Bank remains legally accountable for its own activities. The Board of Governors retains examination, budget, pricing, material-change and capital gates. Hosting, business control, legal operation, contracting, software control, accounting ownership and payment-account liability therefore cannot be collapsed into “Richmond owns the rails.”

The strongest current classification is:

Richmond is the Reserve Bank network's principal shared-technology corporate host and an important national-contract execution node, operating inside a federated constitution whose decisive inter-Bank terms remain nonpublic.

Why this changes the individual-Bank map

Earlier regional work correctly distinguished three visible structural emphases:

  • Kansas City has exposed the access gate, through the limited Tier 3 account and the Kraken/Payward lifecycle;
  • Dallas has exposed the market-plumbing and liquidity-design lane, through public work on Treasury-market structure, collateral and central clearing; and
  • New York remains the execution-and-market-infrastructure node, implementing FOMC directives and operating the SOMA Desk rather than independently setting national policy.

Richmond is the missing fourth type. It exposes the shared operating-control layer: who sets technology policy, hosts national infrastructure, approves its budget, manages the System CIO, signs collective contracts, allocates costs, responds to incidents and remains accountable when the twelve corporations act as one enterprise.

This is structural change through consolidation of operations, not a regional breakaway. Richmond's role helps explain how the Reserve Banks can retain twelve legal entities while centralizing technology and back-office execution.

Evidence and claim key

  • Disclosed: directly stated in an official current instrument or record.
  • Bounded inference: follows from disclosed institutional form but is not an operative delegation or contract term.
  • Not public: the reviewed official record did not disclose the object by the cutoff; this is not proof that the object does not exist.

The July 23, 2026 bylaws are a fresh control object

The live Richmond bylaws were amended and restated on July 23, 2026. They supersede the 2022 bylaws used in earlier workbench research and therefore reset the current local corporate-law baseline.

Five provisions matter for the National IT inquiry:

  1. the board exercises general control over Bank committees and officers;
  2. the president has general charge and control of Bank affairs, but the System CIO is expressly excluded from the president's ordinary unilateral authority to employ, dismiss and set compensation for Bank officers; and
  3. the president may execute—or delegate authority to execute—contracts in the Bank's name, supplying the local corporate-law path beneath NPO contracting;
  4. unless another charter supplies a different rule, a majority of an “other” board committee constitutes a quorum and members attending a quorate meeting may act by affirmative vote, which now supplies the default voting rule for the National IT Committee; and
  5. the board may create committees and receives committee reporting, allowing the National IT and Executive Committee charters to supply the operative intermediate review chain.

Article VIII separately allows the board to adopt resolutions assigning authority during a national emergency. That confirms a local emergency-delegation power, but no current National IT or payment-service emergency resolution was located; it does not answer ordinary incident command or application failover authority.

The supporting July 23 board resolution/minute is not yet public. Richmond says its minutes are released in six-month batches six months after the meetings, and no 2026 batch is listed at the cutoff. The amendment date is therefore an occurred instrument date; the undisclosed deliberation and vote must not be invented.

The current committee page lists the 2026 National IT Committee as Barbara Humpton (chair), Robert Blue, Halsey Cook, Jennifer LaClair and Lisa Lawson (ex officio). That is the current local-board roster only; it does not reveal ITOC membership or the participants in any particular committee vote.

National IT is national even though Richmond is the host

The phrase “Richmond-hosted” must be read institutionally, not geographically.

  • Richmond's current technology recruiting page calls the Bank the home of National IT while describing a nationwide workforce at other Reserve Banks and remote locations.
  • The Board describes Federal Reserve Information Technology (FRIT) as the portion of National IT at Richmond that supplies national infrastructure and business-line technology. FRIT and National IT are not exact synonyms.
  • The 2025 Fedwire disclosures describe geographically dispersed data centers, primary sites and tertiary failover capability without identifying their locations or operating Banks.
  • The Richmond National IT Committee charter itself describes a unified national organization whose people and operations exist inside and outside Richmond.

The host Bank supplies corporate legal capacity, board governance, executives, procurement and accounting. The networked workforce, contracts and committee principals prevent the host address from becoming proof of sole ownership.

The combined Reserve Bank statements eliminate reciprocal inter-Bank amounts. Richmond's reimbursement therefore proves a large national shared-service layer inside its books but cannot identify the ultimate payroll, vendor, cloud, application, data-center or security recipient. The needed bridge is the gross chargeback ledger plus the external accounts-payable and capital-asset schedules.

What Richmond can be shown to control

Disclosed Richmond control or execution:

  • corporate governance of the Richmond Bank and its National IT operation;
  • National IT budget review, local-board approval and recurring performance/risk oversight;
  • System-CIO appraisal and personnel/compensation routing through a multi-body concurrence process;
  • operation of shared technology infrastructure and technical incident work;
  • execution of certain multi-Bank procurement contracts through NPO; and
  • accounting and redistribution of large shared computing/support costs.

Disclosed shared or externally gated authority:

  • ITOC supplies System technology direction, standards, security policy and National IT performance assessment;
  • FRFS/CPE supplies payment-product requirements, software-development oversight and service operations/protocols;
  • Conference of Presidents/Payments Committee supplies FRFS strategy and enterprise oversight;
  • each Bank and board remains legally accountable; and
  • the Board/RBOPS supplies supervision, examinations, budgets and defined material-change/capital gates.

Not shown as Richmond authority:

  • unilateral alteration of Fedwire, FedNow, FedACH or NSS legal rules;
  • payment/master-account access decisions for other Districts;
  • FOMC policy or New York Desk transaction direction;
  • unilateral System-wide IT policy, standards or exceptions;
  • ownership of every National IT application, vendor contract or data center;
  • power to bind every Bank outside the disclosed enterprise/inter-Bank arrangements; or
  • unilateral release, emergency-change, failover or decommission authority for every national application.

Current-instrument corrections

  1. Bylaws: use the July 23, 2026 amended-and-restated bylaws, not the 2022 version.
  2. President profile: an earlier official search profile described the Richmond president as a standing ITOC member, chief executive leader for National IT and direct supervisor of the System CIO. Its official URL returned 404 at this cutoff. Preserve it as historical/search-index evidence, not a confirmed live instrument.
  3. Committee name: the 2021 Richmond charter expands ITOC as “Information Technology Oversight Committee”; the 2025 PFMI disclosures use “Information Technology and Operations Committee.” No public rename instrument was located.
  4. FRIT: the Board describes FRIT as a portion of National IT at Richmond; do not use the names interchangeably.
  5. Barkin's two hats: Richmond president and Payments Committee chair are distinct capacities. The chair role does not turn FRFS into a Richmond-owned operation.

Exact missing operating constitution

The public record supports the high-level RACI but not the enforceable compact. The highest-value missing records are:

  1. Richmond–other-Reserve-Bank National IT agreements, with service catalog, RACI, chargebacks, SLAs, credits/remedies, step-in rights, termination and transition duties;
  2. current ITOC charter, named roster, voting/quorum rule, national-standard exception process, reserved matters, annual National IT evaluation pack and formal name-change history;
  3. National IT Committee packets, quarterly dashboards, minutes, actual votes, notational-vote mechanics and budget workpapers; the local default quorum and attendance-vote rule is now recoverable from the July 23 bylaws;
  4. System-CIO appointment, reporting, delegation, appraisal and compensation instruments, including ITOC and committee concurrences;
  5. current NPO charter, signature/delegation schedule and contract register by award ID, vendor, value, term, application, participating Bank, payer and accountable owner;
  6. application-level technology RACI for Fedwire, FedNow, FedACH, NSS and FedLine: controller, developer, host, contract holder, asset owner, release approver, incident commander, recovery authority and decommission signer;
  7. National IT SLA definitions, exclusions, scorecards, annual assessments, escalation thresholds and remedies;
  8. Board/RBOPS examinations, conditional-approval records, findings, remediation directions and closure evidence; and
  9. gross inter-Bank chargeback, payroll, vendor, cloud, data-center and capital schedules that reconcile Richmond's $1,135,000,000 receipt to external uses.

Research disposition and next gate

The “Richmond as technology center” hypothesis is confirmed with an important qualification. Richmond is not merely physical infrastructure. It has a live corporate board, committee, executive, procurement and accounting chain around National IT. But the System-wide principals and veto/approval gates remain federated.

The next research should not repeat biographies or generic cyber descriptions. It should target the instruments that would turn this high-level RACI into an auditable constitution:

  1. recover the inter-Bank agreements and ITOC/Payments Committee charters;
  2. map actual national contracts and application assets to legal Banks;
  3. reconcile the $1,135,000,000 inter-Bank receipt to service, Bank, payroll, vendor and capital objects; and
  4. use the forthcoming 2026 Richmond board-minute batches to recover the July 23 bylaws action, 2026 budget operation and any change in System-CIO governance.

Until those records appear, the defensible wording is “Richmond-centered shared technology governance inside a twelve-Bank federal enterprise,” not “Richmond controls the Federal Reserve's technology and payments.”
