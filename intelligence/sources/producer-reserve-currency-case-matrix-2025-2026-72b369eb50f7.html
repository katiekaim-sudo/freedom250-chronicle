Producer and Reserve-Currency Gold BOP Case Matrix 2025-2026

Comparative ruling

The four cases answer four different questions. They must not be collapsed into one claim that “gold strengthens a currency.”

Ghana: current-account strength is not reserve capture

External-account layer

IMF staff reported 2025 artisanal and small-scale gold exports of US$10.9 billion, equal to 9.5 percent of GDP. Gold accounted for more than 65 percent of goods exports, while the current account recorded a 7.9 percent of GDP surplus. These facts make gold a first-order current-account object, not a decorative reserve story (SRC-GBOP-004, SRC-GBOP-005).

The staff analysis separately identifies roughly US$5 billion of gold-related short-term net lending (about 4.7 percent of GDP). The IMF report's preliminary Table 5 records a US$9.077 billion current-account surplus, US$156 million capital-account balance, −US$12.560 billion financial-account line, −US$169 million errors and omissions, and a −US$3.496 billion overall balance in its analytical source presentation. Because that financial-account line's perimeter/sign is not the packet's normalized BPM7 total, the country ledger preserves the reported values in notes and leaves financial_account_balance blank rather than silently remapping it. The substantive lesson still holds: a gold export credit can be matched by resident acquisition of foreign deposits or other claims, and it does not follow that the central bank received the dollars.

nonmonetary-gold export credit
  -> exporter/bank acquires foreign-currency claim
  -> possible conversion, repayment or retention
  -> only the absorbed portion enters BoG reserve/intervention books

Reserve and intermediation layer

Gross international reserves reached US$11.9 billion at end-2025 and US$12.2 billion at end-May 2026. BoG bought about US$3.9 billion and sold about US$12.9 billion in the twelve months through May 2026; reported 2025 sales were US$10.6 billion. Those are large two-way intermediation flows, not a one-direction reserve-accumulation series (SRC-GBOP-004, SRC-GBOP-005).

Program NIR further proves why gross reserves, usable reserves and market value must stay separate. End-December 2025 NIR accumulation was US$3.232 billion against a US$1.450 billion floor, while the end-March 2026 outturn was −US$132 million under the successor reporting clock. These values cannot be derived from gold-export value or GIR alone (SRC-GBOP-005).

Audited gold book

At December 31, 2025 BoG reported 598,199.04 fine troy ounces of gold valued at GH¢27.029 billion, or US$4,323.87 per ounce under its reporting valuation. The audited movement schedule separately reports:

The same statements report 2,914,305 ounces of dore purchased, 2,895,426 ounces sold, opening inventory of 7,311 ounces, and closing inventory of 9,283 ounces. The simple disclosed-flow identity leaves 16,907 ounces unexplained:

7,311 + 2,914,305 - 2,895,426 - 9,283 = 16,907 oz

This is a document request, not an accusation of error. Refining loss, settlement timing, assay adjustment, transfers or a different perimeter may explain it (SRC-GBOP-006).

Cost and price boundaries

The audited Gold-for-Reserves result was a GH¢8.849740 billion loss; the separate discontinued Gold-for-Oil result was GH¢0.203034 billion. IMF staff's broader DGPP loss estimate of about GH¢22 billion, 1.5 percent of GDP, or 15.3 percent of gross purchases has a different perimeter and must not be added to or substituted for the audited program line (SRC-GBOP-004, SRC-GBOP-006).

The fixed ECF gold price was US$1,826.92/oz, while the successor PCI uses US$4,608.35/fine troy oz—a 2.522× valuation ratio. This is a program measurement discontinuity, not a transaction or windfall (SRC-GBOP-005).

Currency ruling

The cedi appreciated about 41 percent against the U.S. dollar in nominal terms and about 28 percent in real-effective terms over the reported period. The gold/current-account improvement, debt developments, confidence, policy restraint and BoG intermediation all coexist. The evidence supports “gold was materially inside the adjustment mechanism,” but not “gold caused 41 percent appreciation” (CLM-GBOP-010=R3).

Ghana–BIS: gold finances the BOP without becoming an export

BoG's audited statements disclose a US$228.197 million revolving facility secured by 155,000 fine ounces valued at US$276.85 million at inception. That is approximately 121.3 percent collateral coverage, or a 17.6 percent value margin relative to collateral value. A distinct October 2024 placement settled at US$273.69 million on January 16, 2025. The inception facility and maturity settlement are different clocks and cannot be silently substituted (SRC-GBOP-006).

cash/deposit asset acquired
  <-> foreign secured liability incurred
gold remains collateral, but availability is restricted

The operation therefore finances external liquidity through the financial account. It is neither a nonmonetary-gold export nor proof that both the cash and pledged gold were simultaneously usable reserves.

Uganda: architecture observed, scale unproved

BoU's three-year domestic-gold pilot has a three-tonne cap and pays domestic suppliers in Uganda shillings before offshore refining and possible reserve inclusion. One test purchase occurred in April 2025; the current Article IV does not establish scaled acquisition or a material reserve addition (SRC-GBOP-007; upstream SER-GTIMF-082 through 085).

The domestic purchase is resident-to-resident and therefore DOMESTIC-NOT-BOP. Any later offshore refining fee, nonresident service, monetization or reserve transaction must receive its own accounting leg.

Meanwhile:

  • the current-account deficit narrowed to 6.2 percent of GDP in FY2024/25 from 8.0 percent, and was projected at 7.1 percent in FY2025/26;
  • gross reserves were US$6.1 billion at end-May 2026, but the source reports only 2.7 months of import cover after excluding US$770 million of cross-currency repos;
  • BoU purchased nearly US$3.5 billion in FX during 2025 while the shilling traded within about ±1 percent and appreciated 2.5 percent on average.

Those facts demonstrate FX absorption and reserve-liquidity qualifications. They do not establish that the small gold pilot caused either (CLM-GBOP-015 at R1). No IMF program gold target governs this case.

Sierra Leone: the eligibility/presence control

The TMU permits eligible monetary gold inside gross reserves and excludes pledged, swapped or otherwise unavailable assets. It does not report a gold stock or gold transaction (SRC-GBOP-008).

2025-12-31 −56.7 −44.2 12.5
2026-03-31 9.9 11.8 1.9

The country instead reported reserve cover improving from about 1.5 to 1.9 months, US$26 million of first-quarter FX purchases, about US$70 million of swaps, and a commitment to buy at least US$35 million each six-month test period, with another US$29 million specified for 2026Q2. The leone was broadly stable while the REER was assessed around 8 percent overvalued.

The safe finding is deliberately negative: gold was definitionally eligible, but no gold contribution to the reported NIR outcome is demonstrated (CLM-GBOP-017=UNOBSERVED).

India 1991: historical financing comparator

The historical upstream authority reports a 20-tonne government gold sale/repurchase for US$200 million and a separate US$400 million RBI gold-collateralized borrowing. Combined, the US$600 million represented only about 6.2 percent of the US$9.7 billion FY1990/91 current-account deficit. The balance of the crisis involved other private and official financing, reserve loss, compression and IMF arrangements.

At fiscal year-end, recorded reserves comprised about US$102 million SDRs, US$3.496 billion gold and US$2.236 billion foreign-currency assets, or US$5.834 billion total; import cover was about 2.5 months. The rupee was devalued in two July 1991 steps by about 18–19 percent. Sequence and liquidity role are proven; the proposition that the gold operations alone caused the devaluation is not.

Cross-case balance-of-payments lesson

gold export value
  != exporter FX conversion
  != central-bank FX purchase
  != reserve-asset transaction
  != gross reserve stock change
  != usable NIR change
  != exchange-rate effect

Ghana supplies the positive, large-scale case; Uganda supplies an unscaled pilot; Sierra Leone supplies the definition-only negative control; and India supplies the emergency-financing comparator. Together they show where gold can matter greatly without granting it an automatic or exclusive currency effect.
