The military gained border-land arrest power, not control of civilian courts

Selected Federal border lands now operate as military-installation annexes, letting designated troops protect them and arrest for area-linked offenses before civilian handoff; civilian courts and ordinary Federal offices did not pass to military control.

What we found

  • The process starts with a presidential military mission, then an interagency transfer of administrative jurisdiction over exact Federal parcels, then a service assignment to an existing base and a commander restricted-area order.
  • Under 10 U.S.C. section 2672, the Secretary or Deputy Secretary may designate trained military or civilian personnel to protect War Department property and exercise specified Federal enforcement powers. Civilians arrested under this route cannot be held in a military confinement facility unless separately subject to the UCMJ.
  • DOJ's August 14, 2026 OLC opinion reads the law to permit immediate pursuit and arrest just beyond an NDA line for a crime committed inside. It is an executive legal opinion, leaves far-removed arrests unanswered and says the theory does not license primarily off-base military patrol assignments.
  • The first New Mexico complaints hit a notice problem: a magistrate judge dismissed military-property counts without prejudice because the complaints did not show the defendants knew they entered the newly marked area. Later guilty pleas and Texas convictions remained in ordinary U.S. district courts. The House voted to lower the mental-state barrier and raise penalties, but that amendment did not appear in the enacted FY2026 NDAA.
  • The NDA route now sits beside three separate immigration expansions: CBP's four Customs-Enforcement Areas extend named maritime enforcement powers to 24 nautical miles; a September OLC opinion reads State reporting duties across participating States' component agencies; and ICE used a Defense parent contract vehicle for a San Antonio detention-facility task order. These are functional links, not one legal entity or proved person-level pipeline.

Why it matters

This is a procedural way to widen the domestic military footprint without declaring martial law or invoking the Insurrection Act: change who administers Federal land, make it an installation annex, and use existing property-protection law. Beside it, the government is widening maritime interdiction, State reporting and detention capacity through different old authorities. The key question is whether later records join those modules at the person level.

What to watch

  • on publication or filing · The controlling Attorney General-approved section 2672 guidelines, the full six-area designation roster, or a court merits ruling on an arrest outside an NDA boundary.
  • on any new transfer · Any ordinary civilian Federal office or non-border Federal property moved into War Department jurisdiction for a domestic protective mission.
  • April 15, 2028 · New Mexico PLO 7963 expires unless it is renewed, replaced or made permanent earlier.

Research summary

The executive branch has expanded the military-property perimeter by transferring selected Federal border lands to War Department administrative control, attaching them to existing bases and invoking installation-protection law. Designated personnel may arrest civilians for NDA-linked offenses and, under an August 2026 OLC opinion, pursue them just beyond the boundary before civilian handoff. Civilians remain in civilian custody and U.S. district court; no blanket military authority over Federal offices or changed judicial-district boundary was found.
