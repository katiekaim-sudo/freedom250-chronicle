Impoundment and Rescission — Legal and Entity Map

Updated: 2026-08-15
Status: factual legal map
Rule: Congress rescinded · the President proposed · funds expired while withheld are three different objects. Do not let them impersonate each other.

Short answer

The purse has two doors. The fraud machine asks whether a payment should leave. This map asks whether appropriated money may be unspent.

The Impoundment Control Act of 1974 lets the President propose a rescission by special message and withhold the money for 45 calendar days of continuous session. The money "shall be made available for obligation unless, within the prescribed 45-day period, the Congress has completed action on a rescission bill." Only Congress can cancel the appropriation. That is 2 U.S.C. § 683(b).

Public Law 119-28, signed 2025-07-24, is that bill for the June 3 2025 special message. It is an enacted rescission. The August 28 2025 special message is a later § 683 proposal — 15 items, $4.9 billion as the Federal Register notice itself states. It is not P.L. 119-28. Calling it a "pocket rescission" is the sender's theory. GAO B-330330 (2018) holds the ICA does not permit withholding through the date the money expires.

EO 14215 § 5 is the quiet twin: OMB may adjust independent-agency apportionments, including a bar on spending particular activities, "so long as such restrictions are consistent with law." Apportionment is not a rescission.

2. The legal stack

What the ICA is

Title X of Pub. L. 93-344, classified at 2 U.S.C. §§ 681–688. Section 681 is a disclaimer: the Act does not concede constitutional power either way, does not ratify any impoundment, and does not supersede a law that requires obligation or outlay.

Two permitted withholdings

Device Section What it does Hard stop
Rescission proposal 2 U.S.C. § 683 President transmits a special message. Amounts may be withheld for 45 calendar days of continuous session. They "shall be made available for obligation unless" Congress completes a rescission bill in that window. Funds made available under this procedure "may not be proposed for rescission again." Only an enacted rescission bill permanently cancels. Continuity of session: adjournment sine die breaks it; adjournment of more than 3 days is excluded from the count (§ 682(5)).
Deferral 2 U.S.C. § 684 Temporary withhold. Permitted only for contingencies, savings/efficiency, or as specifically provided by law. "A deferral may not be proposed for any period of time extending beyond the end of the fiscal year" in which the message is sent. Cannot be used to kill a program. § 684(c) says this section does not apply to amounts in a § 683 rescission message.

A "deferral of budget authority" includes withholding, delaying, or any other executive action or inaction that "effectively precludes" obligation or expenditure (§ 682(1)).

Apportionment (the everyday withhold)

31 U.S.C. § 1512: an appropriation for a definite period "shall be apportioned to prevent obligation or expenditure at a rate that would indicate a necessity for a deficiency or supplemental." A reserve may be established only (A) for contingencies, (B) for savings from changed requirements or greater efficiency, or (C) as specifically provided by law. Reserves "shall be reported to Congress as provided in the Impoundment Control Act."

EO 14215 § 5 (signed 2025-02-18, 90 FR 10447) directs the OMB Director, on an ongoing basis, to review independent-regulatory-agency obligations for consistency with the President's policies and to "adjust such agencies' apportionments by activity, function, project, or object," including a prohibition on expending appropriations on particular activities, "so long as such restrictions are consistent with law." The order does not apply to the Board of Governors or the FOMC in the conduct of monetary policy.

That is the money-control twin of Humphrey's. It is still an apportionment. It is not a § 683 rescission.

GAO's pocket holding

GAO B-330330 (Dec. 10, 2018): "The ICA does not permit the withholding of funds through their date of expiration." Amounts proposed for rescission "must be made available for prudent obligation before the amounts expire, even where the 45-day period provided in the ICA approaches or spans the date on which funds would expire." The President "has no unilateral authority to withhold funds from obligation." Congress "did not cede this important power through the ICA."

That opinion overrules earlier GAO notes that had treated late-year messages as able to run out the clock. It is an appropriations-law opinion, not a court judgment.

Suit

2 U.S.C. § 687: if budget authority required to be made available is not, the Comptroller General may sue in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia after filing a 25-day explanatory statement with the Speaker and the President of the Senate.

3. Public Law 119-28 — the enacted object

Cite: Pub. L. 119-28, July 24, 2025, 139 Stat. 467. Short title: Rescissions Act of 2025.
Hook: "Pursuant to the special message transmitted by the President on June 3, 2025 … proposing the rescission of budget authority under section 1012 [2 U.S.C. § 683]."
Effect: "the rescissions described under subsection (b) shall take effect immediately upon the date of enactment."

This is Congress completing a rescission bill. It is the one door that § 683(b) names.

Paragraphs (1)–(19) name dollar figures of unobligated balances. Paragraph (20) rescinds CPB amounts made available, with no dollar figure in the Act.

4. The August 28 2025 special message — a later proposal

Object: Presidential special message under 2 U.S.C. § 683(a), transmitted August 28, 2025; printed as OMB notice "Rescissions Proposals Pursuant to the Congressional Budget and Impoundment Control Act," FR Doc. 2025-16851, 90 FR 42614 (Sept. 3, 2025).

The Federal Register text states: "In accordance with section 1012(a) … I herewith report 15 rescissions of budget authority, totaling $4.9 billion." It names programs of the Department of State, USAID, and International Assistance Programs. Release date: August 28, 2025. Signed for the notice by Russell T. Vought, Director.

That is a proposal. It is not P.L. 119-28. It is not an enacted cancellation.

The White House briefing of August 29, 2025 calls this a "pocket rescission." That is the sender's label. The ICA text has no such term. The legal question is the one B-330330 already answered: whether a § 683 withhold may run through the money's expiration date. GAO says no. A 2026 court merits holding on this specific August 28 package was not retrieved from a court document at this cutoff. Do not write one.

6. Entity Theory read

Congress still writes the appropriation. The ICA is a 45-day proposal window, not a presidential line-item veto — Clinton v. City of New York already killed that form, and B-330330 reads the ICA the same way. What changed in 2025 is that Congress did complete a rescission bill (P.L. 119-28), and OMB's everyday apportionment hand (EO 14215 § 5) now reaches the independent-agency books. Two leashes, two creatures. The enacted Act hits USAID's operating-expense balances and several dissolving-chart independents (IAF, USADF, USIP) without rewriting their organic acts. That is how a consolidated group starves a subsidiary without amending the articles.
