Wave 10 — whole-government merge and adjudication

Controlling answer

The September 16–22 record does not show one government-wide program moving in lockstep. It shows a dense redistribution of decision rights, access gates, record duties and implementation responsibility across otherwise separate systems.

The strongest common movement is procedural and operational:

  • executive instruments assign new interagency duties and future review gates;
  • agencies move approval authority, define who may enter a system, and state which record or evidence controls a decision;
  • temporary industrial, emergency and land instruments keep capacity available while operating proof remains separate;
  • fielding, commissioning and entry-into-force receipts move selected programs beyond planning without proving final delivery or effect; and
  • the Joint Warfighter Evaluation announcement adds an intended senior-officer selection gate without yet proving its implementing instrument, board use or promotion outcomes; and
  • audits, staged source releases and publication records make control and accountability more inspectable without themselves completing a remedy.

That convergence is strong enough to guide the Observatory's plotlines. It is not evidence of one master plan, a single platform, universal execution or a completed institutional settlement.

Cross-plotline reading

1. Authority and selection gates are moving through named controls

The H-1B order, OPM award delegation, DHS assistance finding, Commerce/CBP polysilicon procedure and FCC/Department of War robotics decision all assign who may decide, who must consult, who maintains the list or record, and how a decision can be reversed. They support a plotline of redistributed discretion inside the federal system, not privatization or unitary command.

The Joint Warfighter Evaluation adds a different but related control: an announced assessment gate between O-6 eligibility and one-star selection. The announcement changes stated selection procedure, not the statutory appointment chain, Senate confirmation, or proof of any individual promotion result.

2. Records and identity are increasingly operative gates

Marketplace registration, State FOIA/Privacy Act identity controls, FSA recipient reporting, HIGLAS status design, SSA match instructions, OFLC record custody and the Cold Case/FRUS publication seams all show that record state can control access, review and remedy. A page, list or match is still not the underlying adjudication or recipient outcome.

3. Permission and capacity are advancing faster than proved effects

Temporary import controls, emergency power orders, conditional land and equipment decisions, acquisition proposals, fielding and program entry into force create real capability or permission changes. The campaign repeatedly failed to find the later payment, accepted delivery, final settlement or measured-effect receipt. Those missing clocks are evidence boundaries, not reasons to erase the earlier state change.

4. Publication and source custody remain their own infrastructure

Federal Register filing/publication, direct agency action, NARA availability, FRUS staged release, committee QFR custody and OIG recommendation state often occur on different dates and under different publishers. The campaign's strongest corrections came from refusing to turn a later publication receipt into a second event.
