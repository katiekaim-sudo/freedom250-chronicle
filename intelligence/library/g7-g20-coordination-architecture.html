Research summary

Freedom 250 already captures many downstream G7/G20 subjects but lacks the institutional mandate-to-implementation chain. The strongest 2025–2026 findings are a payments program whose policy milestones have not produced tangible global end-user improvement; a quantified but non-binding G7 permanent-magnet/rare-earth diversification system; practical G20 debt-process machinery without case authority; an IEA/member oil release operating below G7 coordination; and exact future clocks across IMF quota, finance, minerals, ports, digital safety and the U.S. G20 presidency. The financial-rails extension shows that the decisive implementation layer is a constitutional stack from legal claim, access and message through clearing, liquidity, title, cash finality, accounting, correction and loss; project finance travels through separate cash, risk, title/collateral and information/control rails. Treasury published the Asheville Chair's Statement on September 1; China objected to paragraphs 4, 10, 11 and 13, so those passages cannot be presented as universal G20 consensus. Neither forum is a legal or operating authority, and no new plotline is warranted.
