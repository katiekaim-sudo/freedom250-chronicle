# Operation Economic Outcast — Banque Misr UAE delta

## Question and answer

Did Treasury's August 24 forecast produce a named financial-institution action?

**Partially.** On August 28, FinCEN found the five UAE branches of Banque Misr,
collectively defined as **Banque Misr UAE**, to be a financial institution of
primary money-laundering concern and proposed Section 311 Special Measure Five.
The proposal was published September 1 at **91 FR 56085**, document
**2026-17871**, docket **FINCEN-2026-0232**, RIN **1506-AB76**. Comments close
October 1.

If finalized as proposed, covered U.S. financial institutions would be barred
from opening or maintaining correspondent accounts for Banque Misr UAE and
would have to guard against indirect use of their foreign correspondent
accounts. Through the cutoff, this was a proposal—not an operative prohibition.
FinCEN says three direct U.S. correspondent relationships exist but does not
name the institutions or establish that an account closed.

Separately, OFAC made operative blocking designations of Iranian national
**Reza Mohammad Taeedi**, general manager of Bank Melli's Dubai branch, under
E.O. 13224 as amended, and Hong Kong company **Kameng Trading Limited** under
E.O. 13902. OFAC did **not** newly designate Banque Misr UAE itself.

## State firewall

| Object | State through September 3 | Does not establish |

| Taeedi and Kameng designations | operative OFAC blocking designations | designation of Banque Misr UAE, branch closure, forfeiture or SWIFT disconnection |
| Bessent August 24 forecast | partially confirmed under the package's resolution schema | a completed sanction against the named bank or an operating payment outcome |

The object applies only to Banque Misr's UAE branches as defined in the NPRM,
not its Egyptian or other-country operations. It does not prove a Bank Melli
branch closure, asset seizure, universal dollar or SWIFT exclusion, payment
failure or domestic Iranian banking shutdown.
