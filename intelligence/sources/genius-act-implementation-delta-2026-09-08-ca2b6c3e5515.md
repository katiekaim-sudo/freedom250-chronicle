# GENIUS Act Implementation Delta — September 8, 2026

<!-- plain-english-entry:start -->
## In plain English

**Question:** The GENIUS Act is law: which rules, reports, approvals and dates actually stand between the statute and a working U.S. stablecoin regime?

**Answer:** GENIUS is law but not in force until January 18, 2027; the agencies have proposals, not final rules, and three required reports are missing.

**Evidence checked through:** 2026-09-30

The detailed record below keeps the legal wording, source limits and open questions.

## Bottom line

There **has** been additional government movement since the August 17 baseline,
but the core GENIUS system is still not operational.

The strongest new federal events are:

1. Treasury's Section 3 proposal was formally published on August 18 with an
   October 19 comment deadline.[^1]
2. Comptroller Jonathan Gould said on August 19 that the OCC intended to have a
   final rule out “by November.” That is an agency schedule statement, not a
   final rule.[^2]
3. The joint PPSI customer-identification proposal completed its comment period
   on August 21; no final CIP rule was located.[^3]
4. Federal Reserve staff published a September 4 measurement paper explaining
   how stablecoins and tokenized deposits could enter M1/M2 and what reporting
   and double-counting problems must be solved. The paper expressly does not
   represent Board policy.[^4]

No located action at the cutoff supplies the missing core final rules, opens the
Stablecoin Certification Review Committee (SCRC) pathway, publishes the three
still-unlocated statutory reports, approves a named permitted issuer, or changes
the January 18, 2027 outside effective-date backstop.

### Negative federal-register result

A title/full-text Federal Register query from **August 18 through September 8**
located one new item expressly labeled as GENIUS Act implementation: Treasury's
August 18 Section 3 NPRM. A broader “payment stablecoin” query also returned the
SEC proposals and the OCC/FDIC general supervisory rule above. Those broader
hits were reviewed but not promoted into the GENIUS-final-rule column.

This is a bounded negative finding, not a claim that no unpublished agency work
exists.

## Material state-government movement and backfill

The state layer contains more substance than our August package showed. The
Florida and Texas items below are **new to this research record**, but their
underlying governmental action was not necessarily issued after August 17.

### Florida: enacted state-qualified issuer machinery

Florida approved HB 175 on June 26, 2026 as Chapter 2026-176. The substantial
stablecoin-issuer provisions become effective October 1, 2026.[^5] Section
658.997 creates a state-qualified issuer pathway and directs the Office of
Financial Regulation (OFR) to:

- license or certify eligible issuers and establish capital, liquidity,
  operational and risk-management requirements;
- supervise ongoing compliance;
- submit Florida's framework to the SCRC when the federal certification process
  exists;
- decide a substantially complete application within 120 days, with a deemed-
  approval consequence for nondecision;
- avoid treating use of an open, public or decentralized network as a standalone
  reason for denial; and
- recognize tokenized forms of otherwise eligible reserve assets.

This is real enacted implementation architecture, but it does not prove an SCRC
certification, an OFR-approved issuer or live federal permission. The federal
gate that Florida plans to use was still unopened at the cutoff.

Florida also enacted a Department of Financial Services stablecoin pilot in
section 17.72.[^6] It authorizes receipt of fees and certain disbursement/refund
uses, subject to operational prerequisites; receipts are converted to dollars
and deposited with a qualified public depository. The program cannot operate
until at least one qualifying federal- or state-permitted issuer is approved.
The law therefore creates an acceptance plan with an explicit dependency, not a
live state stablecoin payment rail.

### Texas: continuing-supervision and interpretive-guidance plan

The Texas Department of Banking says it currently regulates fiat-backed
stablecoin issuers as money transmitters and intends to continue doing so in a
manner consistent with GENIUS. Its official nondepository page says it plans to
issue interpretive guidance under Texas Finance Code section 152.059.[^7] A
parallel page explains the application route for insured depository institution
subsidiaries and Texas's continuing state-supervision role.[^8]

The pages are a current agency position and plan. No page-level publication date
was located, so this audit does not classify them as a proved post-August 17
release. No Texas SCRC certification or permitted-issuer approval was located.

### New York and North Carolina

- **New York:** NYDFS Part 202 remains proposed; comments are due September 21.
  No final Part 202 rule or SCRC certification was located.[^9]
- **North Carolina:** HB 1029 contains a detailed state stablecoin-regime
  proposal. It passed the House and was referred to Senate Rules on June 11,
  where the official bill history remained at cutoff. It is a legislative plan,
  not enacted law or operational approval.[^10]

If the OCC publishes its qualifying final rule in November, 120 days after that
event would fall in March 2027. On that fact pattern, the January 18, 2027
outside backstop would still arrive first. This is a conditional clock inference,
not a forecast that the OCC will meet its target or that its rule alone completes
the statutory trigger set.

## What we are still waiting for

1. Qualifying core final rules from the primary federal payment-stablecoin
   regulators.
2. A final PPSI customer-identification rule.
3. A final Treasury state-regime similarity rule.
4. SCRC rules or guidance, a submission process, certification decisions and a
   public decision record.
5. Foreign-regime comparability decisions and an operative OCC foreign-issuer
   registration process.
6. Public text or authoritative receipt evidence for the Section 14 study,
   Section 4(a)(6)(C) Treasury–Attorney General report and Section 15 annual
   industry report.
7. A named PPSI approval that has crossed every applicable legal and operational
   gate.
8. Evidence that a state pilot or agency acceptance program has actually begun
   transacting, rather than merely being authorized.

## Research ruling

The implementation picture has changed from **“a proposal is arriving”** to
**“multiple proposal clocks are closing and agencies are publicly scheduling
finalization.”** It has not changed to **“the GENIUS regime is live.”** Florida
now supplies a concrete state-law pathway and pilot dependency chain, but the
federal certification and approval machinery on which that pathway relies is
still missing.

For the broader monetary-stack thesis, the pattern is important: the public and
private system is building separate modules—issuer charter, identity, reserve,
distribution, state supervision, securities perimeter and monetary reporting—
before a single operational issuer pathway is complete. That is evidence of
institutional unbundling and staged acceptance, not yet evidence of completed
stablecoin substitution for bank money or central-bank settlement.
