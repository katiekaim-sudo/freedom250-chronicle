How should the 119th Congress's committee and subcommittee bodies, jurisdictions, proceedings and durable public objects be represented without treating committees as executive subsidiaries?

The short version (draft, not yet reviewed by Katie)

Every congressional committee mapped as its own body, not as a branch of an agency

All 226 current committees and subcommittees of the 119th Congress are mapped as their own nodes. Each gets only as much detail as its public record supports, and nothing is filled in to make the chart look even.

What we found

  • The map treats committees as part of Congress, not as subsidiaries of executive agencies. It ties hearings, actions, documents and results to each body.
  • It separates each step: a hearing, a committee report, a full chamber vote, enactment and the law taking effect. One House wave verified 70 such outcomes across 17 measures.
  • It fixed some records: S. 919 is the March 13, 2025 GENIUS Act vehicle, and H. Rept. 119-7 goes with H.J. Res. 25. The PN711 nomination is traced from receipt to office entry on March 20.
  • For the Kansas City Fed, Treasury and GAO follow-ups, no responses or effects had been confirmed; the Treasury letter's reply was due August 4, 2026.
  • Coverage is uneven: 210 bodies were still unscanned for events, and the House document site began blocking requests partway through collection.

Why it matters

Congressional committees are where much of the procedural rebuilding gets written, through hearings, reports and bills. Mapping them separately lets you see what Congress actually did versus what was said.

What to watch

  • pending · Backfilling the 210 committee bodies not yet scanned for events.
  • pending · Responses or effects from the Kansas City Fed, Treasury and GAO follow-ups, none confirmed at cutoff.
