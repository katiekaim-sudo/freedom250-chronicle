Money From Below — The State Sweep (2026-07-08)

Thirteen states, five research passes, one afternoon. The federal sweeps found the dollar being rewired from above — the Check, the Rails, the Gate, Stablecoins, the Ledger. This sweep asked what the states are doing underneath, and the answer is: building their own. What went into the vault today, and what it says.

The shape of it — two poles, one fight

The braid the Convergence Matrix found — blue-state contestation as the one real cross-arc weave — turns out to have a monetary spine. The states sort into two camps, and both are fighting the federal center, just from opposite doors:

The sound-money belt is building parallel money. Texas mints gold Redbacks, holds $5M of bitcoin through BlackRock's fund, and opened its own stock exchange this very week — test trading began Monday July 6, live trading Friday July 10, the first new major independent US exchange since Nasdaq in 1971. Florida stood up the fullest metal-money plumbing any state has built (two laws, a dead-man switch, near-unanimous votes, live July 1). Louisiana assembled the most complete gold architecture outside Texas — legal tender, gold-backed debit instruments, and now a state-certified gold-payments registry (the Bayou Gold Program) — unanimously and in total silence. Missouri became the eighth gold state. Montana banned the Fed coin and invented the "network token." New Hampshire passed the first bitcoin-reserve law in the nation — and today, this very afternoon, its Governor and Council heard a plan for a $100M municipal bond secured by $160M in bitcoin, the first public debt in America collateralized by crypto.

The counterweight is building walls — and losing its army to the enemy. New York fined Paxos $48.5M on August 6; five days later Paxos announced it was leaving for a federal OCC charter, and by December 12 the OCC had chartered five crypto trusts in one day, three of them defectors from the NYDFS roster. New York's answer came June 9: the first state stablecoin rule engineered to win Treasury's "substantially similar" blessing under GENIUS — the state regulator applying to the federal government for permission to keep existing. Meanwhile the blue states found a weapon nobody expected: gambling law. Massachusetts won the nation's first injunction against Kalshi in January; thirty-eight state attorneys general lined up behind her in April; James sued Coinbase and Gemini on the same theory a week before that. And California raised its licensing wall (DFAL, mandatory July 1, $100k a day) in the same season Newsom signed crypto in as "valid and legal consideration" — wall and welcome mat, same state.

The sleeper is Delaware. The 2017 blockchain-share-ledger law finally got its user: Galaxy Digital moved into Delaware during the exodus, then put its actual SEC-registered stock on Solana in September — legal ownership recorded on-chain, the living bottom-up mirror of the DTC tokenization pilot in the Ledger track. The DExit is real but symbolic (18 of 28 reincorporation votes left in 2025, mostly for Nevada and Texas) while the registry itself grew 15%. Delaware's high court blessed the SB 21 rescue law unanimously in February, and in March the state filed to become a stablecoin chartering state. The registry both sides need is quietly bidding for the money too.

Decree vs. diary, state edition

The federal sweeps taught the habit of checking the decree against the diary. The states version:

  • New Hampshire passed the first reserve law and bought nothing in 14 months. Texas passed second and bought first. The scoreboard of actual state bitcoin: Texas ~$5M. That's the whole list.
  • Florida wrote gold legal tender with near-unanimous votes — and killed its bitcoin-reserve bills twice (May 2025, March 2026), the second time in the same subcommittee, the same week, that moved the gold ratification. Florida's sound money is metallic by choice.
  • Wisconsin, the first pension into bitcoin ETFs, dumped the whole stake in Q1 2025 — days before the tariff shock. The first in was the first out. North Carolina's pension rode its crypto proxies down 50–65% after the October 2025 crash and held.
  • Texas's anti-ESG blacklist died in federal court (Feb 4) while the BlackRock-backed exchange prepared to open — and BlackRock manages the state's bitcoin. The belt fights the asset managers and banks through them, simultaneously.

Empty hunts (the silence that speaks)

No state has yet sued the federal government over GENIUS preemption, OCC charters, or the trust-charter exemption from state money-transmitter law (OCC Interpretive Letter 1192, May 12) — the first such filing is news. The NH treasurer has bought nothing. Galvin has brought no crypto action since March 2024 — the most aggressive state securities cop ceded the beat to the AGs. Mamdani has made no money moves. DFPI has published no license tally. Florida's July 1 gold launch drew almost no coverage. The CalPERS "$500M direct bitcoin allocation" story circulating is false — the exposure is a cratered MSTR bag.
