Bessent asks the IMF to narrow its work and sharpen its scrutiny

Treasury wants a tighter IMF remit; the G-24 contests what stays in scope, and the Board reviews still need guidance and country-level use.

What we found

  • The IMF evaluator found newer work legally within the mandate but faulted piecemeal choices and scarce staff and budget visibility.
  • Bessent called for a narrower core in April; October asks the Fund to implement its completed 2026 reviews and singles out the RST.
  • The Board kept macrocritical climate and digital work possible, and the 2002 lending guidelines remain in force.
  • The RST is active and uses voluntary member contributions; the G-24 asks for an early review while Treasury questions its scope.
  • Pre-review China and U.S. reports already scrutinized both surplus and deficit policies; later country reports are the implementation test.

What to watch

  • Revised IMF surveillance and program guidance, then named country reports and program reviews.
  • Any IMF RST policy review or change and the U.S. legal step for the 16th quota increase.
