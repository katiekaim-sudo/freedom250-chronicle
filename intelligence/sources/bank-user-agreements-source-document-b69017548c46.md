# Bank user agreements — legal-entity and contract-change map

Updated: 2026-07-11  
Status: first full agreement sweep; current terms and proven changes are kept separate  
Scope: consumer deposit, credit-card, digital-service, open-banking, rewards, portfolio-transfer, and servicing agreements for the entities in the banking website sweep.

Document-retrieval results: Agreement document requests — resolution log

## Reading rule

The brand on the card or app is not necessarily the legal counterparty.

For every product, identify separately:

1. **holding company** — owns the corporate group;
2. **bank/issuer** — owes the deposit or extends the credit;
3. **program/brand company** — supplies rewards, merchant access, interface, or marketing;
4. **servicer** — bills, collects, furnishes credit data, handles disputes, or runs the account platform;
5. **network/data intermediary** — routes payments or data;
6. **successor/assignee** — can acquire the account, balance, or agreement.

A current clause is not a recent change. This record uses **CONFIRMED CHANGE** only where an official amendment, prior agreement, merger notice, conversion notice, or dated comparison establishes the before and after state.

## 1. Bank of America — new digital-services arbitration constitution

### 2026-05-18 / 2026-06-02 — 2026 arbitration and mass-claim regime

**Status:** CONFIRMED CHANGE FROM JANUARY 2026 AGREEMENT  
**Legal counterparty:** Bank of America  
**Instrument:** Online Banking Service Agreement

Bank of America's May 18 replacement agreement and current June 2 agreement contain a binding-arbitration clause, jury and class waiver, and a mass-arbitration process. A preserved official January 31 page lacks the new §12 entirely, proving that this digital arbitration constitution was inserted after the January agreement and by May 18. Its scope is not confined to a checking account. It covers claims tied to online/mobile banking, Zelle, Real Time Payments, bill pay, ACH, wires, internal transfers, alerts, Deals, Erica, advertising, and the application, denial, approval or establishment of an online/mobile ID.

Consumer customers have sixty days from first delivery of the arbitration provision to opt out online or by phone. Small-business users do not receive that consumer opt-out. The provision states that it survives account closure, payment, bankruptcy, and sale or assignment.

The mass-arbitration trigger is twenty-five or more coordinated similar claims. Claims must meet individualized filing requirements; a process arbitrator can screen and dismiss deficient claims; each side initially selects fifteen cases, producing thirty-case rounds. The agreement delays fees on unselected claims and permits sanctions under Rule 11-style standards.

**Primary sources:** [Preserved January 31 official page](https://web.archive.org/web/20260131205653/https://www.bankofamerica.com/online-banking/service-agreement.go); [May 18, 2026 agreement update](https://www.bankofamerica.com/content/documents/agreement/OnlineBankingServiceAgreementUpdateEN.pdf); [Current agreement — effective June 2, 2026](https://www.bankofamerica.com/online-banking/service-agreement.go?request_locale=en_US)

### 2026-06-02 — transfer clocks and Deals name change

**Status:** CONFIRMED CHANGE FROM MAY 18 TEXT

The June agreement changed transfer-limit accounting from a rolling-or-calendar method based on customer profile to fixed calendar clocks: the daily period resets at 12:00 a.m. Eastern and the weekly period resets Monday at 12:00 a.m. Eastern. The disclosed $3,500 daily and $10,000 weekly limits did not change. The agreement also replaced “BankAmeriDeals” with “Deals” in the automatic-enrollment/data-sharing clause and the arbitration scope. The bank may share anonymized transaction information with vendors, merchants, third parties and card networks to present offers and administer rewards.

### Legal significance

The digital access layer now has its own dispute constitution reaching multiple payment rails and Erica. The agreement explicitly follows the account through a later sale or assignment. A transfer of the underlying deposit or debt therefore does not necessarily free the customer from this digital arbitration clause.

### Comparison resolved

The January-to-May insertion is proven. The May-to-June comparison separately proves the transfer-clock and Deals terminology changes. Deposit, card, Merrill and small-business agreements remain separate contracts and may carry different dispute rules.

## Grouped findings — legal structure, not product interpretation

### A. Arbitration is being made portable

Chase, BofA, Citi, PNC, Citizens, AmEx, Discover and Merrick use language that reaches successors, assigns, acquired entities, agents or buyers. The contractual dispute shield is designed to travel with an account when the debt or bank changes hands.

### B. Banks are hardening against mass arbitration

The post-class-action response is no longer just “individual arbitration.” Chase, Bank of America, Synchrony and Truist now have specific intake, certification, screening, batching, process-arbitrator, or staged-case mechanics for coordinated individual claims.

### C. A brand can survive a complete legal-counterparty change

- Discover remained Discover while Discover Bank disappeared into Capital One, N.A.
- Ally card customers retained many product terms while Merrick became issuer and Ollo became interface.
- Bilt remained the customer-facing brand while new cards moved from Wells to Column/Cardless and legacy Wells accounts converted to Autograph.
- Apple Card remains Goldman-issued while a future Chase transfer has already been agreed.

### D. The customer may have several contracts for one apparent product

A card can involve a bank agreement, rewards agreement, digital-access agreement, merchant-program terms, privacy notice, network rules, benefit insurance, and a servicing/data-sharing authorization. Arbitration, forfeiture and data rights can differ across them.

### E. Digital-service agreements are becoming operating constitutions

BofA's agreement reaches Erica and multiple payment rails; U.S. Bank's reaches AI model training; Truist's reaches continuous open-banking sharing and instant transfers; Wells binds changes through continued digital use. The digital agreement may now govern more day-to-day conduct than the basic deposit agreement.

### F. Closure, freeze and setoff powers remain broad

Most deposit agreements allow closure without cause, freezes for suspected irregularity or disputes, and setoff against customer funds. These powers belong to the named bank entity and may extend to debts owed to affiliates, owners or beneficial owners depending on the agreement.

### G. Embedded finance requires transaction-role mapping

Cross River makes the legal chain especially visible: brand, marketing platform, originating bank, processor, servicer, credit furnisher, collector and receivable owner may be different companies. A website sweep must capture each role before treating a partner-branded account as the bank's own retail product.

### H. Asset managers require fund-by-fund attribution

Franklin Templeton is not one consumer counterparty. The public parent, distributor, adviser, registered fund, 529 plan, custodian, transfer agent and digital platform can each have a separate legal role. Research and growth statements from the group cannot prove conduct by an individual fund or adviser.
