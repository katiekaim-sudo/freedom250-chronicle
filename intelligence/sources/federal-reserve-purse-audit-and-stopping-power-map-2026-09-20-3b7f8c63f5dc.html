Federal Reserve purse, audit and stopping-power map

Wave: 5 of the Federal Reserve Structure Research Campaign Evidence cutoff: September 20, 2026 Status: current Workbench answer; reconciliation of existing financial, accounting, audit and oversight research Question: Who can see, compel disclosure of, approve, condition, disallow, redirect, enjoin or stop Federal Reserve money and operations? Boundary: Audit, disclosure and criticism are not treated as operational command. A clean audit is not treated as approval of policy, value for money, cybersecurity, uptime or transaction finality.

Bottom line

The Federal Reserve is heavily audited but not by one universal auditor, and most auditors cannot directly stop an operation.

The strongest stopping powers belong elsewhere:

  • Congress can change the funding, remittance, capital, audit and authority statutes and can use compulsory oversight processes, but it ordinarily does not enact annual line-item appropriations for Fed operations.
  • The full Board can approve or condition Board and Reserve Bank budgets, examine Banks, require reports and write-offs, review major initiatives, suspend or remove Bank officers/directors and—after a violation of the Federal Reserve Act—suspend a Bank’s operations, take possession, administer, liquidate or reorganize it.
  • Each Reserve Bank board supervises and controls its own corporation, approves its budget and broad strategy, holds management accountable and controls its general auditor.
  • The FOMC can direct, change or cease open-market operations within its statutory domain; it does not control every Bank expense or payment-service budget.
  • The Treasury Secretary has a statutory prior-approval veto over a new §13(3) emergency facility and controls reimbursed fiscal-agent work through the applicable authority and agreements.
  • Courts can issue binding relief where jurisdiction, cause of action, reviewability and remedy exist.
  • GAO, the OIG, outside auditors and general auditors can inspect, report, recommend, escalate or refer. Their findings can trigger action by a body that has command power, but the finding is not usually the stop order.

The practical rule is:

Follow the money to the legal ledger, then follow the decision to the actor with a valid approval, condition, termination, removal or injunction power. Audit access alone is not control.

These rights can coexist without merging. An auditor may see more than a manager yet possess less stopping power. A budget approver may stop a commitment without knowing every transaction. A court may enjoin an unlawful act without managing the program afterward.

2. The purse is statutory but not ordinarily appropriated annually

Board of Governors

Under 12 U.S.C. §§243–244, the Board assesses the twelve Reserve Banks to fund its estimated expenses. Congress directs that assessment proceeds “shall not be construed to be Government funds or appropriated moneys,” permits the money to remain on deposit at the Banks, and lets the Board determine how its obligations and disbursements are incurred and paid.

That creates a real insulation from ordinary annual appropriations. It does not make the Board private or place the money beyond law. Congress created the stream and can rewrite it; the Board approves its own budget and capital plan; the OIG, outside auditor, GAO and Congress have overlapping review routes.

Reserve Banks

The Banks fund operations from central-bank earnings, priced-service revenue, reimbursements and other authorized income. Their expense, dividends, surplus and remittance treatment is statutory and Board-governed. A year with no current Treasury remittance does not eliminate the Treasury claim: under current accounting, excess earnings first reduce the accumulated deferred asset before cash remittances resume.

4. The GAO perimeter: real audit authority with a protected core

31 U.S.C. §714 begins with a broad command that GAO audit the named financial agencies, including the Board and Reserve Banks. It then excludes from ordinary Board/Bank audits:

  1. transactions for or with foreign central banks, foreign governments and specified international financing organizations;
  2. monetary-policy deliberations, decisions or actions, including discount-window operations, reserve requirements, securities credit, interest on deposits and open-market operations;
  3. transactions made under FOMC direction; and
  4. related communications among System actors.

Dodd–Frank added exceptions. For covered §13(3) facilities and specified covered transactions, GAO may examine operational integrity, accounting, reporting, internal controls, collateral/security policy, favoritism and contractor selection/payment. Reports go to Congress and can face delayed disclosure designed to protect live transactions and counterparties.

That produces two simultaneous truths:

  • “The Fed is never audited” is false.
  • “GAO can freely audit and second-guess the entire monetary-policy process” is also false.

Even inside its audit perimeter, GAO ordinarily reports and recommends. Its report does not itself cancel an operation or fire an official.

5. Outside financial audit: what the clean opinions mean

Federal Reserve Act §11B requires the Board to order annual independent audits of the financial statements of the Board and each Reserve Bank.

2025 Board result

KPMG’s March 12, 2026 Board reports provide financial-statement and ICFR assurance. Its Government Auditing Standards work tested certain laws, regulations and contracts for provisions that could directly and materially affect the statements. It found no reportable instances in that testing, but expressly did not provide a general compliance opinion.

2025 Reserve Bank result

KPMG issued unmodified financial-statement and COSO-2013 ICFR opinions for all twelve Banks and the combined statements. The Reserve Bank statements use the Board’s FAM, a comprehensive basis other than U.S. GAAP, and do not include a statement of cash flows.

The clean opinions support these propositions:

  • the statements are fairly presented in all material respects under the stated basis;
  • ICFR was effective for the audited financial-reporting objective; and
  • no opinion qualification or material weakness was reported in the located 2025 reports.

They do not establish:

  • that every payment or technology control operated perfectly;
  • that a service met its availability target;
  • that a transaction was legally final;
  • that every procurement was economical;
  • that monetary policy was wise;
  • that no fraud or control failure below audit materiality existed; or
  • that every application, vendor, subledger and inter-Bank charge is publicly identifiable.

6. Internal audit: independent access but governance-mediated remedy

The Board’s policy makes each Bank audit committee responsible for the effectiveness and independence of internal audit. The general auditor reports directly to the Bank board through the audit committee and assesses:

  1. controls over financial reporting;
  2. efficiency and effectiveness of operations; and
  3. compliance with law and regulation.

The audit committee can establish the audit plan, protect access, receive confidential complaints, engage advisers, evaluate the general auditor and recommend appointment, reassignment or termination. The Bank board acts on those personnel decisions, with advance consultation through the Board’s Federal Reserve Bank affairs route.

For national financial services, the coordinating general auditor and Conference of General Auditors support multi-Bank assurance and communications to the twelve Bank audit committees. That gives the audit chain reach across the enterprise without turning it into the Payments Committee, CPE, ITOC or legal operator.

8. Accounting visibility is not the same as recipient visibility

The library’s accounting work establishes several layers:

  1. legal-entity books: each Bank records its own assets, liabilities, income, expense and interdistrict position;
  2. service/operator books: designated Banks recognize System service revenue and reimburse provider Banks;
  3. shared-service charges: National IT, pensions and other functions can create large reimbursements at a host Bank;
  4. CASPR cost objects: economic cost assignment need not match the legal entity or cash payer;
  5. combined statements: inter-Bank positions and reimbursements eliminate.

Therefore a combined total can be correct while concealing the gross legal-entity route a control inquiry needs. Richmond’s $1,135,000,000 of 2025 System computing/support reimbursements, Atlanta’s System pension accounting and the operating-Bank service-revenue map are examples of public gross entries whose underlying Bank, project, application, vendor and invoice schedules are not fully disclosed.

The public statements do not provide a recipient-level ledger for:

  • every vendor payment;
  • every inter-Bank charge and allocation driver;
  • every software asset and controlling product office;
  • every project commitment versus expenditure;
  • every Treasury-reimbursed cost; or
  • every counterparty receiving interest or facility proceeds.

This is a disclosure gap, not proof that the payments are unauthorized.

9. Enforcement escalation ladder

An audit issue becomes stopping power only when it crosses into an actor’s authority:

audit finding -> management response -> audit-committee/board escalation -> Board/OIG/GAO/Congress or law-enforcement route -> valid directive, budget condition, personnel action, enforcement order, injunction or statute

Different facts activate different ladders:

  • financial misstatement: management/audit committee/outside auditor/Board;
  • operational control weakness: management, general auditor, Bank board, Payments Committee/ITOC and Board/RBOPS;
  • Board program waste or misconduct: OIG recommendation or investigation, Board correction, congressional oversight, DOJ referral where warranted;
  • Bank Act violation: Board supervision, §248(f) personnel action or the much more severe §248(h) institutional power;
  • unlawful agency rule/action: administrative reconsideration, Board review of delegated action, congressional action or judicial review;
  • monetary-policy disagreement: FOMC process and congressional law/oversight, not ordinary GAO performance substitution.

10. Exact proof objects still missing

  1. current Board resolutions and delegations for Reserve Bank proposal, budget, capital and expenditure approval thresholds;
  2. full BAC and RBOPS budget packets, conditions, releases and post-approval changes;
  3. all twelve Bank internal-audit plans, high-risk deferred/cancelled audits, issue registers, remediation aging and closure evidence;
  4. COGA/CGA charters, rotation, national audit universe, reports and escalation protocol;
  5. application-level vendor, contract, asset, payable, cost-beneficiary and inter-Bank invoice ledgers;
  6. Systemwide CASPR allocation manual and current drivers for FRFS, National IT and other national functions;
  7. comprehensive OIG/GAO/outside-audit crosswalk showing duplicate, excluded and untested subjects;
  8. records of actual Board use of §§248(f), 248(g) and 248(h);
  9. court orders or administrative decisions that actually halted a Fed program or transaction, separated from dismissed or nonreviewable challenges; and
  10. a current gross-to-combined reconciliation for national service and technology charges, without losing the legal payer/recipient chain in elimination.

Wave 5 ruling

The audit architecture is plural and consequential, but most of it is not self-executing. The Federal Reserve’s strongest checks are a combination of Board supervisory and budget power, Bank-board corporate power, FOMC domain-specific direction, Treasury’s narrow statutory vetoes, courts’ case-specific remedies and Congress’s ultimate legislative authority. GAO, OIG, external auditors and general auditors make those powers usable by producing evidence; they do not generally become the operator.

The “self-funded and therefore unaudited” story is wrong. The “audited and therefore fully controlled” response is also wrong. The defensible formulation is:

Congress insulated the ordinary annual purse, preserved a monetary-policy audit core, and then built multiple financial, performance, internal, supervisory and legislative review channels whose findings require a separate actor to impose the remedy.

Non-claims

This report does not conclude that self-funding is private ownership; that no congressional audit exists; that GAO may audit every monetary-policy deliberation; that a clean opinion certifies policy wisdom, legality of every act, zero fraud, cybersecurity, uptime or finality; that OIG recommendations are binding stop orders; that Board general supervision makes every Bank payment a Board payment; that §248(h) has been used in a current case; that combined-statement elimination extinguishes the underlying legal transaction; or that a disclosure gap proves a secret or unlawful expenditure.
