SEC may count a CPA license toward accredited-investor status

The SEC has asked for comment on treating an active U.S. CPA license as an accredited-investor credential; this is a live notice, not a final designation order.

What we found

  • The proposed route would let a state professional license do double duty as a federal private-offering access credential. It would cover any active U.S. CPA license in good standing, not just audit partners, securities specialists or wealthy accountants.
  • NASBA reported 650,667 active CPAs as of August 27, 2026 based on 53 of 55 U.S. licensing jurisdictions. That is a gross credential count, not the number of newly eligible investors; overlap with existing income, net-worth and securities-license routes is unknown.
  • The person must hold the license, keep it active and remain in good standing. Passing the Uniform CPA Examination or IQEX alone would not qualify, and an inactive or suspended license would not qualify.
  • The discipline boundary is unusually narrow: a CPA barred by the SEC for lacking qualifications would not qualify, but some other SEC practice bars would not automatically defeat the route if the state license remained active and in good standing.
  • Public license lookup could make Rule 506(c) verification easier and less invasive than producing tax or wealth records. It would not register the offering, require an issuer or fund to accept the investor, eliminate minimum investments, or supply public-market disclosure and liquidity protections.

Why it matters

The Commission is considering importing state accounting licensure into federal securities law as an investor-access gate. That is a striking form of credential portability: a license designed to authorize accountancy practice could also unlock participation in private offerings.

What to watch

  • pending Federal Register publication · Publication of Release 33-11446 and the resulting exact 60-day comment deadline.
  • pending · Any final Commission order designating the CPA license, and its effective date and conditions.
  • comment period · Evidence on state-to-state licensing variation, discipline, investor sophistication, issuer verification and how many CPAs would actually become newly eligible.
  • pending · FINRA's specifications and administration plan for the separate proposed accredited-investor examination.
