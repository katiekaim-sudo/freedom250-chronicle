Which counterparties became real by the cutoff

The June 20, 2026 evidence audit shows several distinct relationship forms rather than one uniform new body.

TikTok is the clearest completed chain. EO 14352's conditional framework reached implementation agreements, a new legal person—TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC—allocated ownership and control rights, and live operation of the U.S. application. The public record supports an operating corporate counterparty; the purchase price, consideration schedule, and full CFIUS/mitigation agreements were not public.

Fostering the Future created a state-to-federal account bridge. Federal guidance allowed child-welfare agencies or designees to open initial accounts for foster children, and Georgia committed to automatic enrollment. The account and beneficiary architecture was live, but IRS instructions prohibited contributions before July 4, 2026, so no cutoff-safe foster-account deposit existed.

Muslim Brotherhood designations became enforceable exclusions. State and Treasury placed named branches and an individual into FTO/SDGT machinery, creating blocking, reporting, licensing, and transaction prohibitions. No blocked-dollar amount or identified rejected payment was public.

National AI policy acquired a private litigation ally. DOJ created its AI Litigation Task Force and intervened alongside xAI against Colorado's AI law. This was a live legal operation, but the court retained the decision right; no judgment or blanket federal preemption existed by the cutoff.

Marijuana entered a new federal–state legal interface. A final rule placed FDA-approved products and qualifying state-licensed medical marijuana in Schedule III and created an expedited DEA-registration route while leaving other marijuana in Schedule I. The classification was effective; no first registration, prescription, permit, or attributable transaction was isolated.

Proxy-adviser service agreements acquired fiduciary consequences. Labor guidance made actual voting control or qualifying individualized advice capable of creating ERISA fiduciary status. This changed the legal characterization of private service relationships but did not itself prove a changed contract, enforcement action, or vote.

Genesis Mission opened an application and funding channel. DOE advertised roughly $293.76 million with specified Phase I and Phase II structures, but no recipient selection, executed award, obligation, or operating platform was public by the cutoff.

The decisive rejection was the tariff chassis. PRC, Brazil and agricultural bargains were translated into HTSUS/customs terms, but the Supreme Court rejected IEEPA as a tariff authority in February 2026. The executive then terminated the affected duties or migrated surviving pieces to other statutory authorities. The relationships endured; their broad IEEPA enforcement carrier did not.
