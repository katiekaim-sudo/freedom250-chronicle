A. Identity and authority

Field Entry
Currency object Euro; ISO 4217 EUR; shared legal monetary unit, not a sovereign-country proxy
Linked IMF member records Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, Netherlands, Portugal, Slovakia, Slovenia and Spain. The common-policy report is integral to each member’s Article IV consultation; member legal/fiscal records remain separate.
Issuer / note authority ECB and euro-area national central banks within the Eurosystem
Monetary-policy authority ECB Governing Council
FX/intervention authority ECB/Eurosystem for common market operations; EU Council treaty authority over formal exchange-rate agreements or general orientations remains separate under TFEU Article 219
Trade/customs authority EU institutions under exclusive common-commercial-policy competence; European Commission administers/negotiates common policy, with EU legislation and national customs implementation kept distinct
Shared-authority overlay ECB/Eurosystem common monetary authority plus EU common-policy institutions; no fictional euro-area sovereign
Regime stress class Shared free float / reserve, funding and vehicle currency
International role tags REGIONAL, RESERVE, TRADE-INVOICE, FUNDING, VEHICLE, HEDGE, PAYMENT, SDR-BASKET

F. Six-lane worksheet

Lane Grade One-sentence reason Primary evidence + vintage Persistence evidence Shock evidence Competing explanation Confidence Legal/event link Data gap
L1 Domestic anchor B The common monetary system retains a credible 2% anchor with bounded shock-driven inflation strain and no disproportionate activity collapse. CR26/177 pp.7, 44 and authority statement p.91: 2025 inflation 2.1%, GDP growth 1.4%, unemployment 6.3%; June 2026 headline/core inflation 2.8%/2.4%; long-term expectations anchored; ECB raised rates 25 bp on June 11. Inflation declined from 5.4% in 2023 to 2.4% in 2024 and 2.1% in 2025, with output returning near potential. The 2023 deviation prevents an A across the full persistence window. 2026 Middle East energy shock lifted inflation and reduced projected growth to 0.9%, but no second-round wage effects were reported and expectations remained anchored. The current overshoot is principally a temporary terms-of-trade shock; faster energy normalization could restore target without further activity cost. HIGH Article IV: NO-ADVERSE-FINDING-LOCATED; frozen trade events are context only STAFF_CUTOFF=NOT-STATED; June inflation and policy evidence comes from the dated post-cutoff authority statement
L2 Underlying order B Common banking and monetary architecture remains functional, while NBFI opacity, incomplete safety nets and visible member stress dispersion create bounded—not generalized—strain. CR26/177 pp.13, 32–38: banks resilient in aggregate, with adequate capital/liquidity, low NPLs and profits; macroprudential buffers broadly appropriate. 2024Q4/2025Q4 NPL chart discloses sixteen member rows; residential pressure is identified in Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Greece, Ireland, Portugal, Lithuania and Spain; longstanding 2018/2025 FSAP backstop recommendations remain incomplete. Early-2026 shock: German ten-year yield +40 bp; French, Italian and Spanish spreads widened only 0–15 bp; no generalized banking, sovereign-funding or payments fracture. Aggregate strength could mask local fiscal/banking weakness, while unmeasured bank–NBFI and offshore-hedge-fund exposures may be larger than observed. MEDIUM NONE as a primary trade-event mechanism Full 21-member banking/fiscal matrix and granular NBFI/off-balance-sheet exposures are absent; current report itself identifies those data gaps
L3 Exchange order A The free-floating euro absorbed appreciation and geopolitical terms-of-trade moves without report-identified disorder, parallel-market fracture or unsustainable reserve/intervention pressure. Frozen regime sources: de jure/de facto free floating; CR26/177 pp.6–7 and 44: NEER appreciated about 6% February–July 2025, then remained broadly flat; latest 2026 table observations show USD/EUR 1.2 and NEER 100.2. Table history for 2022–2026 and the 2025–26 NEER path show continuous market operation rather than a broken or concealed regime. The early-2026 energy shock caused market repricing and euro depreciation attributable partly to adverse terms of trade, without exchange-market fracture. Bilateral depreciation or appreciation may reflect relative shocks and risk sentiment, not loss of exchange-order credibility. HIGH Trade-event IDs checked; no separately evidenced exchange-order impairment Exact high-frequency volatility, intervention and market-depth series are not itemized in CR26/177; ordinary reserve adequacy is not the central test for this free float
L4 External adjustment B The aggregate external position is currently broadly in line and adjusting, but the persistence window contains a moderately stronger assessment and substantial member imbalance dispersion. CR26/177 Table 2, p.45/49: 2025 CA 1.7% GDP, NIIP +11.0%, staff CA gap +0.9%, REER gap −2.6%; overall position “broadly in line.” Creditor states retain large assets while debtor states carry elevated liabilities and sudden-stop exposure. 2025 ESR assessment of 2024: “moderately stronger,” CA 2.8%, NIIP 10.9%, CA gap +1.0%, REER gap midpoint −3.1%. The 2025 CA narrowed from 2.7% to 1.7%, moving toward balance. Energy and trade shocks weakened services/income and net exports; the 2026 CA is projected at 1.1%, still within a credible aggregate adjustment path. MNE activity, SPEs, retained-earnings adjustments and valuation effects distort CA/NIIP measures; the aggregate can conceal opposing creditor/debtor positions. MEDIUM TP-EUUS-2025-TURNBERRY; TP-EUMERC-2026-PROVISIONAL; TP-US-2026-SECTION122; TP-EU-2026-CBAM—context only unless transmission is observed Final 2026 ESR unavailable; common table does not enumerate each member’s CA/NIIP/REER gap
L5 Current-payment usability NR Currency-wide grading would overclaim because Article VIII restriction/MCP jurisdiction belongs to each sovereign member and the frozen packet does not cover every euro-area member. Execution Annex v1.1 §3 and frozen manifest EUR legal row; France/Germany/Italy subcards are categorical only. NOT-APPLICABLE—coverage failure, not adverse persistence evidence. NOT-APPLICABLE The available member evidence suggests ordinary usability in the three cited records, but it cannot lawfully or evidentially be generalized to the currency area. HIGH Member legal subcards above; trade measures and capital controls kept separate Current official Article VIII/XIV/restriction/MCP coverage for all 21 euro-area IMF members
L6 Systemic service A The euro reliably performs its established reserve, funding, invoicing and payment roles across multiple independently measured channels without material operating impairment. ECB, International Role of the Euro, June 2026, Table 1/§§1.1–1.4: approximately 20% across broad international-use indicators; 20.2% of reserves, 24.6% of narrow international debt securities, 21.7% of international loans, 17.3% of international deposits; almost 60%/64% of extra-area goods/services export invoicing. Broad role was around 19% in 2024 and around 20% in 2025; constant-exchange-rate share rose roughly 1.5 percentage points from 2014 through 2025. Mixed risk-off exchange behavior in 2025/early 2026 and the Middle East depreciation did not interrupt reserve, funding, payment or invoicing service. Fragmented highly rated sovereign debt supply and a 2025 decline in the FX-trading share may constrain future expansion, even though current functions remain broad and reliable. HIGH Frozen trade events checked; none demonstrates systemic-service impairment A same-vintage complete hedge/derivatives, BIS turnover and payment-method matrix would sharpen market-depth attribution
