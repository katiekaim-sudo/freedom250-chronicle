Accounting frame

“Bad debt expense” is not the label most banks use. This analysis keeps three separate objects:

  1. Provision for credit losses (PCL) — the current-year expense or benefit recognized in earnings. It replenishes the reserve after charge-offs and changes the estimate of future losses.
  2. Net charge-offs (NCOs) — balances actually written off during the year, net of recoveries.
  3. Funded allowance for credit losses (ACL / ALLL) — the cumulative contra-asset deducted from gross loans or receivables on the year-end balance sheet.

Only the funded allowance is netted against receivables. Reserves for unfunded commitments are liabilities and are excluded from the net-receivable calculation. Loans measured at fair value or held for sale generally are also excluded from the reserve denominator.

Formula used throughout:

Gross loans or receivables subject to allowance − funded allowance = net loans or receivables

Important denominator notes

  • JPMorgan: the $1.493T balance-sheet loan line also contains held-for-sale and fair-value loans. The table uses $1.409T of retained loans subject to ACL.
  • Bank of America: $3.498B of fair-value-option loans is excluded from the reservable denominator.
  • Citi: $6.855B of fair-value loans is excluded.
  • Goldman: the table uses approximately $213B of amortized-cost loans. Approximately $22B of held-for-sale loans—principally the Apple Card transition portfolio—is outside the ACL calculation at year-end.
  • Franklin Templeton: the $1.229B balance is primarily short-term investment-management fee receivables carried at invoiced amount. Its filing does not provide a gross receivable / doubtful-account allowance bridge comparable to a bank's loan ACL.

Entity-specific comparability breaks to preserve

JPMorgan Chase

The $2.2B Apple Card provision was booked against a forward purchase commitment before Chase owned the receivables. It is expense, but it was not part of the funded allowance netted against 2025 loans.

Capital One / Discover

Capital One's $20.655B PCL is not an ordinary run rate. It includes $8.767B of initial allowance on acquired non-purchased-credit-deteriorated Discover loans. A further $2.870B allowance on purchased-credit-deteriorated loans was acquisition accounting rather than provision expense. Fully charged-off acquired balances and expected recoveries also complicate the 2025 NCO comparison.

Goldman Sachs / Apple Card

Goldman still incurred approximately $1.25B of net charge-offs, but the reserve release was larger, producing a $1.113B net PCL benefit. That is a portfolio-accounting exit event, not evidence that credit losses reversed.

Ally

The April 2025 credit-card sale removed the card book and its reserve from the year-end balance sheet. Ally's 2.54% ending coverage is therefore not the coverage ratio of the pre-sale business mix.

PNC and Fifth Third

PNC's FirstBank acquisition closed January 5, 2026 and Fifth Third's Comerica merger closed February 1, 2026. Neither acquired book is in the FY2025 table. Both entities will have mechanical 2026 jumps in loans, reserves and acquisition-date provision expense.

Factual read

  1. The card specialists reserve far more of receivables. Merrick netted 15.34%, Bread 11.20% and Synchrony 10.06%, versus roughly 1.1%–2.6% for the diversified money-center and regional banks.
  2. Provision and charge-offs are not interchangeable. Synchrony, Ally, Bread, Citizens and Fifth Third charged off more than they provisioned in FY2025; their reserves fell or portfolio mix changed. JPMorgan and Capital One provisioned substantially more, but unusual Apple/Discover transaction accounting explains much of the gap.
  3. Portfolio transfers can overwhelm the credit signal. Capital One's acquisition created expense; Goldman's exit created a benefit; Ally's sale removed receivables and allowance. The same underlying consumer debt can generate different accounting outcomes depending on who owns it and how it is classified at the reporting date.
  4. Franklin Templeton belongs in an ownership/funding map, not this lender comparison. Its debt exposure sits principally in managed funds, CLOs, private-credit vehicles and investments rather than a consolidated consumer-receivable book with a bank-style allowance.
