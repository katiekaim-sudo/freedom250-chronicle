# Wave 1C — Rules and Procurement

**Research window:** January 20 through March 19, 2025.  
**Question:** How did the incoming regime change who could advance a rule, define regulatory cost, bind a contractor, issue a contracting warrant or set an independent agency's priorities before the later GSA/FAR consolidation?  
**Finding:** The early regime did not yet replace the administrative state with one procurement office or repeal whole books of regulation. It installed a series of **review leashes**: appointee approval before publication, an OMB regulatory-cost budget, OIRA review for independent agencies, DOGE/OIRA class lists for proposed rescission, contract-and-grant certifications, and a temporary stop on new contracting-officer warrants. The decision right moved closer to the White House, while legal authorship, APA procedure, statutory mandates, appropriations and individual contract actions remained distributed.

This packet treats astrology as archetypal chapter structure. It does not convert a rule's signature date into astrological proof and does not count repeated appearances of the same slow sky as separate votes.

## The chain in one view

```text
freeze pending rules
    ↓
require appointee approval and OMB/OIRA review
    ↓
change the regulatory-analysis and cost-budget grammar
    ↓
bring independent agencies into the review room
    ↓
make DOGE/OIRA co-readers of the existing rulebook
    ↓
attach policy certifications to contracts and grants
    ↓
pause new contracting warrants while the machinery is reviewed
    ↓
later GSA consolidation and FAR rewrite
```

The recurring institutional act is not “the center writes everything.” It is “the existing author cannot publish, classify, enforce, contract or spend without passing a new review point.”

## 1C.1 — January 20–31: rules wait at the doorway

### January 20 — the regulatory freeze creates an appointee gate

**Mover.** The President, acting OMB Director Matthew Vaeth, OIRA and newly designated agency heads.

**Institutional change.** The [Regulatory Freeze Pending Review](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/regulatory-freeze-pending-review/) barred agencies from proposing or issuing a rule until a post-inauguration appointee reviewed and approved it. Rules sent but not yet published were to be withdrawn; agencies were to consider 60-day postponements for selected published-but-not-effective rules. [OMB M-25-10](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/M-25-10-Implementation-of-Regulatory-Freeze.pdf) supplied the implementation path.

**Maturity.** Immediate operating publication and review gate.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The memorandum did not automatically repeal every pending or final rule. The 60-day delay was conditional, emergency and legal-deadline exemptions remained, and ordinary notice-and-comment or other statutory procedure still governed any durable change.

**Plotline.** Administrative time itself becomes a control surface: hold the object at the publication threshold until the new political principal sees it.

### January 21 — the contract becomes a policy and payment instrument

**Mover.** Agency heads, OMB, the Attorney General, the Department of Labor's Office of Federal Contract Compliance Programs, contracting officers and grant officials.

**Institutional change.** [EO 14173](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/ending-illegal-discrimination-and-restoring-merit-based-opportunity/) revoked EO 11246 but allowed contractors 90 days of continued reliance on the prior scheme. It directed agencies to put two terms into every contract or grant award: compliance with applicable anti-discrimination law was to be treated as material to government payment decisions under the False Claims Act, and the counterparty was to certify that it did not operate unlawful DEI programs. OMB was directed to remove DEI language from acquisition, contracting, grant and financial-assistance procedures.

**Maturity.** Legal direction and a prospective clause architecture. Existing instruments and the 90-day transition retained their own clocks.

**Counterevidence and limit.** A certification clause is not a finding that a contractor violated law, submitted a false claim or must be debarred. The order preserved lawful veterans' and Randolph–Sheppard preferences and First Amendment activity. A payment-materiality statement still requires an actual claim, legal standard and enforcement process before liability.

**Plotline.** The procurement object becomes a behavioral constitution: not merely “what is bought,” but “which institutional commitments become conditions of entry and payment.”

### January 31 — EO 14192 turns OMB into a regulatory budget gate

**Mover.** The President, OMB Director Russell Vought after his February 6 confirmation, agency heads and OIRA.

**Institutional change.** [EO 14192](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/02/06/2025-02345/unleashing-prosperity-through-deregulation) required agencies, unless law prevented it, to identify at least ten existing regulations for repeal when proposing or promulgating a new one. It directed a negative FY2025 incremental-cost total and future agency regulatory-cost allowances, required most rules to appear in the Unified Regulatory Agenda, and made OMB approval the exception gate. Its definition swept in rules, guidance, memoranda, administrative orders, policy statements and interagency agreements while exempting specified security, personnel and other categories.

**Maturity.** A binding executive-branch regulatory-budget direction; individual cost estimates, offsets, waivers and repeals remained to be produced.

**Counterevidence and limit.** “Ten for one” is not ten repealed CFR provisions on signature. The order required OMB implementation guidance, preserved statutory mandates and APA compliance, and allowed waivers. A listed offset is not a legally effective rescission until the controlling procedure completes.

**Plotline.** Regulation is recast as a balance sheet: every new burden must be priced, paired and cleared through the central budget room.

## 1C.2 — February 12–19: the independent rulemaker enters the White House room

### February 12 — the analytical baseline is changed

**Mover.** OMB Director Vought and agency regulatory-policy offices.

**Institutional change.** [OMB M-25-15](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/M-25-15-Recission-and-Reinstatement-of-Circular-A-4.pdf) revoked the November 2023 version of Circular A-4 and reinstated the September 2003 version. The memorandum was only one page, but its target was foundational: the common analytical grammar agencies use for benefits, costs and alternatives in regulatory analysis.

**Maturity.** Effective methodology switch.

**Counterevidence and limit.** Reinstating an analytical circular did not itself decide a rule, numerical cost, discount rate application or enforcement action. Each agency analysis still had to apply the governing law and administrative record to the particular object.

**Plotline.** The regime changes the ruler before measuring the structure. That can create methodological consistency, but it also lets the center define which values become legible as cost or benefit.

### February 18 — EO 14215 creates four independent-agency leashes

**Mover.** OMB, OIRA, the Domestic Policy Council, the National Economic Council, independent-agency chairmen and new Schedule C White House liaisons.

**Institutional change.** [EO 14215](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/02/ensuring-accountability-for-all-agencies/) joined four previously separable controls:

1. significant proposed and final actions from independent regulatory agencies would enter OIRA review;
2. OMB could set performance objectives for agency heads;
3. OMB could review obligations and adjust apportionments by activity, function, project or object, consistent with law; and
4. agency chairs would coordinate priorities and strategic plans with OMB, DPC and NEC through a White House liaison.

**Maturity.** The executive policy and organizational requirements were effective; new OIRA submissions were scheduled to begin within 60 days or when implementing guidance issued.

**Counterevidence and limit.** OIRA review is not authorship. The multi-member commission or statutory officer still held the legal authority assigned by Congress. OMB apportionment was not a rescission or presidential line-item veto. The order explicitly carved out Federal Reserve and FOMC monetary-policy conduct while retaining an executive claim over the Board's supervision-and-regulation functions.

**Plotline.** Independence is not erased as a legal-person category; it is surrounded by review, money, performance, planning and liaison leashes.

### February 19 — EO 14219 creates the DOGE/OIRA rulebook review

**Mover.** Agency heads, DOGE team leads, OMB, OIRA and the Attorney General where consulted.

**Institutional change.** [EO 14219](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/02/ensuring-lawful-governance-and-implementing-the-presidents-department-of-government-efficiency-regulatory-initiative/) required agencies to classify potentially unlawful, overbroad, costly or policy-inconsistent regulations and submit lists to OIRA within 60 days. OIRA and agency heads would then develop a Unified Regulatory Agenda seeking modification or rescission. The order also invited case-specific enforcement deprioritization and early consultation with DOGE/OIRA on potential new rules.

**Maturity.** Review, list and agenda architecture. The 60-day lists were not yet due in this wave.

**Counterevidence and limit.** A class list or agenda entry is not a repeal. A direction to deprioritize must still yield to statutory duties, public-safety obligations and applicable law. The agency head, not the DOGE lead, remained responsible for any case-specific enforcement decision.

**Plotline.** DOGE moves beyond people and payments into the rulebook, but as an embedded reader and consultant rather than a new statutory regulator.

## 1C.3 — February 26–March 19: contracting authority itself is reviewed

### February 26 — EO 14222 pauses new warrants and inventories the purchasing machine

**Mover.** Agency heads, contracting-policy offices, DOGE team leads, contracting officers, GSA and OMB.

**Institutional change.** Alongside the payment-justification system described in Wave 1B, EO 14222 required each agency to review its contracting policies, procedures and personnel within 30 days. During that review, agencies were not to issue or approve new contracting-officer warrants unless the agency head found the approval necessary. Afterward, agency heads were to issue guidance governing new contracts and modifications, while DOGE team leads supplied monthly informational reports.

**Maturity by March 19.** An active review period and a conditional warrant gate. The 30-day completion date and post-review guidance fell after the exact Aries handoff.

**Counterevidence and limit.** Existing contracting officers did not lose their warrants merely because the review began. The agency head could approve necessary new warrants and case-specific contracts. A monthly report did not transfer procurement authority to the USDS Administrator.

**Plotline.** The regime reaches below policy into the credential that lets an official bind the government. Before consolidating buying functions, it asks who may sign.

## What had changed by March 19

- New and unpublished rules were behind an appointee/OIRA review gate.
- OMB had changed the government-wide regulatory-analysis baseline.
- Agency regulatory costs were placed inside a negative budget and future OMB allowance structure.
- Independent regulators were ordered into OIRA, OMB, DPC and NEC review lanes.
- Agency heads and DOGE leads had begun a 60-day classification exercise on the existing rulebook.
- Contract and grant certifications were being redesigned as payment-materiality and policy-compliance objects.
- New contracting-officer warrants were conditionally paused during a 30-day agency review.

## What had not changed merely because these objects existed

- No executive order repealed an entire rulebook on signature.
- OIRA did not become the legal author of every agency rule.
- A regulatory agenda entry or class list was not a final rescission.
- A projected cost offset was not proof that ten rules had legally disappeared.
- Independent agencies did not lose every statutory decision right.
- OMB apportionment did not cancel an appropriation.
- A contract certification did not prove discrimination, falsity, debarment or payment recovery.
- The federal buying function had not yet been transferred wholesale to GSA.
- The FAR had not yet been rewritten.

## Astrological hearing

### Governing hierarchy

The strict governor is still the fixed-rising **2024 Aries ingress**, with **2024 Capricorn** as the nested winter frame. In this organ of the wave:

- Strict-governor Sun in Aries in the 6th, joined Neptune, describes executive identity entering the administrative rulemaking apparatus while clarity and boundary integrity become the central test.
- Mars, the chart ruler, acts from Aquarius in the 4th: systems, frameworks and the state's underlying architecture are the field of action.
- The Jupiter–Venus root loop keeps law, value, public benefit and relationship at the bottom of the argument; the rulebook is not merely technical.
- Venus conjunct Saturn and Jupiter sextile Saturn allow both poles: coherent discipline and legitimate simplification, or a constricting value regime disguised as neutral efficiency.
- In the nested Capricorn frame, Sun in the 3rd naturally emphasizes memoranda, clearance, written analysis and the formal grammar by which policy becomes executable.

The constructive pole is **legible, coherent administration**: duplicative rules are reviewed, costs are made comparable, a politically accountable center coordinates priorities and procurement officials know who may bind the government. The shadow pole is **procedural capture**: the review layer becomes a silent veto over independent judgment, statutory enforcement and lawful counterparties, while “cost” and “lawfulness” are defined by the same center that wants the outcome. The factual wave demonstrates the leashes; it does not adjudicate which pole ultimately governs each use.

## Handoffs kept open

- **March 20:** the exact 2025 Aries ingress begins a new governing year. The same day, EO 14240 begins the proposed GSA common-goods consolidation; it belongs to Wave 2 even though it continues this architecture.
- **March 26:** OMB M-25-20 supplies the implementation grammar for EO 14192 after the handoff.
- **April 3:** OMB M-25-21 and M-25-22 establish federal AI use and acquisition policy.
- **April 9 / May 7:** the unlawful-regulation repeal memorandum and M-25-28 deepen EO 14219; they still require applicable legal procedure.
- **April 15 / May 2:** the FAR overhaul order and M-25-26 begin the next procurement constitution. Agency class deviations later prove operating rule change; the model FAR text is not itself law.
- **April 17:** M-25-24 begins independent-agency OIRA implementation. That is the operational descendant of the February seed, not proof that the process was live by March 19.
- **Independent-agency money clock:** any named apportionment restriction must be separately retrieved; EO authority alone is not observed use.
