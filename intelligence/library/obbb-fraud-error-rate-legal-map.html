The One Big Beautiful Bill makes states pay for SNAP errors; it did not create a fraud rate

The 2025 law made states pay part of SNAP benefit costs based on their error rates and tightened a Medicaid error rule. It did not set a government-wide fraud rate, a single federal profile or any blockchain mandate.

What we found

  • Starting in FY 2028, a state's SNAP payment error rate sets its share of benefit costs: 0% below 6%, then 5%, 10%, and 15% at 10% or more. Before this law, the federal government paid all SNAP benefits.
  • The SNAP error rate counts both overpayments and underpayments, mostly unintended, and USDA and CRS say it is not a fraud rate. The House's 'zero tolerance' section was dropped from the final law.
  • USDA's FY 2025 rates, released June 24, 2026, put the national rate at 10.62% and New York at 13.18%, which would mean a 15% state share. Seven jurisdictions met the delay test: Alaska, Delaware, D.C., Georgia, Illinois, New Mexico and Oregon.
  • States' share of SNAP admin costs rises from 50% to 75% starting October 1, 2026, with no error test. Medicaid keeps its 3% allowed error rate but limits waivers from FY 2030, and adds new address, death-record and six-month eligibility checks starting in 2027.
  • The word 'mismanagement' does not appear in the law; USDA added it later. OPM's federal employee health plan verification rule took effect July 2, 2026, and a three-year audit window runs to July 4, 2029.

Why it matters

The law turns paperwork accuracy into a state money liability. That pushes states to build better identity, address and death-record matching, a strong incentive for shared verification systems without Congress ordering one database.

What to watch

  • 2026-10-01 · States start paying 75% of SNAP admin costs; watch state budgets for how they cover it.
  • pending · USDA's FY 2026 state error rates and each state's choice of FY 2025 or FY 2026 as the base for FY 2028 cost sharing.
  • 2029-10-01 · Deadline for CMS's cross-state system to catch people enrolled in Medicaid or CHIP in more than one state.
