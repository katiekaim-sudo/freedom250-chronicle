Wave 2B — Trade at the Border

Research window: Exact 2025 Aries ingress through exact 2025 Cancer ingress.
Question: Where did the new regime’s policy become an executable institutional machine most quickly, and what survived when the asserted legal authority later failed?
Finding: Trade was the Aries wave’s fastest conversion from command into operation. The reciprocal-tariff order did not merely announce a rate. Its annexes rewrote country and product treatment, inserted Chapter 99 HTSUS headings, named exact entry clocks, defined in-transit exceptions, assigned origin and U.S.-content tests, and routed implementation through Commerce, USTR, DHS/CBP, Treasury and USITC. That gave the policy an immediate border body. The later Supreme Court ruling did not prove that nothing happened; it severed the IEEPA authority for tariffs after the customs machine had operated for months. The emergency and non-tariff powers survived, while duty policy migrated to other statutes and new instruments.

This packet keeps tariff authority, customs schedule, importer liability, collection, economic incidence and later judicial reversal on separate clocks. It does not infer a cash amount or macroeconomic outcome from a rate or entry rule.

The chain in one view

presidential emergency and tariff policy
    ↓
country rates + product exceptions + HTSUS annex
    ↓
USITC schedule revision + CBP/ACE guidance
    ↓
origin, classification, value, entry date and importer-of-record test
    ↓
assessment / liquidation / payment or refund procedure
    ↓
trading-partner response and negotiated modification
    ↓
judicial authority cut and migration to other tariff statutes

The trade machine acquired more body than most Aries initiatives because the legal object already had a mature transaction edge: every covered import had to pass through classification and entry.

Annex discipline

EO 14257 cannot be read from its prose alone. The canonical vault note now owns all three annexes:

  • Annex I: country-specific rates;
  • Annex II: product exceptions; and
  • Annex III: HTSUS modifications.

Later orders often had no new annex because they modified the original headings and notes in their own operative text. A tariff roster that ignores the annexes loses the actual law-like object the importer and customs system had to apply.

2B.1 — April 2–10: command becomes entry logic

April 2 / April 5 — EO 14257 creates the executable chassis

Mover. The President; Commerce; USTR; DHS/CBP; Treasury; State; the National Economic Council; the National Security Advisor; the Senior Counselor for Trade and Manufacturing; and USITC.

Institutional change. EO 14257 declared a trade-deficit emergency under IEEPA and imposed:

  • a general 10 percent additional duty for covered goods entered or withdrawn for consumption beginning 12:01 a.m. EDT April 5;
  • scheduled country-specific rates from Annex I beginning April 9;
  • product and statutory exceptions in Annex II;
  • Chapter 99/HTSUS changes in Annex III;
  • in-transit treatment;
  • special Canada/Mexico and USMCA treatment;
  • a U.S.-content rule for articles with at least 20 percent U.S. value;
  • foreign-trade-zone status rules; and
  • implementation and reporting duties across the trade and security departments.

Maturity. Immediate rule and transaction-routing body. USITC’s archive records 2025 HTS Revision 7 for April 5, sourced to the order.

Counterevidence and limit. A live schedule does not establish who ultimately bore the economic cost, how much cash Treasury received, whether an entry was correctly classified or whether the asserted statute authorized the tariff. Those are later clocks.

Plotline. The executive command becomes a machine-readable border constitution. The decisive actor is no longer only the President; it is the importer presenting a classified object to CBP under the new heading.

April 8–10 — retaliation and alignment rewrite the machine in days

Mover. The President, the PRC State Council Tariff Commission, the same U.S. trade implementation stack and more than 75 trading partners reported as seeking talks.

Institutional change. EO 14259 raised the reciprocal China heading from 34 to 84 percent effective April 9 and increased scheduled low-value postal duties. EO 14266 then:

  • raised the China heading from 84 to 125 percent beginning April 10;
  • suspended the other country-specific Annex I headings for 90 days;
  • retained a 10 percent additional rate for those partners; and
  • changed the scheduled low-value China treatment again.

USITC recorded successive HTS revisions for April 9 and April 11. CBP issued operational guidance identifying the Chapter 99 heading and the April entry clocks.

Maturity. Active schedule and enforcement modification, not merely planned negotiation.

Counterevidence and limit. A heading and nominal rate do not prove a shipment’s origin, final liability or collection. Exclusions, stacking rules, USMCA treatment, in-transit exceptions and classification still matter.

Plotline. The border becomes a feedback surface. Counterparty behavior is translated back into the executable schedule almost immediately.

2B.2 — April 29–May 16: the machine learns priority and refund logic

April 29 — EO 14289 separates overlapping tariff authorities

Mover. The President; DHS/CBP; Commerce; Treasury; USTR; and USITC.

Institutional change. EO 14289 created a priority rule for overlapping automobile, Canada/Mexico emergency, steel and aluminum tariffs. It specified which duties could not stack, which steel/aluminum duties could, and which unrelated duties remained cumulative. It required system and HTSUS changes by May 16 and applied the non-stacking treatment retroactively to qualifying entries from March 4, with refunds through ordinary CBP procedure.

Maturity. The CBP implementation notice made the HTS change effective at 12:01 a.m. EDT May 16 and opened the refund-request path. USITC records Revision 13 on that date.

Counterevidence and limit. A refund procedure is not a computed or paid refund. Each entry still needs its classification, covered authority and procedural record.

Plotline. Once multiple emergency and national-security tariffs occupy the same object, the state needs an internal accounting constitution: which claim has priority, which claims can coexist, and how an overcollection returns.

2B.3 — May 8–14: confrontation becomes negotiated suspension

May 12 — EO 14298 turns talks into a new entry clock

Mover. U.S. and PRC negotiators, the President and the customs implementation stack.

Institutional change. After bilateral discussions, EO 14298 suspended 24 points of the original China reciprocal rate for 90 days, removed the later 84/125-percent escalation headings and restored a 10 percent reciprocal component beginning 12:01 a.m. EDT May 14. It also reduced the low-value ad valorem rate and retained a $100 postal-item alternative.

Maturity. An effective customs-rate change, recorded in USITC’s May 13 revision.

Counterevidence and limit. The order changed this reciprocal component, not every China duty. Section 301, fentanyl and other duties could remain under their own instruments. “Discussions” did not settle the entire trade relationship.

Plotline. The tariff is used as a negotiable term. Confrontation and accommodation are not opposite stories; they are two settings on the same schedule-based lever.

2B.4 — June 16: a partner-specific deal becomes a quota architecture

EO 14309 begins the U.S.–UK implementation chain

Mover. The President, U.K. Prime Minister Keir Starmer, Commerce, USTR, USITC and CBP.

Institutional change. EO 14309 established:

  • a 100,000-vehicle annual tariff-rate quota for U.K. automobiles at a combined 10 percent rate;
  • related treatment for qualifying U.K. auto parts;
  • removal of listed additional tariffs from qualifying civil-aircraft products after implementing notice; and
  • future, conditional steel and aluminum quotas dependent on Commerce’s judgment and U.K. supply-chain actions.

Maturity at Cancer ingress. Signed policy and quota design. Publication occurred June 23, after the Cancer ingress, and the operative U.S. implementation notices followed under the new seasonal frame. USITC’s July 1 revision records the auto, parts and civil-aircraft changes.

Counterevidence and limit. The signed order did not prove quota utilization, a vehicle entry, steel/aluminum quota creation, pharmaceutical preference or increased export sale. Some terms remained conditional or future.

Plotline. The general emergency chassis begins differentiating into negotiated bilateral terms, product books and supply-chain conditions.

2B.5 — February 20, 2026: the authority fails, not the historical operation

The Supreme Court later held in Learning Resources / V.O.S. Selections that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs. Congress had given the President powers to investigate, block, regulate and prohibit transactions in property; it had not silently delegated the power to lay duties.

EO 14389 ended collection of the listed IEEPA ad valorem duties as soon as practicable. It expressly left the national emergencies and non-duty actions in place and did not disturb §232 or §301 duties. A same-day §122 surcharge was a different, capped and time-limited statutory object.

The correct mature chain is:

effective customs machine → litigated statutory basis → IEEPA duty authority rejected → collection terminated → emergency/blocking powers survive → tariff policy migrates to separately authorized statutes

What this does not prove. The Court did not compute refunds, erase the historical entry records, end IEEPA blocking authority or hold that no President can impose any tariff under a statute that actually delegates duties.

What changed during the Aries period

  • The trade-deficit claim became a declared national emergency.
  • Country rates, exceptions and HTSUS headings created an executable customs object.
  • CBP and USITC repeatedly revised operational classification and entry treatment.
  • Trading-partner retaliation or alignment changed rates on exact clocks.
  • Low-value postal articles acquired their own duty choices and system conditions.
  • Overlapping tariff programs acquired priority, non-stacking and refund procedure.
  • Bilateral negotiation began producing product-specific quota and exception architecture.

What did not change merely because the orders existed

  • A headline rate did not determine every importer’s liability.
  • A customs assessment did not establish who bore the economic incidence.
  • An HTS revision did not prove a Treasury cash total.
  • A refund procedure did not prove refund payment.
  • A national emergency did not itself supply tariff authority.
  • An announced deal did not prove quota use, export volume, investment or production.
  • Later judicial invalidation did not mean the machine never operated.
  • Survival of the emergency did not preserve the invalid IEEPA duty.

Institutional theorem

Trade shows the reverse of Education. A department cannot be abolished merely by declaring it closed, but a border schedule can acquire body almost immediately because the inherited institutions already know how to execute it.

The regime succeeds fastest when it can seize an existing transaction throat:

  1. the importer must identify the good;
  2. the HTSUS supplies the classification grammar;
  3. ACE/CBP owns the entry gate;
  4. the duty attaches to a dated entry event;
  5. Treasury can receive what customs lawfully collects; and
  6. courts can later test the authority and order a different legal state.

The control plane becomes real where a required transaction already passes through a canonical record.

Both poles

Constructive pole — a legible negotiating and industrial lever

  • exact rates and exceptions are publicly specified;
  • counterparties can negotiate against visible terms;
  • customs systems can apply, correct and refund treatment;
  • product and supply-chain distinctions replace undifferentiated rhetoric; and
  • the policy can be disciplined by courts and statutory limits.

Shadow pole — emergency government as a programmable tax interface

  • an emergency declaration is treated as sufficient fiscal authority;
  • rates change faster than firms and customs systems can stabilize;
  • classification and origin become opaque pressure points;
  • executive negotiation moves the tax burden without legislative presentment; and
  • the public sees a headline rate while incidence, cash and refunds remain obscure.

The operating record proves the customs machine. The later legal record proves that a working machine can still be built on an unauthorized power.
