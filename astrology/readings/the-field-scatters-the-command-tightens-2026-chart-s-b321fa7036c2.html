Neither fact proves political fragmentation, coordination or control. Together they pose a better mundane question than a family label could: can a closed decision circuit perceive and govern consequences occurring across a widely occupied field?

Two structures with different jobs

  • Distribution geometry measures where the ten traditional bodies stand. The largest empty arc and its complement, occupied span, are factual geometry. They are not a named shape, score or political verdict.

  • Houses locate the actors on the Washington mundane stage. The same root circuit can perform a different job when its members occupy different houses.

Read together, distribution answers how much of the zodiacal field is occupied while the root answers where its sign-based authority resolves.

Phase breathing in the first half

From January through June 15, New Moons repeatedly carry a much larger empty arc than the intervening Full Moons. The New-Moon values are 187.48°, 198.36°, 199.78°, 198.20°, 194.13° and 183.22°. The corresponding Full-Moon values are 161.32°, 170.67°, 141.70°, 112.88°, 112.33° and 135.98°.

The geometry is astronomically intelligible. At New Moon, the Moon doubles the solar side of the wheel and can deepen an already large gap. At Full Moon, the Moon moves to the anti-solar side and often occupies some of that gap. The first half therefore breathes between a more concentrated seed field and a more distributed revelation field without requiring a named shape family.

The March 19 New Moon and March 20 Aries ingress are the most concentrated registered pair, with empty arcs of 199.78° and 199.77°. Their root circuits are different—Jupiter–Moon versus Mars–Jupiter–Moon—but both begin the Aries year with only about 160° occupied.

The July break

The Cancer ingress begins a five-chart corridor:

Cancer ingress → Jun 29 Full Moon → Jul 14 New Moon → Jul 29 Full Moon → Aug 12 eclipse

The crucial change is July 14. Its New Moon leaves only a 149.40° largest gap, far below the 183.22°–199.78° New-Moon range of January through June. The old phase alternation does not return. The Moon is final in Cancer, but the planetary field remains substantially more distributed than at the previous seed phases.

This means the midyear redistribution begins before the August eclipses. The eclipses do not create it from nothing; they continue a transition already visible after the Cancer ingress.

The late-year low-gap corridor

August 28 is the first registered chart whose largest empty arc falls below 104°. Every subsequent registered chart through December 24 remains below 93°. This is the defensible boundary: a measured low-gap corridor, not an automatic family classification.

The astronomy explains the transition. At the August 12 eclipse, the largest gap is 118.01° from Venus in early Libra to Pluto in early Aquarius. By August 28:

  • Venus has advanced through Libra, shrinking that gap;
  • the Full Moon occupies Pisces instead of doubling the solar cluster; and
  • Mars, Jupiter, Sun and Mercury establish separated Cancer–Leo–Virgo centres.

The September 11 New Moon cannot restore the earlier concentration because Venus has moved into Scorpio while the occupied centres remain distributed. The slow outer frame, inner-planet elongations and lunation geometry jointly limit how large an empty arc is available.

Low-gap geometry does not mean chaos, plurality or cooperation by itself. It means only that no very large unoccupied arc organizes the ten-planet set. Aspects, houses, roots and human Judgment decide what the distribution means.

The shadow pole is a self-confirming circuit: each actor derives authority from the next while consequences occur beyond the circuit's attention. The continuity case is simpler: the chart may describe many simultaneous arenas without any actual institutional consolidation.

The December 24 Moon supplies the clearest receipt. Does human need, public response, care, shelter, labor or the national body remain capable of correcting the machine from outside its recursive chain?

What this changes in normal analysis

  1. Distribution continuity: How did largest empty arc, occupied span and boundary bodies change from the preceding governing chart?
