Question: Is the Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond merely the physical host
for shared Federal Reserve technology, or is it one of the Reserve Banks pursuing
a distinct structural redesign through National IT and national procurement?
Short answer
Richmond is more than a server host, but less than a unilateral System
technology sovereign.
The public record now supports a real Richmond corporate-governance chain for
National IT:
System ITOC sets national technology direction and standards -> Richmond's
National IT Committee reviews performance, risk, security, controls and
budgets -> Richmond's president and System CIO manage the function -> the
Richmond board approves the National IT budget and System-CIO personnel
actions -> the Board of Governors and RBOPS retain supervisory, examination,
budget and material-initiative gates.
Richmond also houses the National Procurement Office, which develops and
executes certain contracts sourced collectively for multiple Reserve Banks and
supports National IT. That makes Richmond a corporate, executive, procurement,
security, infrastructure and accounting locus for the shared technology layer.
But different principals govern different objects. The Information Technology
and Operations Committee (ITOC) sets System IT policy and standards. Federal
Reserve Financial Services (FRFS) and the Chief Payments Executive define
payment-service business requirements and oversee product/software development.
Each Reserve Bank remains legally accountable for its own activities. The Board
of Governors retains examination, budget, pricing, material-change and capital
gates. Hosting, business control, legal operation, contracting, software control,
accounting ownership and payment-account liability therefore cannot be collapsed
into “Richmond owns the rails.”
The strongest current classification is:
Richmond is the Reserve Bank network's principal shared-technology corporate
host and an important national-contract execution node, operating inside a
federated constitution whose decisive inter-Bank terms remain nonpublic.
Why this changes the individual-Bank map
Earlier regional work correctly distinguished three visible structural emphases:
Kansas City has exposed the access gate, through the limited Tier 3 account
and the Kraken/Payward lifecycle;
Dallas has exposed the market-plumbing and liquidity-design lane, through
public work on Treasury-market structure, collateral and central clearing; and
New York remains the execution-and-market-infrastructure node, implementing
FOMC directives and operating the SOMA Desk rather than independently setting
national policy.
Richmond is the missing fourth type. It exposes the shared operating-control
layer: who sets technology policy, hosts national infrastructure, approves its
budget, manages the System CIO, signs collective contracts, allocates costs,
responds to incidents and remains accountable when the twelve corporations act
as one enterprise.
This is structural change through consolidation of operations, not a regional
breakaway. Richmond's role helps explain how the Reserve Banks can retain twelve
legal entities while centralizing technology and back-office execution.

The July 23, 2026 bylaws are a fresh control object
The live Richmond bylaws were amended and restated on July 23, 2026. They
supersede the 2022 bylaws used in earlier workbench research and therefore reset
the current local corporate-law baseline.
Five provisions matter for the National IT inquiry:
the board exercises general control over Bank committees and officers;
the president has general charge and control of Bank affairs, but the System
CIO is expressly excluded from the president's ordinary unilateral authority
to employ, dismiss and set compensation for Bank officers; and
the president may execute—or delegate authority to execute—contracts in the
Bank's name, supplying the local corporate-law path beneath NPO contracting;
unless another charter supplies a different rule, a majority of an “other”
board committee constitutes a quorum and members attending a quorate meeting
may act by affirmative vote, which now supplies the default voting rule for
the National IT Committee; and
the board may create committees and receives committee reporting, allowing
the National IT and Executive Committee charters to supply the operative
intermediate review chain.
Article VIII separately allows the board to adopt resolutions assigning authority
during a national emergency. That confirms a local emergency-delegation power,
but no current National IT or payment-service emergency resolution was located;
it does not answer ordinary incident command or application failover authority.
The supporting July 23 board resolution/minute is not yet public. Richmond says
its minutes are released in six-month batches six months after the meetings, and
no 2026 batch is listed at the cutoff. The amendment date is therefore an
occurred instrument date; the undisclosed deliberation and vote must not be
invented.

National IT is national even though Richmond is the host
The phrase “Richmond-hosted” must be read institutionally, not geographically.
Richmond's current technology recruiting page calls the Bank the home of
National IT while describing a nationwide workforce at other Reserve Banks
and remote locations.
The Board describes Federal Reserve Information Technology (FRIT) as the
portion of National IT at Richmond that supplies national infrastructure
and business-line technology. FRIT and National IT are not exact synonyms.
The 2025 Fedwire disclosures describe geographically dispersed data centers,
primary sites and tertiary failover capability without identifying their
locations or operating Banks.
The Richmond National IT Committee charter itself describes a unified national
organization whose people and operations exist inside and outside Richmond.
The host Bank supplies corporate legal capacity, board governance, executives,
procurement and accounting. The networked workforce, contracts and committee
principals prevent the host address from becoming proof of sole ownership.

What Richmond can be shown to control
Disclosed Richmond control or execution:
corporate governance of the Richmond Bank and its National IT operation;
National IT budget review, local-board approval and recurring performance/risk
oversight;
System-CIO appraisal and personnel/compensation routing through a multi-body
concurrence process;
operation of shared technology infrastructure and technical incident work;
execution of certain multi-Bank procurement contracts through NPO; and
accounting and redistribution of large shared computing/support costs.
Disclosed shared or externally gated authority:
ITOC supplies System technology direction, standards, security policy and
National IT performance assessment;
FRFS/CPE supplies payment-product requirements, software-development oversight
and service operations/protocols;
Conference of Presidents/Payments Committee supplies FRFS strategy and
enterprise oversight;
each Bank and board remains legally accountable; and
the Board/RBOPS supplies supervision, examinations, budgets and defined
material-change/capital gates.
Not shown as Richmond authority:
unilateral alteration of Fedwire, FedNow, FedACH or NSS legal rules;
payment/master-account access decisions for other Districts;
FOMC policy or New York Desk transaction direction;
unilateral System-wide IT policy, standards or exceptions;
ownership of every National IT application, vendor contract or data center;
power to bind every Bank outside the disclosed enterprise/inter-Bank
arrangements; or
unilateral release, emergency-change, failover or decommission authority for
every national application.

FRIT: the Board describes FRIT as a portion of National IT at Richmond;

Barkin's two hats: Richmond president and Payments Committee chair are

Research disposition and next gate
The “Richmond as technology center” hypothesis is confirmed with an important
qualification. Richmond is not merely physical infrastructure. It has a live
corporate board, committee, executive, procurement and accounting chain around
National IT. But the System-wide principals and veto/approval gates remain
federated.
The next research should not repeat biographies or generic cyber descriptions.
It should target the instruments that would turn this high-level RACI into an
auditable constitution:
recover the inter-Bank agreements and ITOC/Payments Committee charters;
map actual national contracts and application assets to legal Banks;
reconcile the $1,135,000,000 inter-Bank receipt to service, Bank, payroll,
vendor and capital objects; and
use the forthcoming 2026 Richmond board-minute batches to recover the July 23
bylaws action, 2026 budget operation and any change in System-CIO governance.
Until those records appear, the defensible wording is “Richmond-centered shared
technology governance inside a twelve-Bank federal enterprise,” not “Richmond
controls the Federal Reserve's technology and payments.”
