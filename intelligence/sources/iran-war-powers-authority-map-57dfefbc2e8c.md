# Iran War Powers — Authority Map

Updated: 2026-08-15  
Status: factual legal map  
Rule: A concurrent resolution is not a law. A War Powers letter is not an AUMF. The 60-day clock is not computed here. Keep this note off the strike shelf.

The Iran plotline and Operation Epic Fury (the strikes) stay **events**. This map is the leash.

## Short answer

No 2026 Iran declaration of war. No enacted Iran AUMF. **H.J. Res. 176** (Barrett) is introduced only — referred to House Foreign Affairs 2026-05-07. It is not law.

The President directed force on **2026-02-28** and sent Congress a written report dated **2026-03-02** (printed as **H. Doc. 119-139**). That letter rests on **Article II** (Commander in Chief and Chief Executive). It does **not** cite the 2001 AUMF, the 2002 AUMF, or any other statute as the force grant. Later 2026 letters (June 12, June 29, July 10, August 3) again cite Article II and say they are “consistent with” the War Powers Resolution. They do not enact authority.

**H. Con. Res. 86** is the only 2026 Iran instrument **agreed by both chambers**. House **215–208** (2026-06-03, Roll 199). Senate **50–48** (2026-06-23, Vote 184). Enrolled the same day. It is a **concurrent** resolution under WPR **§ 5(c) / 50 U.S.C. § 1544(c)**. A concurrent resolution is **not presented** to the President. It is **not** a public law. It cannot be vetoed because it never reaches the desk.

No later **joint** resolution directing removal was presented, vetoed, or enacted. Senate discharge votes on the joint-resolution path (**S.J. Res. 172, 180, 181, 184**; motion on **S.J. Res. 185**) all **failed**. **H.J. Res. 156** (clock-compliance joint resolution) is introduced only.

**Claim cut.** “Congress ended the war” is **not established**. What is established: both chambers **expressed** a § 5(c) direction, without a veto-proof statute, without presentment, and without an enacted AUMF.

**The 60-day clock result is not computed here.** § 1544(b) runs only from a report **submitted or required under § 1543(a)(1)** (hostilities). The March 2 letter’s **text** does not name a subsection. The **GPO/House header** on H. Doc. 119-139 classifies the communication under **§ 1543(a)(3)** (substantial enlargement) and **§ 1543(b)** — not (a)(1). Barrett’s **unenacted** recitals say the sixty days have expired. Those recitals are not a court finding and not a statute.

**Control cut.** Who may **start** force: Congress by declaration or specific statute; the President by Art. II within (and in tension with) § 1541(c). Who may **continue** past sixty days under the statute: Congress, by declaration, specific authorization, or a law extending the sixty days — or the President’s written 30-day safety certification (§ 1544(b)). Who may **order removal by statute**: Congress, by **joint** resolution or bill that is presented and either signed or passed over veto (§ 1546a). Who **spoke** on 2026-06-23: both chambers, by concurrent resolution, without presentment.

**Presentment rule, in short.** Art. I, § 7: a bill that has passed both chambers must be presented before it becomes a law. The same clause reaches every order, resolution, or vote that needs both chambers, except adjournment. A **concurrent** resolution is the form Congress uses when it does **not** send the paper to the President. That is why H. Con. Res. 86 can be “agreed” and still not be a statute. *INS v. Chadha*, 462 U.S. 919 (1983), holds that Congress may not alter legal rights and duties by a legislative veto that skips presentment. This map does not need the case name to see the cut: **no presentment, no law.**

## 3. What 2026 instruments actually exist

### A. Standing law (not Iran-specific)

**War Powers Resolution**, P.L. 93-148 (1973-11-07), 50 U.S.C. §§ 1541–1548. Live at cutoff.

- **§ 1541(a).** Purpose: collective judgment of Congress **and** the President on introduction into hostilities, or into situations where imminent involvement is clearly indicated, and on continued use.
- **§ 1541(c).** Commander-in-Chief introduction power “are exercised only pursuant to” (1) declaration, (2) specific statutory authorization, or (3) national emergency created by attack on the United States, its territories or possessions, or its armed forces.
- **§ 1542.** Consult in every possible instance **before** introduction; consult regularly after.
- **§ 1543(a).** Absent a declaration, 48-hour **written** report to the Speaker and the President pro tempore when forces are introduced (1) into hostilities / imminent hostilities, (2) into foreign territory, airspace, or waters equipped for combat (supply/replacement/repair/training excepted), or (3) in numbers that **substantially enlarge** combat-equipped forces already there. The report must set forth circumstances, constitutional and legislative authority, and estimated scope and duration.
- **§ 1543(c).** While forces remain in such a situation, report at least every **six months**.
- **§ 1544(b). The 60-day clock.** Within sixty **calendar** days after a report is submitted **or required** under **§ 1543(a)(1)**, whichever is earlier, the President **shall terminate** that use unless Congress has declared war or enacted a specific authorization, has extended the sixty days by law, or cannot meet because of armed attack on the United States. One written 30-day extension for unavoidable military necessity while bringing forces out.
- **§ 1544(c).** Notwithstanding (b), if forces are in hostilities abroad without declaration or specific statute, the President shall remove them if Congress so directs by **concurrent resolution**.
- **§ 1546a.** Expedited **joint** resolution or bill requiring removal; presentment; veto-message clock. This is the post-presentment path.
- **§ 1547(a).** No inference of authorization from an appropriation or a treaty unless the text says it is specific statutory authorization within the meaning of the chapter.
- **§ 1547(d).** The chapter does not grant the President any introduction authority he would not have had without it.

**2001 AUMF**, P.L. 107-40 (2001-09-18), note under 50 U.S.C. § 1541. Force against nations, organizations, or persons the President determines planned, authorized, committed, or aided the September 11 attacks, or harbored them. Congress declared it specific statutory authorization under WPR § 5(b). **It does not name Iran.** The 2026 Iran letters retrieved here do **not** cite it.

**2002 Iraq AUMF**, P.L. 107-243. **Repealed** by P.L. 119-60, div. H, title LXXXIII, § 8370(2) (2025-12-18). Not a live Iran grant.

## 5. Entity Theory read

The federal group is a stack of persons. **Control is not ownership.** Four clocks stay apart: **charter** (who may send force) · **notice** (the report and the sixty days) · **the body in theater** (who is actually shooting) · **the purse** (who pays).

Congress still holds the **declare-war charter**. It did not use it. It also holds the **AUMF charter**. It introduced one (H.J. Res. 176) and left it in committee. It holds a **removal charter** that works only if it uses a form that becomes law — a presented joint resolution, or the purse. It used the **old concurrent form** instead. Both chambers signed the same paper. The paper never became a group instrument that binds the President as law.

The President holds the **command charter**. He used it, on paper, as Article II plus a WPR-consistent report. He did not ask Congress for an AUMF. He did not cite the 2001 statute. The Department of War / Defense is the **operating subsidiary**. It moves when commanded. It does not own the war.

H. Con. Res. 86 is a **control signal** from the two legislative persons to the executive person. It is not a charter amendment. It is not a merger. It is not a wind-up. Treating it as “Congress ended the war” collapses expression into enactment — the same error as treating an EO as repeal of an organic act.

The 60-day provision is a **statutory terminate-use duty** that turns on a defined report. The President’s clerks (or the House clerk) labeled the first paper as enlargement, not hostilities. Congress, in an unenacted AUMF, labeled it as a lapsed clock. Those are two stories about the same letter. This map records both and does not pick a winner the documents do not pick.

Later June–August letters describe **new** shoots after a recited April 7 ceasefire and a June 17 MOU. Whether each shoot is a new introduction, a continuation, or a defense carve-out is a **new clock**, not a recap of February. Do not fold them into one war-ended / war-continues slogan.
