2026-06-25 · Executive Office of the President · Presidential Document · AI & computing

EO 14413 — Ushering in the Next Frontier of Quantum Innovation

Signed June 22, 2026 (published in the Federal Register June 25 as EO 14413), a whole-of-government sprint to keep America first in quantum. It stands up the QC-ADDS effort to build the first science-grade quantum computer and deliver it to an Energy Department lab, orders next-generation quantum sensors fielded by 2028, reconstitutes the National Quantum Initiative Advisory Committee, expands the quantum counterintelligence team, and ties the rising power of commercial quantum machines to the coming migration off today's breakable cryptography. The founding charter of the quantum build — sister to the Genesis Mission and the AI-security order. Chart cast for the signing day; provisional until the Federal Register assigns its number.

2026-06-25 · Executive Office of the President · Presidential Document · AI & computing

EO 14412 — Securing the Nation Against Advanced Cryptographic Attacks

Signed June 22, 2026 (published June 25), the defense half of the quantum pair: orders the whole federal government onto NIST post-quantum cryptography — every agency names a PQC migration lead within 30 days, high-value systems must use quantum-proof key establishment by end-2030 and signatures by end-2031, and federal contractors get dragged along through a coming FAR rule. The stated fear is 'harvest now, decrypt later' — adversaries hoarding today's encrypted traffic to crack it once their quantum machines wake up.

2026-06-17 · National Aeronautics and Space Administration · Press Release · Space

NASA Announces Public-Private Partnership To Advance Mars Science

Under NASA's first six-year reimbursable Space Act Agreement, NASA will provide the Aeolus atmospheric-science payload while Relativity Space supplies the spacecraft, rocket, and operations to deliver it to Mars for a 2028 launch. The deal is a commercial-innovation model meant to expand mission cadence and gather the atmospheric data needed for future crewed Mars landings.
