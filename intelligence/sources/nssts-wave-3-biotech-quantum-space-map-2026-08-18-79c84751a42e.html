Bottom line

Wave 3 adds 50 evidence-graded company edges and 43 primary-source objects. The combined standalone map now contains 125 company edges, 113 source objects and nine sector lanes.

The three sectors occupy sharply different maturity structures:

  • biotechnology has substantial funded activity, but the scarce proof is screening eligibility, repeatable scale, cGMP transfer, validated lots and customer or regulator acceptance;
  • resilient PNT already contains production incumbents, flown hardware and active receiver designs, while fault-tolerant quantum computing remains in government verification or risk-reduction stages;
  • space, hypersonics and missile sensing contain dense contract, task-order, launch and test rails, but launch, landing, flight and award states still do not automatically equal operational acceptance.

The governing path remains:

CET priority
  -> mission
  -> acceptance bottleneck
  -> federal owner and program
  -> contract / prototype / test / qualification lane
  -> exact legal company
  -> validated scale, field result, launch checkout or operational acceptance

Biomedical mission, regulatory evidence and manufacturing acceptance

Question and scope: A biomedical mission, credible evidence and a manufacturable therapy are complementary parts of the acceptance chain. EO 14355’s pediatric mission is maintained in the AI official inventory; the questions here are what supports a regulatory decision and what proves production. September 7 reconciliation follows the six named Tech records and directly identified returns; all original Wave 3 company counts and judgments retain their August 18 cutoff.

The January 2025 FDA draft proposes a context-specific credibility process: define the question and model role, assess influence and consequences, plan and perform assessment, document results and determine adequacy. It discusses independent testing, uncertainty and ongoing model maintenance. The January 7 notice is draft availability, not an approval. Drug discovery and unrelated internal efficiency uses are outside its stated scope. Neither the “deregulate” label nor AI participation establishes accepted evidence or an approved medicine.

The saved GIVE record’s URL points to THRIVE, a separate precision-genetic-medicine program. The correct GIVE launch release describes a September 25, 2025 manufacturing funding opportunity, with distributed production and automated quality assurance. The GIVE program concentrates on automated RNA manufacturing and integrated quality control. Do not convert domestic first-production ambitions into proof that an existing overseas capability was re-shored.

A September 1, 2026 ARPA-H return announces five teams and up to $125 million for development. Centillion Biosciences, HDT Bio, Massachusetts General Hospital and Waterfall Scientific cover manufacturing plus quality control; the University of Utah team covers integrated quality control. This is a real change from opportunity to named performers. It does not add accepted production capacity to the August 18 company registry or establish disbursement, affordable delivered therapy or patient benefit. The earlier solicitation anticipated other transactions, while the later release calls them contract awards; exact award instruments were not reviewed. The original planning-versus-build tests below still apply.

The planning-versus-build firewall

DBIMP planning awards identify important candidates but do not establish plant construction. Checkerspot, Industrial Microbes and other planning recipients move closer to the buildout only when a site, build instrument, equipment, commissioning clock and qualification plan appear.

BioMADE membership is not used as PATH evidence. BioMADE, ARMI|BioFabUSA and ATI remain noncompany program or consortium nodes.

Maturity ruling

Resilient PNT is substantially more mature than quantum computing:

  • Lockheed Martin is an operating GPS production incumbent.
  • Vector Atomic hardware was delivered, integrated and launched on X-37B, although public on-orbit performance acceptance remains missing.
  • BAE and Trimble hold current MAVRC design agreements, still before prototype downselection and certification.
  • Microsoft and PsiQuantum entered the final government verification-and-validation phase of US2QC; that stage is not a published utility-scale validation result. The maintained Quantum and Cryptographic Acceptance Map, “Three federal architectures must remain separate,” distinguishes US2QC from QBI Stage C even though DARPA describes their technical goals as equivalent. This is a program-label clarification, not a new company-performance census.
  • Atom, IBM, Quantinuum and QuEra remain in QBI Stage B or related risk-reduction and testbed lanes.
  • The 2026 quantum CHIPS actions are letters of intent, not definitive agreements or qualified foundry output.
  • IonQ has the broadest visible convergence path: computing and networking programs plus the optical-clock manufacturing contract. Its facility and qualified clocks remain future objects.

Space policy, authorization and mission acceptance

Question and scope: Space strategy, permission to operate and a delivery agreement answer different parts of how a mission reaches acceptance. These three original Tech questions belong beside the existing launch, checkout and performance distinctions, not in a separate package. September 7 review is bounded to the named instruments and release; the company map’s August 18 observations remain dated.

EO 14369, signed December 18 and published December 23, 2025, joins exploration, national-security architecture, commercial acquisition and space-nuclear policy. Correct the capture’s “fielding” shorthand: §2(b)(i) targets prototype missile-defense development and demonstration by 2028. Section 2(d)(ii) targets a lunar reactor ready for launch by 2030. Prototype, readiness, launch and operation are different results. Its agency plans and reviews need their own completion records.

EO 14335, signed August 13 and published August 19, 2025, directs lawful review of Part 450 and environmental burdens. It does not itself replace the licensing rule, waive required environmental law or license a particular launch. Commerce’s 150-day task is a proposal for individualized novel-space mission authorization excluding human spaceflight; coastal-consistency review, spaceport coordination and leadership changes are separate tasks. Subsequent rules, permits and decisions are the evidence of changed permission.

NASA’s June 17, 2026 Aeolus release, updated June 18, describes a six-year reimbursable agreement and planned 2028 launch with Relativity. NASA provides the four-instrument atmospheric payload, integration and science operations for at least one Martian year; Relativity provides spacecraft, rocket and cruise operations and maintains the spacecraft. The instruments address winds/ozone/temperature, clouds, energy balance and global images. This specifies a mission division of work; it does not show launch, science return or accepted performance. The executed agreement and reimbursement terms were not reviewed. These policy, permission and payload sources complement the company chains below without promoting their maturity.

Execution evidence stays attached

The map preserves adverse or incomplete results:

  • Blue Origin has a Lane 2 position but no FY2026 Lane 2 mission.
  • York spacecraft are launched, not yet accepted as an operational mesh.
  • Intuitive Machines IM-2 landed on its side and returned limited data.
  • Astrobotic received new awards after Peregrine failed to land.
  • AMDT3, FOO Fighter, HALO Europa and Astroscale remain build or demonstration clocks, not operating capabilities.
  • United Launch Services remains a core incumbent while Vulcan anomaly and mission-reassignment clocks remain visible.

Exact-entity seams

Corporate-family compression is especially unsafe in this wave:

  • Blue Origin LLC and Blue Origin Florida LLC occupy different NSSL award objects.
  • York Space Systems, LLC is the operating awardee; its parent remains distinct.
  • Capella Space Corp. survives as the SDA awardee after IonQ's acquisition.
  • Vector Atomic, Inc. remains the relevant sensor subsidiary even when IonQ is the parent and clock-manufacturing prime.
  • Dynetics Inc. is the glide-body awardee; parent Leidos has separate test-rail contracts.
  • Northrop Grumman Systems Corp. and Northrop Grumman Corporation must not be silently substituted.
  • BAE Systems IE&SI is not interchangeable with other BAE space subsidiaries.
  • Lockheed Martin Corporation and Lockheed Martin Services LLC occupy separate prime and task-order objects.

The remaining dedicated NSSTS sweep should close the three principal unmapped CET families:

  1. communications and networking;
  2. directed energy;
  3. information management and cybersecurity.

That wave should terminate at fielded network performance, accepted laser/microwave hardware or test result, and deployed/certified cyber, identity, software-supply-chain or operational-technology security—not at a generic product announcement.
