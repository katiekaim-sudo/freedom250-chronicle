Research summary

A multi-wave physical, commercial, identity, permission, payment and observation system for the Iran/Middle East war shock, Russian crude and product corridors, Venezuelan deal execution, U.S. production-processing-trading hub, global replacement supply and LNG. The September 15 package includes a validated working globe projecting governed routes without treating forecasts, permissions or targets as observed movement. Diesel has a dedicated network: ten material June 2026 U.S. destination lanes represent about 0.953 million barrels per day, while Russian rerouting and Northwest Europe replacement routes are retained only as historical baselines. A new supplier test finds direct Gulf replacement exposure in the United Kingdom and Netherlands, combined Russia/Gulf displacement in Brazil and mostly indirect competition for U.S. barrels across the selected Latin American buyers. The June U.S. network is overwhelmingly supplied from PADD 3/Gulf Coast, with PADD 5 support to Mexico, Chile and Peru. The current shortage is selective: global inventories and diesel/distillate are genuinely tight, while U.S. commercial crude is nearly flat year over year and jet, propane and forecast natural-gas storage do not show a national shortage. The United States already exports at globally material scale with or without Venezuela. Removing Russia sanctions would first lower shipping and payment friction and reroute crude already reaching the market; the public arithmetic production-to-capacity gap is about 0.94 million barrels per day and refinery damage still constrains the product most urgently needed.
