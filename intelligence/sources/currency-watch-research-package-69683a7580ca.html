G20 Currency Watch — Research Package

Status: active — six-system v1.1 pilot sealed; remaining eleven source rows frozen; Wave 1 TRY/BRL/RUB sealed after a controlled sequential rerun; Wave 2 readiness is next; a separate July 27 JPY authority/event delta is active without reopening the sealed card.
Evidence cutoff: 2026-07-20 for the frozen card program; 2026-07-27 for the dated JPY delta only.
Question: Are the G20 monetary systems doing the jobs assigned by the IMF Articles of Agreement, and how do tariffs, trade flows, invoicing, reserves, exchange policy and IMF transactions change that answer?

Short answer

Build the public surface as a currency watch, but grade the monetary system behind each unit. The Articles bind IMF members and their policies, not a banknote in isolation. The watch therefore needs three separate outputs:

  1. a categorical legal-status record for IMF obligations and any formal Fund finding, approval or exception;
  2. a regime-adjusted performance report card for price stability, exchange order, external adjustment, current-payment usability and resilience; and
  3. a trade-transmission record showing whether tariffs, invoicing, intervention, controls and financing actually move the balance of payments.

This is a cross-cut over the existing TOKEN / STATE-LEDGER / METAL / WALL / PLUMBING taxonomy. It is not a sixth pole. It measures the condition and use of the unit moving through all five.

Current JPY post-cutoff delta

The July 27 work records an authority and policy-message event, not a regrade. The morning Diet event carries the direct yen-confidence and BOJ-coordination propositions; same-day broadcaster reporting carries the evening fiscal and affordability wording until the official Kantei transcript appears. Current official data support the narrow June “lowest G7 headline CPI” claim, show a large policy contribution to that ranking, leave “highest G7 real-wage growth” unverified on a comparable panel, and confirm a material yen/contract-currency import-price wedge. The sealed JPY profile remains C/B/B/A/A/A.

Strongest early finding

The tariff lane is not merely adjacent to this watch. The live U.S. surcharge was imposed under 19 U.S.C. § 2132, “Balance-of-payments authority.” That statute permits a temporary surcharge or quota to address a large and serious U.S. balance-of-payments deficit, prevent imminent significant dollar depreciation, or cooperate in correcting international disequilibrium. The February 20, 2026 proclamation expressly used the deficit branch for covered entries, subject to listed exemptions and Section 232 non-stacking, and set a conditional July 24, 2026 end clock for goods entered for consumption or withdrawn from warehouse.

The court clock is separate. On May 7, the Court of International Trade held the proclamation unauthorized and granted plaintiff-specific relief. The Federal Circuit stayed that relief pending appeal on June 11, so collection continued while the appeal remained unresolved. Neither court event silently changes the scheduled July 24 clock.

But legal purpose is not demonstrated effect. The IMF's 2026 U.S. Article IV assessment of the broader U.S. tariff regime—not an isolated causal assessment of the Section 122 surcharge—said tariffs would have a relatively modest effect on the trade deficit while raising goods prices, reducing activity and causing spillovers. The Currency Watch should therefore grade the claimed adjustment tool against the later trade, price, saving-investment, FX and current-account record.

Scope

Included

  • the 19 country members of the G20;
  • the 17 unique currencies those countries use;
  • the euro as one currency object plus separate France, Germany and Italy policy records and a euro-area shared-policy overlay;
  • EU and African Union institutional context without inventing a separate AU currency;
  • Articles I, IV, V, VI, VIII, XIV, XIX and XXX; the Integrated Surveillance Decision and relevant Selected Decisions;
  • Article IV consultations, External Sector Reports, AREAER, WEO, BOP/IIP, reserves, exchange arrangements and official central-bank records;
  • tariffs, quotas, export controls, invoice/payment/funding currency, customs incidence, export-proceeds rules, intervention, capital controls, swap lines, IMF purchases/repurchases and SDR exchanges when they affect the watch.

Excluded from phase one

  • a prediction that one currency will replace another;
  • a single blended league-table score;
  • treating every intervention as manipulation;
  • treating a tariff announcement as a current-account improvement;
  • treating local-currency payment as de-dollarization of funding, hedging, collateral, insurance or reserves;
  • treating IMF purchases, SDR trades, central-bank swap lines and commercial FX swaps as one instrument.

Current research verdict

The useful question is not “which currency went up?” It is:

Did this monetary system preserve domestic and external order, permit adjustment and current payments, and carry its actual international load without shifting destructive costs into another lane?

That question lets tariffs, trade deals, sanctions, local-currency corridors, reserve shifts, IMF financing and private payment rails enter one watch without becoming one object.
