## Inclusion rules

- One entry = one legally or operationally meaningful case event (not one docket filing), imported from the audited Judicial Money Map. Descriptions state procedural effect, then institutional effect; no motives.
- Case IDs (JM-…) and event IDs (E-…) refer to [Judicial Money Map — Source Document](../sources/judicial-money-map-source-document-d2f844afe3d8.html), which carries the dossiers, crosswalks, party ledgers, and verification notes. The registry grows with each map revision — never trust a count written in prose; the seed builder prints the live totals.
- Eight tracks: **Throne** (removal & independence) · **Purse** (funding, rescue money, conservators & review bars) · **Gates** (Reserve accounts) · **Perimeter** (charters, preemption, market lines) · **Sword** (sanctions & emergency power) · **Hoard** (sovereign assets) · **Vault** (seizure, forfeiture & digital custody) · **Ledger** (payment data, privacy & AML).

## Charting convention (Katie's rulings, 2026-07-11 and 2026-08-09)

- **Anchor: HH:MM** — a verified clock time preserves the event's true instant. A non-D.C. source clock is converted to that instant, then displayed in Washington Eastern time and cast with Washington angles.
- **Anchor: daytime** — released or heard during the day, no verified clock → noon Washington time.
- **Anchor: date-only** — nothing about the clock is public (e-filings, signed orders, effective dates) → midnight Washington time.
- **Anchor place "unlocated"** — the source site remains flagged as unlocated; the chart is still Washington.
- Scheduled deadlines keep the standing rule: midnight Washington time, day of.

## Case dossiers (parsed into the view — keep the #### format)

One dossier per case: the caption, the question presented, the operative state at 2026-07-11, and the money stakes. Facts from [Judicial Money Map — Source Document](../sources/judicial-money-map-source-document-d2f844afe3d8.html) §1–2; update the **State** line as cases move.

#### JM-01 — Cook

**Caption:** Trump v. Cook — SCOTUS No. 25A312; below D.D.C. and D.C. Circuit  
**Question:** May the President immediately remove a sitting Fed Governor "for cause," without notice and a chance to answer — and is the Governor's tenure protection constitutional?  
**State:** Stay denied 2026-06-29. Fed Governor for-cause protection held constitutional; removal requires notice and an opportunity to respond. Cook keeps her seat and votes while the merits continue below.  
**Stakes:** One Governor's chair is one FOMC vote — and the ruling drew the constitutional fence around the Fed itself: the one agency the removal revolution doesn't reach.

#### JM-02 — Slaughter

**Caption:** Trump v. Slaughter — SCOTUS No. 25-332; below D.D.C.  
**Question:** Does the FTC's for-cause removal protection survive Article II?  
**State:** Reversed 6–3 on 2026-06-29: FTC protection unconstitutional; Humphrey's Executor no longer shields the modern commission. The Fed was expressly treated as a distinct historical arrangement.  
**Stakes:** Every independent financial commission except the Fed is now removable at will — the President's hand reaches SEC-style bodies, and only the central bank keeps its carve-out.

#### JM-03 — CFPB funding

**Caption:** CFPB v. Community Financial Services Ass'n — SCOTUS No. 22-448 (2024)  
**Question:** Is the CFPB's capped draw from the Federal Reserve a valid appropriation?  
**State:** Upheld 7–2 (2024): a standing source-and-purpose appropriation satisfies the Appropriations Clause.  
**Stakes:** Self-funding through the Fed is constitutional in principle — which made the later fight (JM-04) about starving the channel in practice, not its legality.

#### JM-04 — NTEU v. Vought

**Caption:** NTEU v. Vought — D.D.C. 1:25-cv-00381; D.C. Cir. 25-5091 & 26-5220 (en banc)  
**Question:** May the executive stop work, refuse funding, cancel contracts, and RIF an agency Congress created — and can courts stop it?  
**State:** Preliminary restraints partly hold; panel vacatur wiped by en banc grant; 2026-06-19 limited remand with jurisdiction retained. No final rule on how small the CFPB may lawfully become.  
**Stakes:** The live test of whether an agency can be killed by management rather than statute — the purse fenced but not filled.

#### JM-05 — Consumers' Research

**Caption:** FCC v. Consumers' Research — SCOTUS No. 24-354 (2025)  
**Question:** Did Congress unconstitutionally delegate revenue power over the Universal Service Fund, or the FCC subdelegate it to a private administrator?  
**State:** Upheld 6–3 (2025): adequate guidance, agency retains final say, private role advisory only.  
**Stakes:** The public-regulator/private-administrator funding machine survives — the closest analogue blessing hybrid money plumbing run through private bodies.

#### JM-06 — Custodia

**Caption:** Custodia Bank v. Federal Reserve Board & FRB Kansas City — D. Wyo.; 10th Cir. 24-8024; SCOTUS No. 26-62
**Question:** Does statutory eligibility give a state-chartered bank a right to a Reserve Bank master account?  
**State:** Tenth Circuit: no — Reserve Banks keep discretion to deny eligible institutions. Cert petition filed one day before the extended deadline; disposition pending.  
**Stakes:** THE gate case. Every crypto bank, fintech trust, and state money experiment needs the Fed's rails; whether access is a right or a favor is now before the Court — or settled for a generation if cert is denied.

#### JM-07 — Banco San Juan

**Caption:** Banco San Juan Internacional v. FRBNY & Board — S.D.N.Y.; 2d Cir. 25-1144  
**Question:** May a Reserve Bank terminate an existing master account under contract and risk process?  
**State:** Affirmed 2026-05-13: no statutory right to continued service; FRBNY's termination authority stands.  
**Stakes:** The gate swings both ways — an account once granted can be taken. Second and Tenth Circuits now converge on Reserve Bank discretion.

#### JM-08 — Fourth Corner

**Caption:** Fourth Corner Credit Union v. FRB Kansas City — D. Colo.; 10th Cir. 16-1016 (2017)  
**Question:** Must a Reserve Bank serve a state-chartered cannabis credit union?  
**State:** Fractured 1-1-1 judgment; amended lawful-purpose complaint allowed; no entitlement established. Later Custodia law rejects the entitlement reading.  
**Stakes:** The first crack at the gate question — three judges, three theories, no rule — showing why Custodia's published holding matters.

#### JM-09 — TNB USA

**Caption:** TNB USA v. FRBNY — S.D.N.Y. 1:18-cv-07978 (2020)  
**Question:** Did FRBNY unlawfully stall a "narrow bank" built to pass interest-on-reserves to depositors?  
**State:** Dismissed as unripe (2020) — no final denial, no merits ruling.  
**Stakes:** The narrow-bank idea — 100%-reserve money without lending — died in the waiting room, not in court. The gate can win by never answering.

#### JM-10 — Lacewell

**Caption:** Lacewell (NYDFS) v. OCC — S.D.N.Y.; 2d Cir. 19-4271 (2021)  
**Question:** May the OCC charter a national bank that takes no deposits (the fintech charter)?  
**State:** Merits vacated for ripeness — OCC never issued the charter, so its authority remains legally unresolved.  
**Stakes:** The unanswered question under every new OCC trust and stablecoin charter: nobody yet knows if the nondepository national charter survives a merits fight.

#### JM-11 — Cantero

**Caption:** Cantero v. Bank of America — SCOTUS 22-529 (2024); 2d Cir. on remand; new petition No. 25-1313  
**Question:** When does a federal bank charter preempt state financial law?  
**State:** 2024: nuanced Barnett-Bank "significant interference" test, no categorical preemption. On remand the bank won again (2026-05-05); a second cert petition is pending.  
**Stakes:** The method that will decide the GENIUS-era fights — whether a federal stablecoin charter displaces state money-transmission, reserve, and consumer rules.

#### JM-12 — Dealer Rule

**Caption:** Nat'l Ass'n of Private Fund Managers v. SEC — N.D. Tex. 4:24-cv-00250 (2024)  
**Question:** Could the SEC define proprietary Treasury-market liquidity providers as "dealers"?  
**State:** Rule vacated nationwide (2024-11-21); no appellate reversal located. The separate 2023 central-clearing mandate was NOT vacated.  
**Stakes:** Drew the perimeter of who must register in the Treasury market just as the clearing mandate rewires it — the plumbing rule survives, the perimeter rule died.

#### JM-13 — Van Loon

**Caption:** Van Loon v. Treasury/OFAC — W.D. Tex.; 5th Cir. 23-50669 (2024)  
**Question:** Are immutable smart contracts "property" of a sanctioned person under IEEPA?  
**State:** Fifth Circuit: no — immutable code can't be owned or controlled. Treasury delisted Tornado Cash 2025-03-21; the case ended after remand.  
**Stakes:** The first judicial boundary on sanctioning code itself: OFAC keeps power over people and mutable property, but the protocol layer slipped the blocklist.

#### JM-14 — Coin Center

**Caption:** Coin Center v. Treasury — N.D. Fla.; 11th Cir. 23-13698  
**Question:** Parallel Tornado Cash challenge — property theory plus First Amendment claims.  
**State:** Argued 2024-11-19; Treasury's delisting changed the operative state mid-appeal.  
**Stakes:** The twin that never got its own holding — mootness by agency retreat, a pattern this map sees repeatedly.

#### JM-15 — IEEPA tariffs

**Caption:** Learning Resources v. Trump / Trump v. V.O.S. Selections — SCOTUS 24-1287 & 25-250 (2026)  
**Question:** Does IEEPA's emergency import power include tariffs?  
**State:** Decided 2026-02-20: no — tariff power is a tax power belonging to Congress. Blocking and sanctions authorities expressly distinguished and preserved.  
**Stakes:** Clipped the broadest emergency-money tool while leaving the sanctions sword intact — the Court drawing lines INSIDE emergency power, not around it.

#### JM-16 — Bank Markazi

**Caption:** Bank Markazi v. Peterson — SCOTUS 14-770 (2016)  
**Question:** May Congress write an attachment statute for one identified pile of blocked central-bank assets in pending litigation?  
**State:** Upheld 6–2: §8772 changed the governing law without dictating findings; ~$1.75B went to terrorism-judgment creditors.  
**Stakes:** The precedent that makes "untouchable" sovereign reserves touchable by one statute — the exact template a Russian-assets vesting fight would test.

#### JM-17 — Collins

**Caption:** Collins v. Yellen — SCOTUS 19-422 (2021)  
**Question:** Could shareholders unwind the FHFA net-worth sweep, and was the Director's removal protection valid?  
**State:** Sweep attack barred by HERA's anti-injunction clause; single-director removal protection unconstitutional but no automatic unwinding without proven harm.  
**Stakes:** Government took the GSEs' entire profit stream and the courts couldn't touch the act — only the org chart. The purse's hardest lesson.

#### JM-18 — Perry Capital

**Caption:** Perry Capital v. Lew — D.D.C.; D.C. Cir. (2017)  
**Question:** Could courts enjoin the Third Amendment net-worth sweep as beyond conservator power?  
**State:** No — §4617(f) bars equitable relief while FHFA acts as conservator; contract claims took a separate, narrower path.  
**Stakes:** The review-bar template: while the conservator works, the courthouse door is shut. Foundational for every future resolution fight.

#### JM-19 — New York v. Trump

**Caption:** New York (19 states) v. Trump — S.D.N.Y. 1:25-cv-01144; 2d Cir. 25-1860  
**Question:** Was DOGE-team access to Treasury's central payment systems (and their PII) lawful?  
**State:** Modified preliminary injunction: access only for trained, vetted personnel under documented conditions. Appeal pending.  
**Stakes:** Who may read — and hold — the pipes every federal payment runs through. The operative restraint on payment-system access nationwide.

#### JM-20 — Alliance v. Bessent

**Caption:** Alliance for Retired Americans v. Bessent — D.D.C. 1:25-cv-00313 (2026)  
**Question:** Was the same Treasury data access reviewable under the APA?  
**State:** Summary judgment for Treasury (2026-03-05): intra-agency access by Treasury employees was not final agency action; ultra-vires review unavailable.  
**Stakes:** The twin that lost — showing the finality doctrine can swallow a payment-privacy claim whole. The S.D.N.Y. injunction (JM-19) is what actually binds.

#### JM-21 — NSBA

**Caption:** NSBA v. Yellen — N.D. Ala. (2024); 11th Cir.  
**Question:** Is the Corporate Transparency Act within Congress's enumerated powers?  
**State:** Held unconstitutional as to the named plaintiffs only; appeal's importance faded when Treasury exempted domestic entities (2025).  
**Stakes:** The constitutional question was never settled nationally — the reporting regime narrowed by agency choice, not judgment, and could widen the same way.

#### JM-22 — Texas Top Cop Shop

**Caption:** Texas Top Cop Shop v. Garland — E.D. Tex.; 5th Cir.; SCOTUS 24A653  
**Question:** Could one district court stop CTA beneficial-ownership reporting nationwide?  
**State:** Universal injunction stayed by SCOTUS 2025-01-23; FinCEN's 2025-03-26 rule then exempted all domestic entities — the operating state comes from the agency, not a final holding.  
**Stakes:** The whole domestic ownership-registry question now rests on a revocable rule. The database perimeter is policy, not law.

#### JM-23 — Forcht Bank

**Caption:** Forcht Bank v. CFPB — E.D. Ky. 5:24-cv-00304; 6th Cir. 25-6164  
**Question:** Did the 2024 open-banking (§1033) data rule exceed the statute and unlawfully lean on private standard-setters?  
**State:** Enforcement enjoined, compliance dates stayed; CFPB is rewriting the rule; appeal open.  
**Stakes:** Whether Americans' bank-account data becomes portable by mandate — and who writes the technical standards — is suspended mid-air.

#### JM-24 — Chatrie

**Caption:** Chatrie v. United States — SCOTUS 25-112 (2026); 4th Cir. en banc below  
**Question:** Is acquiring geofence location data a Fourth Amendment search?  
**State:** Yes (2026-06-29). Bank-records doctrine (Miller) expressly not decided.  
**Stakes:** The first crack in the third-party dam. If the logic reaches financial telemetry, the entire payment-surveillance ledger needs a warrant.

#### JM-25 — Culley

**Caption:** Culley v. Marshall — SCOTUS 22-585 (2024)  
**Question:** Does due process require a separate preliminary hearing while seized property awaits forfeiture trial?  
**State:** No — a timely merits hearing suffices (6–3).  
**Stakes:** The state may hold what it seizes for the duration. Applied to digital assets, custody-pending-forfeiture is the whole game (see JM-36).

#### JM-26 — Freeman

**Caption:** Freeman v. FDIC — D.C. Cir., 56 F.3d 1394 (1995)  
**Question:** May courts restrain the FDIC's receivership powers?  
**State:** Foundational no — §1821(j) bars it unless FDIC acts clearly outside statute; claims go through FIRREA's process.  
**Stakes:** Resolution happens before litigation finishes, always. The FDIC analogue of the HERA bar — and untested against the 2023-era estates.

#### JM-27 — Starr — US

**Caption:** Starr International v. United States — Ct. Fed. Cl.; Fed. Cir. (2017); cert denied 2018  
**Question:** Was taking 79.9% of AIG's equity as rescue consideration authorized by §13(3)?  
**State:** Trial court said no but awarded nothing; Federal Circuit vacated the merits ruling (claims belonged to AIG). No binding illegality holding survives.  
**Stakes:** The government kept everything the rescue took and no precedent says it couldn't — the vacated opinion is a ghost that haunts the next crisis.

#### JM-28 — Starr — FRBNY

**Caption:** Starr International v. FRBNY — S.D.N.Y.; 2d Cir. (2014)  
**Question:** Did FRBNY owe Delaware fiduciary duties to AIG shareholders during the rescue?  
**State:** No — federal common law displaced conflicting state fiduciary rules for a Reserve Bank's stabilization acts.  
**Stakes:** A Reserve Bank rescuing the system answers to federal function, not shareholder law — the public/private seam ruled in favor of the public hand.

#### JM-29 — Bloomberg

**Caption:** Bloomberg L.P. v. Board of Governors — S.D.N.Y.; 2d Cir. (2010); cert denied 2011  
**Question:** Were crisis-era borrower and loan records disclosable under FOIA?  
**State:** Yes — Exemption 4 rejected; the Board released borrower-level records 2011-03-31.

#### JM-30 — Fox

**Caption:** Fox News Network v. Board of Governors — S.D.N.Y.; 2d Cir. (2010)  
**Question:** Which Reserve-Bank-held records count as Board records for FOIA?  
**State:** Further search/disclosure ordered; Bank loan files are not automatically Board records — the structural distinction survives.  
**Stakes:** The twelve Banks' private corporate form still shields their papers unless the Board holds them — the transparency line runs through the System's hybrid anatomy.

#### JM-31 — Chrysler

**Caption:** In re Chrysler / Indiana Pensions v. Chrysler — Bankr. S.D.N.Y.; 2d Cir.; SCOTUS (2009)  
**Question:** Was the Treasury-financed §363 sale a sub-rosa plan that trampled secured-creditor priority, funded by TARP beyond its statute?

**Stakes:** The rescue playbook: move fast, close the deal, moot the challenge. Secured creditors learned priority bends when Treasury funds the buyer.

#### JM-32 — Murray

**Caption:** Murray v. Treasury & Board — E.D. Mich.; 6th Cir. (2012)  
**Question:** Taxpayer Establishment Clause challenge to AIG rescue funds touching Sharia-compliant products.

**Stakes:** Taxpayers as such can't reach the rescue power — standing doctrine guards the purse's outer wall.

#### JM-33 — Global Relief

#### JM-34 — Exxon

**Question:** Did OFAC give fair notice that accepting Sechin's signature violated Russia sanctions?

#### JM-35 — Gratkowski

#### JM-36 — Battle Born

#### JM-37 — Celsius

**Caption:** In re Celsius Network — Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 22-10964 (2023)  
**Question:** Did Earn-account customers or the estate own the deposited crypto?  
**State:** Terms of use transferred title — Earn assets are estate property; customers hold unsecured claims. Plan effective 2024-01-31.

#### JM-38 — BlockFi

**Caption:** In re BlockFi / Meghji v. United States — Bankr. D.N.J. 22-19361; Adv. 23-01144

#### JM-39 — Dames & Moore

#### JM-40 — NML/BCRA
