Crypto Infrastructure Hub — 2026-09-01

Purpose

This package owns the governed source registry and generated projection for the Observatory's first-class Crypto Hub reference view. Tokenization remains a major use case, but the governed scope is broader: who steers, builds, operates, regulates, connects and settles each crypto infrastructure stack?

The hub's central finding is that the relationship web is dense while actual corporate control is sparse. Ownership, minority investment, board influence, registration, commercial partnership, operating structure, policy advocacy, technical integration, asset flow, historical origin and proposed-rule overlap are therefore separate edge classes. Proposal overlap marks where a current capability touches the SEC's proposed architecture without turning that overlap into approval or a final-rule compliance finding. Ripple, XRPL, RLUSD and XRP remain separate objects; Securitize's affiliates remain separate regulated legal entities; and Wormhole remains an interoperability layer rather than an issuer, transfer agent, custodian or title authority.

The registry currently contains 225 nodes, 259 typed relationships, 151 source objects, 19 governed ecosystem profiles, three governed process maps, nine governed asset traces, eight analytical lenses and eight operational clocks. The profiles cover Ripple/XRPL, Securitize, Ethereum, Hedera, Stellar, Canton, Solana, Avalanche, Chainlink, Bitcoin, DTC/DTCC tokenization, cash legs and tokenized bank money, Circle/USDC/Arc, Coinbase/Base/Tokenize, Algorand, XDC, IOTA/TWIN and bounded Quant orchestration, with Wormhole now visible as its own interoperability stack. Each follows a common walkthrough: steering, operation, execution, native asset, external asset authority, access, technical finality and legal settlement or remedy.

The schema-v4 process layer answers a different question from an ecosystem profile or an asset trace: for each lifecycle stage, which exact ecosystem controls, operates, integrates or depends on the relevant rail? The three initial maps are Tokenization, Stablecoins, and FX & cross-border. Each uses ten readable macro stages backed by the deeper claim, authority, admission, funding, instruction, execution, clearing, technical-state, authoritative-record, cash, finality, posting, servicing, correction, default, backstop and proof families. Every visible role references existing governed nodes, relationships and primary sources. Operating clock, scale evidence and stress evidence remain separate dimensions.

Ethereum appears in all three process maps, but its role is not the same in each. It operates live execution and consensus for tokenized assets and native USDC state and transport. In the FX map it appears only as a transport layer: the registry does not assign Ethereum the currency quote, conversion, funding, clearing, payout, issuer, reserve or redemption functions.

Ripple's tokenization view now makes three different collateral ideas visible without treating them as one capability. Fubon Bank's 2023 e-HKD pilot is a historical external dependency in Underlying & collateral: the tokens represented bank charges over property on a private ledger built with XRPL technology, not title deeds or assets controlled by Ripple on the public XRPL. Licuido remains admission, issuance and market-access buildout because its collateral-mobility announcement does not prove custody or control of the underlying asset. Evernorth and Pathfinder are separately identified XRP treasury and custody actors, not tokenized-asset controllers; their pending combination, restricted pre-close XRP and proposed XLS-66 participation do not fill a tokenization process cell.

Hedera, Solana, Avalanche, Stellar, Algorand and XDC now receive the same native-USDC treatment in the Stablecoins map: each operates token-state execution and network transport, while Circle and its named counterparties remain external dependencies for issuer authority, reserves, liability records, direct redemption and remedy. Hedera also appears in FX only for one completed Aberdeen-Lloyds-Archax pilot in which tokenized fund units and gilts moved as collateral. Canton separately records one completed Treasury-for-USDCx cash-leg event while leaving USDCx issuer and redemption terms unresolved. Neither single-event receipt establishes a production corridor or general service.

The SEC proposal-overlap view makes the proposed accountability sequence inspectable from issuer and recordkeeping transfer agent through the authoritative master securityholder file, linked systems, wallet records, governance, reporting, corrections and downstream services. The network view separates protocol governance, operators, execution layers, native-token economics and named product connections rather than collapsing a company, network and token into one object.

Boundaries

  • Evidence cutoff: 2026-09-02.
  • SEC Release 34-106246 / S7-2026-30 is a proposal, not a final rule.
  • A registration describes the named legal entity and permitted role; it is not approval of every affiliate, product, chain or transaction.
  • A minority investment or board seat is not control unless a source proves control.
  • A technical bridge moves messages or supported representations; it does not itself determine beneficial ownership, legal title, custody or finality.
  • A chain's native fee, burn, staking or governance economics describe network utility; they do not determine the authoritative securityholder record or establish value capture from a tokenized product.
  • Technical finality, the authoritative legal record and the cash or redemption remedy are separate endpoints. A DTC token, Base wallet, trade credential or bank-money representation does not inherit legal title merely by settling on a chain.
  • DTC limited production in July 2026 does not backdate the expected October general-service launch; Arc private mainnet does not make the planned September 16 public mainnet or its announced founding-validator cohort live; Circle National Trust's effective charter does not prove reserve-management activation or operating volume; Ripple and Coinbase preliminary charter approvals do not make those proposed trust banks open.
  • Coinbase's minority Circle stake and USDC distribution/reserve-income economics are material company relationships, but they do not make Coinbase the USDC issuer, reserve obligor or sole stablecoin-network governor.
  • Fubon Bank's 2023 e-HKD pilot proves one historical home-equity-lending test in which tokens represented property liens or bank charges on a private ledger built with XRPL technology. It did not tokenize title deeds, use the public XRPL mainnet, establish a current production service or transfer the authoritative property record to Ripple or XRPL.
  • Licuido's announced collateral-market mobility supports Ripple's admission, issuance and market-access buildout, but it does not prove custody, immobilization or duplicate-issuance control for an underlying asset.
  • Evernorth and Pathfinder remain legal entities separate from Ripple. Their reported pre-close XRP holdings and BitGo custody arrangements describe a corporate treasury path; the pending combination and proposed Single Asset Vault/XLS-66 participation do not prove a completed listing, operating lending volume, collateral control or a tokenization or FX route. XLS-66's documented protocol design is uncollateralized lending, not an underlying- asset collateral system.
  • Company announcements support the stated relationship and status only; they do not prove scale, universal production use or token value capture.
  • A process cell is not a yes/no capability score. It records one exact stage-specific role and its evidence ceiling. An operator or integrator does not inherit the controller's authority, the cash system's finality or a public institution's backstop.
  • Ethereum confirmation can establish USDC token state and transport without establishing Circle's liability record, reserve position, direct redemption, an FX conversion, cross-border purpose or recipient bank payout.
  • Native USDC support on Hedera, Solana, Avalanche C-Chain, Stellar, Algorand and XDC likewise proves a supported token-state and transport rail, not issuer authority, use volume, FX conversion, bank payout or stressed-redemption performance.
  • The Aberdeen-Lloyds-Archax event proves one Hedera-based tokenized-collateral pilot for FX trades; the collateral movement is not either currency leg and does not establish an FX quote, conversion, clearing or production corridor.
  • The completed Canton Treasury-for-USDCx event proves one synchronized tokenized-security/tokenized-cash exchange. It does not establish the exact USDCx issuer or redemption chain, recurring volume or bank cash payout.
  • Blank process cells mean that no governed primary-source receipt is mapped; they do not prove that a capability does not exist.
  • The map is Workbench evidence and an Observatory projection, not investment advice, a price forecast or a vault doctrine owner.
