Wave 3 — Federal Institutions, Functions and Records Timeline

Research window: January 20, 2025 through September 2, 2026.

Governing correction from Katie

The overarching analytic lens is an economic institution legitimacy crisis, not merely an economic institutional crisis.

The sources and actors do not need to use the phrase “legitimacy crisis.” This timeline identifies observable proxies that can bear on legitimacy: bypasses and workarounds, exit or refusal, conflicting authority claims, repeated non-delivery, selective treatment, opacity, reliance shifts, uncorrected failure, loss of institutional monopoly or recognition, burden allocation and remedy failure.

No event below, by itself, proves that legitimacy rose or fell. Direct means the event bears directly on the grounds of legitimacy—lawful authority, responsibility, delivery, transparency, contest or correction. It does not mean public acceptance was measured. The accepted-legitimacy clock remains unobserved unless a separate receipt establishes it.

Legend

Legitimacy-proxy codes:

  • AC — authority conflict
  • BY — bypass or workaround
  • ND — non-delivery or capacity loss
  • OP — opacity or record gap
  • RS — reliance shift
  • ST — selective treatment
  • RM — remedy or correction
  • LM — loss of institutional monopoly or recognition
  • BA — burden allocation
  • PC — positive-control modernization

Clock codes:

  • TC — technical capability
  • AD — adoption or operation
  • LA — legal authority
  • SS — standards or supervision
  • AR — authoritative record
  • FR — failure or remedy
  • AL — accepted legitimacy

Δ identifies the clocks changed by the dated object. = identifies material clocks that did not change merely because the object existed. Charter, personnel, function, money and court clocks remain distinct throughout.

Audit-level observations

These are timeline-design findings, not thesis conclusions:

  1. The accepted-legitimacy clock is not measured here. The record supplies legitimacy proxies and institutional facts, not a direct public-trust series.
  2. The legal-person clock changes least often. Most movement is in personnel, routing, data, standards, procurement, interfaces and review rights.
  3. Courts and Congress operate on separate clocks. They narrow carriers, restore functions, permit limited procedures, preserve charters or lawfully cut accounts; they should not be flattened into binary wins and losses.
  4. Several of the strongest legitimacy indicators are mismatches: - operational control moves while legal responsibility stays; - information sharing expands before an authoritative inventory exists; - exclusion and screening capability advances before correction is demonstrated; - staffing contracts while statutory duties remain; - guidance and deviations become operative before unified final rules; and - AI deployment advances faster than public control evidence.
  5. Positive-control cases belong in the same timeline. Defend the Spend, digital retirement intake, eOPF, Federal HR 2.0, the SEC accounting unit and NARA’s AI-record guidance are genuine attempts to rebuild capacity, visibility or remedy.
  6. The rival explanations remain live. Many events can be read as ordinary modernization, efficiency reform, lawful political control or fragmented technological adaptation rather than legitimacy deterioration. The legitimacy reading strengthens only where authority conflict, non-delivery, opacity, unequal burden or failed correction is independently evidenced.
  7. No one master plan is established. Convergent effects across personnel, money, records, procurement and AI do not by themselves prove centralized coordination across every domain.

Claim firewall

  • An executive direction is not a final rule, completed migration, transaction or lawful outcome.
  • A proposal is not law; an enacted law is not implementation.
  • A personnel plan, petition, classification or ratio is not an individual action.
  • A public feed is not a complete ledger; a machine-readable record is not necessarily true.
  • A risk signal or match is not eligibility, debt, fraud or guilt.
  • A contract ceiling or award is not an obligation, acceptance, adoption or performance result.
