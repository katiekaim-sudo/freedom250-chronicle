SEC Credit-Rating Recordkeeping Orders — Maturation Audit, 2024–2026

Bottom line

On September 3, 2024, the SEC entered six settled administrative orders against nationally recognized statistical rating organizations for failures to retain electronic communications about credit-rating activity. The orders fixed the legal state immediately: all six firms admitted the facts and violations, accepted censures and cease-and-desist relief, and incurred civil penalties totaling more than $49 million.

The later state is narrower. Four firms—Fitch, HR Ratings, Moody's and S&P—were required to retain independent compliance consultants. Those undertakings created a review, report, implementation, one-year evaluation, internal-audit, discipline-notice and certification process. A.M. Best and Demotech did not receive the consultant mandate because the SEC credited their earlier efforts and cooperation. The consultant cases therefore support remediation required and underway, but the public orders alone do not support remediation successfully completed.

What the September 3 objects established

The SEC's release and the six orders identify the firms and penalties:

Firm Release Penalty Independent consultant
A.M. Best Rating Services 34-100902 $1 million no
Fitch Ratings 34-100903 $8 million yes
HR Ratings de México 34-100904 $250,000 yes
Demotech 34-100905 $100,000 no
Moody's Investors Service 34-100906 $20 million yes
S&P Global Ratings 34-100907 $20 million yes

The common failure was not merely use of a fashionable messaging app. The orders concern communications about initiating, determining, maintaining, monitoring, changing or withdrawing ratings that the firms were required to retain. In the Fitch order, the SEC found widespread and longstanding use of texts, WhatsApp and WeChat, including at senior levels, and found that the missing records likely affected the Commission's examinations and investigations. That distinction matters: the enforcement object concerns the regulator's ability to reconstruct and test a consequential decision process.

The four-firm remediation clock

The Fitch order is representative of the mandatory consultant structure. It requires:

  1. consultant retention within 30 days of the order;
  2. the engagement letter to SEC staff within 60 days of the order;
  3. a comprehensive review within 90 days of that engagement letter;
  4. a detailed report within 45 days after the review;
  5. adoption of recommendations within 90 days of the report, subject to a defined dispute-and-alternative process;
  6. a second evaluation beginning one year after the main report;
  7. separate internal-audit work;
  8. two years of prompt discipline notices to SEC staff;
  9. six-year preservation of compliance records; and
  10. written certification no later than 60 days after completion of the undertakings.

These are procedural maximums, not proof of actual milestone dates. The order also permits SEC staff to extend procedural dates for good cause. A public reader therefore cannot reverse-engineer the actual consultant report date or the completion date from the order alone.

Why March 3, 2026 is an information boundary, not a completion receipt

The main consultant report and related written communications were intended to remain nonpublic except for a court order, party agreement, SEC determination, or other legal requirement. The one-year evaluation begins only one year after the report. Depending on the actual engagement, report and extension dates, the second review could have been beginning, underway, completed, or not yet due on March 3, 2026. The public order does not resolve which state applied.

That creates a precise evidence ladder:

final order and admissions → mandatory remediation process → nonpublic consultant findings → later evaluation and certification

Only the first two rungs are established by the public objects reviewed here. The existence of a mandate is not the same as a public receipt for its success. The nonpublic-report clause is not itself evidence of concealment or failure; it is an explicit feature of the order's compliance design.

Bounded current check

A bounded search of the SEC's public site through September 25, 2026 found the 2024 press release, the six orders, later SEC listings and staff references to the orders, but did not surface the confidential consultant reports or a public SEC completion determination for the four consultant undertakings. This is a retrieval finding, not proof that no report, certification, extension or staff communication exists.

Source and comparison boundary

This package was selected from the official political record independently of any astrology interpretation. Its value to a later comparison is negative as well as positive: the same source can prove an enforcement seed and a required maturation process while refusing the stronger claim that the public later saw the decisive remediation evidence.
