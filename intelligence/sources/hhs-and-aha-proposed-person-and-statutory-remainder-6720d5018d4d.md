# HHS and AHA — Proposed Person and Statutory Remainder

Updated: 2026-08-15  
Status: factual legal map  
Rule: A press release is not a charter. A budget justification is not an organic act. A preliminary injunction is not a merits repeal.

This note is the **third emptying**. [Department of Education — Consummation Map](../sources/department-of-education-consummation-map-23a7ccec3cea.html) is the Department that still exists. [USAID — Statutory Person and Operating Death](../sources/usaid-statutory-person-and-operating-death-a4950011ce2c.html) is the independent establishment that still exists. This note is HHS: the Department still exists; SAMHSA still exists; the Administration for a Healthy America is a request. Do not merge. Do not braid the MAHA Commission into AHA.

## Short answer

The Department still exists. House OLRC prints 42 U.S.C. § 3501 as law in effect on August 14, 2026. Reorganization Plan No. 1 of 1953 created the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare. 20 U.S.C. § 3508 redesignated it the Department of Health and Human Services on May 4, 1980. No retrieved statute repeals that person.

SAMHSA still exists. 42 U.S.C. § 290aa(a), text in effect August 14, 2026: “The Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration … is an agency of the Service.” It is headed by an Assistant Secretary for Mental Health and Substance Use, appointed by the President with the advice and consent of the Senate. § 290aa(c)(1). No retrieved statute repeals that sentence.

The Administration for a Healthy America is a **proposed** operating box. The March 27, 2025 HHS Communiqué announced it. The FY 2026 AHA Congressional Justification is the **inaugural request**. The HRSA Administrator’s transmittal: “We look forward to working with Congress **to create** the Administration for a Healthy America.” A request to create is not a charter.

Congress did not write the FY 2026 check to AHA. P.L. 119-75 (Feb. 3, 2026) still appropriates under the headings **Health Resources and Services Administration** and **Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration**. The words “Administration for a Healthy America” do not appear in that public law.

*New York v. Kennedy*, D.R.I. No. 1:25-cv-00196 (July 1, 2025): preliminary injunction against implementing the March 27 RIFs and sub-agency restructuring as to the sub-agencies named in the motion. A PI is not a merits holding. It is not a repeal of § 3501 or § 290aa.

Four clocks. None of them is the others.

**Control cut.** Who may **create** AHA: Congress, by a statute or an enacted heading that names it. The Reorganization Act’s transmit clock in 5 U.S.C. § 905(b) ran out on December 31, 1984. Who may **empty** SAMHSA’s building: the Secretary can try, inside law. Who may **repeal** § 290aa: only Congress. Those are different leashes.

## 2. Four clocks

**Clock 1 — statutory existence.** Live for HHS. Live for SAMHSA. **Not established** for AHA.

- HHS: Reorg. Plan No. 1 of 1953 § 1: “There is hereby established an executive department, which shall be known as the Department of Health, Education, and Welfare.” 20 U.S.C. § 3508(a): that Department “is hereby redesignated the Department of Health and Human Services.” 42 U.S.C. § 3501 still points at the 1953 plan. Text in effect August 14, 2026.
- SAMHSA: 42 U.S.C. § 290aa(a) still names the Administration as an agency of the Public Health Service. The Assistant Secretary is PAS. § 290aa(c)(1).
- AHA: no establishment statute retrieved. The FY 2026 CJ says the Administration “plans to establish” AHA and asks Congress **to create** it. A 5 U.S.C. § 903 reorganization plan for HHS in 2025–26: **not retrieved**. 5 U.S.C. § 905(b): a reorganization-plan provision may take effect only if transmitted on or before **December 31, 1984**. That path is dead.

**Clock 2 — personnel.** Cut claimed; enjoined in part.

The March 27 Communiqué: when combined with early retirement and “Fork in the Road,” “a total downsizing from 82,000 to 62,000 full-time employees.” Regional offices “reduced from 10 to 5.” That is an agency announcement. It is not a headcount audit.

*Kennedy* (July 1, 2025; revised August 12, 2025) enjoins further execution of existing RIF notices, issuance of further RIF notices, and additional administrative-leave placements as to a **named** list (FDA CTP; Head Start; specified CDC components including NIOSH; ASPE’s poverty-guidelines shop). **AHA is not on that list. SAMHSA is not on that list.** Official 2026 HHS headcount after that order: **not retrieved**. A PI is not a restored-workforce census.

**Clock 3 — function transfer.** Announced. Not enacted.

March 27 Communiqué would create AHA by combining OASH, HRSA, SAMHSA, ATSDR, and NIOSH; move ASPR into CDC; stand up an Assistant Secretary for Enforcement over DAB / OMHA / OCR; merge ASPE with AHRQ into an “Office of Strategy”; fold ACL programs into ACF, ASPE, and CMS; consolidate 28 divisions into 15.

The FY 2026 AHA CJ is not the same list. It says AHA would combine programs “previously administered through” OASH, HRSA, SAMHSA, NIEHS, “and several centers of” CDC. Two official papers, two absorption lists. That is the tell: the proposal is not a charter.

Reorg. Plan § 6 lets the Secretary authorize another officer or agency **of the Department** to perform the Secretary’s functions. That is internal delegation. It is not a repeal of § 290aa. Reorg. Plan § 7 lets the Secretary centralize procurement, budget, personnel, and other common services — “Provided, That no professional or substantive function vested by law in any officer shall be removed from the jurisdiction of such officer under this section.”

**Clock 4 — appropriations.** Still pointed at the old headings.

P.L. 119-75 (Feb. 3, 2026) still writes:

- **Health Resources and Services Administration** — Primary Health Care $1,858,772,000; Health Workforce $1,413,776,000 (further HRSA subheadings follow in the same title).
- **Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration** — Mental Health $2,790,407,000, including the Mental Health Block Grant $991,532,000 and other statutory set-asides under titles III, V, and XIX of the PHS Act.

**“Administration for a Healthy America” does not appear** in P.L. 119-75. NIOSH remains an enacted CDC heading. Division B, Title II, **§ 239**: HHS “shall support staffing levels necessary to fulfill its statutory responsibilities,” and the Secretary must submit a detailed public plan to the appropriations committees **not less than 60 days** before moving CDC functions to another HHS component, relative to how those functions are funded in the Act. A 60-day notice is a condition. It is not a transfer. It is not an AHA charter.

The FY 2026 AHA CJ asked for $20.6 billion ($14.1 billion discretionary; $6.5 billion mandatory and other). A request is not an enacted heading.

## 3. The March 27 Communiqué — what it orders and what it cannot

HHS press release, March 27, 2025: “HHS Announces Transformation to Make America Healthy Again.” Archived official HTML retrieved. The *Kennedy* court calls this paper the “March 27 Communiqué.”

**Announces.** 28 divisions into 15, including AHA. Centralize HR, IT, procurement, external affairs, and policy. Regional offices 10 to 5. Workforce 82,000 to 62,000 with early retirement and Fork in the Road. AHA would combine OASH, HRSA, SAMHSA, ATSDR, and NIOSH. ASPR to CDC. New Assistant Secretary for Enforcement. ASPE + AHRQ → Office of Strategy. ACL programs into ACF / ASPE / CMS. “This reorganization will not impact Medicare and Medicaid services.”

**Cannot.** A press release cannot repeal 42 U.S.C. § 290aa. It cannot create a new executive department or a new statutory administration. It cannot rewrite P.L. 119-75. It is an agency instrument. Treat it as Clock 3 paper.

## 4. The FY 2026 AHA justification — a request, not a person

HHS, *Fiscal Year 2026 — Administration for a Healthy America — Justification of Estimates for Appropriations Committees*. Inaugural CJ. Transmitted on **HRSA letterhead** by Thomas J. Engels, HRSA Administrator.

Load-bearing sentences:

> The Trump Administration **plans to establish** the Administration for a Healthy America (AHA) as an outcome-driven institution in public health.

> I am pleased to transmit the inaugural Congressional Justification of the Administration for a Healthy America (AHA) **request** for the Fiscal Year (FY) 2026 Budget.

> In alignment with the **proposed** Department of Health and Human Services reorganization, AHA combines several overlapping disease prevention and chronic care programs…

> We look forward to working with Congress **to create** the Administration for a Healthy America…

The dollar line in that letter is $20.6 billion total ($14.1 billion discretionary; $6.5 billion mandatory and other). That is the request. Congress answered by funding HRSA and SAMHSA.

## 5. *New York v. Kennedy* — what the PI actually does

**Cite:** *State of New York et al. v. Kennedy*, C.A. No. 25-cv-196-MRD-PAS (D.R.I. July 1, 2025) (DuBose, J.). Memorandum and Order granting ECF No. 43. RECAP: `gov.uscourts.rid.59507.73.0.pdf`.

**What the court said it was reviewing.** The March 27 announcement that HHS would cut employees, reorganize sub-agencies, cut regional offices in half, “and create a new division called the Administration for a Healthy America.”

**Likelihood of success (PI posture).** The States showed a likelihood of success that the action was arbitrary and capricious and contrary to law. Constitutional claims not reached. Standing found. CSRA channeling rejected. Tucker Act rejected. March 27 treated as final agency action for APA review.

**July 1 operative injunction.** Defendants “are ENJOINED from taking any actions to implement or enforce the planned RIFs or sub-agency restructuring announced in the March 27 Communiqué … **with respect to the specific sub-agencies and programs that are the subject of the instant motion**,” including further RIF execution, new RIF notices, and additional administrative leave. Stay of the PI denied in the same order.

**August 12 revised injunction (ECF 89).** The list is named: (1) FDA Center for Tobacco Products; (2) Head Start and Head Start regional staff; (3) CDC components NCHHSTP, Division of Reproductive Health, **NIOSH**, Office on Smoking and Health, NCEH, and NCBDDD; (4) ASPE’s Division of Data and Technical Analysis (federal poverty guidelines). **AHA is not named. SAMHSA is not named.** NIOSH — which the Communiqué said would be combined into AHA — **is** named, as a CDC component.

**Conclusion sentence (not a merits holding):**

> The Executive Branch does not have the authority to order, organize, or implement wholesale changes to the structure and function of the agencies created by Congress.

**Later court paper, not walked as holdings.** First Circuit stay denied September 17, 2025 (*New York v. Kennedy*, 155 F.4th 67) — a stay denial, not a merits affirmance. The Government then dismissed the appeal. Motion to dismiss the amended complaint denied April 7, 2026. No merits judgment at cutoff. A PI is Clock 2 / Clock 3 paper. It does not flip Clock 1.

## 6. MAHA is a different object

EO 14212 (Feb. 13, 2025) established the President’s Make America Healthy Again Commission. Later orders (14221, 14355, 14407, 14414) cite that Commission. A commission is advice and coordination. It is not AHA. It is not SAMHSA. Do not write “MAHA created AHA.”

## 8. Entity Theory read

The federal group still consolidates a Department of Health and Human Services. Inside that Department, Congress still names SAMHSA as an agency of the Public Health Service and still writes the check to SAMHSA and HRSA.

Control of the *story* moved. HHS announced a new person, drafted a budget as if the person were real, and put the request on HRSA letterhead. That is the same move as Education and USAID, with one extra twist: here the replacement vehicle was never chartered. Education still has § 3411. USAID still has § 6563. SAMHSA still has § 290aa. AHA has a Communiqué and a request.

The entity-chart flags “SAMHSA dissolving” and “AHA new” name the **intent** clock. They do not flip Clock 1. Leave the SAMHSA node up until Congress repeals § 290aa. Do not promote AHA to a statutory person until Congress writes one.

A hollowed administration is still a legal person if the Code says it is. A requested administration is not a legal person because a justification says “create.”
