# Wave 4D — Institutional Continuity, Hiring and Human Systems

**Research window:** Exact 2025 Libra ingress through exact 2025 Capricorn ingress.  
**Evidence cutoff:** June 20, 2026, 11:59 p.m. EDT. Later facts are quarantined as handoffs.  
**Question:** Did five Libra-period orders all restructure the state, or did they operate at very different depths—from preserving advisory bodies and closing offices for two days to controlling every vacancy, changing pay tables and rebuilding foster-care data and account interfaces?  
**Finding:** They operated at very different depths. EO 14354 preserved twenty-two advisory committees and delegated their Federal Advisory Committee Act administration; it did not prove that every committee met or produced advice. EO 14356 replaced a temporary leadership-approval phase with a durable vacancy-by-vacancy hiring gate, annual staffing plans and quarterly updates; OPM/OMB guidance made the system administratively operative, although no complete public agency-plan corpus exists. EO 14359 acquired the richest human-services descendants: a new child-welfare performance pilot, licensing guidance and a state-to-Federal Trump Account route for foster youth. EO 14368 became official pay tables and a one-percent statutory-pay increase with locality percentages held at 2025 levels. EO 14371 produced two actual closure days with public-need exceptions and then expired. The packet therefore shows **selective continuity around a deeper change in who may enter, how vulnerable people are measured and matched, and how the state-worker relationship is priced**.

The common ladder is:

`institution retained or program named → decision gate → record / plan / table → agency or State implementation → person-level effect → appeal / correction / continuity test`

## 4D.1 — September 29: continuity is actively selected

[EO 14354](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/10/03/2025-19284/continuance-of-certain-federal-advisory-committees) continues twenty-two named advisory committees until September 30, 2027. The list crosses preservation, fellowships, telecommunications security, industrial security, trade and environment, HIV/AIDS, intellectual disabilities, radiation and worker health, physical fitness, veterans' small business, intergovernmental affairs, national monuments, religious liberty, HBCUs and science and technology.

It also delegates presidential Federal Advisory Committee Act functions to named department heads. That matters because continuation is not passive. The order chooses which consultation rooms remain legally available and who administers them.

**Maturity.** Effective legal continuation and administrative delegation.

**Counterevidence and limit.** A committee's continued charter is not proof that it met, had a quorum, issued recommendations or changed a decision. Nor does the list prove that omitted advisory bodies were abolished; each has its own authority and termination clock.

## 4D.2 — October 15: the hiring freeze becomes a recurring selection interface

[EO 14356](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/10/20/2025-20434/ensuring-continued-accountability-in-federal-hiring) belongs to a longer sequence:

1. the January 20 hiring freeze temporarily blocked covered vacancies and new positions;
2. an April extension carried the general freeze through July 15;
3. the July 7 memorandum required presidentially appointed leadership approval through October 15; and
4. EO 14356 replaced that expiring phase with a standing management architecture.

The order generally bars filling a vacant Federal civilian position or creating a new one unless the action complies with law, the Merit Hiring Plan and the new process. Each agency must establish a **Strategic Hiring Committee** chaired by the deputy agency head and including the chief of staff. The committee approves each vacancy. Agencies must send written records of approved hires to OPM, create annual staffing plans with OPM and OMB, and update those plans quarterly beginning in fiscal-year 2026's second quarter.

The process has broad exceptions—among them EOP positions, political appointments, noncareer SES, intelligence and national-security functions, immigration and public-safety roles, temporary organizations, agency-head exceptions and OPM exemptions. The order also forbids using contracts merely to evade the hiring restriction and says implementation must not impair Social Security, Medicare or veterans' benefits.

### Guidance turns the order into an operating management system

OPM and OMB issued [implementation guidance on November 5, 2025](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/implementing-executive-order-14356-ensuring-continued-accountability-in-federal-hiring.pdf). It preserved the existing one-hire-for-four-departures constraint until an agency's staffing plan was approved, supplied committee and plan instructions, and required quarterly maintenance. OPM's March 18, 2026 description of its workforce agenda treated Strategic Hiring Committees and staffing plans as operating components rather than future proposals.

This is a real decision-right change. A program office may still know whom it wants, but the vacancy must pass a cross-agency management gate and enter a central record before the person can be hired.

**Maturity.** Effective administrative rule plus government-wide implementation guidance; agency committees and plans represented by OPM as active.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The public record in this packet does not contain every committee membership, vacancy approval, annual staffing plan, quarterly update or the order's 180-day OPM/OMB report. The process does not repeal merit principles, veterans' preference, appropriations limits or statutory appointment qualifications. Approval of a vacancy is not selection, onboarding or productive work.

## 4D.3 — November 13: foster care becomes both a measurement problem and an ownership interface

[EO 14359](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/18/2025-20982/fostering-the-future-for-american-children-and-families) establishes the Fostering the Future initiative and directs HHS to modernize child-welfare data, create an annual scorecard, improve caregiver recruitment and matching, use predictive analytics and AI where appropriate, expand educational and scholarship routes, encourage faith-based participation, recover returned funds and design an online platform through the National Design Studio.

The important institutional question is who is being changed. The order does not Federalize State foster-care custody. It asks HHS to change the Federal measurement, support and resource interfaces through which State, territorial and Tribal systems are judged and assisted.

### December 2025: the scorecard idea enters an actual review process

[CFSR Technical Bulletin 14](https://www.cfsrportal.acf.hhs.gov/document/download/WdZJrn) launched the **A Home for Every Child** Program Improvement Plan pilot. It lets jurisdictions in different stages of Child and Family Services Review Round 4 orient improvement work around safe prevention, home availability and permanency rather than the prior prescriptive PIP design. Later technical instructions identify a home-to-child core measure and four related measures and provide a data-upload template.

This is not merely a press release. It changes the program-improvement interface for participating jurisdictions: the measure, data submission and Federal review route. But a new measure can improve focus or narrow reality. A higher home-to-child ratio does not by itself establish placement quality, child safety, cultural fit or durable permanency.

On May 5, 2026, ACF issued [updated national model foster-family-home licensing standards](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/), including kin-specific standards and updated responsibilities for licensing agencies. These are Federal model standards and guidance, not automatic amendments to every State code or completed home license.

### June 2026: the initiative acquires an account carrier supplied by a separate statute

On June 11, Treasury and the First Lady announced [Fostering the Future Accounts](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0530). A State, territorial or Tribal child-welfare agency that is legal guardian for an eligible child with a Social Security number may elect to open an initial Trump Account by filing Form 4547. Treasury said agencies could direct qualifying survivor benefits into the account, subject to the annual contribution limit. HHS/ACF issued final guidance on June 12, and twenty-three governors had publicly pledged participation.

The legal vehicle is crucial: the One Big Beautiful Bill Act created Trump Accounts under 26 U.S.C. § 530A. EO 14359 and the First Lady's initiative did not create the tax account. They created a guardian-and-administration bridge so a child without a conventional parent account-holder could enter the statutory vehicle.

Treasury's June 11 remarks said the accounts would launch July 4. At the June 20 evidence cutoff, the eligibility rule, agency route, form and State pledges existed; an identified foster child's opened and funded account did not yet appear in the public record.

**Maturity.** Operating Federal program-improvement pilot; revised licensing guidance; final account guidance and a multi-State commitment architecture.

**Counterevidence and limit.** Guidance and pledges are not account opening, deposit, investment performance or benefit preservation. Routing survivor benefits into a long-term account can build ownership, but it also raises custody, immediate-needs and fiduciary questions. The public platform, national scorecard and every State implementation remained incomplete.

## 4D.4 — December 18: the state-worker relationship is converted into tables

[EO 14368](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/23/2025-23844/adjustments-of-certain-rates-of-pay) does not reorganize the workforce. It translates a presidential alternative pay plan into ten schedules for General Schedule, Foreign Service, Veterans Health Administration, Senior Executive Service, Executive Schedule, Vice President and Congress, judicial officers, uniformed services, locality payments and administrative law judges.

OPM's [January 2026 Pay Adjustments memorandum](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/january-2026-pay-adjustments.pdf) supplies the operative reading:

- a **1.0 percent across-the-board increase** for statutory pay systems;
- locality percentages held at their 2025 levels;
- tables generally effective on the first day of the first applicable pay period beginning on or after January 1—January 11 on the standard biweekly cycle;
- Executive Schedule rates raised one percent, rounded to the nearest $100;
- SES, SL/ST, ALJ and related systems carried through their own statutory rules; and
- selected law-enforcement positions receiving an additional special-rate increase to about **3.8 percent total**, under OPM authority.

The [Washington-Baltimore-Arlington table](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/pay-leave/salaries-wages/salary-tables/26Tables/html/DCB.aspx), for example, incorporates the one-percent General Schedule increase while keeping the locality percentage at 33.94 percent. The tables are the execution layer. They determine authorized gross rates; payroll systems and each employee's grade, step, position, cap and status determine the person-level receipt.

**Maturity.** Complete legal schedules, OPM implementation memorandum and published salary tables, followed by applicable payroll effective dates.

**Counterevidence and limit.** “One percent” is not a universal one-percent paycheck increase. Political pay freezes, pay caps, SES performance determinations, special rates, locality, military tables, judicial rules, deductions and effective-pay-period clocks differ. The order does not prove a particular net deposit.

## 4D.5 — December 18: two closure days demonstrate bounded executive administration

[EO 14371](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/23/2025-23847/providing-for-the-closing-of-executive-departments-and-agencies-of-the-federal-government-on) closes executive departments and agencies on December 24 and December 26, 2025. Agency heads may retain offices or employees on duty for national security, defense or other public need, and the days are treated within existing pay and leave statutes.

The event matured completely and briefly: the dates occurred, OPM administered the operating status and the order exhausted itself. That makes it useful negative evidence. Not every executive order is a seed of lasting regime architecture.

**Maturity.** Bounded operational closure with agency-level exceptions.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The order did not create a permanent holiday, amend office charters, guarantee leave to every worker or suspend essential functions.

## The human-systems theorem

These five objects reveal two different state behaviors.

The first is **continuity maintenance**:

- retain selected advisory rooms;
- publish the annual price of public labor; and
- pause ordinary offices without interrupting essential government.

The second is **upstream selection**:

- require a central committee and staffing plan before a vacancy becomes a hire; and
- define the data, ratio, matching system, guardian and account route through which a foster child becomes legible to Federal policy.

The legal persons mostly survive. The regime changes the interfaces around them. The deepest question is not “Was the institution abolished?” It is **who controls entry, what record counts as need or merit, and which relationship the system is designed to preserve**.

## Both poles

### Constructive pole — selection becomes accountable stewardship

- advisory knowledge remains available instead of being discarded wholesale;
- workforce growth is tied to an explicit plan, written approval and public priorities;
- child-welfare measurement can focus on safe homes and permanency rather than administrative volume;
- foster children gain an account-opening route that conventional family structures otherwise deny them;
- pay rules become explicit and operational; and
- essential government continues through bounded closure days.

### Shadow pole — care and merit become central classification systems

- selected consultation can become curated legitimacy;
- central hiring gates can privilege political alignment and slow mission staffing;
- AI matching and a single ratio can make children legible while flattening their lived circumstances;
- asset ownership can substitute rhetorically for immediate services and accountable guardianship;
- a small nominal pay adjustment can coexist with workforce pressure; and
- emergency exceptions can make the burden of continuity uneven.

Both poles remain live. Committee minutes, vacancy records, State plans, placement outcomes, account receipts, payroll records, grievances and audits—not the astrological rhyme—show what matured.
