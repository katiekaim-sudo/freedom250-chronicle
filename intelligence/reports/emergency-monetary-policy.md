# Federal Emergency Technology and Crypto Activation Map

**As of:** 2026-07-15  
**Status:** source-first U.S. federal authority map; pending review and selective vault incorporation  
**Read first for:** U.S. federal contracting, pre-positioning, activation and control questions

**Broader context:** `FINTECH_CRYPTO_INFRASTRUCTURE_DEEP_DIVE.md` and `EMERGENCY_EVENT_MONETARY_POLICY_RESEARCH_PACKAGE.md`
**Boundary:** institutional research, not legal advice and not a forecast of an emergency

## Short answer

The federal government does not have one switch that turns a private entity's technology into emergency infrastructure.

The realistic sequence is:

`commercial capability -> eligible legal entity -> ordinary contract or regulatory permission -> operational authorization and integration -> continuity pre-positioning -> incident-specific task/order/facility -> exit, review and loss allocation`

An emergency can shorten procurement, prioritize a contract, mobilize industrial capacity, assign a federal mission, open a liquidity facility, or restrict financial transactions. It does **not** normally cure a missing charter, appropriation, system authorization, settlement account, sanctions program, reserve arrangement or authoritative record.

For the monetary plotline, the practical rule is:

> The federal system does not suddenly “pick a coin.” It activates an already permissioned legal entity, product and operating connection under a particular authority.

Crypto's shortest route into a national-security or national-emergency event is therefore not a public token becoming sovereign money. It is an already regulated stablecoin, custody, messaging, settlement or orchestration service being used as a supplemental rail inside an existing federal, bank, card, clearing or Treasury-controlled process.

## The minimum three-clock threshold lens

These three clocks answer the narrow question of when a private technology can enter federal use. They do not replace the emergency-policy package's fuller clocks for continuity/finality, liquidity/collateral, guarantee/resolution, and exit/loss allocation.

| Clock | What changes | Controlling question |
|---|---|---|
| **Technology clock** | software, wallet, chain, validator, API, custody or orchestration is technically available | Can it perform the function under stress? |
| **Legal-object clock** | a particular token, deposit claim, stablecoin, security or reserve asset becomes eligible | What is the claim, who owes it, and may this entity issue, hold or transfer it? |
| **Activation clock** | a named official uses a named authority for a named incident or program | What factual predicate, determination, order, contract or declaration activates it? |

The same brand can occupy several clocks through different subsidiaries. The contract may be with a cloud or wallet company; the regulated object may be issued by a trust or bank subsidiary; final dollars may move through a settlement bank; and the public network may supply only transport.

## Degrees-of-separation map

This is a plausibility and distance map, not a mandatory chronological escalation. Degrees 4–6 are parallel statutory routes that can be used separately or together; a DPA priority order does not require an earlier FEMA task, and an IEEPA control regime does not require the government to procure the controlled technology.

| Degree | Federal state | What has to be true | What it does **not** prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — commercially available** | private capability exists | product can be bought or licensed | federal eligibility, security approval or crisis role |
| **1 — legally eligible** | entity/product has the necessary charter, license, registration, reserve structure or exemption | the exact legal person and exact product may perform the exact function | contract award, account access or operational integration |
| **2 — contracted, system-authorized and connected** | contract/vehicle plus agency system authorization, interfaces, identity, audit, reconciliation and settlement arrangements exist | agency can lawfully and operationally use it | emergency designation or priority |
| **3 — pre-positioned** | continuity plan, disaster registry, rated-order readiness, alternate-site plan, telecom priority or incident playbook names the capability | the connection can be activated faster | automatic use in every incident |
| **4 — incident activated** | contracting officer, regulator, FEMA, Treasury, Federal Reserve, DoD or another authorized official makes the required determination/order | a defined function is used for a defined incident and period | permanent regime change |
| **5 — priority or capacity mobilized** | Defense Production Act or urgent-acquisition findings support priority, allocation or industrial expansion | delivery/capacity can outrank ordinary commercial demand | ownership transfer or blanket commandeering of the company |
| **6 — transaction/control regime** | National Emergencies Act-linked authority, IEEPA, sanctions, communications or other specific statute is invoked | specified property, transfers, communications or counterparties may be controlled | general power over all technology or all crypto |

The most important plotline implication is that an emergency rewards **pre-positioning**. A company already at degrees 2–3 is much more plausible than a technically superior company still at degrees 0–1.

## Federal activation routes

### 1. Ordinary and emergency procurement

An agency can acquire private technology without any emergency declaration. Federal Acquisition Regulation Part 18 then identifies flexibilities for emergency acquisitions. Several are available whenever their underlying conditions are met, without a formal emergency declaration. Other triggers include a contingency operation, defense or recovery from a cyber or CBRN attack, international disaster assistance, or a presidential emergency/major-disaster declaration.

“Unusual and compelling urgency” can justify limiting the number of sources. It does not erase the required justification, approval and documentation for limited competition, fiscal law, conflicts rules, cybersecurity, data handling or the agency's responsibility to authorize the deployed system. A waiver from the normal electronic-funds-transfer requirement for an urgent acquisition changes how the government may pay the contractor; it does not authorize the government to disburse program funds in a cryptocurrency.

**Primary authority:** [FAR Part 18](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/part-18); [FAR 18.124, electronic-funds-transfer waiver](https://www.acquisition.gov/far/18.124)

### 2. Defense Production Act priority and capacity

The Defense Production Act can move an entity's technology from an ordinary commercial queue into a national-defense priority program. Title I permits priority performance and required acceptance of contracts or orders, and allocation of materials, services and facilities, when the statutory and delegated findings are met. “National defense” is broad: it includes homeland security, emergency preparedness, critical-infrastructure protection and restoration, and designated critical technologies as well as military needs.

Title III can support purchases, purchase commitments or capacity expansion for an industrial resource or critical technology item. It requires written findings that the capability is essential to national defense, that industry cannot provide it in time without action, and that the intervention is the most cost-effective, expedient and practical route.

This route can prioritize or expand a custody, cybersecurity, communications, semiconductor or payment-adjacent capability. It does not itself make a cryptoasset legal tender, an eligible Federal Reserve settlement asset, or a Treasury payment method.

**Primary authority:** [50 U.S.C. chapter 55, Defense Production](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title50%2Fchapter55); [50 U.S.C. §4552 definitions](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A50+section%3A4552+edition%3Aprelim%29); [50 U.S.C. §4533, Title III](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=2023&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-2023-title50-section4533); [FEMA DPA fact sheet](https://www.fema.gov/print/pdf/node/468881)

### 3. DoD urgent capability route

DoD has a statutory urgent-acquisition and deployment route for capabilities that can be fielded rapidly, generally do not require substantial development and are proven or available. Separate rapid-contracting authority covers contingency operations and defense/recovery from cyber and CBRN attacks, among other missions.

This route favors technology that already works and can be deployed inside the required security and support environment. An “innovative” prototype is not automatically operationally acceptable. A prototype other transaction can move into follow-on production only if the statutory and solicitation conditions were built into the route.

**Primary authority:** [10 U.S.C. §3601](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title10-section3601); [10 U.S.C. §4022 follow-on production implementation](https://www.acquisition.gov/dfars/206.001-70-exception-prototype-projects-follow-production-contracts)

### 4. Stafford Act and FEMA mission assignments

After a major-disaster declaration, the President can direct federal agencies to use their authorities and resources for federal assistance. FEMA uses mission assignments for both Stafford Act emergency and major-disaster declarations to task other federal agencies. The mission assignment is normally to the federal agency, not directly to the private firm; the tasked agency can then use its own contracts and authorities.

FEMA's 2024 policy also allows certain federal-operations-support mission assignments before a declaration when a declaration is reasonably likely **and** imminent. The practical private-company seam is therefore prior registration, contracting readiness and agency integration—not a company declaring itself disaster infrastructure.

**Primary authority:** [42 U.S.C. §5170a](https://uscode.house.gov/quicksearch/get.plx?section=5170a&title=42); [FEMA Mission Assignments Policy, 2024](https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_policy-104-010-3_mission-assignments_2024.pdf); [SAM.gov Disaster Response Registry](https://sam.gov/entity-information)

### 5. Critical-infrastructure coordination

Treasury is the Sector Risk Management Agency for the Financial Services Sector. Its government coordinating structure and CISA's national cyber-response architecture organize information, incident support and public-private coordination.

Critical-infrastructure status is not a procurement award, a regulatory approval or a command to use a firm's product. It can shorten the distance to an incident table and improve information sharing, but a separate authority must still contract, authorize, fund or direct the concrete action.

**Primary authority:** [Financial Services Sector Government Coordinating Council Charter](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-01/Financial%20Services%20Sector%20GCC%20Charter-508_0.pdf); [National Cyber Incident Response Plan update draft](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-01/NCIRP%20Update%20Public%20Comment%20Draft%20508c_.pdf)

### 6. Operational authorization remains a separate gate

Emergency procurement does not automatically authorize a cloud, wallet or ledger to process federal data. FedRAMP provides reusable security evidence for cloud services; the consuming agency still makes its own authorization decision and accepts the risk for its system. A time-limited authorization may support a pilot, but it is still an authorization—not a suspension of the authorization requirement.

For a monetary rail this gate expands beyond cybersecurity: identity, sanctions screening, records retention, privacy, audit, reconciliation, key recovery, incident response, legal finality and the final cash account all have to work.

**Primary authority:** [FedRAMP scope](https://www.fedramp.gov/2026/scope/); [FedRAMP agency use](https://preview.fedramp.gov/2026/agencies/use/); [OMB M-24-15 process overview](https://www.fedramp.gov/preview/2026/authority/m-24-15/process/)

### 7. SAFETY Act designation is liability treatment, not adoption

The SAFETY Act can designate or certify qualified anti-terrorism technologies and attach specified liability protections. Prior governmental use, demonstrated utility and immediate availability can matter to the review. Designation or certification is not a federal procurement, grant or endorsement and does not order an agency to deploy the technology.

For an entity map, this is a useful **risk-allocation clock**: it can make deployment more commercially supportable without changing the technology's monetary, contracting or operational-authorization state.

**Primary authority:** [6 U.S.C. §441](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title6-section441); [SAFETY Act FAQ](https://www.safetyact.gov/faqs/)

### 8. National emergency and foreign-threat controls

A declaration under the National Emergencies Act is not a blank check. The President must identify the statutory provisions being used. Under IEEPA, the predicate is an unusual and extraordinary threat substantially originating outside the United States plus a declared national emergency. The resulting authorities can regulate or prohibit foreign exchange, transfers, payments, securities and property interests.

Crypto's most mature emergency role here is as an **object of control**. OFAC requires blocked virtual currency to be denied access and reported; it need not be converted into fiat. IEEPA can therefore control a token transfer or wallet relationship, but it does not make a token a federal payment rail.

**Primary authority:** [50 U.S.C. §1631](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A50+section%3A1631+edition%3Aprelim%29); [50 U.S.C. §1701](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A50+section%3A1701+edition%3Aprelim%29); [50 U.S.C. §1702](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=2023&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-2023-title50-section1702); [OFAC FAQ 646](https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/646)

### 9. Communications priority is upstream of the chain

Under 47 U.S.C. §606(c), the required proclamation and national-security/defense necessity finding can support government use or control of radio stations or devices, suspension or amendment of rules, and station/device closure with the statute's compensation requirement. Statutory carrier preference and priority under §606(a) is limited to wartime. In ordinary preparedness, CISA's GETS, WPS and TSP programs are the pre-positioned priority mechanisms for essential users.

Blockchain availability remains downstream of power, telecommunications, cloud, DNS, key infrastructure and endpoint access. A token cannot solve a communications outage merely because its ledger is distributed. Pre-enrollment materially shortens priority-service activation; an emergency request may still be possible but is slower and conditional.

**Primary authority:** [47 U.S.C. §606](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title47-section606); [CISA Wireless Priority Service](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/services/wireless-priority-service-wps); [CISA priority telecommunications services](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/news/resilient-together-priority-telecommunications-services-pts)

## Crypto-specific federal seams

### A. Once effective, the GENIUS Act creates a real “unusual and exigent” stablecoin lever

The 2025 GENIUS Act, codified at 12 U.S.C. chapter 56, contains the closest statutory match to the question.

Under 12 U.S.C. §5902(c)(2), if the Treasury Secretary determines that unusual and exigent circumstances exist, Treasury may provide **limited safe harbors** from the section's issuer restriction. Treasury must first justify the determination to the chairs and ranking members of Senate Banking and House Financial Services; the justification may include a classified annex.

The scope is narrow. It is a safe harbor from the specific §5902(a) prohibition. It does **not** by itself:

- make the stablecoin legal tender or a Federal Reserve liability;
- make it an appropriated federal payment;
- grant the issuer a Federal Reserve account;
- create deposit insurance, a Treasury guarantee or emergency lending;
- waive sanctions, AML, system authorization, procurement or accounting rules;
- make a payment stablecoin not issued by a permitted payment stablecoin issuer eligible wholesale collateral or a bank settlement asset.

The same section expressly says a payment stablecoin not issued by a permitted payment stablecoin issuer is not eligible as cash/cash-equivalent margin or collateral for specified regulated market actors and is not acceptable as a wholesale settlement asset among banking organizations or payment infrastructure.

**Primary authority:** [12 U.S.C. §5902(c), (g) and (h)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title12-section5902)

### B. GENIUS also creates emergency brakes

Under 12 U.S.C. §5906(e), after at least 48 hours' notice to the State regulator, the Federal Reserve Board **may** take emergency enforcement action against a State-qualified issuer or institution-affiliated party for a chapter 56 violation; the Comptroller **shall** act against a State-qualified issuer that is a nonbank entity. After the separate serious-risk finding, the Board may impose necessary restrictions and the Comptroller shall impose them. Those restrictions may include limits on stablecoin redemptions, affiliate transactions and risk transfer from a holding company or affiliates.

This authority is not a rescue facility. It is a crisis-control brake. The OCC's March 2026 **proposed** rule would look at immediate risk to the issuer or U.S. financial system, State-regulator action, risks to holders and other appropriate factors. Because the rule is proposed, those criteria are not yet a final operating rule. Proposed §15.16 implements only the Comptroller's §5906(e)(2) route; its factors do not define Treasury's separate §5902(c)(2) safe harbor.

**Primary authority:** [12 U.S.C. §5906(e)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title12%2Fchapter56); [OCC proposed GENIUS Act rule, proposed §15.16](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/federal-register/2026/91fr10202.pdf)

### C. GENIUS contains explicit national-security and intelligence waivers

For a foreign stablecoin issuer that fails to comply with a lawful order, §5907 provides a process that can culminate in a prohibition on U.S. digital-asset service providers facilitating secondary trading. Treasury, in consultation with the Director of National Intelligence and Secretary of State, may waive that specific §5907(b)(1)(B) trading restriction when it determines the waiver is in the national-security interest of the United States. An agency head may separately waive §5907 for authorized intelligence activities or activities necessary to carry out or assist U.S. law enforcement.

This is an unusually direct crypto/national-security seam—but again, it is a waiver from a trading restriction, not a declaration that the foreign stablecoin is federal money.

**Primary authority:** [12 U.S.C. §5907(c)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title12%2Fchapter56)

### D. Governability is built into issuer eligibility

The statute defines a “lawful order” to include an authorized federal requirement to seize, freeze, burn or prevent transfer of specified payment stablecoins or accounts, subject to the stated particularity and review conditions. A permitted issuer must have the technological capability to comply and must comply. Treasury retains its existing authority to block, restrict or limit U.S.-jurisdiction dollar-stablecoin transactions.

That means the federal architecture first makes the rail **controllable**. In a crisis, this supports sanctions, forfeiture, law enforcement and containment more clearly than it supports sovereign emergency spending.

**Primary authority:** [12 U.S.C. §§5901(16), 5902(h), 5903(a)(6)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title12%2Fchapter56)

### E. Current effective-state caution

The chapter's general effective date is the earlier of 18 months after July 18, 2025—January 18, 2027—or 120 days after the primary federal payment-stablecoin regulators issue final implementing regulations. The statutory rulemaking deadline is July 18, 2026.

As of this cutoff, no final implementing regulation by a primary federal payment-stablecoin regulator was located. The OCC and FDIC framework materials remain proposals; OCC reporting forms are a proposed information collection, not implementing regulations; and the joint customer-identification proposal has comments due August 21, 2026. Those materials do not start the 120-day clock. The prudent status for the chapter 56 framework and its emergency mechanisms is therefore **enacted; delayed effective date; implementation rulemaking in progress**, not active production authority. Stablecoin activity operating under existing State or other federal law is a separate clock.

**Primary authority:** [12 U.S.C. chapter 56 effective-date notes and §5913](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&path=%2Fprelim%40title12%2Fchapter56); [OCC proposed rule](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-3.html); [FDIC proposed rule](https://www.fdic.gov/news/financial-institution-letters/2026/notice-proposed-rulemaking-establish-genius-act); [joint customer-identification proposal](https://www.federalreserve.gov/apps/proposals/FR-2026-0005-01/details); [OCC proposed reporting forms](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-24.html)

### F. The 2025 reserve order is custody, not an emergency payment rail

The March 2025 executive order created a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve and U.S. Digital Asset Stockpile from finally forfeited assets. The Bitcoin reserve is to be held as a reserve asset under applicable law; additional acquisition must be budget neutral. The order does not create a general authority to use Bitcoin or stockpiled assets for federal disbursement, collateral support or emergency liquidity.

The January 2025 digital-assets order and May 2026 financial-technology integration order matter as **pre-positioning**: they support lawful public-blockchain and stablecoin activity and direct regulators to examine integration and account-access processes. They are policy and regulatory-development stages, not incident activation orders.

**Primary authority:** [Strategic Bitcoin Reserve executive order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/03/establishment-of-the-strategic-bitcoin-reserve-and-united-states-digital-asset-stockpile/); [January 2025 digital-financial-technology order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/strengthening-american-leadership-in-digital-financial-technology/); [May 2026 integration order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/05/integrating-financial-technology-innovation-into-regulatory-frameworks/)

## Financial-emergency authorities are not technology-adoption authorities

| Authority | Activation threshold | What it can do | Crypto boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Federal Reserve Act §13(3)** | unusual and exigent circumstances; affirmative vote of at least five Board members; broad-based program; prior Treasury Secretary approval; evidence adequate credit is unavailable elsewhere; statutory solvency and security conditions | lend through an emergency facility | supplies liquidity against eligible collateral; does not choose a coin or guarantee its holders |
| **FDIC systemic-risk exception** | FDIC is receiver for the insured depository institution; written two-thirds recommendations by the FDIC and Fed Board; Treasury Secretary determination in consultation with the President | permit FDIC, as receiver, to depart from least-cost and anti-loss-protection constraints and take necessary action or assistance to wind up the institution | can change treatment in the bank resolution; does not automatically protect stablecoin holders |
| **Designated FMU emergency liquidity** | unusual and exigent circumstances; affirmative vote of a majority of Board members then serving; Treasury consultation; adequate credit unavailable | allow a Reserve Bank to lend to a designated financial market utility | only a designated FMU route, not a general blockchain or issuer facility |

**Primary authority:** [12 U.S.C. §343(3)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A12+section%3A343+edition%3Aprelim%29); [Federal Reserve §13(3) rule](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20151130a.htm); [12 U.S.C. §1823 systemic-risk exception](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title12-section1823); [Payment, Clearing and Settlement Supervision Act](https://www.federalreserve.gov/frrs/regulations/payment-clearing-and-settlement-supervision-act.htm)

These authorities can stabilize the dollar, bank or clearing balance sheets underneath a tokenized system. They do not collapse token, reserve, issuer and settlement-bank liabilities into one object.

## Federal payments remain a separate legal clock

31 U.S.C. §3332 requires non-tax federal payments to be made by electronic funds transfer, subject to Treasury-prescribed waivers and methods; the statute's definition excludes payments under the Internal Revenue Code. Treasury's current rule recognizes a disaster-area waiver for payments made within 120 days after a declaration, with a Fiscal Service request required for an extension. Its procurement waiver covers unusually and compellingly urgent need or a sole source where the government would be seriously injured unless payment uses a non-EFT method, and also requires a waiver request. A waiver from mandatory EFT is permission not to use the usual EFT route; it is not affirmative authority to pay disaster assistance in USDC, RLUSD, PYUSD, XRP, BTC or another cryptoasset.

A federal crypto/stablecoin disbursement program would still require, at minimum:

1. statutory program and appropriations authority;
2. an authorized Treasury disbursement method and accountable officer/financial-agent chain;
3. an eligible legal recipient and identity/entitlement record;
4. an eligible issuer, asset and redemption arrangement;
5. a contract or other lawful operating relationship;
6. sanctions, AML, privacy, audit and records controls;
7. agency system authorization and incident controls;
8. reconciliation to the government's authoritative accounts;
9. a final cash, loss and exit rule.

The more plausible near-term architecture is a stablecoin or tokenized-deposit rail behind a federal contractor, Treasury financial agent, bank or card network, while the government's legal obligation and accounting remain denominated and discharged in dollars under existing authority.

**Primary authority:** [31 U.S.C. §3332](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title31-section3332); [Treasury Fiscal Service agency guidance](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/payments-from-government/direct-deposit/agency-guidance); [Treasury disaster and urgent-payment waiver fact sheet](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/payments-from-government/direct-deposit/fact-sheet)

## Where crypto could realistically enter a crisis chain

### 1. Supplemental dollar transport

A permitted dollar stablecoin could move a dollar-denominated claim across a public or permissioned network when a correspondent route is slow, unavailable or outside operating hours. The chain still needs a permitted issuer, funded reserves, functioning redemption bank, lawful counterparties, communications and an authorized governmental or contractor payment process.

### 2. 24/7 treasury, margin or collateral movement

Tokenized deposits, Treasury claims or stablecoins could help move liquidity between approved participants outside older message windows. Technical transfer does not decide legal title, clearing eligibility, haircut, Federal Reserve collateral eligibility or insolvency treatment. The authoritative custodian, depository, clearinghouse and central-bank rules still control.

### 3. Continuity, provenance and recovery

Distributed records, signed attestations and programmable workflows could support reconciliation, supply-chain provenance or alternate-site continuity. A replicated ledger is useful only if the government knows which copy is authoritative, who can correct it, and how compromised keys or endpoints are recovered.

### 4. Custody, seizure and sanctions execution

This is the most legally developed federal role. Issuers and custodians can be required to block, freeze, burn, prevent transfer or report specified assets under valid authority. The same programmability that makes the rail portable makes it a control surface.

### 5. Government reserve custody

The Strategic Bitcoin Reserve creates a federal custody and asset-management plotline. It does not establish an emergency spending, price-support or Federal Reserve settlement plotline.

## Which private-stack entities are closest

This is a function map, not a current approval roster. The visible brand, stablecoin issuer, trust/bank subsidiary, reserve custodian and product obligor may be different legal persons; for example, the PYUSD issuer and the relevant Ripple product issuer must be recorded separately from the PayPal and Ripple brands.

| Company family from the private-stack sweep | Plausible crisis function | Distance-shortening evidence required | Remaining federal gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **regulated stablecoin issuers and bank/trust subsidiaries** — Circle entities, Paxos entities, Ripple entities, PayPal's separately issued product, and bank issuers | tokenized dollar transport, controlled mint/burn, redemption, freeze | existing charter/license/issuer authority now; exact permitted-payment-stablecoin issuer and product status after the framework becomes effective; reserves, lawful-order capability and redemption banks | GENIUS effective rules, exact issuer/product approval, program authority and settlement integration |
| **card/payment orchestrators** — Visa, Mastercard, Fiserv, Stripe/Bridge, bank processors | familiar customer/merchant front end with tokenized settlement behind it | existing network rulebook, bank relationships, fraud/dispute and reconciliation systems | federal contract/financial-agent route, product authorization and final dollar leg |
| **custody/key-policy firms** — Anchorage, BitGo, Coinbase Custody, Fireblocks and regulated trust entities | government or contractor custody, signing policy, recovery, transaction control | regulated custody entity, audited controls, lawful-order and recovery capability | exact contracting entity, authority to hold the asset, system authorization and loss allocation |
| **messaging/interoperability firms** — Swift, Chainlink, Quant, Digital Asset and network adapters | cross-system instruction, proof, synchronization and exception handling | incumbent institutional integration and operational controls | federal system authorization, authoritative-record rule and incident playbook |
| **public chains and protocol sponsors** — XRPL, Stellar, Hedera and peers | transport, token representation, distribution or timestamping | proven performance and integration through regulated gateways | public-chain code is not a federal counterparty; a contracted/regulated legal entity must carry the obligation |

The shortest-distance entity is not necessarily the issuer of the visible token. It may be the settlement bank, card network, custodian, Treasury financial agent, cloud provider or regulated subsidiary already attached to the federal operating chain.

## Scenario chains

### Cyberattack on a payment or banking utility

`incident isolation -> authoritative-ledger/finality decision -> alternate communications and processing -> intraday liquidity -> regulated adapter or supplemental rail -> reconciliation -> loss allocation`

Crypto could supply an alternate transport or signed record. It cannot determine which corrupted or divergent ledger is legally authoritative, and it cannot supply central-bank liquidity by itself.

### Natural disaster disrupting branches, cash and telecom

`Stafford declaration or imminent support -> FEMA mission assignment -> agency/contractor activation -> priority communications -> identity and entitlement -> disbursement route -> claims and fiscal loss`

A wallet/stablecoin channel is plausible only if users, issuer, redemption and communications were pre-positioned. The faster near-term role is likely a bank/card/financial-agent program with tokenized backend settlement rather than direct federal token distribution.

### Foreign sanctions or national-security event

`NEA declaration + specified IEEPA authority -> OFAC designation/license -> issuer/custodian/wallet controls -> correspondent and exchange response -> permitted humanitarian/intelligence/law-enforcement exceptions -> exit or continuing controls`

Here the crypto rail is first a controllable property and compliance network. GENIUS adds lawful-order capability and specific foreign-issuer waiver mechanisms, including a national-security waiver from a secondary-trading restriction once the framework is effective.

### Dollar or stablecoin run

`redemption demand -> reserve/custodian liquidity -> settlement-bank liquidity -> issuer supervisory action -> possible redemption/activity restriction -> bank/FMU systemic tools if independently eligible -> resolution and holder priority`

The Federal Reserve can support eligible banks or designated utilities under their own authorities. That support does not automatically become a loan to the stablecoin issuer or a guarantee of par to token holders.

### Defense or supply-chain mobilization

`agency requirement -> DPA/urgent-acquisition finding -> rated order or capacity investment -> contractor delivery -> agency authorization and deployment -> termination/normalization`

Crypto may support traceability, secure identity, supply-chain finance or payment reconciliation. The DPA order prioritizes the contracted capability; it does not federalize the whole network or validate the native token.

## What a proclamation cannot do by itself

A national emergency declaration does not, without the relevant statute and required findings:

- turn a cryptocurrency into U.S. legal tender;
- appropriate money or create a benefit program;
- make Treasury use a stablecoin for federal disbursements;
- grant an issuer a bank charter, Fed master account or deposit insurance;
- make an unpermitted token eligible wholesale collateral or settlement money;
- waive sanctions, AML, privacy or federal cybersecurity requirements;
- make protocol finality equal legal finality;
- reconstruct a compromised authoritative ledger;
- restore power, telecommunications, cloud or endpoint access;
- allocate losses among issuer, reserve custodian, bank, network and holder.

## Plotline synthesis

The federal emergency constitution activates **functions**, not brands:

`procure -> authorize -> connect -> prioritize -> fund/liquefy -> control -> resolve -> exit`

The private crypto stack supplies possible components at several points:

`wallet/customer gate -> regulated issuer or custodian -> tokenized claim -> network -> orchestration -> settlement bank/Fed/Treasury dollar -> legal finality`

The two stacks meet only where a specific legal entity, product, contract, authorization and incident authority line up.

This produces the clean degrees-of-separation answer:

1. **Public chain alone:** farthest from federal emergency monetary use.
2. **Public chain plus regulated gateway/custodian:** technically usable, still program-dependent.
3. **Permitted issuer plus reserve/redemption banks and lawful-order controls:** eligible monetary adapter once the governing framework is effective.
4. **Existing federal contractor/financial agent/network with an authorized stablecoin or tokenized-deposit backend:** closest plausible deployment route.
5. **Named emergency order, safe harbor, mission assignment, rated order or facility draw:** actual activation event.

The strongest new seam is not “crypto becomes the emergency dollar.” It is:

> ordinary regulatory integration creates a governable dollar-token rail; a later emergency can then accelerate, narrow, prioritize, waive or restrict a specific function.

The GENIUS Act makes that unusually visible. It contains an emergency safe harbor, emergency redemption/activity brakes, lawful-order freeze/burn capability, and national-security/intelligence/law-enforcement waivers—while leaving federal payments, Federal Reserve access, liquidity and legal tender on separate statutory clocks.

## Promotion tests for the Observatory

Do not promote a company announcement or emergency slogan. Promote only a dated legal or operational event that moves one of these clocks:

1. final GENIUS implementing regulations and the resulting effective date;
2. Treasury regulation defining or operationalizing the §5902 unusual-and-exigent safe harbor;
3. a permitted-payment-stablecoin issuer approval for an exact legal entity and product;
4. an executed federal contract or Treasury financial-agent arrangement using a stablecoin/tokenized-deposit component;
5. a production agency authorization naming the exact system and function;
6. a DPA rated order or Title III award for a named monetary/custody/critical-technology capability;
7. a FEMA or other agency incident task that actually uses the private rail;
8. a Federal Reserve account/access decision or designated-FMU status affecting the final cash leg;
9. an IEEPA/OFAC, GENIUS lawful-order or national-security waiver applied to a named stablecoin/issuer;
10. an emergency enforcement directive restricting redemptions or affiliate activity;
11. a federal disbursement rule or statute affirmatively authorizing a tokenized payment method;
12. exit, termination, repayment, reconciliation and final loss allocation.

## Status vocabulary

- **available:** commercial technology exists;
- **eligible:** entity/product can legally perform the function;
- **contracted:** a contract, vehicle or other lawful commercial relationship exists for the named function;
- **system-authorized:** the agency has accepted the operational/system risk for the named deployment;
- **pre-positioned:** contract, integration or contingency route exists before the incident;
- **activated:** authorized official has used the route for the named event;
- **temporary:** incident-bounded authority or directive is operating;
- **final:** the named rule, decision or approval is final in legal form; do not infer effective, consummated, activated or production;
- **expired/terminated:** emergency authority or facility has ended;
- **resolved:** claims and losses have reached their terminal legal treatment.

## Bottom line

Yes, private crypto technology can be used in a federal emergency or national-security context—but “emergency” is usually the last gate, not the first.

The entity needs to arrive with its legal person, product permissions, security authorization, contracts, interfaces, reserve/redemption/custody chain, lawful-order controls and final dollar route already substantially in place. The emergency authority then accelerates or directs a specific function.

In the Observatory's monetary architecture, crypto is currently closest to the **adapter and control plane**—transport, custody, orchestration, programmable restrictions and cross-system continuity. It is much farther from becoming the sovereign money, automatic emergency collateral or federally guaranteed claim.
