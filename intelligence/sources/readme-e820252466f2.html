January 26–February 1, 2025 Authority-to-Operations Window

Answer first

The fixed January 26–February 1, 2025 action window contains at least four independently material federal commands that had acquired inspectable operating machinery by July 29, 2026:

  • the military-readiness order became a sequence of Defense Department personnel policies, identification procedures and litigation-responsive implementation guidance;
  • the Guantánamo migrant-operations memorandum became a named interagency operation with transfers, eligibility criteria, transportation, detention, housing and Inspector General oversight;
  • the education-choice order became a series of Department of Education guidance lanes for existing State and federal-program flexibilities; and
  • the ten-for-one deregulation order became OMB counting and cost-offset rules, then fed a government-wide rewrite of the Federal Acquisition Regulation through model text, agency class deviations and formal proposed rules.

The four objects do not form one policy program. Their common factual grammar is narrower and still significant:

executive direction → named administrative carrier → operating rule or procedure → review, litigation or formalization

The result is not proof that each policy succeeded. It is proof that the window was not merely rhetorical: selected commands crossed into personnel administration, interagency operation, program guidance and acquisition rulebooks before the July 29, 2026 comparison date.

Research question

Which material federal authority objects signed or initiated from January 26 through February 1, 2025 had acquired a verifiable administrative or operating form by July 29, 2026, and what state had each reached by the September 25, 2026 evidence cutoff?

Scope and method

This is a fixed action-date hearing, not a Federal Register publication census. Several signed instruments were published after the window. Signature, publication, guidance, implementation, operation, litigation, proposed rulemaking and observed outcome remain separate clocks.

The four objects were selected independently for material authority, traceability into a later operating carrier and availability of primary-source receipts. The set is not exhaustive. It does not include every order, statute, tariff action, education measure, immigration action or military directive in the period.

1. Military personnel policy: command becomes a governed personnel process

Executive Order 14183 directed the Defense Secretary to revise accession and retention standards, issue implementation guidance, identify affected policy and report the steps taken. DoD then produced an ordered implementation chain:

  • February 7, 2025: the Secretary paused specified new accessions and medical procedures and directed further guidance;
  • February 26: Personnel and Readiness issued additional implementation guidance;
  • March 4 and March 21: follow-on instruments addressed waivers and military- department identification;
  • May 6: the Supreme Court stayed the preliminary injunction in Shilling v. United States pending appeal; and
  • May 8: DoD issued litigation-responsive implementing policy.

Current DoD guidance and FAQs preserve that sequence as the operative personnel route. The factual maturation is therefore more exact than “the military changed policy”:

presidential standard → accession pause → identification and waiver process → court intervention → revised implementation route

The official record proves an administrative process. It does not, by itself, prove a final merits judgment, improved force readiness, uniform execution or the number and experience of affected personnel.

2. Guantánamo migrant operations: capacity direction acquires an operator

The January 29 memorandum directed the Defense and Homeland Security Departments to expand the Migrant Operations Center at Naval Station Guantánamo Bay. DoD publicly described the plan as already moving on the same day. The first ten transferred detainees arrived February 4.

The operation later received a distinct institutional identity: Joint Task Force Southern Guard. The Lead Inspector General's first report, covering July 1 through December 31, 2025 and published in March 2026, describes the federal effort to support DHS transportation, detention and housing. It also records eligibility criteria and implementation challenges.

That supplies a strong same-object chain:

capacity memorandum → first transfers → named joint task force → criteria and workflow → Inspector General oversight

The report does not collapse every individual into one legal category. A final order of removal, selection for transfer, transportation to the base, detention, repatriation and judicial review remain separate legal and operating states.

3. Educational freedom: one direction becomes several bounded guidance lanes

Executive Order 14191 directed Education to explain how States could use existing formula funds for educational-choice initiatives and required several departments to review discretionary grants and program mechanisms.

The Department of Education's maintained guidance page shows that the order did not mature as one universal program. Instead, Education issued distinct guidance objects:

  • March 31, 2025: Title I, Part A guidance;
  • May 7: unsafe-school choice guidance;
  • June 26: parental choice in schools identified for support and improvement;
  • July 29: ESEA flexibility and waivers; and
  • August 21: equitable-services school choice.

The factual maturation is therefore direction → route clarification, not direction → universal benefit delivered. Each State, program, waiver, eligible provider, funding stream and student outcome retains its own decision and receipt clock.

4. Deregulation: a counting rule becomes a procurement operating system

Executive Order 14192 required agencies, subject to its exceptions and lawful limits, to identify at least ten existing regulatory actions for elimination for each new regulatory action and to offset new incremental costs.

OMB Memorandum M-25-20 of March 26 supplied definitions, counting rules, cost-netting requirements and the FY2026 regulatory-cap route. Executive Order 14275 of April 15 then applied the deregulatory premise to federal procurement. OMB Memorandum M-25-26 of May 2 assigned the Office of Federal Procurement Policy and FAR Council to lead the rewrite.

The Revolutionary FAR Overhaul moved through two operating lanes:

  1. rewritten FAR parts issued as model deviation text for agency adoption and immediate use through class deviations; and
  2. later formal rulemaking to codify the streamlined text.

By the July 29, 2026 comparison date, agencies were operating under multiple deviations, the first proposed-rule release had been published June 23 and the FAR Council had issued July 1 updates. This is a genuine rulebook maturation, but not yet one final codified FAR. Adoption of model text also does not prove the claimed savings, competition or delivery effects.

Cross-object finding

All four branches move authority into the people and systems that must carry it:

  • personnel officers identify, waive, retain or separate;
  • Defense and Homeland Security transport, detain, house and review;
  • State and federal education administrators interpret funding and waiver routes; and
  • acquisition officials, contracting officers and vendors work through new deviation text while formal codification proceeds.

The common maturation is therefore administrative embodiment. A command acquires a carrier, a workflow and a review surface. That is stronger than a same-theme match and narrower than a claim of policy success.

Non-findings and boundaries

  • The four-object set is materiality-selected and not exhaustive.
  • No causal connection among the four objects was located.
  • A signed order is not implementation; guidance is not adoption; a transfer is not final removal; a class deviation is not a final FAR rule.
  • The military policy's litigation history is not reduced to the Supreme Court stay; later merits and individual cases require their own dockets.
  • The Inspector General report establishes an official oversight record, not independent proof of every agency assertion or individual disposition.
  • The education guidance set does not prove State participation or student receipt.
  • FAR rewrite activity does not itself prove cost savings, better competition or faster mission delivery.
  • No astrology interpretation is part of this factual package.
