Independent power for major military installations

Official study title: FORTRESS America and Military Installation Energy Independence

What did FORTRESS America change, and what authority, scope, financing, construction and operating-test receipts are required before major military installations actually possess independent power?

Independent power for major military installations

FORTRESS America makes installation power a central military program, but it does not yet say which sites count, what loads must run, how long they must run or who will pay and operate the systems.

What we found

  • The September 30 announcement creates a five-pillar program and sets independent power for every major U.S. military installation as the energy objective; it does not establish that every base is covered or already self-sufficient.
  • The biggest legal and technical mismatch is scope: FORTRESS says major installations with an undefined load, while 10 U.S.C. §2920 covers each installation but only the energy required for critical missions.
  • Existing delivery tools are already substantial: installation energy plans, ERCIP military construction, PPAs, enhanced-use leases, performance contracts, utility agreements, microgrids and black-start exercises.
  • Army SCI, Janus, Eielson and Project Pele are still at solicitation, conditional selection, negotiation, licensing or prototype stages. Operating microgrids at Yuma, Miramar, New London, Fort McCoy and Sigonella have different load and duration boundaries and cannot be counted as one uniform capability.
  • The program becomes operationally meaningful when it publishes a covered-site list, load and duration standards, resources and comparable black-start, islanding and endurance results.

Why it matters

The military is trying to turn its domestic basing network from a collection of utility customers and local resilience projects into a coordinated power-and-survival portfolio. If implemented, FORTRESS could join mission priorities, private capital, microgrids, reactors, cyber controls and recurring readiness tests under one command layer.

What to watch

  • 2026-10-30 or earlier on publication · Program-office establishment, named leadership, charter, first implementation guidance and assignment of existing installation-energy programs.
  • source-triggered · A binding definition of major installation and independent power, covered-site list, critical/full-load denominator, outage duration, fuel and test standard.
  • budget or transaction trigger · FYDP/budget line, appropriation, transfer, obligation, executed lease or PPA, financing close, licence, interconnection or construction award.
  • 2028-09-30 and end FY2030 · First directed domestic-installation reactor operation and evidence that §2920 critical-mission availability requirements are actually met.

Research summary

FORTRESS America is a real department-wide convergence program whose energy objective is independent power for every major U.S. military installation. The office and objective are established, but the covered-site list, meaning of independent power, critical-versus-full-load denominator, duration, technology, funding and acceptance test remain unresolved. Existing statutes, installation plans, finance authorities, microgrids and reactor pilots supply delivery lanes but do not prove fleet-wide readiness.
