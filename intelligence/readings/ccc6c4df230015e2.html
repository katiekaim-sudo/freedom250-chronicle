In plain English
Question: When the administration says trillions of dollars are coming into or being invested in the United States, which legal and economic objects are included and who supplies the cash and carries the credit risk for real industrial projects?
Answer: The administration's $18–20 trillion investment total is not a pile of construction money. It mixes multi-year company spending plans, foreign-government frameworks, trade and purchase figures and some real projects, and each step from announcement to operation is separate.
Evidence checked through: 2026-07-31
The detailed record below keeps the legal wording, source limits and open questions.
Question
When the administration says trillions of dollars are “coming into” or being invested in the United States, what legal and economic objects are included—and, for the construction and industrial projects that are real, who supplies the cash and carries the credit risk?
The question is not whether a project is desirable, nor whether tokenized money could ever be useful. It is whether each dollar is an announced intention, sponsor equity, parent-company capital expenditure, bank/project debt, bond/private-placement debt, private credit, public support, tax attribute, customer prepayment, utility/ratepayer infrastructure, foreign-parent funding, or actual construction expenditure.
Starting finding
The headline aggregate is not presently a disclosed trillion-dollar construction-debt pipeline. It combines multi-year corporate spending plans, foreign-government investment frameworks, procurement and trade figures, domestic company allocations, and some named physical projects. A political announcement is not a financing commitment; a financing commitment is not a draw; a draw is not construction spend; construction spend is not commercial operation.
The nearest official foreign-investment check is BEA’s 2025 new-FDI release: actual expenditures to acquire, establish or expand U.S. businesses were $232.2 billion, of which acquisitions were $218.4 billion and greenfield establishment/expansion $13.8 billion; planned future expenditure attached to those transactions was $284.5 billion. This does not measure all domestic corporate capex, but it prevents a headline commitment total from being described as actual FDI-funded construction.

August 10 AI-infrastructure capital-stack extension
The AI-infrastructure lane distinguishes five financed objects: GPU lease-fund
assets, ring-fenced GPU project collateral, AI-cloud operating-company assets,
data-center real estate, and dedicated power equipment. Their customer
obligations, creditor rights and residual-value losses do not sit in the same
entity. The NVIDIA six-platform announcement remains MOU-only; the comparison
ledger uses executed and disclosed transactions to show what a completed
ownership, debt-service and loss chain would require.
Core rule
Do not ask “where are the trillions?” until the numerator exists.
First decompose the claim. Then trace each project’s capital stack and risk-transfer chain:
sponsor / parent → project SPV → equity + debt + public support + contracted revenue → construction draws → completed asset → operating cash flow → debt service / loss allocation
Banks may originate or arrange part of the debt, but they need not hold it all. Large credits can be syndicated or distributed to other banks and institutional holders; bonds, private credit, infrastructure funds, insurers, pensions, foreign investors, public support and customer contracts may each carry a different part of the stack.
Boundaries
This is a workbench package, not a vault master narrative.
“Own funds” are not a sufficient answer: they may be retained earnings, new equity, parent debt, intercompany funding, or proceeds from asset sales.
A loan guarantee, conditional commitment, tax credit, tax-credit transfer, incentive, PPA/offtake or land lease is not automatically construction cash.
Crypto is a separately falsifiable lane. No token, stablecoin or blockchain feature counts as funding without the issuer/SPV, legal instrument, investor/lender cash source, collateral if any, and borrower receipt.
