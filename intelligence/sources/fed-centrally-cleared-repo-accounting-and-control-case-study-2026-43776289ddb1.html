Federal Reserve centrally cleared repo — accounting and control case study

Cutoff: 2026-07-25
State: saved Workbench research; no Chronicle landing
Scope: standing overnight repurchase agreement operations, with the centrally cleared version treated as a proposed operating model rather than an approved or live facility
Dollar rule: every amount is written in full nominal dollars; no letter-suffix scaling is used
Method: one transaction followed through authority, execution, novation, collateral, settlement, accounting, software, fees, loss allocation and disclosure

1. Accountant's answer

The current standing repo is not centrally cleared.

Today the Federal Open Market Committee directs the New York Fed's Open Market Trading Desk to make overnight secured financing available for the System Open Market Account. Eligible counterparties submit propositions through the New York Fed's proprietary FedTrade Plus system. BNY then acts as tri-party agent, taking custody of collateral, valuing it, applying margin and settling the transaction on BNY's books.

The Federal Reserve's accounting is straightforward:

  • the repo is a financing transaction;
  • the Federal Reserve records an asset, securities purchased under agreements to resell;
  • interest income accrues over the overnight term;
  • the repo asset and related income are participated among all 12 Reserve Banks using their domestic SOMA shares; and
  • the transaction reverses at maturity when the counterparty repurchases the collateral.

The central-clearing proposal would insert a private clearinghouse between the Federal Reserve and the market counterparty. That can give dealers and banks a common legal counterparty against which qualifying repo and reverse-repo positions may be netted. The stated policy benefit is lower intermediary balance-sheet cost and greater willingness to use the standing operation when market repo rates rise.

The accounting question is therefore not simply, "Should the Fed modernize the plumbing?" It is:

Should the Reserve Banks assume new clearinghouse, margin, default-fund, settlement-bank, software and concentration obligations in order to reduce the gross balance-sheet exposure of private intermediaries and improve the Federal Reserve's interest-rate-control tool?

No final public design answers that question as of the cutoff. New York Fed staff said on 2026-07-09 that the operations remain uncleared and that staff continue to evaluate the benefits and costs.

This is sometimes described on the New York Fed website as being "cleared and settled" on BNY's platform. That does not mean central-counterparty clearing. BNY is agent and custodian. It does not novate the repo and replace the two principals with itself as buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer.

Conceptual Federal Reserve accounting for a $1,000,000,000 award

The exact FR 34 entry sequence and BNY subaccounts are not public at the transaction level. The following is the economic and published-statement mapping, not a claim about undisclosed account numbers.

Moment Federal Reserve balance-sheet effect Income effect Control record
trade award normally no published balance until settlement none yet FedTrade Plus award
same-day settlement repo financing asset increases by $1,000,000,000; reserve balances supplied to the banking system increase correspondingly, subject to the exact settlement routing interest starts accruing BNY tri-party books plus Reserve Bank/SOMA ledgers
overnight accrual at 3.75 percent using a 360-day convention principal remains $1,000,000,000 approximately $104,166.67 of one-day interest SOMA income ledger
maturity repo asset decreases by $1,000,000,000; corresponding reserve balances are extinguished as principal returns accrued interest is collected BNY unwind plus Reserve Bank/SOMA ledgers

The interest illustration is:

$1,000,000,000 × 3.75% ÷ 360 = $104,166.67

The actual operation's governing day-count convention and any holiday term must control a real calculation.

Participation among the 12 Reserve Banks

New York executes and holds the repo for SOMA, but the economic asset is not New York's alone. The Financial Accounting Manual requires repo agreements to be participated to every Reserve Bank using its designated domestic SOMA share. The same is true of the related interest income.

That produces two simultaneous records:

  1. operating custody and execution: New York, FedTrade Plus and BNY; and
  2. System economics: an undivided participation allocated across the 12 Reserve Banks.

The participating percentages are recalculated through the annual interdistrict settlement process. Specific collateral is not assigned as a standalone security package to each Bank.

4. What central clearing would change

Proposed lifecycle

FOMC authorization and chosen membership model
    ↓
New York Desk awards trade through FedTrade Plus
    ↓
trade is submitted to a Treasury clearing agency
    ↓
clearing agency accepts and novates the eligible trade
    ↓
Federal Reserve faces the clearing agency
    ↓
clearing agency faces dealer, bank, sponsor or agent member
    ↓
margin, clearing-fund and funds-only obligations are calculated
    ↓
securities and cash settle through clearing-bank/Fedwire/NSS infrastructure
    ↓
Reserve Banks book SOMA repo, interest, fees and any collateral accounts
    ↓
close leg settles; clearing, margin and default-management records remain auditable

The repo would still be a secured financing asset. Central clearing changes the legal counterparty, netting set, margin system, loss waterfall and settlement instructions; it does not turn the financing into an outright purchase of Treasury securities.

The five separate clocks

Clock Binding event Likely controller in a FICC design
execution award accepted New York/FedTrade Plus
comparison trade data match FICC
novation FICC becomes central counterparty under its rules FICC
securities settlement collateral delivery is effective on the authoritative securities ledger or clearing-bank books Fedwire Securities and/or BNY
funds-only settlement net cash adjustments debit or credit Federal Reserve accounts DTC as FICC settlement agent using the Federal Reserve's National Settlement Service

An execution is not a novation. A novation is not securities delivery. A securities delivery is not the same event as final central-bank-money settlement.

5. The membership decision is the real control decision

FICC is the obvious case study because it has the mature Treasury repo service, but the Federal Reserve has not selected FICC. ICE Clear Credit and CME Securities Clearing are also registered Treasury clearing agencies. The accounting consequences differ by clearinghouse and access model.

Model A — New York becomes a direct FICC Netting Member

Structure

Federal Reserve Bank of New York ↔ FICC ↔ dealer/bank member

What it accomplishes

  • FICC becomes the legal counterparty after novation.
  • Qualifying private intermediary positions against FICC may enter the same netting set.
  • The Federal Reserve avoids using a private dealer as its sponsor or agent.

Unresolved rule and accounting gates

  • FICC's listed ordinary categories include banks, dealers, futures commission merchants, interdealer brokers, government-securities issuers, insurance companies, registered clearing agencies and registered investment companies.
  • A Federal Reserve Bank is not expressly named as a repo Netting Member category.
  • Rule 2A permits an "Other Applicant" to be considered if direct access would provide material benefit and FICC establishes appropriate standards.
  • FICC may create another category, but a new category and its standards require SEC approval.
  • The rulebook already refers to Federal Reserve Banks in the processing of Treasury auction purchases. That proves an operational connection, not that a Reserve Bank is an admitted repo Netting Member.
  • An ordinary direct Netting Member must be able to meet clearing-fund, margin, funds-only and other obligations. The public record does not say whether the Federal Reserve would accept ordinary loss-mutualization duties or receive a central-bank exception.

Assessment: structurally cleanest, but not ready under a publicly complete membership and loss-allocation record.

Model B — New York becomes a Sponsored Member

Structure

Federal Reserve Bank of New York
          ↕ private Sponsoring Member
          ↕
         FICC ↔ dealer/bank member

The sponsor is the processing gateway and assumes defined guaranty, reimbursement, collateral and indemnity responsibilities. This model could produce central clearing without direct Federal Reserve membership.

Control problem: a private financial institution would stand between the central bank's monetary-policy operation and the clearinghouse and would be paid for doing so. That introduces sponsor credit, pricing, conflict, portability and concentration questions. No public evidence shows the Federal Reserve chose this structure.

Assessment: technically plausible but institutionally awkward.

Model C — New York is an Executing Firm Customer of an Agent Clearing Member

Structure

Federal Reserve Bank of New York
          ↕ private Agent Clearing Member
          ↕
         FICC ↔ dealer/bank member

The FICC rulebook says the Agent Clearing Member acts as agent but remains fully liable to FICC for all financial and other obligations. FICC's obligations run to the Agent Clearing Member, not directly to the executing customer.

Control problem: the Federal Reserve would rely on a private agent that is the legal obligor to FICC and the recipient of FICC payments and deliveries.

Assessment: available as a general market structure; no evidence makes it the proposed Federal Reserve structure.

Model D — special central-bank category or bespoke rule

Structure

Federal Reserve Bank of New York ↔ FICC special central-bank account

A dedicated category could specify:

  • whether Federal Reserve capital or a clearing-fund deposit is required;
  • whether the Federal Reserve participates in mutualized losses;
  • whether FICC can assess the Federal Reserve after another member defaults;
  • whether the Federal Reserve can be suspended or defaulted;
  • whether margin is posted by the Federal Reserve, its counterparties, or both;
  • whether funds settle directly in the Federal Reserve's own account rather than through a private Funds-Only Settling Bank;
  • what happens when FICC is unavailable; and
  • how counterparty-level data and two-year Federal Reserve disclosures interact with clearinghouse reports.

Assessment: most likely to expose the actual policy bargain. It would also create the clearest public rule-filing trail.

Model E — another Treasury clearing agency

ICE Clear Credit or CME Securities Clearing could compete for the role. That would change membership, default-fund, settlement-bank and software interfaces. No public source reviewed here establishes that either was selected for standing repo.

The key incidence finding

The public argument identifies a private accounting friction: the market intermediary cannot net an uncleared Federal Reserve repo against centrally cleared positions. Central clearing may reduce that private gross balance sheet. The Federal Reserve would incur at least some combination of:

  • system development;
  • clearing fees;
  • clearing-bank charges;
  • margin and collateral administration;
  • legal and membership work;
  • testing and contingency operations; and
  • concentration and default-management exposure.

That does not prove the proposal is a subsidy or a bad policy. It proves the cost-benefit analysis must show both sides of the ledger.

Software-accounting rule

The FAM makes the control entity more important than the coding location:

  • preliminary design, alternatives, vendor selection and proof-of-concept costs are expensed;
  • qualifying development, integration, coding and pre-acceptance testing costs can be capitalized;
  • internally developed software requires at least $100,000 of qualifying cost for capitalization;
  • externally purchased software uses a $25,000 threshold;
  • maintenance, routine fixes, user training and post-implementation testing are generally expensed;
  • a new interface can qualify as a capital improvement;
  • the Reserve Bank hosting the product/function office that controls the software generally records the asset, even if another Bank develops it or a vendor hosts it; and
  • useful life generally cannot exceed five years without Board staff approval.

Therefore a centrally cleared standing repo project could generate both capitalized software and current expense. The public budget does not isolate FedTrade Plus, a future clearing interface, or the controlling host Bank well enough to calculate either amount.

9. The BNY concentration does not disappear

Current standing repo already relies on BNY for:

  • custody;
  • collateral pricing;
  • margin;
  • cash and securities settlement; and
  • the maturity unwind.

FICC's 2026 fee schedule also identifies BNY as its clearing agent bank and passes actual BNY settlement charges, Fedwire charges and some daylight overdraft charges to members.

A FICC layer could therefore produce:

New York Desk → FICC → BNY → Fedwire/NSS

instead of:

New York Desk → BNY

Central clearing would add a guaranty and netting layer but may preserve the same private settlement-bank dependency. The public case for the proposal should disclose:

  1. whether BNY remains the sole or primary clearing agent;
  2. the backup route if BNY is unavailable;
  3. whether bilateral BNY settlement remains an authorized fallback if FICC is unavailable;
  4. which event constitutes final settlement for Federal Reserve accounting; and
  5. who bears extra liquidity cost when FICC, BNY, Fedwire Securities or NSS is unavailable.

FICC rules permit an extended Fedwire failure to qualify as possible "good cause," but a clearing-bank problem does not cancel a member's obligation. A Funds-Only Settling Bank refusal or failure generally leaves the Netting Member responsible for paying through the prescribed alternate route.

The SEC separately said on 2026-07-20 that a Treasury clearing agency may permit required market transactions to settle bilaterally during a clearing- agency outage. Federal Reserve transactions are exempt from the mandate, so this is not a ready-made Federal Reserve fallback rule. It is evidence that a bilateral contingency is legally conceivable and should be designed in advance.

Federal Reserve refuses an FICC assessment not applicable central-bank membership rules must say whether this is permitted and what follows

The Federal Reserve is exempt from the SEC clearing mandate partly because the SEC judged central-bank transactions to present limited to no contagion risk to Treasury clearing agencies. Voluntary entry into a CCP should not silently reverse that rationale by making the Federal Reserve an ordinary mutualized loss bearer.

12. What the public story says the project is for

The strongest stated chain is:

repo market rate rises
    ↓
dealer considers standing repo
    ↓
uncleared Fed trade cannot be netted against its cleared book
    ↓
gross balance-sheet cost makes dealer reluctant
    ↓
standing facility is used less than pure price economics would suggest
    ↓
market repo pressure can transmit into the federal funds rate
    ↓
central clearing creates a common counterparty and possible accounting netting
    ↓
dealer uses standing repo more readily
    ↓
Fed can control the policy rate with a smaller reserve buffer

This is a coherent monetary-policy explanation. It also reveals that the proposal is not primarily about making the Federal Reserve's own accounting simpler. It is about making a Federal Reserve transaction fit the private intermediaries' centrally cleared accounting and risk architecture.

13. What remains unknown in public

Accounting

  • the clearinghouse that would become the Federal Reserve's legal counterparty;
  • whether the repo remains disclosed in exactly the same combined-statement line;
  • whether the FAM will be updated: its January 2026 repo paragraph still describes agreements with primary dealers, while the current FOMC directive expressly includes depository institutions;
  • the Federal Reserve account for clearing-fund deposits and segregated margin;
  • whether clearing fees are treated as a SOMA expense, New York operating expense or System shared-service expense;
  • whether costs and losses are participated using domestic SOMA shares;
  • whether a direct Federal Reserve clearing-fund contribution is required;
  • and whether any default assessment can reduce Reserve Bank net earnings and Treasury remittances.

Contracts and counterparties

  • FICC, ICE or CME selection;
  • direct, sponsored, agent or special membership;
  • BNY's future role and price;
  • any sponsor or agent;
  • outside counsel, systems integrator, cloud provider or software vendor; and
  • liability and indemnity terms.

Technology

  • FedTrade Plus accounting owner and current net book value;
  • interface budget;
  • FICC message and acknowledgment design;
  • reconciliation system of record;
  • cyber and outage fallback;
  • production date; and
  • which Reserve Bank hosts the controlling product office.

Risk and governance

  • loss waterfall;
  • liquidity commitments;
  • assessment cap;
  • central-bank default treatment;
  • bilateral fallback;
  • data-access and public-disclosure terms; and
  • audit rights.

These are disclosure gaps, not proof of misconduct. This workbench does not recommend or prepare a records request.

Falsifiers

This analysis must be revised if a primary source shows any of the following:

  1. an FOMC vote already approved centrally cleared standing repo;
  2. a Treasury clearing agency was already selected;
  3. New York already has direct Netting Member status for repo;
  4. the Federal Reserve will bear no clearing fees, margin, default-fund, software or settlement cost;
  5. BNY will not participate in the selected design;
  6. the operation will not be booked in SOMA;
  7. the direct counterparty remains the awardee after clearing rather than the clearinghouse; or
  8. a different accounting-netting mechanism solves the identified private balance-sheet friction without central clearing.

15. Bottom line

The proposed change is not "the Fed joins a modern platform." It is a choice about legal counterparty, private netting benefit, default mutualization, settlement-bank concentration and software ownership.

The clean accounting test is:

  1. name the legal counterparty after novation;
  2. name every cash, collateral, margin, fee and loss object;
  3. identify the entity that books each object;
  4. identify the Reserve Banks that ultimately absorb the expense or exposure;
  5. disclose which private parties receive the netting benefit and service revenue; and
  6. show the fallback transaction when the clearinghouse or BNY is unavailable.

Until those six items are public, central clearing remains a policy proposal with a credible rate-control rationale but an incomplete cost and control ledger.
