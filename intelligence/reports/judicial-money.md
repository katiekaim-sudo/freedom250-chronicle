## 0. How to read this map

This is a factual spine. It does not assess whether separate suits share a plan, purpose, actor, or strategy. Each statement is tagged or written so its layer is visible:

- **Source fact** — what a court, clerk, statute, or agency record says happened.
- **Party allegation** — a pleaded or argued claim, not a fact found by a court.
- **Holding** — the rule needed to decide the matter.
- **Dicta / separate writing** — reasoning not necessary to the judgment, or a concurrence/dissent.
- **Procedural effect** — what the judgment, stay, injunction, mandate, or remand did to the case.
- **Institutional effect** — what changed in real operations.
- **Inference** — a reasoned connection drawn from separate facts.
- **Speculation** — a possible future meaning; never treated as source fact.

### Time-and-place rule

Noon charts and default Washington charts are not used. An electronic filing does not prove that counsel, the clerk, the judge, or the affected machine acted in Washington. Unless the public record supplies a time, the field reads **not publicly stated**. The map keeps separate:

1. judicial signing time;
2. CM/ECF or appellate docket-entry time;
3. public-release time;
4. judgment date;
5. mandate date;
6. legal effective time;
7. courthouse or hearing site;
8. clerk/e-filing locus;
9. affected institution's operating site.

**Charting convention:** for an in-person hearing, use the hearing's actual start at the courthouse. For a released opinion, use a verified public-release time at the court if available; otherwise keep it untimed at the courthouse. For an injunction with an express effective time, the legal-effective event may be charted separately. For an agency response, use the agency's documented implementation time and operating locus. A proposed anchor below is analytical metadata, not a claim about where a distributed electronic act “occurred.”

### Asset-state vocabulary

These states must remain separate throughout the emergency-lending, sanctions, forfeiture, bankruptcy, and sovereign-assets records:

| Asset state | Possession / title / control | Main legal source | Event that changes the state |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pledged collateral | Borrower ordinarily retains title; lender receives a security interest and enforcement rights defined by contract and law | Federal Reserve Act, facility terms, UCC and applicable collateral law | Advance, collateral pledge, margin call, repayment, default, or foreclosure |
| Emergency-facility asset | Asset or loan may be held directly by a Reserve Bank or through a Treasury-backed special-purpose vehicle; exact title follows facility documents | FRA §13(3), Treasury equity/protection agreement, LLC and transaction documents | Board authorization, Treasury approval, Reserve Bank advance, SPV purchase, wind-down |
| Blocked property | Title ordinarily does not transfer to the United States; transactions and transfers are prohibited and the property is immobilized | IEEPA, TWEA, OFAC regulations and license | Designation/blocking instruction, license, delisting, vesting statute, or forfeiture |
| Seized property | Government has taken possession or control pending adjudication; final title has not necessarily been determined | Warrant/statute, Supplemental Rule G, 18 U.S.C. §§981, 982 and 21 U.S.C. §853 as applicable | Warrant execution, transfer to government-controlled wallet/account, release, forfeiture judgment |
| Civilly forfeited property | In rem judgment determines forfeiture of the property; relation-back and innocent-owner rules depend on statute | 18 U.S.C. §981 and 21 U.S.C. §881, among others | Default, summary-judgment, or trial forfeiture judgment; appeal and mandate may delay sale |
| Criminally forfeited property | Forfeiture follows a defendant's conviction or plea and reaches property defined by the offense statute; third parties use ancillary proceedings | 18 U.S.C. §982; 21 U.S.C. §853; Fed. R. Crim. P. 32.2 | Preliminary order, conviction/sentence, ancillary judgment, final order |
| Bankruptcy-estate property | Estate receives the debtor's legal and equitable interests at petition; customer ownership depends on contract, trust, bailment, custody, and tracing law | 11 U.S.C. §§541, 542 and applicable nonbankruptcy law | Petition, ownership ruling, turnover, plan confirmation, distribution |
| Attached sovereign asset | Asset remains immune unless an FSIA or other statutory exception permits attachment/execution; central-bank property receives additional protection | FSIA §§1609–1611; TRIA §201; asset-specific statutes such as 22 U.S.C. §8772 | Blocking, attachment writ, turnover judgment, statutory change, execution, appellate mandate |
| Vested sovereign property | Congress or the Executive under granted wartime authority transfers title to the United States rather than merely blocking it | Statute and any delegated vesting authority; historically TWEA rather than ordinary IEEPA blocking | Vesting order and any judicial review |

An agency press release saying that assets were “seized,” “blocked,” “recovered,” or “held” does not by itself establish the final title state. The controlling warrant, forfeiture order, facility document, bankruptcy order, or attachment judgment must be checked.

## 2. Case-family dossiers: law, seam, and two timelines

### A. Federal Reserve constitutional structure and Governor removal

#### JM-01 — Cook

**Affected entities:** Board of Governors; FOMC indirectly; President; Supreme Court; D.D.C.; D.C. Circuit.  
**Monetary function:** voting control over monetary policy, supervision, reserve requirements, discount policy, and Board oversight of Reserve Banks.  
**Public/private seam:** a federal Board governs a system whose operating Banks have separate corporate form; removal of one public Governor can change the balance of the hybrid FOMC.  
**Law:** Federal Reserve Act, 12 U.S.C. §242 (“for cause”); Article II; Fifth Amendment due-process arguments; traditional equitable relief.

**Question presented:** whether the President could immediately remove a sitting Governor for asserted cause, without prior notice and a chance to answer, and whether the Governor's statutory tenure protection may constitutionally block the removal while the case proceeds.

**Claims / defenses:** Cook alleged that no valid statutory cause or required process supported the removal and that the Board should not treat the seat as vacant. The Government argued cause existed, the President's Article II power displaced or narrowed statutory protection, and the injunction was improper.

Sibling control map (not a rewrite): Independent-Agency Officer Removal — Slaughter and Cook

**Holding:** on the stay application, the Court held that a fixed term removable only for cause carries a requirement of notice and some opportunity to respond; Cook did not receive it. The majority also held Fed Governor removal protection consistent with the Constitution because the Fed fits a distinct historical central-bank tradition. The application for stay was denied.

**Remedy / limits:** preliminary injunction left in place. The Court did not finally determine whether the underlying mortgage allegations amount to statutory “cause” after lawful process. Footnote language leaves room for a new attempted removal following adequate process.

**Case life:** 2025-08-25 purported removal → 2025-09-09 preliminary injunction → D.C. Circuit stay litigation → 2026-01-21 Supreme Court argument on stay → 2026-06-29 stay denied → lower-court merits proceedings remain.

**Institutional effect:** prior rule (Governor serves a fixed term subject to removal for cause) → attempted executive displacement → injunction preserves Cook's seat and votes → Supreme Court confirms a constitutional zone of Fed independence and procedural floor → final cause determination still open.

**Primary sources:** [Supreme Court opinion/order](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a312_5468.pdf) · [Supreme Court docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25a312.html)

#### JM-02 — Slaughter

Sibling control map (not a rewrite): Independent-Agency Officer Removal — Slaughter and Cook

**Holding:** 15 U.S.C. §41's FTC removal restriction violates Article II; *Humphrey's Executor* no longer saves the modern FTC structure. **Fed limit:** the majority treated central-bank independence as a distinct historically grounded arrangement. That is a legal distinction, not a finding that every Fed regulatory function is immune from Article II analysis.

**Case life:** 2025 complaint and injunction → Supreme Court administrative/stay relief → 2025-12-08 merits argument → 2026-06-29 reversal/remand → mandate not separately verified in the public record used here.

**Institutional effect:** multi-member independent commissions previously relied on for-cause tenure → presidential removal control expanded → Fed alone retains the specific central-bank carve recognized in *Cook* and *Slaughter* → other financial regulators with commission structures face greater executive control unless a separate historical rule applies.

**Primary sources:** [Opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25-322_new_4gcj.pdf) · [Docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-332.html)

**Scope limit:** *Cook* does not establish that Reserve Banks are federal agencies, that all Fed supervision is constitutionally insulated, or that Congress may not restructure the System.

### B. Reserve Bank account access: the gate cases

#### JM-06 — Custodia

**Law:** 12 U.S.C. §§248a, 342, 461; Administrative Procedure Act; Federal Reserve account-access guidelines.  
**Question:** does an eligible state-chartered nonmember depository institution have a nondiscretionary right to a Reserve Bank master account and services?  
**Holding:** no. The Tenth Circuit held the statutes give Reserve Banks discretion to deny eligible institutions; it affirmed judgment for FRBKC and the Board. The panel also rejected the APA and mandamus routes presented. Judge Tymkovich dissented.

**Case life:** 2022-06-07 complaint over delay → 2023 denial supplied a final gate decision → 2024-03-29 district summary judgment for Fed → 2024 appeal → 2025-10-31 published panel opinion → 2026-03-13 revised/second published docket entry → 2026-05-29 cert extension to 2026-07-11 → petition not yet located at this check.

**Institutional effect:** prior uncertain statutory gate → FRBKC denial → district and circuit law confirm discretion → Custodia remains outside direct Fed rails and must use correspondents/private rails → proposed Payment Accounts remain a policy choice, not a remedy commanded by this case.

**Primary sources:** [Tenth Circuit opinion](https://www.ca10.uscourts.gov/sites/ca10/files/opinions/010111327582.pdf) · [Tenth Circuit case page](https://www.ca10.uscourts.gov/opinion/24-8024-0) · [Supreme Court extension docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25A1320.html)

#### JM-07 — Banco San Juan

**Law / claims:** FRA, APA, contract, due process. BSJI sought to prevent FRBNY from terminating a long-held master account. FRBNY relied on account agreements, operating circulars, risk findings, and statutory discretion.  
**Holding:** no statutory entitlement to continued master-account service; FRBNY was not reviewable as an APA agency on the theory advanced; contractual provisions authorized termination.

**Case life:** 2023-07-24 complaint + preliminary-injunction motion → 2023 preliminary relief denied → 2025-03-09/10 dismissal and judgment → 2025-05 appeal → 2026-05-13 affirmance → mandate not separately verified.

**Institutional effect:** existing account → proposed termination → no injunction → account gate closes under contract/risk process → Second and Tenth Circuits now converge on Reserve Bank discretion.

**Primary sources:** [District preliminary-injunction opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1:2023cv06414/602767/106/) · [Second Circuit docket/opinion index](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/circuit-courts/ca2/25-1144)

#### JM-08 and JM-09 — Fourth Corner and TNB

*Fourth Corner* exposed the split before the Fed issued its account-access guidelines: one judge saw statutory entitlement, one would remand for a lawful-business account, and one rejected relief. The judgment allowed an amended lawful-purpose theory, not a cannabis-banking master-account right. *TNB* was dismissed before final agency action. Together they show why *Custodia* matters: it supplies a later published circuit holding on the merits.

**Primary sources:** [Fourth Corner opinion page](https://www.ca10.uscourts.gov/opinion/16-1016) · [TNB dismissal](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1:2018cv07978/500217/52/)

**Cross-family finding:** the cases support a wide federal gate, but not an unlimited one. Eligibility, account access, Fed membership, deposit insurance, chartering, and access to central-bank credit are distinct decisions.

### C. Stablecoin charters, OCC authority, and state-law preemption

#### JM-10 — Lacewell

**Question:** may OCC charter a special-purpose national bank that does not accept deposits, thereby displacing parts of state licensing and supervision?  
**District holding:** OCC lacked that authority; the court set aside the rule as applied to nondepository fintech applicants.  
**Appellate holding:** DFS lacked a ripe injury before OCC chartered such an entity; judgment vacated and case ordered dismissed. The Second Circuit did not decide the NBA merits.

**Institutional effect:** threatened federal charter/preemption → district nationwide restraint → appellate vacatur → OCC charter channel remains legally available but merits risk is unresolved. New national trust-bank charters use a related but not identical statutory path; do not call *Lacewell* a stablecoin holding.

**Primary source:** [Second Circuit opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca2-19-04271/pdf/USCOURTS-ca2-19-04271-0.pdf)

#### JM-11 — Cantero

**Law:** National Bank Act; Dodd-Frank Act, 12 U.S.C. §25b; New York interest-on-escrow statute.  
**Supreme Court holding:** courts must apply the practical *Barnett Bank* standard—whether state law prevents or significantly interferes with national-bank powers—through a nuanced comparison, not a categorical OCC-style preemption rule.  
**Current posture:** after remand, the Second Circuit again ruled for Bank of America on 2026-05-05. Petition No. 25-1313, filed 2026-05-22, asks the Supreme Court to review that application. No cert disposition as of the status date.

**Institutional effect:** broad categorical preemption test rejected → case-specific significant-interference test controls → national stablecoin/trust issuers cannot assume every state money-transmission, reserve, consumer, or property rule is displaced merely by a federal charter.

**Primary sources:** [2024 opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/602us1r29_0861.pdf) · [new cert docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-1313.html)

**Search result:** no located federal or state merits judgment, as of 2026-07-11, decides preemption under the GENIUS Act for a permitted payment-stablecoin issuer. Charter approvals and OCC interpretations are agency events, not judicial holdings.

### D. Treasury clearing and market structure

#### JM-12 — Dealer Rule

**Law:** Exchange Act definitions of “dealer” and “government securities dealer”; APA; 2024 SEC Dealer Rule.  
**Holding:** SEC exceeded its statutory authority by defining dealer activity too broadly and untethering the line from the trader/dealer distinction. The court vacated the rule nationwide under the APA.  
**Remedy:** final vacatur, not a preliminary stay.

**Case life:** 2024-03-18 complaint → cross-motions → 2024-11-14 in-person oral argument → 2024-11-21 memorandum opinion and final judgment vacating rule → no operative appellate reversal located.

**Institutional effect:** proposed registration, capital, reporting, and supervision perimeter for proprietary Treasury and crypto traders → vacatur → those firms do not become dealers under this rule → Treasury central clearing proceeds under the separate 2023 clearing rule.

**Primary sources:** [District opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/texas/txndce/4:2024cv00250/387652/46/) · [SEC adopting statement and rule link](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/uyeda-statement-dealer-trader-020624)

**Search result:** no direct merits suit against the SEC's December 2023 Treasury central-clearing rule was located in the primary and authoritative docket sources reviewed. Industry objections in FICC rulemaking comments and implementation requests are not lawsuits. The absence of a found case is not proof that no sealed, newly filed, or unindexed case exists.

### E. Treasury sanctions, emergency power, forfeiture, and financial property

#### JM-13 / JM-14 — Tornado Cash

**Question:** are immutable smart contracts “property” or an “interest in property” of a sanctionable foreign person under IEEPA and Treasury regulations?  
**Van Loon holding:** no. Immutable contracts cannot be owned or controlled and therefore fell outside the statutory property power on the record before the court. The Fifth Circuit reversed; it did not hold that all decentralized protocols or associated persons are immune from sanctions or criminal law.  
**Agency response:** Treasury removed Tornado Cash sanctions on 2025-03-21. This operational response is separately chartable from the appellate judgment.

**Case life:** 2022 designation → 2023 complaint → 2023 summary judgment for Treasury → 2024-09-17 appellate argument (time not verified here) → 2024-11-26 reversal → 2025 delisting and district remand/end.

**Institutional effect:** addresses and contracts blocked as sanctioned property → appellate boundary on immutable code → Treasury delisting → OFAC retains power over persons, mutable property, services, and transactions within IEEPA.

**Primary sources:** [Fifth Circuit docket/opinion index](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/circuit-courts/ca5/23-50669) · [Treasury delisting](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0057) · [Coin Center Eleventh Circuit argument page](https://www.ca11.uscourts.gov/node/5952)

#### JM-15 — Learning Resources / V.O.S.

Sibling control map (not a rewrite): IEEPA — Tariff Dead, Block Live

**Holding:** IEEPA does not authorize tariffs; tariff power is a tax power vested in Congress, and the statute's regulation-of-importation language does not silently transfer it. The Court distinguished blocking and sanctions authorities.  
**Jurisdiction:** the Court affirmed the Federal Circuit/Court of International Trade route and ordered the parallel D.D.C. case dismissed for lack of jurisdiction.

**Institutional effect:** emergency order imposed economy-wide import taxes → lower courts restrained/invalidated → Supreme Court removes IEEPA tariff tool → Treasury/President retain sanctions tools Congress actually specified.

**Primary sources:** [Opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1287_4gcj.pdf) · [Docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/24-1287.html)

#### JM-16 — Bank Markazi

**Holding:** Congress may amend the law governing attachment of specified foreign sovereign assets even when the statute identifies pending litigation, so long as it supplies a new legal standard rather than directing courts to make false findings.  
**Institutional effect:** blocked central-bank assets became available to satisfy the judgments. The decision recognizes Congress's power to change the governing attachment law for identified assets and litigation.

**Primary source:** [Supreme Court docket and argument materials](https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/audio/2015/14-770)

#### JM-25 — Culley

**Holding / limit:** due process requires a timely forfeiture merits hearing, not a second preliminary retention hearing. The opinion concerns state-seized vehicles; applying it to Treasury-held digital assets or federal forfeiture requires separate statutory analysis.

**Primary source:** [Opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/23pdf/22-585_k5fm.pdf)

### F. Regulatory independence, removal, agency funding, and self-funding

#### JM-03 — CFPB funding

**Question:** does Dodd-Frank's authorization for CFPB to draw capped amounts from the Federal Reserve violate the Appropriations Clause?  
**Holding:** no. An appropriation identifies a source and a purpose; Congress may make a standing appropriation outside annual bills.  
**Limit:** the decision did not decide later statutory questions about what counts as Federal Reserve “combined earnings” or whether an acting director may deliberately request no funds.

**Case life:** 2022 Fifth Circuit invalidation → 2023-10-03 Supreme Court argument → 2024-05-16 reversal → remand and mandate → payday rule litigation continues on other grounds.

**Institutional effect:** CFPB funding mechanism threatened → Supreme Court validates statutory channel → later political and statutory actions reduce operations anyway → *NTEU v. Vought* tests those later acts.

**Primary sources:** [Opinion/docket index](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/slipopinion/23)

#### JM-04 — NTEU v. Vought

**Claims:** plaintiffs allege work stops, terminations, contract cancellations, data loss, office closure, refusal to draw funds, and mass RIFs amount to unlawful abolition of an agency Congress created. **Key procedural events:**

- 2025-03-28 preliminary injunction preserving staffing, records, contracts, complaint functions, and funding steps;

- 2025-08-15 divided panel vacated much of the injunction on jurisdiction/finality grounds;

- 2026-02-24 en banc argument;
- 2026-06-19 limited remand to the district court while the en banc court retained jurisdiction;
- No. 26-5220 remains open in connection with later district action.

**Institutional effect:** attempted rapid shutdown → district restraint maintains a working floor → panel briefly narrows restraint → en banc process and later orders preserve uncertainty → CFPB continues in a much reduced interim state; no final merits rule yet defines how small it may become.

### G. FDIC resolution, FHFA conservatorship, emergency authority, and review bars

#### JM-17 / JM-18 — Collins and Perry Capital

**Law:** Housing and Economic Recovery Act, especially 12 U.S.C. §4617(f); separation of powers; Administrative Procedure Act; contract law.  
**Holding:** when FHFA acts within conservator powers, courts may not restrain or affect those powers. An unconstitutional removal restriction does not automatically void agency actions; a plaintiff must show compensable harm caused by the removal defect.

**Case life:** 2008 conservatorships → 2012 net-worth sweep → 2013 shareholder suits → 2017 D.C. Circuit review-bar ruling → 2021 Supreme Court judgment/remand → later damages/contract proceedings did not restore the pre-sweep operating rule.

**Institutional effect:** private shareholders/government conservator seam → Treasury sweep continues through litigation → statutory equitable attacks blocked → release remains an executive/Treasury/FHFA policy and contract question unless Congress changes the law.

**Primary source:** [Collins opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-422_new_c0n2.pdf)

#### JM-26 — FDIC receivership bar

**Rule:** 12 U.S.C. §1821(j) prevents courts from restraining or affecting FDIC's exercise of receiver/conservator powers; review remains possible when FDIC acts outside those powers, and claims for money may follow FIRREA's administrative process.  
**Institutional effect:** resolution action can become operational before ordinary litigation finishes. This is the strongest FDIC analogue to HERA §4617(f), but the statutes, entities, and asset paths are separate.

**Open gap:** no recent merits decision located in this pass directly alters the 2023 SVB/Signature receiverships' core payment, deposit-transfer, or bridge-bank operations. Bankruptcy and tax-refund disputes around SVB Financial Group are consequential asset-allocation cases, but they do not presently displace FDIC's core receiver authority and are reserved for a later resolution-estate submap.

### H. Payment surveillance, financial privacy, AML, and financial-data law

#### JM-19 — New York v. Trump

**Law:** APA; Privacy Act; Internal Revenue Code confidentiality; Constitution/statutory payment authority allegations.  
**Holding at preliminary stage:** states showed likely APA defects and risk from rushed access to sensitive data; narrow relief barred DOGE-linked access until Treasury supplied training, vetting, authority, mitigation, and reporting-chain information. The court refused to enjoin all work on automated or manual payment-integrity processes.

**Case life:** 2025-02-07 complaint → 2025-02-08 TRO → 2025-02-14 hearing → 2025-02-21 preliminary injunction → later modifications (Dkts. 139, 156) → 2025-07-29 appeal No. 25-1860 → pending.

**Institutional effect:** broad new access → emergency lock → trained/vetted, role-based interim access under modifications → Treasury payment control remains with Treasury, subject to privacy and process restraints; appeal may redraw the boundary.

**Primary sources:** [TRO](https://nysd.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/25cv1144%20Order%20on%20TRO%202%208%2025_0.pdf) · [preliminary-injunction opinion](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/court-filings/state-of-new-york-et-al-v-donald-trump-opinion-and-order-2025_0.pdf)

#### JM-20 — Alliance v. Bessent

**Holding:** plaintiffs plausibly alleged standing based on sensitive financial-data privacy, but discovery showed authorized access was intra-Treasury access by Treasury employees. The challenged internal sharing was not final agency action; a single unauthorized external email did not prove an agency policy; alternative Privacy Act/IRC remedies defeated ultra-vires review. Summary judgment entered for Treasury without oral argument.

**Case life:** 2025-02-03 complaint → 2025-02-06 consent order limiting access → 2025-02-24 preliminary-injunction hearing → 2025-03-07 PI denied → discovery/administrative record → 2026-03-05 summary judgment for defendants.

**Institutional effect:** temporary consent restraint → no independent D.D.C. injunction → S.D.N.Y. injunction remains the actual operational restraint → the D.D.C. judgment treats the proven internal access policy as outside APA final-agency-action review.

**Primary sources:** [Complaint](https://cases.justia.com/federal/district-courts/district-of-columbia/dcdce/1:2025cv00313/277055/1/0.pdf) · [consent order](https://docs.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/district-of-columbia/dcdce/1:2025cv00313/277055/13) · [2026 merits opinion text](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/69aa6c9f242b4b135c64928e/amp)

#### JM-21 / JM-22 — Corporate Transparency Act cases

**Law:** Corporate Transparency Act, 31 U.S.C. §5336; Commerce, taxing, foreign-affairs, and Necessary and Proper powers; First and Fourth Amendment claims; APA.  
**Conflicting procedural effects:** Alabama final judgment protected only named plaintiffs; Texas universal injunction stopped the reporting rule nationwide; Fifth Circuit stay orders changed twice in days; Supreme Court stayed the Texas universal injunction on 2025-01-23; a separate *Smith* injunction remained briefly.

**Agency implementation response:** Treasury/FinCEN first paused penalties, then on 2025-03-26 exempted all entities created in the United States and all U.S. persons, leaving reporting mainly for qualifying foreign entities. That agency rule—not a final nationwide constitutional holding—created the present operating state.

**Primary sources:** [Supreme Court stay opinion/order](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/24a653_c07d.pdf) · [FinCEN current rule explanation](https://www.fincen.gov/boi/Reference-materials) · [Treasury implementation release](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0060)

#### JM-23 — Forcht Bank

**Claims:** CFPB exceeded §1033, delegated too much to private standard setters, imposed unsafe data-sharing duties, and acted arbitrarily. CFPB under later leadership agreed the rule needed substantial reconsideration but did not by agreement alone erase a final rule.

**Procedural effect:** district court stayed compliance dates and preliminarily enjoined enforcement; first large-provider deadline moved from 2026-04-01 to 2026-06-30 and is not operative while the injunction/reconsideration posture controls. Consolidated Sixth Circuit appeal No. 25-6164 is open.

**Institutional effect:** mandatory open-banking interface → litigation stay → no compelled production under the 2024 rule → CFPB rule rewrite in progress.

**Primary sources:** [CFPB rulemaking page](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/personal-financial-data-rights/) · [Sixth Circuit docket](https://dockets.justia.com/docket/circuit-courts/ca6/25-6164)

#### JM-24 — Chatrie

**Holding:** acquiring Google's dense location-history response to a geofence warrant was a search. The Court's judgment is about location privacy and the Fourth Amendment; it does not overrule *United States v. Miller* for bank records. **Inference:** it supplies a digital-age argument against mechanically applying old third-party doctrine to comprehensive financial telemetry, but that next step remains unheld.

**Primary sources:** [Opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25-112_0am4.pdf) · [Docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-112.html)

### I. Emergency lending and bailout authority

#### JM-27 / JM-28 — The two AIG rescue cases

The two *Starr* cases arise from the same rescue but decide different claims against different defendants.

**Source facts:** On 2008-09-16 the Board authorized FRBNY to lend AIG up to $85 billion under then-existing FRA §13(3). Rescue documents included an approximately 79.9% equity/control term. FRBNY later used Maiden Lane vehicles in the unwind and counterparty transactions.

**JM-27 holding and procedural effect:** After trial, the Court of Federal Claims held §13(3) did not authorize taking equity as consideration, but found no damages because rescue left shareholders better off than bankruptcy. The Federal Circuit vacated that merits judgment because the direct claims belonged to AIG, ordered dismissal, and did not decide legality. Certiorari was denied. The vacated trial holding is a historical case event, not binding law.

**JM-28 holding:** The Second Circuit held federal common law displaced Delaware fiduciary rules that conflicted with FRBNY's federal stabilization functions. It also sustained dismissal on the pleaded fiduciary relationship.

**Case-life clocks:**

- JM-27: 2011-11-21 complaint → 2014 trial → 2015-06-15 trial opinion → 2015-06-17 judgment → 2016-11-04 appellate argument → 2017-05-09 vacatur/dismissal order → 2018-03-26 cert denied.
- JM-28: 2011-11-21 complaint → 2012-11-16 dismissal → 2013-09-17 argument → 2014-01-29 affirmance → 2014-06-30 cert denied.

**Institutional-effect clock:** private AIG liquidity crisis → FRBNY loan plus equity/control term → federal ownership/recapitalization → assistance repaid/unwound → no damages and no operative judgment invalidating equity term → Congress independently amended §13(3) to require broad-based facilities, Treasury approval, insolvency limits, collateral protection, and disclosure.

**Primary sources:** [CFC trial opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/federal-claims/cofce/1:2011cv00779/26547/443/) · [Federal Circuit opinion](https://www.cafc.uscourts.gov/opinions-orders/15-5103.opinion.5-5-2017.1.pdf) · [Federal Circuit argument/audio](https://www.cafc.uscourts.gov/11-04-2016-2015-5103-starr-international-company-v-us-audio-uploaded/) · [Supreme Court response brief/docket record](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/starr-intl-co-v-united-states) · [Second Circuit JM-28 opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/12-5022/12-5022-2014-01-29.html) · [New York Fed AIG repository](https://www.newyorkfed.org/aboutthefed/aig)

#### JM-29 / JM-30 — Emergency-loan transparency

**Question:** which borrower, amount, collateral, maturity, and term records had to be disclosed under FOIA, and when did Reserve Bank material become a Board record?

**Holdings:** *Bloomberg* rejected FOIA Exemption 4 because the requested reports documented government lending actions rather than information “obtained from” borrowers. *Fox* required further search/disclosure but preserved a structural distinction: individual Reserve Bank loan files are not automatically Board records; Bank-held material maintained for Board administrative purposes may be.

**Case-life clock:** 2008 FOIA requests/complaints → 2009 conflicting district outcomes → tandem 2010-01-11 Second Circuit argument → 2010-03-19 disclosure/search rulings → 2011-03-21 cert denials → 2011-03-31 Board production.

**Institutional-effect clock:** borrower-level lending data withheld → disclosure ordered and stayed through review → Board publishes the records → Dodd-Frank independently creates a prospective delayed-disclosure and reporting framework for later §13(3) facilities.

**Primary sources:** [Bloomberg opinion](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/historical/fct/frsbog/fedres_bloomberg_20110331.pdf) · [Fox opinion](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/files/docs/historical/fct/frsbog/fedres_foxnews_20110331.pdf) · [Supreme Court 2011-03-21 order list](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/032111zor.pdf) · [Fed implementation account](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/testimony/alvarez20110601a.htm)

#### JM-31 — Chrysler

**Question:** whether Treasury-financed transfer of Chrysler assets through Bankruptcy Code §363 was an impermissible sub rosa plan, violated secured-creditor priority, or used TARP beyond EESA.

**Procedural result:** Bankruptcy Court approved the sale. The Second Circuit affirmed and held pension funds lacked standing to press the TARP issue, without deciding Treasury's statutory/constitutional authority. Justice Ginsburg temporarily stayed the sale on 2009-06-08; the Court denied a stay the next day. After closing made the dispute moot, the Supreme Court granted certiorari, vacated the appellate judgment, and ordered dismissal. The sale remained effective under §363(m); the vacated opinion is not precedent.

**Case-life clock:** 2009-04-30 petitions → 2009-05-31 sale/EESA opinions → 2009-06-01 sale order → 2009-06-05 argument/affirmance → 2009-06-08 temporary stay → 2009-06-09 stay denied → closing → 2009-12-14 GVR as moot → 2010-01-25 appellate dismissal.

**Institutional-effect clock:** no private rescue financing → Treasury/Canada DIP and sale funding → brief judicial pause → New Chrysler receives operating assets → old estate retains claims/proceeds → merits precedent disappears but transaction stands.

**Primary sources:** [Supreme Court stay docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docketfiles/08a1096.htm) · [official stay opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USREPORTS-556/USREPORTS-556-960) · [DOJ brief with lower opinions/history](https://www.justice.gov/d9/osg/briefs/2009/01/01/2009-0285.resp.pdf)

#### JM-32 — Murray

**Holding:** Sixth Circuit affirmed on taxpayer-standing grounds. The asserted downstream support for Sharia-compliant AIG products was incidental to a secular rescue program and did not fit the narrow *Flast* exception. The judgment did not approve or disapprove §13(3) or TARP's core bailout authority.

**Case life:** 2008 complaint → 2011-01-14 summary judgment without oral argument → 2012-04-20 appellate argument → 2012-06-01 affirmance.

**Primary sources:** [District opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/michigan/miedce/2:2008cv15147/235630/92/) · [Sixth Circuit opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca6/11-1063/11-1063-2012-06-01.html)

**Current §13(3) operating law:** post-Dodd-Frank emergency facilities must be broad-based, approved by Treasury, designed for system liquidity rather than a failing firm, protected by adequate collateral, unavailable to insolvent borrowers, and terminated in an orderly fashion. [Federal Reserve Act §13](https://www.federalreserve.gov/aboutthefed/section13.htm) · [2015 implementing rule](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20151130a.htm)

### J. Sanctions, forfeiture, and digital-asset custody

#### JM-33 — Global Relief Foundation

**Holding:** IEEPA “interest” may turn on beneficial control/use rather than bare domestic corporate title. In emergency circumstances, no preseizure hearing was required; the court could review classified material ex parte. The panel left the factual merits of the permanent designation for further review and made no criminal-guilt finding.

**Case-life clock:** 2001-12-14 interim block/search → preliminary relief denied → 2002-10-18 permanent SDGT designation → 2002-12-31 circuit ruling → 2003-11-10 cert denied.

**Institutional-effect clock:** operating nonprofit accounts → complete block → limited OFAC licenses for expenses → permanent designation → operations effectively end without a forfeiture judgment transferring title.

**Primary sources:** [Seventh Circuit opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/315/748/632995/) · [DOJ cert opposition and appendix](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/global-relief-fund-v-snow-opposition) · [Supreme Court order](https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/111003pzor.pdf)

#### JM-34 — Exxon Mobil

**Holding:** OFAC's regulations, FAQs, and public statements did not give ascertainably certain notice that receiving Sechin's signature on Rosneft agreements was a prohibited service. The court vacated the penalty on due-process fair-notice grounds and did not reach Exxon's other statutory/APA theories.

**Institutional effect:** $2 million enforcement penalty → judicial vacatur → underlying Russia sanctions remain; OFAC must state the prohibited theory with adequate clarity before imposing this civil penalty.

**Primary source:** [N.D. Texas opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/texas/txndce/3:2017cv01930/290850/110/)

#### JM-35 — Gratkowski

**Holding:** public blockchain data and the Coinbase transaction records obtained on this record carried no reasonable expectation of privacy. The Fifth Circuit treated the exchange records as analogous to bank records and also found the good-faith exception independently sufficient.

**Limit:** the court did not decide bulk, prolonged, or every later form of blockchain analysis; it decided suppression under the record before it.

**Institutional-effect clock:** pseudonymous public transfers → analytics identify a cluster → exchange KYC records connect cluster to a person → ordinary warrant reaches devices/property.

**Primary source:** [Fifth Circuit opinion](https://www.ca5.uscourts.gov/opinions/pub/19/19-50492-CR0.pdf)

#### JM-36 — 69,370 Bitcoin / Battle Born

**Holding:** At the summary-judgment standing stage, claimants needed evidence beyond an ownership assertion. A screenshot found on a bankruptcy debtor's laptop did not connect that debtor or asset purchasers to lawful ownership of the specific wallet. Claims were stricken; the court did not conduct a full merits trial over every historical Silk Road ownership assertion.

**Case-life clock:** 2020 federal seizure and owner consent → 2020-11-05 civil complaint → competing claims → 2022-03-25 claims stricken → 2023-08-18 affirmance → 2023-12-12 rehearing denied → 2024-10-07 cert denied.

**Institutional-effect clock:** Individual X controls private keys → government obtains control → competing claimants fail standing proof → government custody/title position remains → 2025 executive policy generally directs qualifying finally forfeited federal BTC toward reserve custody, but wallet-specific transfer remains unverified.

**Primary sources:** [DOJ complaint](https://www.justice.gov/d9/press-releases/attachments/2020/11/05/complaint_crypto_seizure.pdf) · [district order](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/3:2020cv07811/368440/104/) · [Ninth Circuit disposition](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/memoranda/2023/08/18/22-16348.pdf) · [Supreme Court petition record](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/23/23-1164/308371/20240425142311406_FINAL%202024-04-25%20Battle%20Born%20Petition%20PDFA.pdf)

#### JM-37 — Celsius

**Holding:** For customers bound by the operative Terms of Use, title to Earn assets transferred to Celsius and the assets entered the estate under 11 U.S.C. §541. Earn users held unsecured claims. The ruling reserved individualized contract-formation defenses, fraud/breach and securities issues, and ownership of Custody, Withhold, and Borrow assets.

**Remedy:** court declared ownership and authorized sale of approximately $18 million in Earn stablecoins for estate administration. Plan confirmed 2023-11-09, effective 2024-01-31; distributions followed.

**Institutional-effect clock:** account interface/customer expectation → title-transfer and rehypothecation terms control → estate owns Earn pool → customers receive unsecured claims → stablecoins may be sold → plan distributions replace withdrawal rights.

**Primary sources:** [official bankruptcy opinion](https://www.nysb.uscourts.gov/sites/default/files/opinions/312902_1822_opinion.pdf) · [official court page](https://www.nysb.uscourts.gov/content/re-22-10964-mg-celsius-network-llc-6)

#### JM-38 — BlockFi

**Wallet holding:** qualifying custodial Wallet assets were customer property rather than estate property, subject to stated avoidance, setoff, insider, transfer-timing, and related limits. This contrasts with Celsius because the product terms and custody structure differed.

**Forfeiture-adversary holding:** court denied the United States' dismissal motion. Forfeitable property actually identified in warrants could relate back and stay outside the estate, but government stepped only into the criminal defendants' actual/traceable rights; named-account warrants did not automatically transfer unrelated commingled estate assets. A later stay request was denied, and more than $35 million remained restrained during tracing. Later settlement/allocation must be verified before entry as final fact.

**Case-life clock:** 2022 criminal warrants and platform pause → Chapter 11 → 2023-05-17 Wallet Order → 2023-05-22 adversary complaint → 2023-10-10 dismissal denied → 2024-01-30 stay denied → later disposition unverified.

**Primary sources:** [official BlockFi court page](https://www.njb.uscourts.gov/BlockFi) · [2023 forfeiture/bankruptcy opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-njb-3_23-ap-01144/pdf/USCOURTS-njb-3_23-ap-01144-0.pdf) · [2024 stay opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-njb-3_23-ap-01144/pdf/USCOURTS-njb-3_23-ap-01144-1.pdf)

### K. Foreign central-bank and sovereign assets

#### JM-39 — Dames & Moore

**Holding:** IEEPA authorized blocking/transfer of Iranian assets and nullification of attachments. Suspension of private claims for the Iran–U.S. Claims Tribunal was upheld in the narrow combination of congressional authorization/acquiescence and longstanding executive claims-settlement practice. **Holding:** approximately $105 million in BCRA's FRBNY account was immune under FSIA §1611(b)(1). “Held for its own account” turns on central-banking functions; ordinary *Bancec* alter-ego analysis does not by itself erase the central-bank-specific protection.

**Case-life clock:** 2005 attachment request → 2010 district alter-ego/attachment orders → 2010 appeal → 2011-07-05 reversal/remand.

**Institutional-effect clock:** protected reserve account → temporary judicial restraint → appellate restoration of immunity → FRBNY remains protected custody location absent waiver or specific superseding law.

#### JM-41 — Republic of Argentina v. NML

**Holding:** FSIA contains no discovery-immunity provision limiting ordinary postjudgment discovery into a sovereign's worldwide assets. Discovery does not establish situs, ownership, attachment, or execution.

**Case-life clock:** 2010 bank subpoenas → 2011 compel order → 2012 Second Circuit affirmance → 2014-04-21 Supreme Court argument → 2014-06-16 affirmance → 2016 settlement/dismissal.

**Primary sources:** [official opinion in U.S. Reports volume 573](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/boundvolumes/573bv.pdf) · [argument transcript](https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/argument_transcripts/2013/12-842_2135.pdf)

#### JM-42 — Rubin

**Holding:** FSIA §1610(g) identifies property interests available once immunity is removed; it does not independently remove execution immunity. Terrorism creditors needed another §1610 or TRIA basis. Antiquities held by the University of Chicago and Field Museum remained immune.

**Institutional-effect clock:** museum custody under sovereign ownership claim → creditor execution attempt → final immunity → no turnover.

**Primary sources:** [Supreme Court docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/16-534.html) · [argument transcript](https://www.supremecourt.gov/oral_arguments/argument_transcripts/2017/16-534_if5n.pdf)

#### JM-16 / JM-43 — Peterson I and II

**Peterson I holding:** Congress changed substantive law through 22 U.S.C. §8772 for identified Iranian assets and litigation without impermissibly directing false judicial findings. Turnover of roughly $1.75 billion remained valid. It did not erase §1611(b)(1) generally.

**Peterson II current posture:** The assets are a claimed $1.68 billion right to payment credited in Luxembourg after bond payments moved through New York correspondent rails. In 2024 the Second Circuit dismissed the turnover claim against Bank Markazi for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction, sustained personal jurisdiction over Clearstream, rejected Clearstream's constitutional challenge, and remanded ownership/Rule 19 issues. Cert denied 2025-06-23. On 2026-03-31 Judge Preska again found Bank Markazi beneficially owned the proceeds and granted summary judgment for creditors. No later appeal or completed custodial transfer was located.

**Primary sources:** [Bank Markazi docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/14-770.html) · [2024 Second Circuit Peterson II opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USCOURTS-ca2-23-00614) · [2026 district opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1:2013cv09195/421817/409/)

#### JM-44 / JM-45 — Crystallex and the CITGO creditor queue

**Holdings:** *Crystallex* held PDVSA was Venezuela's alter ego under *Bancec* and allowed attachment of PDVH shares, U.S.-situs commercial property. *OI European Group* held later political/governance changes did not end alter-ego status when both recognized interim authorities and the Maduro government were considered.

**Procedural versus operational state:** attachment and judicial-sale process remain valid, but execution/closing requires a specific OFAC license. Court authority to sell and Treasury authority to license are separate gates. No completed divestiture should be recorded until license and closing are verified.

**Primary sources:** [Crystallex Third Circuit opinion](https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/182797p.pdf) · [OI European Third Circuit opinion](https://www2.ca3.uscourts.gov/opinarch/231647p.pdf) · [current OFAC FAQ 1246](https://ofac.treasury.gov/faqs/1246)

### L. Bank failures, deposit insurance, bridge banks, and resolution estates

#### JM-46 — SVB Financial Trust

**Seam and posture:** The dispute separates the 2023 systemic-risk protection of Silicon Valley Bank depositors from the receiver's later claims against the bank holding company. Related bankruptcy proceedings were moved into district-court litigation because the FIRREA/systemic-risk questions were substantial. After plan effectiveness, SVB Financial Trust succeeded to the claims. **Case-life clock:** SVB closure → systemic-risk determination → parent Chapter 11 → adversary complaint → reference withdrawn → California merits litigation → plan confirmation/effectiveness → partial settlement → partial summary judgment → scheduled trial.

**Institutional-effect clock:** ordinary insurance ceiling → all bank deposits transferred/protected → holding-company access withheld → receiver claims setoff → $1.7 billion baseline stipulated → trial determines reductions → later estate/special-assessment accounting.

**Primary sources:** [official N.D. Cal. case page](https://cand.uscourts.gov/cases-e-filing/cases/524-cv-01321-blf/svb-financial-trust-v-federal-deposit-insurance-corporation) · [reference-withdrawal opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1%3A2023cv07218/604443/34/) · [2026 order](https://cases.justia.com/federal/district-courts/california/candce/5%3A2024cv01321/425822/284/0.pdf) · [FDIC special assessment](https://www.fdic.gov/deposit-insurance-assessments/special-assessment-pursuant-systemic-risk-determination)

#### JM-47–49 — Signature and First Republic transfer boundaries

**JM-47 holding:** deposit status turned on the failed bank's books at default. Money allegedly diverted in 2019 was neither an insured Signature deposit in March 2023 nor a liability assumed by Signature Bridge or Flagstar. A damages claim against a failed bank is not automatically a deposit. [Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1%3A2023cv03775/598313/75/)

**JM-48 procedural effect:** Signature-related pre-failure liabilities retained by FDIC-R remained subject to FIRREA's administrative claims and exhaustion channel. The ruling did not decide claimant entitlement on the merits. [Order](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1%3A2023cv04242/599182/29/)

**JM-49 holding:** the First Republic/JPMorgan successor action seeking to stop former advisers from asserting counterclaims in FINRA was unripe because the arbitrators had not decided whether to hear the claims. Dismissal without prejudice left the P&A allocation unresolved. [Opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-cand-4_24-cv-02574/pdf/USCOURTS-cand-4_24-cv-02574-0.pdf) · [FDIC P&A](https://www.fdic.gov/system/files/2024-07/first-republic-p-and-a.pdf)

**Shared institutional clock:** bank closes → bridge bank or immediate acquirer takes only specified assets/liabilities → FDIC-R retains residual claims → deposit insurance, receivership waterfall, contract assumption, and successor liability remain separate questions.

#### JM-50–54 — ownership, exhaustion, restraint, and repudiation baselines

**JM-50 holding:** *Rodriguez* abolished the judge-made *Bob Richards* presumption for consolidated tax refunds. State law and the parties' tax-allocation contract decide whether value belongs to the holding-company bankruptcy estate or bank receiver. [Supreme Court docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/18-1269.html)

**JM-51 holding:** WaMu noteholders could not intervene based on contingent fear that FDIC/JPMorgan liability litigation would deplete the receivership. The court did not resolve the underlying P&A allocation. [D.C. Circuit opinion](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2013/05/12-5170-1437143.pdf)

**JM-52 holding:** claims arising from WaMu's pre-receivership conduct were jurisdictionally barred without FIRREA presentment. This is an exhaustion ruling, not a merits finding that no wrong occurred.

**JM-53 holding:** §1821(j) barred an injunction restraining FDIC-R even where Bank of America alleged that mortgage assets were merely custodial and never belonged to Colonial Bank; damages/administrative routes remained. [Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca11/09-14739/200914739-2011-02-28.html)

**JM-54 holding:** receiver repudiation converts performance rights into statutorily limited actual-direct-damages claims; it does not make contract value disappear. [DOJ procedural history](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/waterview-mgmt-co-v-fdic-opposition)

**Institutional-effect clock:** failed-bank appointment → immediate asset transfer or repudiation → anti-injunction/exhaustion rules preserve receiver operation → ownership and damages proceed through designated channels → estate priority determines recovery.

### M. Clearinghouses, default waterfalls, SRO power, and settlement finality

#### JM-55–58 — uniform rules, fair procedure, capital, and regulatory approval

**JM-55 holding:** *Whistler* conflict-preempted state claims that would alter an SEC-approved NSCC stock-borrow/fail-management program, while rejecting field preemption. Federal uniformity protects approved design, not every act of a clearing utility. [Ninth Circuit opinion](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2008/08/21/0616088.pdf)

**JM-56 holding and response:** SEC held that a DTC global lock denied or limited services “with respect to” the issuer and therefore required notice, an opportunity to respond, and a supporting record. DTC retained substantive exclusion power and later formalized lock/chill procedures. [SEC opinion](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/opinions/2012/34-66611.pdf) · [rule proceeding](https://www.sec.gov/file/rel-no-34-71745)

**JM-57 holding:** SEC could not simply accept OCC's assertions about its capital target, replenishment commitment, dividends, refunds, and competition effects. The approval was remanded without vacatur to prevent operational disruption; later reapproval supplied authority. The court did not find the waterfall terms substantively unlawful. [D.C. Circuit opinion](https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2017/08/16-1061-1687695.pdf) · [2019 SEC order](https://www.sec.gov/rules/sro/occ/2019/34-85121.pdf)

**JM-58 holding:** petitioning received Noerr-Pennington protection, and the CFTC-approved CBOT open-interest migration received implied antitrust immunity because CFTC actively reviewed authority, competition, and the rule. Casual or unsupervised conduct does not receive automatic immunity. [Seventh Circuit opinion](https://media.ca7.uscourts.gov/cgi-bin/rssExec.pl?Path=Y2020%2FD03-23%2FC%3A18-3558%3AJ%3AManion%3Aaut%3AT%3AfnOp%3AN%3A2491263%3AS%3A0&Submit=Display)

#### JM-59 — MF Global customer property

**Holding/effect:** MF Global's broker liquidation, holding-company Chapter 11, CME clearing relationship, and customer pools remained legally distinct. Court orders transferred roughly 15,000 positions and about $1.45 billion in collateral; commodity customers recovered through statutory pools and settlements. Sapere could not automatically impose commodity-broker priority on separate holding-company estates.

**Institutional-effect clock:** segregated-customer rule and clearing novation → FCM collapse/shortfall → emergency bulk transfers and pro rata distribution → settlements and regulatory restitution → final customer recovery. Central clearing protected counterparties and enabled transfer; it did not prevent failure or erase customer shortfalls.

**Primary sources:** [CFTC case repository](https://www.cftc.gov/IndustryOversight/Intermediaries/mfglobal) · [official bankruptcy page](https://www.nysb.uscourts.gov/content/re-11-02790-mg-mf-global-inc-et-al) · [CFTC restitution release](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/6904-14)

#### JM-60–61 — safe-harbor and live member-access boundaries

**JM-60 holding:** *Merit Management* looks to the overarching transfer the trustee seeks to avoid. A protected bank/broker/clearing conduit does not alone invoke §546(e) when the ultimate parties are unprotected; the customer/agent definition was left open. [Supreme Court docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/16-784.html)

**JM-61 current posture:** Alpine alleges NSCC/DTC exclusion, capital, and adjudicatory powers are sufficiently governmental to trigger structural constitutional limits. District court denied interim relief and dismissed, finding no likely state action and emphasizing SEC review. The Tenth Circuit heard argument on 2026-01-21; no decision was located by status date. [2024 opinion](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-utd-2_23-cv-00782/pdf/USCOURTS-utd-2_23-cv-00782-0.pdf) · [SEC proceeding](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/administrative-proceedings/3-20238)

### N. Payment-rail finality, reversal, blocking, and public-payment control

**Controlling distinction:** technical settlement finality does not equal immunity from restitution, attachment, sanctions blocking, forfeiture, benefit exemption, or Treasury offset. Preserve payment-order creation, rail acceptance, Reserve Bank/commercial-bank posting, recipient availability, recall, freeze, judgment, and recovery as separate events.

#### JM-62–63 — Regulation J and the Article 4A chain

**JM-62 holding:** Regulation J supplied the federal beneficiary-identification rule for the Fedwire transfer and preempted inconsistent state negligence/wrongful-payment theories. [Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/64/944/631685/) · [Federal Reserve Regulation J collection](https://www.federalreserve.gov/frrs/regulations/regulation-j-collection-of-checks-and-other-items-by-federal-reserve-banks-and-funds-transfers-through-fedwire.htm)

**JM-63 holding:** Article 4A's refund obligation runs link by link. An originator cannot skip its own receiving bank and recover from a downstream intermediary; the structure allocates intermediary insolvency risk. [Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/160/97/533961/)

**Institutional-effect clock:** sender issues order → banks accept link-by-link obligations → rail settlement becomes final between participants → failed transfer invokes immediate-bank refund chain rather than end-to-end ownership fiction.

#### JM-64–66 — mistaken payment and the limits of exclusivity

**JM-64 holding:** *Banque Worms* permits a creditor without notice to retain mistaken payment of a debt then due. It is a New York restitution defense, not a universal rule that completed wires cannot be recovered.

**JM-65 holding:** Revlon lenders could not keep about $500 million mistakenly paid years before maturity because they had inquiry notice and no present entitlement. The Second Circuit reversed, and funds were returned. The record's minute-by-minute operating sequence is preserved in the charting index. [Second Circuit opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/21-487/21-487-2022-09-08.html) · [district opinion hosted by FRBNY](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/Microsites/fmlg/files/2021/20cv6539-citibank-opinion)

**JM-66 holding:** Article 4A did not displace conversion/unjust-enrichment allegations about knowingly applying a payment to a regulator-frozen BCCI account where the alleged misconduct fell outside Article 4A's express loss allocations. [Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/951/403/1381447/)

#### JM-67–69 — sanctions interruption and blocked-EFT ownership

**JM-67 holding:** Iraq retained a contingent interest in letter-of-credit funds when the block attached; a district court could not compel OFAC to issue an execution license. Blocking maintained the status quo rather than adjudicating final ownership. [D.C. Circuit opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/27/695/541871/)

**JM-68 holding:** the Second Circuit applied New York Article 4A: neither originator nor beneficiary owns a midstream EFT; the immediately preceding sender's interest determines whether sovereign property reached the blocking bank.

**JM-69 holding:** the D.C. Circuit declined to use Article 4A for a TRIA sanctions collision and permitted federal tracing of Iranian funds where no innocent intermediary asserted an interest. This is a genuine methodological tension, not proof of coordinated litigation. [Opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/cadc/21-7036/21-7036-2022-08-16.html)

#### JM-70–72 — benefit protection after payment versus federal offset before payment

**JM-70/JM-71 holdings:** Social Security protection follows qualifying funds into a readily withdrawable account and defeats state reimbursement/attachment. Modern 31 C.F.R. Part 212 uses ACH coding and a protected-amount review to operationalize this boundary. [Part 212](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/31/part-212) · [Treasury FAQ](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/payments-from-government/direct-deposit/faq-garnishment)

**JM-72 holding:** later federal law expressly allowed Treasury to offset Social Security payments for old student-loan debt notwithstanding §407. Treasury's pre-disbursement match/withholding is not garnishment of a completed bank deposit. [U.S. Reports](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USREPORTS-546/USREPORTS-546-142) · [TOP authorities](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/debt-management/treasury-offset-program-top/legal-authorities-quick-reference)

**Institutional-effect clock:** agency certifies benefit → Treasury applies statutory offset if authorized → net payment leaves Treasury → ACH-coded protected benefit reaches bank → institution must preserve protected amount against most private/state garnishment.

## 11. Source and verification notes

1. Supreme Court opinions and dockets were checked directly through `supremecourt.gov`.
2. Tenth Circuit opinions were checked through `ca10.uscourts.gov`; Second, Fifth, Eleventh, and D.C. Circuit items use official pages where indexed and authoritative docket mirrors where the official public interface did not expose stable document links.
3. Treasury, FinCEN, CFPB, OCC, SEC, FDIC, and FHFA operating effects should be refreshed from agency pages whenever this map is updated.
4. “No case found” is a bounded research result as of the status date, not a universal negative.
5. Mandate dates remain **not publicly stated / not yet verified** unless expressly listed. A decision date is not silently treated as a mandate date.
6. For chart work, retrieve the actual hearing transcript header, minute entry, Notice of Electronic Filing, or appellate mandate before assigning an exact clock time.
7. A vacated opinion is retained as a historical case event but is labeled nonoperative. This rule applies especially to the CFC merits holding in JM-27 and the Second Circuit opinion in JM-31.
8. Certiorari denial is recorded as final procedural state, not as Supreme Court approval of the lower court's reasoning.
9. Blocking, seizure, final forfeiture, bankruptcy-estate ownership, attachment, execution, licensing, and actual asset transfer are recorded as separate legal states.
10. Current agency implementation materials were checked for §13(3), OFAC virtual-currency custody, the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve, CITGO licensing, and domestic BOI operations. Wallet/account-level implementation remains unverified unless an official record identifies it.
11. FDIC-C, FDIC-R for a failed bank, FDIC-R for a bridge bank, the bridge bank, the holding company, and the acquiring bank are not treated as one actor or one estate.
12. A payment's technical acceptance, interbank finality, customer-account credit, beneficial ownership, sanctions status, restitution exposure, and garnishment/offset status are separate fields.
13. SEC/CFTC approval, clearinghouse rule operation, judicial review, and production activation are separate events. No located merits challenge to the Treasury-clearing mandate is not a judicial endorsement.
