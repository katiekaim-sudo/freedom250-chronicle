# Tribal Data Center Return Refresh — October 5, 2026

## In plain English

**Question:** What happened to the Cherokee report, the Pocatello/Hoku appeal,
DOE's Tribal-energy awards and the Tribal nuclear/data-center pairing question?

**Answer:** Cherokee Nation published the full task-force report. It does not
approve a data center; it recommends continuing sovereign review and early
consultation, with the Nation favoring workforce, supplier, ratepayer and
community-benefit leverage over direct hyperscale investment. Pocatello's
official October 1 agenda prepared adoption of a decision upholding denial of
the former Hoku-site permit, but the final adopted decision and any judicial
review still need to be captured. DOE selections for the $50 million Tribal
energy round are expected around November 20, and the nuclear webinar still
does not name a Tribal data-center/microreactor project.

## 1. Cherokee Nation's report is public and the old non-detection is wrong

The 37-page **Report on the Impact of Data Centers on the Economy and Natural
Environment of the Cherokee Nation Reservation** is dated June 30 and was
published on the Nation's task-force page on August 5. It answers the task
force's full mandate and incorporates **1,593 citizen survey responses**.

The report identifies Google at Pryor plus Project Clydesdale near Owasso and
Project Mustang in Claremore as the known hyperscale projects within the
Reservation, while stressing that NDA use, fragmented permits and missing
registration rules prevent a complete project census.

Its institutional ruling is more important than its project count:

- land status controls the available Cherokee authority; the Nation retains
  full policy power over tribally owned and trust land but uses consultation,
  advocacy and intergovernmental influence on non-Tribal land;
- direct Cherokee investment in hyperscale data centers is not a strong fit,
  and scarce trust land should not become a tax-arbitrage device;
- workforce and supplier opportunities are the lowest-risk route to citizen
  benefit;
- data-center costs should follow cost causation, long-term service agreements
  and developer-funded infrastructure rather than ordinary ratepayers;
- where development proceeds, enforceable community-benefit terms should
  address local hiring, wages, infrastructure cost sharing, public reporting
  and early-exit risk;
- water, air, energy and cultural effects require project-specific facts and
  early consultation, not generic averages; and
- the task-force members should form an advisory committee to review proposed
  projects, participate in business consultations and advise the Principal
  Chief.

The report also records a live internal-use boundary: Cherokee Nation counted
3,970 Copilot actions in the measured month, estimated at less than 1.2 kWh,
and says its IT controls inherit data-boundary, privacy and exclusion rules.
That does not measure every embedded AI service or outside hyperscale load.

Primary sources: [Cherokee task-force page](https://www.cherokee.gov/our-government/executive-branch/task-forces/data-center-task-force/),
[full report](https://www.cherokee.gov/media/qpwnx21l/data-center-task-force.pdf).

## 2. Pocatello moved from appeal uncertainty to a prepared denial disposition

The Hearing Examiner denied CUP 26-003 in May because the applicant had not
supplied enough evidence on public facilities, environmental effects and
health/safety. The Shoshone-Bannock Tribes' July 7 official statement supported
that result and framed the former Hoku site as ceded land with unresolved
electricity-rate, water and environmental concerns.

Pocatello's October 1 official agenda then placed on the consent agenda a City
Council decision “upholding the denial” of Lex Development's reconsideration
request. That is a materially later official procedural object. At this cutoff,
the public agenda center did not expose adopted minutes or the signed written
decision, so the final adoption, reasoning, judicial-review petition and any
specific treatment of treaty or consultation arguments remain open.

Primary sources: [Hearing Examiner decision](https://pocatello.gov/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Minutes/_05142026-1900),
[Shoshone-Bannock statement](https://www.sbtribes.com/shoshone-bannock-tribes-reaffirm-opposition-to-proposed-ai-data-center-in-pocatello/),
[October 1 City Council agenda](https://pocatello.gov/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Agenda/_10012026-1953?html=true).

## 3. The Tribal-energy selection clock is November, not July

DOE's informational-webinar record supplies the later programme clock that the
August return lacked: selections are estimated around **November 20, 2026** and
final awards around **February 3, 2027**. The July 24 deadline was only the
application close.

No public applicant list or selection tied to data-center power, transmission
or site work was located by October 5. The funding opportunity allows
community-scale deployment and both community-scale and large-scale planning;
eligibility does not establish that a data-center project applied.

Primary source: [DOE NOFO webinar record](https://www.energy.gov/indianenergy/notice-funding-opportunity-informational-webinar-unleashing-tribal-energy-development),
[DOE funding announcement](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-announces-50-million-investment-advance-affordable-reliable-and-secure).

## 4. The nuclear webinar still produced no named compute-power project

DOE's webinar archive and August 13 materials remain general education on
civilian nuclear technologies, small modular reactors, microreactors and
federal support. No named Tribe, site, reactor developer, data-center sponsor,
agreement or follow-up work pairs nuclear generation to Tribal compute.

DOE's separate Tribal Data Center Exploration Cohort is an education and
discussion lane, not a building commitment or nuclear partnership.

Primary sources: [DOE nuclear webinar](https://www.energy.gov/indianenergy/events/tribal-pathways-nuclear-energy-technology-opportunities-and-federal-support),
