Wave 4C — Mission Science, Data Platforms and Knowledge Jurisdiction

Research window: Exact 2025 Libra ingress through exact 2025 Capricorn ingress.
Evidence cutoff: June 20, 2026, 11:59 p.m. EDT. Later facts are quarantined as handoffs.
Question: When five Libra-period orders elevated pediatric-cancer AI, a national scientific platform, Federal AI policy, space superiority and cannabinoid research, did they create one centralized science system—or five different institutional routes by which the state can select, organize and test knowledge?
Finding: They created five different routes. Pediatric-cancer AI entered an existing NCI data ecosystem with an announced funding increase and an April 2026 investigator-interest notice. Genesis acquired the strongest new mission carrier: DOE ownership, 26 named challenges, national-laboratory teams, a consortium, a $293 million application round and an international partnership plan. Federal AI policy acquired a litigation task force, legislative recommendations and one Colorado intervention, but no enacted national standard or blanket State-law preemption. Space policy acquired nuclear guidance, NASA solicitations and an agency realignment, but no lunar base, reactor or proved $50 billion investment outcome. Marijuana/CBD research acquired a presidential rulemaking and evidence-development command, while marijuana remained Schedule I at the cutoff and the formal hearing still lay ahead. The wave is therefore not “science centralized.” It is knowledge missionized through distinct legal persons, records, access rules and maturity clocks.

The shared ladder is:

public problem → mission designation → responsible institution → data / model / evidence carrier → funding or rulemaking gate → experiment / case / procurement → validated result → remedy or revision

4C.1 — September 30: pediatric-cancer AI enters an existing data body

EO 14355 is deliberately narrow. It does not authorize a general Federal medical-AI system. It directs the MAHA Commission, HHS, OSTP and the Special Advisor for AI and Crypto to accelerate AI-driven work through the existing Childhood Cancer Data Initiative and NCI-designated cancer centers.

The named work includes:

  • consolidating multimodal data into AI-ready infrastructure;
  • improving trial-participant selection;
  • analyzing multi-omics, imaging, response, progression and toxicity;
  • improving clinical-trial design, access, recruitment and interpretation;
  • increasing support from existing Federal funds and private engagement; and
  • advancing interoperability standards while preserving patient and parent control of health information.

The institutional body predates the order. CCDI already had a cloud-based ecosystem, clinical and registry data platforms, a participant index, federation resources and a data catalog. The order changes priority and scale; it does not create those objects from nothing.

Funding direction becomes visible, but the award clock remains separate

On the signing date, HHS and NIH announced that CCDI funding would rise from $50 million to $100 million. The announcement tied the increase to data-centered research and private AI collaboration. The order itself directs increased investment from existing Federal funds and remains subject to appropriations. The release is therefore an authoritative program-allocation announcement, not by itself an appropriation, award roster, obligation or outlay ledger.

On April 15, 2026, NIH posted pediatric and AYA cancer as a highlighted research topic, expressly seeking AI/ML methods for data integration, small-sample problems, privacy-preserving sharing and AI-ready tools. NIH says applicants should use an appropriate parent announcement or other broad opportunity; the topic is not itself a NOFO.

Maturity. Existing data ecosystem plus priority and budget-direction change, followed by a live investigator-interest signal.

Counterevidence and limit. No cutoff-safe record in this packet identifies an EO-specific award, model-validation result, clinical-trial enrollment change or patient outcome. Data availability is not consent, interoperability, scientific validity or cure.

4C.2 — November 24: Genesis receives the wave's strongest mission carrier

EO 14363 establishes the Genesis Mission and puts implementation inside DOE. The Secretary is responsible for priorities and integration; the APST supplies general leadership through the National Science and Technology Council.

Its intended American Science and Security Platform joins:

  • national-laboratory and cloud high-performance computing;
  • AI agents, models, simulation and optimization tools;
  • Federal, proprietary, open and synthetic data;
  • classification, privacy, intellectual-property and export-control gates;
  • autonomous and AI-augmented laboratories and manufacturing; and
  • standardized partnership, data-use, model-sharing, ownership and licensing terms.

That makes the platform a federated research permission system, not a single unrestricted database. Every user, dataset, model, facility and result keeps its own authority and access condition.

January–June 2026: mission architecture becomes administratively real

By the evidence cutoff, DOE had created several descendant objects:

  • a January 16 RFI seeking university, industry, philanthropic and workforce input;
  • a February 9 Genesis Mission Consortium designed to promote solicitations, execute agreements and track project success;
  • 26 national science and technology challenges announced February 12;
  • a March 17 $293 million Request for Applications, inviting interdisciplinary teams to compete across more than 20 challenges;
  • five visible national-laboratory workstreams for data, infrastructure, models, challenges and public-private partnerships; and
  • an April 3 public description of the platform and collaboration structure.

These are not merely a name and logo. They are mission intake, team structure, project-selection criteria and a funding competition. At the cutoff, however, the application round had not yet become the July 22 project-selection portfolio. The later selections must not be pulled backward into this wave.

June 4: the platform acquires an international partnership plan

DOE and Japan announced a planned five-year, $1 billion Genesis partnership, described as $500 million from each nation and subject to future appropriations. Eleven joint teams were to connect twelve DOE national laboratories, one Office of Science user facility and twelve Japanese research institutions across quantum science, fusion, biotechnology, materials, particle physics and autonomous laboratories.

That is a real intergovernmental program plan with named institutions and team architecture. It is not $1 billion already appropriated, obligated or paid, and it does not prove that the American Science and Security Platform was fully integrated.

Maturity. Legally established mission; operating program office and collaboration architecture; project and partner intake; funding solicitation; named international team plan.

Counterevidence and limit. The 240-day robotic-facility review, 270-day initial-operating-capability attempt and first annual report were not due by June 20. No cutoff-safe record establishes a completed platform-wide job joining multiple labs, datasets, models and instruments. “Manhattan Project” is ambition, not maturity.

4C.3 — December 11: AI policy becomes a Federal challenge machine, not national law

EO 14365 addresses jurisdiction rather than scientific operation. It directs:

  • an AI Litigation Task Force to challenge State laws;
  • a Commerce evaluation of State AI laws;
  • potential BEAD and discretionary-grant conditions;
  • possible FCC reporting/disclosure and FTC deception-policy proceedings; and
  • White House legislative recommendations for a national framework.

The order expressly leaves several areas outside its proposed-preemption command, including lawful State child-safety law, State procurement/use of AI and most AI-compute and data-center infrastructure rules.

The carrier forms and acts

The Attorney General's January 9, 2026 memorandum established the AI Litigation Task Force, naming the Attorney General or designee as chair and the Associate Attorney General as vice chair. In March, the White House published legislative recommendations for a national AI framework.

On April 24, DOJ moved to intervene in xAI's Colorado litigation, challenging SB 24-205. That is the first clean operating receipt in this packet: a named Federal legal person, case, pleading and requested remedy.

It is still an allegation and litigation position. No court judgment had invalidated Colorado's law at the cutoff. The legislative recommendation was not enacted. The FTC's proposed policy statement arrived after the cutoff on July 1 and is quarantined from the maturity finding.

Maturity. Task force established, policy recommendation published and one concrete intervention filed.

Counterevidence and limit. An executive order cannot declare every State AI law void. Congress, agency jurisdiction and Article III courts retain separate decision rights. One intervention is not a national rulebook.

4C.4 — December 18: space superiority becomes guidance, solicitations and agency form

EO 14369 combines four mission families:

  1. Moon return, permanent lunar presence and Mars preparation;
  2. missile defense, cislunar threat detection and allied space security;
  3. commercial investment, launch cadence, spectrum and an ISS successor; and
  4. nuclear power, weather services, traffic management and physical standards.

It sets ambitious output targets—Americans on the Moon by 2028, initial permanent-outpost elements by 2030, a lunar reactor ready for launch by 2030 and at least $50 billion of additional space-market investment by 2028. Those are future targets, not findings.

The order also changes the institutional room. It revokes the prior National Space Council order, makes the APST the overall coordinator, directs reviews and reforms, prefers commercial solutions and flexible acquisition vehicles, and changes space-traffic wording from “free of direct user fees” to availability for commercial and other relevant use.

April–May 2026: plans acquire administrative body

The White House issued NSTM-3 on April 14, 2026, supplying guidance for the National Initiative for American Space Nuclear Power. Among other features, it calls for a reactor producing at least 100 kWe, milestone-based acquisition, government-use and march-in rights, commonality across designs and a DOE industrial-base assessment.

NASA's March 24 Ignition event produced a phased Moon Base plan and multiple RFIs or draft RFPs covering lunar payloads, commercial lunar transportation, CLPS 2.0, low-Earth-orbit destinations and transportation, and other mission infrastructure. The public CLPS 2.0 draft contemplated a long ordering/execution horizon and a ceiling up to $6 billion. Solicitation is not award, flight or delivered infrastructure.

On May 22, NASA announced an agencywide realignment, including a unified human-spaceflight directorate and a research/technology/aeronautics organization carrying nuclear-power and propulsion work. That is a real organizational state change.

Maturity. Operative policy, nuclear guidance, acquisition intake and internal agency reorganization.

Counterevidence and limit. No lunar base, lunar-surface reactor, completed commercial-space-station replacement, responsive security architecture or $50 billion investment receipt existed by the cutoff. A target and RFI are not hardware.

4C.5 — December 18: marijuana and CBD move into an evidence hearing

EO 14370 addresses a different knowledge dispute: the gap between widespread State-sanctioned use, Federal Schedule I status and incomplete clinical evidence.

It directs the Attorney General to complete the marijuana Schedule III rulemaking under 21 U.S.C. § 811; directs White House work with Congress on the statutory hemp/CBD definition; and assigns HHS, FDA, CMS and NIH to develop real-world-evidence methods and models capable of informing access and standards of care.

At the cutoff, marijuana remained in Schedule I. DEA had scheduled formal hearing proceedings for June 29, after the cutoff. No final rescheduling rule, new FDA approval, congressional CBD amendment, HHS real-world-evidence framework or EO-specific research award was located in this packet.

Even a future move to Schedule III would not legalize every State-market product or make it FDA-approved. Scheduling, research registration, drug approval, prescribing, dispensing, tax treatment and State law remain different clocks.

Maturity. Presidential direction to complete an already pending formal rulemaking and build a research/evidence pathway; adjudicative and program outcomes unresolved.

Counterevidence and limit. An asserted medical use is not proof of safety or efficacy for every product, dose, route or population. Continued Schedule I status is not proof that the research command had no effect; it means the decisive legal gate had not yet closed.

The mission-state theorem

The institutional change in this packet is not that one office “owns science.” It is that political priority increasingly arrives as a mission, and the mission then recruits an operating assemblage:

executive priority → department owner → cross-agency council → platform / docket / solicitation → private and academic participants → access and evidence rules → measured output

That can weaken older institutional boundaries without abolishing the institutions themselves:

  • NCI remains the cancer-science holder, but its data ecosystem is redirected toward AI-ready mission work;
  • DOE national laboratories remain separate legal and scientific bodies, but Genesis tries to make their capacity callable through one mission platform;
  • States retain lawmaking power, but the Federal executive builds a litigation and funding strategy against selected AI rules;
  • NASA remains NASA, while its internal structure and acquisition posture are reorganized around national-policy outputs; and
  • DEA retains the formal scheduling hearing even after the President directs the desired pace and destination.

The regime is not replacing every institution. It is teaching surviving institutions to serve a selected mission through shared interfaces.

  • scarce pediatric-cancer data can be connected and made more useful without waiting for one institution to own every sample;

Shadow pole — mission selection becomes jurisdiction over truth

  • political urgency can privilege favored research questions, firms and models;

  • a desired scheduling outcome can outrun the independent evidentiary and adjudicative process.

Both poles remain live. Publications, access logs, awards, cases, experiments, peer review, clinical results, launches and appeals—not the astrological rhyme—show which matured.
