2026 Long-Clock seed-degree corridors

The ten canonical conjunction families were already present in the Long Clocks engine, and individual seed-degree relays had already appeared in the 2026 readings. What had not been done was one governed census across all twenty-nine registered 2026 ingress and lunation charts.

That census changes the emphasis. The interesting pattern is not that many planets make some major aspect to some old degree. With ten seeds, fourteen raw chart points and five admitted major aspects, a broad one-degree search almost guarantees contacts. The distinctive pattern is that the old conjunction coordinates repeatedly occupy the lights, horizon and meridian—the places where a chart begins, culminates or becomes visible.

This companion does not replace any locked reading, conjunction dossier or Long Clock phase chapter. A seed hinge is continuity, not a new governor.

Ten histories make nine coordinate corridors

The ten canonical display anchors do not make ten independent places. The January 2020 Saturn–Pluto conjunction at 22°46 Capricorn and the November 2020 Jupiter–Pluto conjunction at 22°52 Capricorn are only 0.087792° apart. They remain two different relationship histories, but they share one degree corridor for contact counting.

The audit therefore carries:

  • ten separately named conjunction families;
  • nine deduplicated degree corridors;
  • twenty-nine registered 2026 chart frames;
  • the five standing major aspects; and
  • an exact-strength band of 0.25° or less, with one degree retained only as strict context.

The Sun–Moon, ASC–DSC and MC–IC pairs are each counted as one axis. Both endpoints remain visible in the data, but an opposition to the MC and a conjunction to the IC do not become two votes.

The result is a hinge pattern, not an everything pattern

Only three receipts are the obvious case of one of the conjunction's own bodies returning to its seed geometry. Most body contacts are carried by a third body, and almost half of the entire exact-strength set lands on chart hinges.

The wide one-degree lane is useful mainly as a warning: all twenty-nine charts and all nine corridors receive at least one contact somewhere. That lane is too populated to prove importance by itself. At exact strength, six charts remain clean controls and the shared Saturn–Pluto/Jupiter–Pluto Capricorn corridor is the only corridor with no receipt.

The Barbault Basket has two of its own seeds on the frame

The July 14 New Moon is the only registered 2026 chart with two exact-strength hinge corridors belonging to two different Basket relationships. The Basket is therefore not only a complete graph of current aspects. Its chart frame also selects two of the six historical conjunction places: Uranus–Neptune on the horizon and Jupiter–Uranus in the lights.

Katie's live thesis—that the Barbault Basket may ultimately tell the story of AI and superintelligence—remains human-authored and genuinely interesting. It does not prove that the configuration means AI, does not establish astrological causation, and does not let a coincident technology event ratify the reading by itself. The configuration, its astronomical motion, and the factual AI record must remain separate at the source and meet only in an explicit comparison layer.

What this changes in the normal reading sequence

Seed-degree contacts deserve a small factual lane after authority, lunation or ingress ownership has already been established:

  1. name the canonical conjunction family and exact display anchor;
  2. state the live chart point, major aspect and orb;
  3. identify whether it is a body crossing or a light/angle hinge;
  4. collapse co-located seed corridors and opposite axis endpoints;
  5. collapse repeated structural families such as the four cardinal ingress Suns; and
  6. ask whether the contact materially deepens Judgment without creating a new vote.

The result is a continuity instrument. It tells us which old relationship history is standing at the door of a newer chart. It does not tell us, by itself, what event will walk through.
