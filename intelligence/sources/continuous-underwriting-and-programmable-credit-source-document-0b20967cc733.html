Continuous Underwriting and Programmable Credit — Source Document

Controlling finding

Banking is moving toward continuous underwriting, but the strongest current model is not a public-wallet credit score. It is data-linked credit with cash or collateral control.

Production merchant lenders already use high-frequency platform activity to identify borrowers, size offers, monitor eligibility and collect repayment from the same payment stream they observe. Distributed-ledger technology is further advanced in secured wholesale funding, tokenized collateral, electronic loan records and digital-asset margin arrangements. The public primary record does not yet show a regulated U.S. bank replacing ordinary commercial underwriting or enterprise-wide financial reporting with a borrower’s stablecoin history or DLT ledger.

The likely transition is:

periodic borrower package
  -> governed operating, cash and collateral feeds
  -> continuously updated accounting and covenant views
  -> AI exception analysis
  -> bounded automated draw, sweep, hold or margin response
  -> named human and legal authority for exceptions, correction and enforcement

This changes the frequency and control surface of credit. It does not eliminate the borrower, the contract, accounting policy, collateral law or the institution that bears loss.

Evidence-state ruling

Layer Current public state What is established What is not established

Tokenized secured finance Production operator evidence J.P. Morgan Kinexys uses blockchain deposits and tokenized collateral in secured wholesale funding A new operating-company cash-flow credit standard

AI plus blockchain warehouse credit Executed facility with forward-looking operating claims A named warehouse facility and AI analytics provider exist Recurring 24/7 blockchain settlement, transaction history or loss performance

The distinction among production, executed, pilot, announced, proposed and legally permitted must remain explicit.

The clocks that must not be collapsed

Clock Governing question Typical authority or evidence
Borrower clock Which legal entity owes the obligation, and which accounts, wallets and affiliates are inside the perimeter? organizational documents, KYC/KYB, beneficial ownership, account and wallet-control records
Operating-evidence clock What business event occurred, when, and under whose control? processor, bank, ERP, device or registry record; source identity; signatures; change history
Accounting clock When and how is the event recognized, valued, accrued, consolidated or impaired? GAAP policy, subledgers, estimates, reconciliations and period close
Credit-decision clock When did the lender approve an exposure, limit, advance rate, covenant or exception? credit memorandum, policy engine and authorized approval
Funding clock When did a commitment become an available and then an actual borrower advance? executed facility, conditions precedent, borrowing request and lender advance
Collateral clock When did ownership, control, perfection, priority and eligibility become effective? governing law, security agreement, control agreement, filing, custody and borrowing-base rules
Settlement clock When did the cash or token transfer become technically final and usable by the recipient? bank account, token network, custodian, redemption and payment-system rules
Legal-discharge clock When was the obligation legally paid, reduced, corrected, accelerated or enforced? contract, governing law, agent determination, court or regulator

A single ledger entry can be relevant to several clocks without controlling all of them.

What production platform lending proves

Square

Block’s filed annual report says Square Financial Services uses seller transaction history for underwriting and collections, generally collects a fixed percentage of card transactions, and sizes loans relative to expected Square gross payment volume. The same platform can identify the seller, observe much of the relevant revenue stream and control repayment from processed receipts.

Shopify

Shopify’s current program terms say eligibility is continually evaluated using sales, disputes, returns, customer interactions, tenure, failed debits and other platform information. For new U.S. loans accepted from March 9, 2026, collection is attempted first from the Shopify Payments balance. Shopify’s 2025 Form 10-K reports $4.2 billion of merchant loans and merchant cash advances purchased during the year.

Stripe

Stripe says eligibility is updated daily using payment-processing history and related information, while repayment is withheld as a percentage of sales. U.S. loans are issued through a bank partner.

These are private-ledger prototypes of continuous underwriting. Their strongest feature is not simply fresh data; it is the joined structure:

verified merchant + operating ledger + payment stream + collection control

They do not prove that the platform sees every bank account, liability, related party, contingent obligation or off-platform sale.

What DLT has changed already

Secured funding and collateral operations

J.P. Morgan describes Kinexys Digital Financing as a production secured-funding system using tokenized collateral entitlements and blockchain-based deposits. The operational gain is faster collateral movement, delivery-versus-payment coordination and programmed maturity. Public evidence does not show that wallet history replaced ordinary counterparty or credit approval.

Loan records and distribution

Figure’s February 2026 SEC filing says 91% of loans originated through its loan-origination system in the third quarter of 2025 used DART, its Provenance-based eNote and lien registry. This is meaningful production use of DLT in the loan record and transfer layer. It is not evidence that the ledger replaced income verification, asset valuation, lien validity or servicing controls.

Executed digital-currency lending

The filed February 2026 Galaxy–Forward Industries master digital-currency loan agreement includes blockchain confirmation, custody, collateral, margin calls, liquidation, a first-priority security interest, market-disruption provisions and governing-law remedies. It also references audited statements in a party-equity definition when available. The transaction demonstrates that onchain delivery can coexist with conventional financial evidence and legal controls.

Early AI and blockchain warehouses

Forum Markets disclosed a $10 million revolving auto-loan warehouse facility and named AI analytics and blockchain-settlement providers. The executed facility is stronger evidence than a concept announcement. The filed release does not publish the complete facility agreement, transaction hashes, recurring settlement history, underwriting model or loss record; the 24/7 settlement and future tokenization claims remain forward-looking.

GENIUS Act — exact boundary

Public Law 119-27 was approved on July 18, 2025. Section 20 makes the Act effective on the earlier of:

  • January 18, 2027, which is 18 months after enactment; or
  • 120 days after the primary federal payment-stablecoin regulators issue any final implementing regulations.

As of this note’s cutoff, principal federal implementation measures remained in proposal or rulemaking stages. The Act:

  • establishes a framework for permitted payment-stablecoin issuers, reserves, redemption, custody, compliance and supervision;
  • distinguishes a payment stablecoin from a deposit, including a deposit recorded using DLT;
  • preserves banking institutions’ authority, when otherwise lawful, to issue digital representations of deposits, use DLT for books and records and intrabank transfers, and provide specified custody services; and
  • requires issuer-level reserve disclosure and accountant examination or certification processes.

It does not:

  • establish a new commercial-loan underwriting test;
  • make stablecoin transaction history complete borrower accounting;
  • prove beneficial ownership or business purpose for every wallet transfer;
  • perfect a lender’s security interest in the borrower’s assets; or
  • eliminate GAAP, lender policy, bank supervision, KYC/AML, sanctions or contract remedies.

Stablecoin regulation makes the payment claim and its issuer more legible. It does not certify the creditworthiness of the holder.

Stablecoin funding creates three distinct credit regimes

  1. Disbursement or repayment rail. The lender underwrites substantially as before, then sends or receives stablecoin. Settlement hours and treasury operations change more than credit criteria.
  2. Controlled collateral. A token or stablecoin backs the loan. Custody, legal control, valuation, liquidation venue, margin, concentration and chain-disruption rules become central.
  3. Operating evidence. Stablecoin receipts and payments become part of a live borrower record. This is the more transformative case, but only if addresses are legally attributable, material inflows and obligations are inside the perimeter, transactions are economically classified, privacy rights permit use, and the lender controls or can reach repayment.

No located primary evidence shows a material U.S. bank commercial loan underwritten solely from stablecoin operating flows.

GAAP and assurance boundary

DLT can be strong at freshness and tamper evidence. A lender still needs completeness, attribution, economic classification and assurance.

Evidence object Strongest proposition Missing proposition if used alone
Public-chain transfer an address transferred a token at a network-confirmed time legal party, business purpose, accounting treatment, enterprise completeness and legal discharge
Processor or bank feed attributed activity in connected accounts unconnected accounts, accruals, side agreements, future obligations and asset existence
ERP or asset subledger events processed under configured rules reality of source events, correct rules, complete consolidation and valid overrides
Compilation organized financial presentation without assurance independent accuracy or conformity assurance
Review limited assurance, principally through inquiry and analytics audit-level testing and comprehensive control assurance
Audit reasonable assurance on period-end statements under an identified framework continuous correctness after the report date or certainty about forecasts
Continuous-control attestation defined controls operated and exceptions were tested over a period judgments and obligations outside the attested perimeter

For larger C&I, venture and project loans, the likely near-term form is a two-speed evidence package:

  • continuous operating, cash and collateral data for monitoring and triggers; and
  • periodic GAAP statements and independent assurance for enterprise-wide completeness, consolidation, valuation, estimates, obligations and governance.

Assurance is likely to migrate toward data lineage, system perimeter, reconciliations, eligibility rules, model governance, key custody, exceptions and correction controls. Annual audits, legal opinions, appraisals, field examinations, management forecasts and physical verification remain important where exposure and risk justify them.

Minimum constitution for programmable credit

A live system can reduce dependence on conventional borrower reporting only to the extent that it is:

  1. attributable to the exact borrower, assets, accounts and authorized agents;
  2. complete across material accounts, entities and obligations;
  3. controlled at source ingestion, rule execution, access and override;
  4. reconciled to independent bank, custody, registry or counterparty records;
  5. economically classified under the accounting and covenant rules the lender actually uses;
  6. legally effective for payment, collateral, priority, default and enforcement;
  7. independently tested at a frequency proportionate to risk;
  8. governed for AI/model validation, drift, exceptions and human override; and
  9. correctable through a visible restatement, hold, cure, waiver, reversal or compensating-entry process.

If a condition is absent, live evidence should supplement—not silently replace—the conventional evidence package.

Federated-network structure

The durable architecture is unlikely to place every function on one public chain:

Function Authoritative object Likely control point
Business event sale, shipment, meter, work or service state processor, ERP, device operator, registry or accredited signer
Legal identity and perimeter borrower, affiliate, account, wallet and agent authority corporate records, bank, custodian and identity provider
Title and collateral ownership, control, lien, eligibility and priority governing law, registry, custodian, secured party and agent
Money and settlement deposit, payment stablecoin, redemption and usable proceeds bank, issuer, payment system and account agreement
Credit commitment, limit, advance rate, covenant and exception lender or licensed balance-sheet provider
Accounting and assurance recognition, valuation, completeness and correction borrower books, accountant, auditor and applicable standards
Default and adjudication hold, cure, acceleration, recovery and final discharge contract parties, trustee or agent, regulator and court

DLT can make state portable and tamper-evident. Contracts and law make that state operative. A capital provider still decides whether to bear the risk.

Return gates

Reopen the current ruling when primary evidence establishes one of the following:

  1. a regulated U.S. bank completes an operating-company loan that expressly accepts a governed DLT enterprise ledger in place of conventional statements;
  2. a filed agreement or bank policy reduces compilation, review or audit requirements because continuous ledger/control assurance is the accepted substitute;
  3. a named business loan publishes the stablecoin funding, borrower receipt, repayment, collateral, legal-finality and performance chain;
  4. a production facility combines bank, processor, ERP, tax and collateral data across providers rather than inside one platform’s walled garden;
  5. a standard setter, banking agency or court recognizes a defined continuous-control evidence package for financial-statement or covenant purposes; or
  6. comparable outcomes show improved approval, pricing, loss, default and bias performance from live-data underwriting.
