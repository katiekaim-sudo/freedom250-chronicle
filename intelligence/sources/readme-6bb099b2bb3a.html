Why this exists

The Workbench already held strong China-specific research on the September state visit, IMF surveillance, trade, permanent magnets, digital money and selected security questions. It did not have a country-level orientation explaining how those pieces join.

This package supplies that missing first map. It is not a complete China encyclopedia and does not flatten Chinese, U.S. or multilateral positions into one supposedly neutral account.

Bottom line

There is no single “China relationship.” The United States and China operate through several overlapping relationships:

  • head-of-state political steering;
  • diplomatic and security management;
  • trade negotiation and tariff administration;
  • technology and export-control permissions;
  • monetary, exchange-rate and financial surveillance;
  • military deterrence and crisis communication;
  • the Taiwan legal-security interface;
  • climate, health, counternarcotics, education and people-to-people cooperation;
  • competition over institutions, standards, infrastructure and global governance.

Each lane has different actors, instruments and evidence of success. A leaders' consensus can guide the whole relationship without changing a tariff line, export license, military posture or payment route. Conversely, a narrow administrative action can materially change trade while leaving the political relationship formally unchanged.

Governing boundary

  • Chinese official sources establish China's formal institutions, stated policies and published acts; they do not independently verify every implementation or outcome claim.
  • U.S. official sources establish U.S. law, policy, actions and attributed assessments; they do not become neutral descriptions of Chinese intent merely because they are official.
  • Multilateral reports add common definitions, comparisons and surveillance findings; their staff, executive-board, member-response and implementation layers must remain separate.
  • Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan are never silently merged into a mainland-China statistic, customs territory, legal rule or operating system.
