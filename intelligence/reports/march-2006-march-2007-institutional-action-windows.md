# March 2006 and March 2007 Institutional Action Windows

## Answer first

The fixed March 2006 and March 2007 windows do contain a coherent institutional
pattern, but not one continuous political object.

- **March 26–April 1, 2006** concentrated efforts to convert broad political
  purpose into machinery: a Senate immigration vehicle moved through committee;
  the Senate passed lobbying and legislative-transparency rules; the United
  States, Mexico and Canada named border, identity, cargo, energy and critical-
  infrastructure workstreams; and the UN Security Council put Iran's nuclear
  file onto a timed compliance track.
- **March 16–22, 2007** concentrated a different stage of the same institutional
  problem: who controls executive personnel, records and compelled testimony.
  The Senate acted to restore a time limit on interim U.S. Attorney appointments,
  the White House offered interviews under restricted conditions, and a House
  Judiciary subcommittee authorized subpoenas.

The strongest cross-window recurrence is therefore not “the same event.” It is
the struggle to turn political will into an enforceable instruction while
another institution demands a record, a procedure or a limit on the decision
right. Continuity to any 2025–2026 matter must still be proved object by object.

Anniversaries, commentary and dramatic but non-operative statements were not
enough. The windows were not enlarged to capture later outcomes.

## Window one — March 26–April 1, 2006

### Immigration and border authority moved through committee

The Senate Judiciary Committee completed final markup of a comprehensive
immigration measure on March 27. The later committee report records hundreds of
circulated amendments, more than sixty votes, fifty-four accepted amendments
and a 12–6 vote to report the bill. On March 30 the committee text became a
substitute vehicle for S. 2454. The related S. 2611 was not introduced until
April 7 and later passed the Senate, but no House–Senate agreement was reached.

**Lifecycle state inside the window:** committee report / substitute vehicle,
not enactment and not an operating removal regime.

### The Senate passed a transparency and lobbying measure

On March 29 the Senate passed S. 2349, the Legislative Transparency and
Accountability Act, 90–8. That was chamber passage, not enactment. A later,
different vehicle became the Honest Leadership and Open Government Act of 2007.

**Lifecycle state inside the window:** one-chamber passage; later legal reform
must not be backdated into March 2006.

### North American cooperation acquired named operating workstreams

The March 30 U.S.–Mexico meeting and March 31 Security and Prosperity
Partnership statement joined immigration, border security and shared
responsibility to more specific work: a North American Competitiveness Council;
pandemic coordination; energy research, infrastructure and regulatory
compatibility; critical-infrastructure and emergency-response planning; risk
screening and information sharing at the border; biometrics and secure
documents; electronic cargo information; and law-enforcement liaisons.

The statement also described existing or pilot activity, including
fingerprint-sharing work and voluntary repatriations. Those are operating
receipts for particular programs, not proof that every announced workstream was
already implemented. A 2007 follow-up later identified completed products such
as a pandemic plan, regulatory-cooperation framework, intellectual-property
strategy and an energy science-and-technology agreement.

**Lifecycle state inside the window:** political agreement plus named work
programs, with implementation varying by object.

### Iran moved onto a timed compliance track

On March 29 the UN Security Council issued Presidential Statement
S/PRST/2006/15, calling on Iran to comply with IAEA requirements and requesting
an IAEA report in thirty days. The statement was not yet an Article 41 sanctions
resolution. The same institutional file later moved to Resolution 1696, which
demanded suspension and set an August 31 deadline, and then to Resolution 1737,
which imposed sanctions, supply and technology controls, service restrictions
and asset freezes.

**Lifecycle state inside the window:** formal call plus reporting clock. The
later binding demand and sanctions are subsequent maturations of the same UN
nuclear file, not accomplishments of the March statement itself.

## Window two — March 16–22, 2007

### Congress moved to limit interim U.S. Attorney appointment power

The Senate began floor consideration of S. 214, the Preserving United States
Attorney Independence Act, on March 19 amid oversight of U.S. Attorney removals.
It passed the bill 94–2 on March 20. The measure restored a 120-day limit for an
Attorney General-appointed interim U.S. Attorney and returned appointment
authority to the district court after that period. It became law on June 14.

**Lifecycle state inside the window:** Senate passage. Enactment followed later.

### The White House offered access while limiting the form of testimony

On March 20 the White House Counsel said the Department of Justice had produced
more than 3,000 pages and offered private interviews with current and former
White House officials. The offer excluded an oath, a transcript and subsequent
subpoena. President Bush separately defended the executive's discretion to
replace political appointees while opposing subpoenas.

**Lifecycle state inside the window:** document production and a conditional
interview offer; not compelled testimony and not a negotiated settlement.

### A House subcommittee authorized subpoenas

On March 21 the House Judiciary Subcommittee on Commercial and Administrative
Law authorized its chair to issue subpoenas for Karl Rove, Harriet Miers,
William Kelley, Scott Jennings and related documents. Authorization did not
equal issuance. The subpoenas were issued later, and the executive-privilege
confrontation matured after the fixed window.

**Lifecycle state inside the window:** subpoena authority created; service,
compliance, privilege assertion and enforcement remained later clocks.

## The comparison that survives source separation

| Institutional problem | 2006 expression | 2007 expression |
|---|---|---|
| Converting policy into action | Committee vehicle, trilateral workstreams, UN compliance clock | Appointment legislation, document production, subpoena authorization |
| Decision right under dispute | Border/removal design, cross-border coordination, nuclear compliance | Removal of prosecutors, interim appointments, access to advisers and records |
| Record or procedure that binds power | Amendments, chamber vote, named implementation tracks, IAEA report | Statutory time limit, document production conditions, subpoena process |
| Maturity at window close | Mostly initiation and specification | Active interbranch contest, still short of final enforcement |

This comparison supports a bounded historical observation: both windows placed
executive or international action beside a mechanism that tried to specify,
document, time-limit or review it. It does **not** establish astrological
causation, recurrence of one political event, or continuity to a later case.

## Nulls, limits and return gate

- No single object was found that runs continuously through every material item
  in both windows.
- No factual claim is made here about a 2025 or 2026 continuation.
- The review did not attempt a complete census of every federal or international
  action during either week.
- A later comparison should return only for the same statute, case, agency
  guidance, international file, named infrastructure program, decision right or
  other traceable object—not merely similar rhetoric.
