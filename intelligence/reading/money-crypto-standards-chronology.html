Analytic and evidence boundary

Economic Institution Legitimacy Crisis is the overarching analytic lens. It is not a phrase that institutions, governments, firms, or source documents must use before the pattern can be studied. The chronology therefore records observable proxies as well as explicit authority disputes:

  • bypass, workaround, refusal, exit, or substitution;
  • incompatible claims about who may decide or which record controls;
  • reliance moving toward a new operator, standard, ledger, or route;
  • repeated non-delivery or implementation delay;
  • selective treatment or uneven admission;
  • opacity in decision, record, correction, or loss allocation;
  • uncorrected failure or ineffective remedy;
  • loss of institutional monopoly or cross-border recognition;
  • burdens shifted without matching voice, authority, or remedy; and
  • successful institutional absorption that weakens a crisis reading.

No one proxy proves a legitimacy crisis. Each entry keeps the observed object, the relevant proxy, the clocks that changed, the clocks that did not change, and the claim limit separate. Rival explanations include ordinary modernization, prudent phased implementation, designed constitutional tension, capacity reform, risk containment, competitive pluralism, and successful incumbent adaptation.

The chronology does not assign a legitimacy-crisis state to any whole institution. Direct legitimacy relevance below means direct evidence of an authority, capacity, accountability, recognition, or implementation conflict that bears on legitimacy. Mechanism/context means an enabling technological, legal, operational, or institutional change whose legitimacy meaning depends on later behavior, performance, acceptance, correction, or stress.

Clock key

Code Clock Question
TC technical capability What can the system demonstrably do?
AD adoption or operation Who is actually using or relying on it, and at what scope?
LA legal authority Who may lawfully decide, admit, compel, pay, deny, record, or settle?
SS standards and supervision Which rulebook applies and who supervises the operator and exceptions?
AR authoritative record Which record controls when technical, legal, accounting, or institutional states diverge?
FR failure, correction, and remedy Who can pause, reverse, correct, compensate, adjudicate, or absorb loss?
AL accepted legitimacy Who accepts or rejects the result, and what behavior demonstrates that?

Important boundary: none of the retained events directly measures accepted public legitimacy. AL therefore remains unchanged or unmeasured throughout. The timeline supplies observable authority contests, standards-to-delivery gaps, reliance shifts, accountability redesigns, and technology-to-structure lags. It does not substitute those proxies for direct acceptance evidence.

Accepted-legitimacy evidence

No event in this chronology directly changes or measures AL. The corpus currently supplies:

  • authority contests and court-routed remedies;
  • standards-versus-implementation gaps;
  • delivery and performance findings;
  • selective admission and bounded access;
  • reliance on new private or technical operators;
  • accountability redesigns; and
  • technology-to-structure lags.

A later legitimacy-evidence lane should seek trust series, participation and exit behavior, institutional refusal or compliance, adoption under available alternatives, complaints and appeal use, market reliance under stress, cross-border recognition practice, burden incidence, correction time, compensation, and first-failure experience.

Full-stack closure

No reviewed system closes the entire chain at systemic scale:

technical event → lawful authority → authoritative title or money record → final cash → accounting close → failure → correction → loss allocation → accepted result.

That absence is a research gap and a governance question. It is not by itself proof of institutional illegitimacy, technological failure, or inevitable replacement.

Wave 4 handoff fields

For each retained event, Wave 4 should test:

  1. which legitimacy dimension is actually implicated—mandate, representation, technocratic competence, procedure, distribution, output, or recognition;
  2. whether the proxy repeats across time or remains a one-off;
  3. which affected party changes behavior;
  4. whether ordinary authorization, supervision, adjudication, or correction resolves the issue;
  5. whether a private or international substitute gains practical recognition;
  6. who bears the burden, cost, delay, or loss;
  7. which rival explanation fits the facts best; and
  8. what dated return would strengthen, weaken, or falsify the legitimacy-crisis reading.
