NFDC Charter Deep Dive — The Missing Operating Constitution

Current saved study

The Charter appears to be the people, membership and operating-governance constitution for a prosecutor-led, multi-agency center; it is not publicly shown to be the legal authority for every dataset, feed, analytic system, investigation or payment action. Six OIGs (USDA, DOL, HUD, VA, SBA, SSA) publicly call themselves signatories, but the signed text was not publicly retrievable through the cutoff. The Charter is operationally consequential but not the whole machine: data agreements, State MOUs, technical annexes, Privacy Act/SORN, system authorization, records schedules and case-specific rules still control what moves.

Federal and Florida Fraud Agreements — AI Oversight Interlock

Current saved study

'The agreements are the data constitution for an AI-ready fraud network, not proof that an AI system is already making fraud, benefit or payment decisions.' Ohio's MOU authorizes recurring public corporate data, defers schemas to an annex, moves no funds and requires DOJ validation; Florida has two distinct announced instruments (Sunbiz corporate route; State Treasury payment-transaction route) atop a pre-existing State enforcement edge; Charter and data-sharing agreements are separate instrument families; PRAC/PACE is the strongest proved AI bridge, but no NFDC AI system is identified.

What federal MOUs do, and where their power stops

Current saved study

'An MOU records how offices will use powers they already have together: who leads, supplies staff or data, performs work, pays, keeps records, and resolves disputes.' The title does not decide legal effect; read authority, operative clauses, signers, funding instrument and implementation record. Five jobs: coordinate, supply services/staff, share data, set procedures, move/reimburse money. No override of the Privacy Act or other disclosure limits.

Justice Department opinion widens which state offices must report people unlawfully present

Current saved study

A September 1, 2026 Justice Department legal opinion reads a 1996 welfare-law reporting duty to cover all of a participating state's agencies, not just the welfare office. It also drops the old rule that a final removal order was needed before the state 'knew'. No new procedure or actual report has followed yet.

The One Big Beautiful Bill makes states pay for SNAP errors; it did not create a fraud rate

Current saved study

The 2025 law made states pay part of SNAP benefit costs based on their error rates and tightened a Medicaid error rule. It did not set a government-wide fraud rate, a single federal profile or any blockchain mandate.
