## The distinction governing this pass

Signs and Houses are cousins, not synonyms.

```text
Sign = how movement is configured and what equipment that configuration supplies
House = where and in what national arena the chart acts
```

Modality supplies movement. Element shapes that movement. The unique Sign is an
independently governed collective expression of both. A conventional Sign,
Planet, or House analogy creates no semantic assignment among them.

## Structural foundations for the Signs

### Modality — how movement develops

- **Cardinal:** opens a phase, mobilizes available material, and establishes
  direction across a threshold.
- **Fixed:** consolidates what has formed and sustains its continuity,
  concentration, and development.
- **Mutable:** modifies, redistributes, or releases an established phase toward
  transition.

### Element — what shapes and carries the movement

- **Fire:** ignition and animation.
- **Earth:** practical and material reality.
- **Air:** thought and exchange.
- **Water:** emotion and feeling.

These seven inputs are intentionally spare. They should help us understand a
Sign without importing civic topics, chart arenas, or a Planet's motivation.

## Separate structures retained

### Exact angles

ASC, DSC, MC, and IC remain their own owner family. The MC is an exact sensitive
point that may fall in the 9th, 10th, or 11th Whole Sign House. Angle semantics
will receive a separate review rather than entering any House candidate.

### Derivative Houses

The exact turn is:

```text
landing = ((base_house − 1) + (relative_house − 1)) mod 12 + 1
```

The turn locates an arena. It does not decide what happens there. The 144 routes
should reference the twelve radical House owners and a small generic turned-role
grammar; they are not 144 independent doctrines. Their current workbook prose
contains many narrativized conclusions, so those sentences remain provisional
examples pending individual review.

A turned reading also retains the source chart's perspective. A Washington,
D.C. chart describes the United States' experience of another actor; that
actor's own national reality requires its own chart.
