# FORTRESS America and Military Installation Energy Independence — Research Package

**Status:** `current`
**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-09-30 ET
**Question:** What did FORTRESS America change, and what would have to happen before major military installations actually possess independent, tested power?

## Answer

On September 30 the Department of War established **FORTRESS America**—Federal Operations for Resilience, Troop Resources, Energy, Supply, and Survivability—as a five-pillar homeland-defense resilience program. The accessible official release says the energy pillar will ensure that every **major U.S. military installation generates its own independent power** so bases can remain operational if cyberattack or sabotage takes down the civilian grid.

That moves installation power from a dispersed collection of statutes, service plans, microgrids, leases and reactor pilots into a named department-wide convergence program. The signed memorandum's indexed text directs a program office under the Under Secretary for Acquisition and Sustainment within 30 days, with a Senior Executive Service lead, two-star military adviser and support from the Military Departments, Defense Agencies and Field Activities.

The announcement does **not** provide a covered-installation list, a definition of independent power, a critical-versus-full-load requirement, an endurance period, a technology mix, a funding total, a project schedule or an acceptance test. The correct state is:

> program established → implementation constitution not yet public → delivery unproven

## The central scope mismatch

FORTRESS and existing law create overlapping but nonidentical objects:

| Object | Covered estate | Covered load | Clock/state |
|---|---|---|---|
| FORTRESS announcement | every **major** U.S. military installation | “independent power,” undefined | official objective; no completion date in accessible release |
| 10 U.S.C. §2920 | **each installation** | 100% of energy load required for critical missions | 99.9% minimum availability by end FY2030; mission-specific standards can run to 99.9999% |
| FY2021 NDAA net-zero note | at least 10% of **major** installations | annual energy net-zero plus water or waste net-zero | FY2035 |
| Next Great American Base | one new model base | described as completely off the commercial grid | announced design objective, not an operating site |

The current Base Structure Report surface defines a “large site” as at least 100,000 acres or at least 4.5 million square feet. Section 2864 uses the current BSR “large site” meaning for “major military installation.” The September 30 release does not expressly adopt that statutory cross-reference, so it is the best public scope candidate—not a confirmed FORTRESS site list. Multiple sites may also belong to one installation.

## What “independent power” must resolve

The phrase can describe very different capabilities:

1. generation physically located on an installation;
2. a mission enclave or selected critical loads able to island;
3. all critical missions able to island;
4. the installation's full electrical load able to island;
5. stated endurance under fuel, storage and maintenance assumptions;
6. annual net energy production equal to consumption; or
7. permanent normal-operation disconnection from the commercial grid.

FORTRESS confirms the objective but does not choose publicly among these levels. Existing policy favors mission assurance, on-site distributed energy, microgrids and full-time installed sources. That posture normally remains grid-connected and islands when needed; it is not synonymous with permanent isolation.

## The pre-existing implementation constitution

FORTRESS inherits a mature authority and planning stack:

- **10 U.S.C. §101** defines energy resilience as the ability to avoid, prepare for, minimize, adapt to and recover from disruption so mission assurance and readiness continue or are rapidly reestablished.
- **10 U.S.C. §2920** requires each installation's critical-mission load, duration, current sources and interruption endurance to be identified; it favors diverse on-installation generation, microgrids and full-time sources over emergency-only generators.
- **Installation Energy Plans** are the planning object that joins mission requirements, risk, infrastructure and candidate mitigations.
- **Black Start Exercises** physically terminate off-installation energy before backup systems start, then test start, transfer and load-carrying performance. Tabletop exercises are not equivalent operating proof.
- **Energy Resilience and Conservation Investment Program** projects use Defense-wide MILCON for resilience and mission assurance.
- **Third-party-finance authorities** include PPAs, enhanced-use leases, energy-savings performance contracts, utility energy-service contracts and 10 U.S.C. §2922a agreements. Each assigns ownership, operation, access, price and emergency rights differently.
- **Cybersecurity and microgrid design criteria** govern facility-related control systems and resilient microgrid engineering; generation capacity alone is not a secure operating capability.

## Delivery lanes already in motion

### Army private-capital generation

The Army Strategic Capital Initiative's June 2026 solicitation sought private financing, construction, operation and decommissioning at seven installations. Normal operation serves the commercial market; following a qualifying outage the project must island and support the installation for at least 14 days. The September 18 announcement identified six conditional developer/site pairings. These are early lease-solicitation selections, not executed leases or operating plants.

### Nuclear paths

- **Army Janus:** five vendor/site pairings announced August 27; contractor-owned and -operated model; up to $2.2 billion in milestone-based awards over FY2027–FY2031. Design, authorization, construction, criticality and delivery remain.
- **NWS Crane:** the Department announced a joint Navy/Army Janus commercial micro-modular reactor deployment no later than September 2028 for critical operations. The announcement describes the intended service and ownership model; final agreement, authorization, construction, criticality and accepted delivery remain.
- **Eielson Air Force Base:** proposed privately developed microreactor of up to 5 MW for critical infrastructure; the Oklo notice of intent begins negotiation and remains conditional on NRC licensing and final award.
- **Project Pele:** transportable prototype at Idaho National Laboratory. It can prove reactor technology and transportable operation; it is not yet an installation power service.

### Operating and bounded examples

- **MCAS Yuma:** the official 2016–17 description says the utility-partner microgrid provides backup capacity for 100% of current and projected base requirements. That is a full-load design/availability example, not fleet-wide proof.
- **MCAS Miramar:** the microgrid was completed and exercised in 2021. Official material describes enough mixed-source capacity to power the station for up to 21 days, while the reported full-scale exercise proved islanding of mission-critical operations. Design endurance and observed test scope must remain separate.
- **SUBASE New London:** an operating islanded microgrid combines a 10.75 MW combined-heat-and-power plant with first-right access to a 7.4 MW fuel-cell park during interruption, plus cybersecure controls and load shedding.
- **Fort McCoy Main Gate:** a $1.4 million microgrid opened in 2026 for the gate and nearby mission-essential facilities, with a stated minimum 14-day capability. It does not cover the full installation.
- **DLA Distribution Sigonella:** two battery/solar systems activated for DLA facilities with generator support and a stated 14 days on one fuel load. It is a tenant-facility capability, not proof that all NAS Sigonella loads are independent.

These examples show that the engineering is not hypothetical. They also show why every claim needs a load boundary, duration and test record.

## What would make FORTRESS materially new

The program becomes more than a coordination label when it does one or more of the following:

- publishes a binding definition and covered-site list;
- reconciles the “major installation” objective with §2920's every-installation critical-load standard;
- establishes a common load, duration, fuel, cyber and test baseline;
- identifies budget lines, transfers, private-finance pipelines and accountable owners;
- consolidates or assigns authority among Energy Resilience and Optimization, Infrastructure Modernization and Resilience, the services, DLA Energy and installation commanders;
- turns site plans into executed agreements and commissioned assets; and
- publishes comparable black-start, islanding, endurance and recovery results.

## Bottom line

FORTRESS America is best read as a new **portfolio and command layer** over an older energy-resilience system. Its significance is the attempt to synchronize installation power with supply-chain, reserve, hardening, cyber, space and spectrum resilience. Its weakness at announcement is that the word “independent” has no public technical constitution. Until scope, loads, duration, ownership, funding and tests are fixed, “all bases self-sufficient” remains an overclaim.
