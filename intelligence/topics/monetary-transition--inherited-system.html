The inherited money system: claims, access, clearing and loss

Who issues money, who may hold central-bank or settlement accounts, how do claims clear, and who supplies liquidity when payment chains fail?

The inherited system is a layered constitution rather than one ledger. The Federal Reserve keeps the dollar unit and crisis backstop; Treasury is the sovereign issuer-side and fiscal authority; and Reserve Banks act as Treasury's bank and fiscal agents in designated operations. Banks, market utilities, card networks and private intermediaries create, route, record and absorb different claims. New instruments enter this structure; they do not erase the older issuer, access, collateral, liquidity or loss boundaries.

Public money and the clearing core

Congressional oversight is the outer control constitution for the Federal Reserve. Congress can amend or repeal the System, the Senate confirms Governors, committees compel testimony and build legislative records, GAO audits within statutory scope, outside auditors and the Board–CFPB OIG inspect defined functions, and courts review qualifying legal disputes. Those controls defeat a literal fourth-branch classification, but they do not amount to day-to-day command. Self-funding, long protected terms, nonconfirmed Reserve Bank presidents, GAO monetary-policy exclusions, the OIG's limited reach over ordinary Reserve Bank operations and the Congressional Review Act's monetary-policy exception leave the immediate policy core deliberately insulated.

The Federal Reserve's dollar role is a congressional statutory operating arrangement rather than a private agreement or surrender of U.S. monetary sovereignty. Congress defines the national unit and legal-tender framework; Treasury mints coins, retains capped United States-note authority and physically produces Federal Reserve notes; the Board orders and authorizes those notes; Reserve Banks issue them into circulation and maintain central-bank account balances. Commercial-bank deposits and payment stablecoins are separate private dollar claims. Congress can amend, transfer or repeal this architecture by ordinary federal legislation, while executive orders, regulations and service contracts cannot rewrite the governing statutes. The 1913–2026 change history shows Congress repeatedly exercising that power across Reserve Bank duration, Board/FOMC governance, emergency lending, gold and note rules, policy objectives, payments, interest on reserves and digital money. Legislative dissolution and violation-based forfeiture are separate termination routes; the statutory dollar is a unit of account shared by sovereign and private claims, not a synonym for any one issuer's note.

The June 12 Oklahoma City field hearing is a useful test of how those boundaries are enforced politically. Representative Warren Davidson alleged continuing CBDC work; former Kansas City Fed president Esther George said she was unaware of it. The exchange was not an admission by a current Fed policymaker. The enacted §16A prohibition postdated the hearing, while its House text predated it; the public project record proves historical research and prototypes but not issuance of a prohibited U.S. CBDC. Section 11(h) would require a specific Reserve Bank violation of the Federal Reserve Act and Board action, neither of which the hearing established.

Fostering payment and settlement system safety and efficiency is a major Federal Reserve function, but it is not a unitary third mandate parallel to the monetary-policy objectives in Federal Reserve Act §2A. It is a distributed responsibility assembled from specific statutory powers and policies: Reserve Bank operation and accounts, Monetary Control Act pricing and access, intraday liquidity, payment regulations, supervision and FMI oversight, plus research and coordination. Safe, efficient and accessible must be tested separately across validation, clearing, funding, settlement, finality, recording and remedy rather than inferred from the speed or branding of one rail.

The Fed, Treasury and Reserve Banks perform different jobs. Fed accounts, Fedwire and central-bank settlement are access-controlled services; Treasury is the sovereign issuer-side and fiscal authority, while Reserve Banks act as Treasury's bank and fiscal agents in designated operations; DTC, NSCC and FICC maintain distinct securities, clearing and collateral functions. The current Fed and clearing work supports a narrower finding than abolition or replacement: private clearing can move into the middle while the Fed retains the currency unit, backstop and selected settlement powers.

The proposed Payment Account and related access debates show why access is a legal and supervisory question. National ex ante constraints coexist with individual Reserve-Bank adjudication; an application, eligibility determination, operating access and completed settlement are separate clocks. A proposed centrally cleared repo arrangement remains a proposal until the named membership, sponsorship, collateral, settlement and default records exist.

The Kraken example makes these distinctions concrete. Kansas City Fed’s May 8 disclosure describes Fedwire Funds-only access for Kraken Financial under existing authority, with restricted balances and no intraday or discount-window credit or interest. It does not grant access to the exchange or all Payward companies. The account, the earlier Payment Account RFI and the later May proposal have different decision clocks. Interest-rate constitution: target, transmission and member pricing

The FOMC does not set one universal interest rate. It votes on a target range for the overnight federal funds rate. The Board separately sets interest on reserve balances; the FOMC directs New York Desk standing-repo, reverse-repo and securities operations; Reserve Bank directors establish discount rates subject to Board review; and the New York Fed calculates EFFR and SOFR from specified transactions. Treasury auction yields, secondary-market yields, bank prime rates and customer loan contracts have their own price-setting mechanisms.

The September 16, 2026 action shows the clock separation. The FOMC raised the target range to 3.75–4.00 percent effective September 17; the implementation note set IORB at 3.90 percent, standing repo at 4.00 percent, ON RRP at 3.75 percent and primary credit at 4.00 percent. The next observed EFFR, any bank prime change, a lender quote, a contract reset and cash payment remain later and separate events.

Below-market pricing is not one legal category. Ordinary private discounts, tax below-market loans, bank-insider preferences, public credit subsidies and credit-union cooperative pricing have different authorities and consequences. Credit unions can return cooperative value through lower member loan rates, but charter and membership, maximum-rate, official-preference, fair-lending, tax, capital and safety-and-soundness rules still govern the particular loan. A rate below a bank average is not by itself evidence of an improper subsidy.

Overnight money plumbing: reserves, rollover and the policy anchor

Overnight federal-funds borrowing is daily balance-sheet plumbing, not necessarily emergency financing. Customer payments, securities settlements, tax flows and clearing entries redistribute reserve balances every business day. A bank may borrow to restore a preferred end-of-day buffer, while today's larger federal-funds segments also include Federal Home Loan Bank lending to foreign banking organizations for rate arbitrage and to large banks for liquidity-regulation motives. Federal Reserve research places more than 90 percent of current lending with FHLBs and reports only about $2–$6 billion per day in the traditional bank-to-bank segment during the cited study period.

A federal-funds loan settles today and matures the next business day. Repayment may come from incoming payments, deposits, maturing or sold assets, repo, central-bank credit or a new loan. When new borrowing replaces the matured claim, it is a separate rollover/refinancing event; dependence on renewal creates liquidity risk. The principal transfer can be final over Fedwire while the credit remains unsecured: no identified asset is pledged, so the lender still bears the borrower's general credit risk until repayment.

EFFR is the policy operating anchor because reserve balances are the banking system's central-bank settlement asset and the Fed controls the return available on eligible balances through IORB—not because federal funds is the largest credit market or the contractual index for every loan. A direct reserve balance is an asset of the eligible account holder and a Reserve Bank liability recorded through a master account; a commercial nostro/vostro account is a claim on a correspondent bank. ON RRP gives eligible nonbank cash investors an overnight Federal Reserve alternative, moving the composition of Fed liabilities from bank reserves toward an ON RRP liability when private alternatives are less attractive.

Private claims and ordinary account holders

A commercial-bank deposit is a bank liability; a stablecoin, tokenized deposit, card credential and custody balance are different claims with different issuers, redemption rules and legal remedies. The private monetary stack maps issuers, custodians, networks, merchant acceptance and adapters without finding one unified ledger. A public transfer record can improve visibility while reserves, beneficial ownership, direct redemption, finality and remedy remain institutionally governed.

The September 24 Fed and CFTC return makes representation portability more concrete without collapsing those claims. The Fed proposes to admit specified reserve assets in tokenized form only when the token confers identical legal rights to the traditional asset. CFTC staff says an otherwise eligible collateral or customer-fund investment may retain eligibility in tokenized form when the holder receives the same or functionally equivalent legal and economic rights and all custody, liquidity, concentration, maturity and other controls remain satisfied. Neither object approves a network or turns the tokenized reserve or collateral asset into the payment-stablecoin liability it may support.

The foundational bank-versus-credit-union map adds the legal constitution beneath those accounts. It separates national and state bank charters, federal and state credit-union charters, stock and mutual ownership, member fields, FDIC and NCUSIF insurance, holding companies and CUSOs, public-deposit authority, tax treatment and failure resolution. Florida and Texas operate active state-CU systems with public-deposit access; New York has an active state-CU system but retains a bank/trust-company municipal-deposit rule; Wyoming has public-deposit access for federally chartered CUs but no current state-CU law.

A September 15 state-credit-union audit finds a narrower state-level transition than a private proposal for fifty state-owned credit unions. Florida and Wyoming recently admitted ordinary member-owned credit unions to public-deposit programs; Wyoming's Stable Token Commission said that change may facilitate credit-union participation, while Florida's stablecoin pilot ordinarily converts receipts to dollars held at a qualified public depository. Neither state has created an operating state-owned credit union or demonstrated a credit-union role in the digital-money chain. Public-depository approval, an actual deposit, a resulting loan and a digital-money role remain separate clocks.

Retail account agreements make the inherited system tangible. Holds, setoff, intermediaries, outages, arbitration and user-control limits are contract and operating facts. Retail debt adds the lender–merchant–servicer–buyer–regulator chain and keeps origination, servicing, sale, charge-off and loss ownership separate.

Credit approval: evidence, judgment and responsibility

The mortgage standards investigation distinguishes accepted scoring models from the risk a lender will accept. Fannie’s DU gate removal, GSE score competition, rent/cash-flow evidence and electronic verification change different parts of approval. Debt capacity, collateral, product eligibility and lender requirements remain. Fannie reports more than 21,000 applications with improved recommendations from permissioned rent data during September 2021–May 2026; that is neither additional closed loans nor a September 2026 score-reform result. FHA has announced model expansion but current inspected instructions retain existing policy pending implementation. The broader March 2026 mortgage-credit order directs agencies to consider further changes, which need their own implementing instruments.

The operating follow-up locates judgment in income classification, investigation of contradictions, validation boundaries and lender management of quality control. Fannie’s 2026 changes reduce specified document and repeat-verification requirements, introduce employer-level validation and replace fixed prefunding sampling with lender-designed risk assessment. Conditional enforcement relief changes contractual exposure. These are changes to the distribution of work and responsibility; their net effect on cost, staffing and processing time has not been established.

Retail and business lending change through different mechanisms. Named retail deployments use current account data and automated processing within lender policies. SBA’s March 2026 7(a) Small rules replace agency SBSS screening with documented repayment coverage and bank-activity corroboration. OCC/FDIC commercial guidance and the interagency model-risk framework emphasize management judgment. Separate CFPB final rules change fair-lending governance and narrow future business-data reporting; those changes do not share the mortgage initiative’s authority or implementation clock.

One mortgage through legal books and consolidated accounts

The mortgage accounting investigation follows balanced journals across the originator, warehouse lender, Fannie, trust, investor and Fed. A consolidated securitization can restore Fannie’s cash while retaining the mortgage asset and adding external trust debt. Repo introduces a separate institutional borrowing; a Fed purchase replaces the seller’s MBS with a bank deposit and creates reserves at its bank. Internal claims eliminate only within the stated reporting boundary. Selected gross claims, household debt, net assets and income are different measures. The repayment example follows principal through the same layers in reverse.

Liquidity, collateral and failure

Clearing is not settlement, collateral is not cash, and a backstop is not an ordinary operating account. The transition research therefore tracks representation, authoritative record, money, collateral, liquidity, accounting and failure as separate states. Technical atomicity cannot by itself supply legal finality, loss allocation or an exit route.

The inherited system's persistence is clearest under stress: access, liquidity and emergency authority are controlled by named institutions and legal instruments. Emergency monetary-policy research maps DPA, Stafford, IEEPA, NEA and adjacent authorities as possible activation routes, while the judicial money map tests the perimeter through master-account, preemption, charter, sovereign-asset and bank-failure cases.

The September 10 financial-market-infrastructure crosswalk identifies the international FMI/PFMI framework, U.S. Title VIII financial-market-utility category, Fed policy and Regulation HH, and SEC/CFTC clearing rules as distinct layers. Our liquidity research already cites PFMI; the follow-through maps named clearing, payment and digital arrangements to their respective authorities and connects transfer-agent record control to distinct settlement and risk functions. A standard used as an analytical benchmark is not by itself an applicability determination.
