Retail and business credit approvals: judgment, evidence and administration
Research ID: mortgage-credit-score-reform (the existing credit-standards investigation). Evidence checked September 7, 2026, America/New_York. This comparison extends the mortgage standards and judgment study. The retail-debt entity spine retains its separate historical portfolio and loss-ownership scope.
Question: How have U.S. consumer and business credit approval processes changed, particularly in who exercises judgment, which evidence is needed, and how lenders administer and defend their decisions?
Answer: There is no single cross-market reform. Documented retail implementations put more current data and verification into automated decisions. SBA's 7(a) Small program replaces an agency score screen with explicit repayment evidence. Commercial-bank supervision gives management more responsibility for defining and managing risks. Separate 2026 rules change fair-lending governance and the future reporting burden. These mechanisms must be examined individually; none alone measures net processing costs or the quality of approvals.

2. Retail: greater automation within lender-specific policies
Affirm: more current information in each transaction decision
On January 14, 2026, Affirm said richer balance and cash-flow signals were already in use for Affirm Card customers with linked external accounts or Affirm Money accounts. Expansion to other customers was prospective. Its transaction-by-transaction assessment predates this enhancement. This is evidence of a specific deployment, not universal BNPL adoption. Affirm announcement
Interpretation: the decision can use a more current financial picture, while implementation work shifts toward account permission, data availability and model oversight. The release does not establish net staffing savings or universal borrower benefits.
Personal loans: a lender can retain policy while automating execution
Cobalt Credit Union's June 25, 2025 announcement confirms personal-loan originations through Upstart began in March. Applicants had to meet Cobalt's credit policies and completed membership and closing through its branded digital process. Auto refinancing was planned in that announcement, not established as live. Cobalt/Upstart implementation
Upstart's 2025 Form 10-K reports 91% of platform loans were fully automated, meaning no human intervention by Upstart. This was also 91% in 2024. It demonstrates an established operating model, not a new 2025 increase, a Cobalt-specific measure, or absence of all lender governance. Upstart filing
Auto: automation and human review can coexist
OneMain's January 2025 direct-auto securitization disclosure describes proprietary risk scoring, income/debt assessment and calculation of disposable income. Electronic income verification can reduce document collection; alternative evidence and specified exceptions remain. Branch personnel review applications within delegated limits, with larger/out-of-parameter requests sent centrally. This is a documented hybrid workflow, not proof that the process began in 2025 or that all auto lenders operate this way. OneMain disclosure, printed pages 88–89
Cards: do not import mortgage documentation rules
Regulation Z section 1026.51 requires card issuers to consider ability to make required minimum payments before opening an account or raising its limit. The framework permits information from applicants, existing relationships, third parties and qualifying estimation methods. It does not impose a mortgage-style universal paystub package; stricter under-21 conditions apply. This flexibility is a continuing baseline, not a Pulte-era rule change. Credit-card ability to pay
3. SBA: the clearest change from a score screen to documented analysis
SBA's supplemental Notice 5000-876777 controls over the January notice's replaced SOP amendments. From March 1, 2026, SBA stopped providing and screening 7(a) Small applications with SBSS. The Small channel is capped at $350,000; Express is separately governed. Lenders may still use permitted business scoring for credit history, with the result and acceptable range documented, and may not rely solely on consumer scores. Repayment analysis requires DSCR of at least 1.10:1, historical or projected, with retained financial support. Two recent months of primary operating-account activity generally corroborate obligations; narrow exceptions apply. Controlling supplemental notice
SBA's March 31 FAQ makes the before/after explicit: a passing SBSS previously satisfied repayment determination; now acceptable coverage plus bank-activity corroboration provides that assurance. The account review includes merchant cash advances and similar sales-based obligations. The credit memorandum still addresses operations, management and the request. Seasonal comparisons should use the corresponding prior-year interim period. SBA clarification
Illustration: a business has apparently adequate annual earnings, but regular account withdrawals reveal a sales-based financing obligation missing from its debt schedule. The lender must reconcile the repayment calculation with those withdrawals. This illustrates a shift in required reasoning and evidence; it is not an observed borrower case.
SOP 50 10 8.1 is already published but becomes effective October 1, 2026. Its future changes must not be described as September requirements. This pass did not obtain the full SOP DOCX, so it does not claim an exhaustive audit of collateral, guarantees, equity injections, ownership eligibility or October changes. SBA issuance notice
4. Commercial credit: management discretion and repayment analysis
On December 5, 2025, OCC and FDIC withdrew their leveraged-lending guidance and FAQs. The replacement statement directs banks to define leveraged loans consistently, set risk appetite, assess repayment and deleveraging capacity, challenge projections, monitor refinancing risk and independently assess purchased participations. It also covers pipeline risks for loans held or distributed. OCC/FDIC statement
This is a two-agency action. The Federal Reserve's current credit-risk index still lists its leveraged-lending guidance; the examined materials do not establish matching Federal Reserve withdrawal. Fed guidance index
OCC's same-day venture-lending update explicitly places risk appetite, limits, documentation, risk ratings and reserves with bank management. Its repayment analysis distinguishes cash available for debt service from cash likely to be consumed by operations; uncommitted fundraising and recurring revenue alone do not establish sustainable repayment. This updated guidance articulates a supervisory approach using existing law, rather than proving every analytical principle is new. OCC Bulletin 2025-45
Interpretation: financial statements are the starting evidence. Commercial judgment assesses their assumptions, future cash availability, enforceable support and the conditions protecting repayment over time.
5. Model oversight changes across bank credit businesses
The April 17, 2026 interagency framework replaced prior model-risk guidance. It emphasizes tailoring to model materiality and institutional complexity, with greatest relevance above $30 billion in assets and possible relevance to smaller institutions with significant model exposure. It is supervisory guidance, not independently enforceable standards. SR 26-2, FDIC applicability
The operative text describes expert challenge, validation, monitoring, vendor oversight and documentation. It recognizes that model developers make judgments about data, design and assumptions. Generative and agentic AI are outside this guidance's scope; other governance and legal obligations still apply. Revised model-risk guidance
Interpretation: automation embeds judgment in system design and operating limits. A lender still has to decide whether a model fits its applicants, whether its results remain reliable and what to do when it does not.
6. Administrative and fair-lending changes must be separated
Section 1071 changes future business-data collection
The May 1, 2026 final rule is effective June 30, with compliance January 1, 2028. It raises the lender threshold from 100 to 1,000 covered originations in each of two consecutive years, narrows the small-business revenue definition from $5 million to $1 million, excludes additional products and removes fields including denial reasons and pricing. This changes coverage calculations, systems and reporting preparation; it does not mean businesses above $1 million cannot obtain loans. Final rule, 91 FR 23530
Separate Regulation B amendments affect decision governance now
The April 22 final rule became effective July 21, 2026. It adopts CFPB's interpretation that ECOA does not recognize disparate-impact liability, revises discouragement provisions and restricts special-purpose credit programs. Intentionally discriminatory proxies remain prohibited. These are substantive changes to the agency's regulatory framework; this research does not characterize them as a final judicial resolution or determine every other applicable law. Final rule, 91 FR 21620
For-profit special-purpose programs cannot use race, color, national origin or sex as their qualifying characteristics under the revised framework, and supporting written plans have further requirements. Current official materials reflect the amendments; this pass did not inspect the litigation docket. CFPB current regulation notice
Applicant notices remain a different record
Removing denial reasons from 1071 reporting does not eliminate required explanations to applicants. Regulation B retains business-specific notice provisions, including differing routes for smaller and larger businesses, and requires specific actual reasons where a statement is required. A score explanation and a lender's decision explanation are distinct. Section 1002.9
CFPB withdrew its BNPL interpretive rule and certain AI adverse-action circulars in May 2025. Those withdrawals must not be treated as repeal of the underlying statutes and regulations or as proof of causation for a lender's model changes. BNPL status, withdrawn guidance
The personal-financial-data-rights rule is another timing boundary: CFPB says its compliance dates were stayed by a court on October 29, 2025, with reconsideration underway. Voluntary account-data underwriting is not evidence that universal mandatory open-banking implementation has occurred. CFPB implementation status
7. What the comparison establishes
The strongest common thread is a reallocation of judgment, not a uniform migration toward either human or automated approval. The meaningful tests are which tasks disappear, which evidence replaces them, who handles exceptions, who owns the policy and who must explain or defend the result.
A lender-specific follow-up would compare actual document requests, manual referrals, time resolving contradictions, cost per completed application, model changes, notice accuracy and material defects. This pass establishes rules and named deployments, not a nationwide causal estimate. It does not refresh every historical portfolio in the retail-debt spine, audit all state consumer/business finance laws, or measure all banks' private policies. The latest mortgage score changes are not the legal source of these separate retail and business developments.
