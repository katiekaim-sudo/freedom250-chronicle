Japan: if a bank or payment system fails, who takes the loss, in what order?

On the Japan money map (as of September 15, 2026), a failure moves through 5 separate steps: what sets it off (Ordinary liquidity stress, Operational outage, Systemic failure classification); the private first response (Commercial banks, Japan Securities Clearing Corporation, Zengin System and More Time, FMI default waterfalls); the Bank of Japan's liquidity step (BOJ Policy Board, BOJ current-account balances, BOJ-NET Funds Transfer System, BOJ Article 33 lending, BOJ Article 37 outage lending, BOJ Article 38 special support), which lends against collateral and does not cover a final loss; resolution (Cabinet and Prime Minister, Financial Services Agency, Deposit Insurance Corporation of Japan, TLAC and creditor loss, Deposit protection and transfer); and where the loss finally lands (Fraud compensation and restitution, JPYSC trust stablecoin, Possible retail CBDC, Industry contributions, Conditional fiscal subsidy or guarantee). Public money is a last, conditional step that needs legal and budget approval.
