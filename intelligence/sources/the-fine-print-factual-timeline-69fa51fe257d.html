The fine print — factual timeline

Updated: 2026-07-12 Status: working factual spine Scope: the consumer side of the money story — the customer contract, the legal person behind the brand, embedded credit, the customer-data-to-product loop, and who owns a consumer loan after it is made.

Why this exists

The Money Machine, the Monetary Chronicle, the Castration Timeline and the Judicial Watch all read the money story from above — the rails, the charter, the master account, the bench. This is the same rewiring read from below: what changed in the paper an ordinary person signs.

Under the canonical US chart the money story has two poles. The rails and the coin are the 2nd. This is the 8th — other people's money, debt, and the terms on which you are lent to.

Inclusion rules

  • Entries record a dated public action, filing, agreement edition, merger, conversion, publication or formally scheduled effective date.
  • Descriptions state what the cited source says. They do not assign motives.
  • Occurred — it has happened. Scheduled — a future date formally established. Public position — a stated management target or strategy. Research — the documented state of a contract stack or research program as read on the sweep date.
  • A current clause is not a recent change. Where the packet's before/after proof failed audit, the entry is written as a current term and says so. See the provenance block on the source documents — those corrections are binding here.
  • Month-precision dates get no chart (month precision = no honest chart).

Tracks

  • The Crowd — arbitration, class waivers, and the hardening against mass filings. What happens to people acting together.
  • The Mask — the brand stays still while the legal person behind it changes.
  • The Hook — credit placed at the point where a purchase was blocked by price.
  • The Eye — the customer's own data, turned into research, turned into a product.
  • The Owner — who originates, who services, who furnishes the credit data, and who actually ends up holding the paper.

2018

2018-11 — Comenity's notice-and-cure machinery already in place

Status: Occurred Entities: Comenity Bank; Comenity Capital Bank Track: The Crowd

The November 2018 forms already required at least thirty days' pre-suit notice and a cure period, an individually retained lawyer, and a personally signed arbitration rejection. They also offered a $5,100 special payment to a claimant who beat the bank's rejected pre-demand offer — an incentive the current form no longer contains. The baseline matters: the hardening did not begin in 2025. It deepened.

Source: 2018 Comenity Bank form (CFPB archive)

2020

2020-11 — The Cross River origination chain enters the public record

Status: Occurred Entities: Cross River Bank; Affirm; Upstart Track: The Owner

The program agreements filed as SEC exhibits set out the shape that later becomes standard. Cross River is the named lender. Affirm purchases most of the loans, becomes the owner of the associated customer information, and acts as the contractually designated servicer. Upstart processes applications under bank-controlled guidelines and maintains the register of who owns the note. The borrower deals with a brand that is neither the lender nor, ultimately, the owner.

Source: Affirm program agreement (SEC)

2023

2023-06-14 — Cross River's own terms take effect

Status: Occurred Entities: Cross River Bank; CRB Group, Inc. Track: The Crowd

Individual AAA arbitration, class and representative waiver, a sixty-day pre-arbitration notice period, and a thirty-day mailed opt-out after account opening. Covered parties reach the bank and its broadly defined affiliates, officers, agents and representatives. Notably, a customer may reject a future arbitration change within thirty days and keep the earlier language — a right most of the big banks do not grant.

Source: Cross River Terms of Service

2024

2024-11-17 — Chase replaces its deposit-account arbitration section

Status: Occurred Entities: JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. Track: The Crowd

The change notice replaced the arbitration section outright and gave existing customers until January 16, 2025 to opt out by phone. The replacement reaches past, present and future claims — accounts, transactions, products, services, advertising, applications, denials, approvals, employees, agents. It bars class, representative, consolidated and public-injunctive relief. And the definition of "we" expands: if Chase assigns the account to an unaffiliated third party, that assignee is folded into the protected class. The shield is written to travel with the debt.

Source: Chase 2024 agreement and replacement notice

2025

2025-04-01 — Ally's card book closes on CardWorks; Merrick becomes the bank

Status: Occurred Entities: Ally Financial; CardWorks; Merrick Bank; Ollo Track: The Mask

$2.3 billion of receivables and 1.3 million active cardholders change hands. Merrick Bank becomes the issuing bank and the entity that reports the customer to the credit bureaus; Ollo becomes the servicing interface. Ally says the promotional APR, credit line, benefits and due date all stay the same, and the account history, alerts, paperless settings, scheduled payments and autopay carry over. The card number even survives — only the expiration and CVV change. Everything the customer can see was engineered to hold still.

Source: Ally / CardWorks sale

2025-05-18 — Discover Bank merges into Capital One, N.A.

Status: Occurred Entities: Discover Bank; Capital One, N.A. Track: The Mask

The bank named Discover ceases to exist. The word "Discover" does not. The brand, the account number, the rewards and the interface all remain; the agreements become agreements with Capital One, N.A. Governing law moves from Delaware to Virginia — a fact that comes from the cardmember agreement itself, not, as the source packet stated, from the merger FAQ.

Source: Capital One / Discover merger FAQ

2025-06-30 — "Discover" is redefined to mean Capital One

Status: Occurred Entities: Capital One, N.A. Track: The Mask

The post-merger cardmember agreement opens by defining its terms: "'We,' 'us,' 'our,' and 'Discover' refer to Capital One, N.A., the issuer of your Card." The old Discover arbitration architecture largely survives inside the new bank — individual AAA arbitration, class waiver, thirty-day mailed opt-out, survival after sale or assignment. One legal bank now carries two visibly different inherited contract families. The merger did not harmonize them.

Source: Discover Secured Cardmember Agreement, June 30 2025

2025-10-15 — Wells adds an adult-control regime to Zelle for minors

Status: Occurred Entities: Wells Fargo Bank, N.A.; Zelle; Early Warning Services Track: The Eye

Customers aged thirteen to seventeen come under an adult-control regime: a co-owner may revoke permission, which removes the enrolled email or phone number and cancels pending and scheduled payments. At eighteen the customer may enroll alone. A small entry, kept because it dates the moment the payment rail started carrying an age-and-permission layer.

Source: Wells Fargo — Zelle update notice

2025-11-20 — Citi's deposit agreement closes the door behind you

Status: Occurred Entities: Citibank, N.A. Track: The Crowd

Individual AAA arbitration, no class or representative or combined claims, and a forty-five-day mailed opt-out — but only for new accounts. Customers who failed to reject under the prior agreement do not get a new opt-out. The relationship is defined as debtor-creditor, not fiduciary. Citi keeps setoff without notice against most accounts for debts of an owner or beneficial owner, and may close with or without cause. Arbitration survives termination, bankruptcy, and sale of the account or the debt.

Source: Citibank Consumer Deposit Account Agreement

2025-12 — Synchrony's Pay Later reaches 6,200 merchant locations

Status: Occurred Entities: Synchrony Bank Track: The Hook

Pay Later expands to more than 6,200 merchant locations in 2025, including Lowe's and JCPenney, spanning Pay-in-4 through monthly loans as long as eighty-four months. Synchrony's own data claim merchants offering Pay Later beside revolving credit see at least a 10% average sales lift, and that Pay Later brings net-new customers who can later be migrated into private-label and co-brand cards.

Source: Synchrony 2025 annual report

2026

2026-01-07 — Goldman hands Apple Card to Chase; no customer paper exists

Status: Occurred Entities: Goldman Sachs Bank USA; Apple; JPMorgan Chase Track: The Mask

Goldman and Chase announce the transition of the Apple Card program — more than $20 billion of balances, expected to move without customer reapplication, Mastercard staying as the network, account data transferring. Expected timing is roughly twenty-four months. Goldman remains the issuer throughout. No Chase customer agreement, privacy notice, assignment notice or change-in-terms package has been issued. The corporate sale and the consumer contract run on different clocks, and the customer's clock has not started.

Source: Goldman — Apple Card transition

2026-01-16 — Truist's deposit agreement demands a handwritten signature

Status: Occurred Entities: Truist Bank Track: The Crowd

Individual JAMS arbitration, a required pre-dispute notice and informal-resolution period, express recognition of mass-arbitration procedures, and a thirty-day opt-out that must be personally signed and sent certified or overnight. Arbitration demands themselves require personally handwritten signatures. Class, consolidated, collective and representative relief are all barred. Truist may close without cause or notice and exercise setoff for debts owed to Truist and related entities.

Source: Truist Bank Services Agreement

2026-02-01 — Comerica becomes a division of Fifth Third

Status: Occurred Entities: Fifth Third Bank, N.A.; Comerica Bank; Fifth Third Bancorp Track: The Mask

The merger closes. Fifth Third Bank, N.A. is the surviving bank and becomes the counterparty on existing Comerica swaps. Comerica-branded systems, branches and customer-facing materials remain through conversion — "Comerica Bank, a division of Fifth Third Bank, N.A." Deposits held at the two banks immediately before closing stay separately insured for at least six months. The account mapping, fee comparison, arbitration notice and digital-migration booklet are not yet public.

Source: Fifth Third — Comerica merger closing

2026-02-07 — The Bilt card splits into three companies

Status: Occurred Entities: Bilt; Wells Fargo Bank, N.A.; Column N.A.; Cardless Inc. Track: The Mask

Legacy Bilt cards were usable through February 6. Customers who chose "Bilt 2.0" opened a new credit account with Column N.A., serviced by Cardless — while keeping the card number, the autopay and the saved merchant credentials, so that the change was nearly invisible at the point of use. A Wells balance moved only by an optional "Special Transfer," which the new agreement treats as a purchase and excludes from both the introductory APR and new-card rewards. Customers who kept the Wells account had it converted to a Wells Fargo Autograph. The new Column agreement carries its own forty-five-day opt-out and reaches Column, Cardless, servicers, agents, affiliates, assignees, successors, related merchants and collectors.

One brand. Three legal persons. The rewards, the credit, the billing, the data and the dispute rights now sit in different contracts with different companies.

Source: Bilt Card 2.0 transition record

2026-02 — U.S. Bank's digital agreement claims customer input as training data

Status: Research Entities: U.S. Bank National Association; U.S. Bancorp Advisors LLC Track: The Eye

The Digital Services Agreement carries a section headed "Use of artificial intelligence": "You acknowledge that we may use any data that is input by you to further train or develop AI models or additional AI tools, or for quality control, analytics, product improvement, compliance, customer service, offers, and other business purposes." The page was last modified 2026-02-20.

Audit note — this is a CURRENT TERM, not a proven change. The source packet dated the clause's insertion to February 2026 using two Internet Archive snapshots, which the audit could not open. The clause is real and verbatim. The moment of its arrival is not established. Do not build a timing argument on this entry until the snapshots are re-verified.

Source: U.S. Bank Digital Services Agreement

2026-02-12 — Truist opens continuous open-banking data sharing

Status: Occurred Entities: Truist Bank; Mastercard Track: The Eye

Truist launches a Mastercard-connected open-banking experience. The separate open-banking terms authorize Truist to share account data with a connected company continuously, and continued use after a revision counts as acceptance of it.

Source: Truist — open banking

2026-02 — BofA opens relationship pricing to thirty million more clients

Status: Occurred Entities: Bank of America Track: The Hook

BofA Rewards is extended to any client with an active personal checking account — more than 30 million additional people made eligible. Card bonuses range from 10% to 75% depending on relationship tier. The structure is stated plainly: use checking as the entry account, connect the card and the Merrill assets, then price the whole relationship rather than the single product.

Source: Bank of America — BofA Rewards expansion

2026-03 — Cross River raises $50M to build both sides of the trade

Status: Occurred Entities: Cross River Bank; CRB Group, Inc. Track: The Owner

The parent raises $50 million to expand AI, crypto, embedded finance, international operations and product partnerships, supporting more than 100 technology partners. Cross River is simultaneously building the origination infrastructure and the capital that buys the resulting assets: up to $360 million of Parafin small-business receivables, a $100 million facility backed by Point home-equity-investment contracts, and up to $250 million of Figure crypto-backed loans. It can appear in a single transaction as bank, infrastructure provider, warehouse lender, or receivable buyer.

Source: Cross River — $50M growth capital

2026-03 — Synchrony batches the crowd into groups of fifty

Status: Occurred Entities: Synchrony Bank; CareCredit; PayPal Track: The Crowd

The current CareCredit agreement (revision code 3/2026) carries a "MASS FILING PROCEDURES" section: coordinated demands "shall be grouped into batches of no more than 50 demands per batch by state of residence, and then alphabetically by last name… One Merits Arbitrator shall be assigned for each batch, who shall provide for resolution of each batch as a single arbitration with one set of filing and administrative fees." Plus a mandatory individualized Notice of Dispute, a sixty-day informal-resolution period, and Rule 11-style certification. The same architecture appears in the PayPal card terms — it is not merchant-specific.

(The source packet dated this 2026-01-01; that date is not established. The revision code is the defensible anchor.)

Source: CareCredit Account Agreement (current)

2026-03-15 — Chase's mass-arbitration machine

Status: Occurred Entities: JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. Track: The Crowd

The current Deposit Account Agreement defines a mass arbitration at twenty-five or more coordinated claims and builds a procedure for it: claimant-specific transaction facts, damages calculations and authorization; a process arbitrator empowered to screen and dismiss at the threshold; sanctions; and an initial thirty cases — fifteen chosen by each side — sent through merits arbitration before any later rounds. Customers may opt out only within sixty days of opening the account. The provision continues after closure, after full payment, and after bankruptcy.

Source: Chase Deposit Account Agreement, current edition

2026-03-23 — The Bank of America agreement that has no arbitration clause

Status: Occurred Entities: Bank of America Track: The Crowd

The Online Banking Service Agreement in force on this date — still the live document at the bank's own URL as of the audit — contains no arbitration section at all. The word does not appear. This is the "before" state, and it is the honest one: it tightens the insertion window to under eight weeks and it does not depend on the Internet Archive.

Source: Bank of America — Online Banking Service Agreement

2026-04-08 — Truist rewrites arbitration inside the app agreement

Status: Occurred Entities: Truist Bank Track: The Crowd

Truist's official notice adds instant-network bill pay and instant external transfers, moves limits and cutoffs into the app itself, revises the service-guarantee conditions — and changes the mutual arbitration, jury-waiver and litigation-class-waiver provisions. The dispute terms now ride in the same document as the transfer limits.

Source: Truist — service agreement updates

2026-05-18 — Bank of America builds a dispute constitution for the whole digital layer

Status: Occurred Entities: Bank of America Track: The Crowd

The replacement agreement inserts a full §12, "Disputes and Arbitration," into a bank agreement that had none. Its scope is not a checking account. It reaches, in the document's own words, "Zelle Network Services, Real Time Payment Network Service, Bill Payment Services, ACH, Wire and Internal Transfer Services, E-Bills, Online Banking Alerts, BankAmeriDeals, and Erica Bank of America's Virtual Financial Assistant" — and "the application for, or the denial, approval or establishment of your Online/Mobile ID account."

Twenty-five or more coordinated claims trigger the mass-arbitration procedure: a process arbitrator screens for threshold compliance, merits cases proceed in waves of thirty with fifteen selected by each side, and Rule 11-style sanctions are available. Consumers get sixty days from first delivery to opt out. Small-business customers get no opt-out at all. The provision survives account closure, payment in full, bankruptcy, and sale or assignment.

Ending roughly seventeen years in which the bank had no pre-dispute arbitration clause.

Source: Bank of America — May 18 2026 agreement update

2026-05 — Goldman cuts the bottom quintile's cash flow to 0.8%

Status: Occurred Entities: Goldman Sachs Research Track: The Eye

Goldman lowers its estimate of consumer discretionary-cash-flow growth from 5.1% to 3.7% — and to 0.8% for the lowest-income quintile. In the same period its private-credit work is watching redemption requests, valuation opacity, defaults, and the growing overlap between bank lending, public credit and private funds. Goldman is exiting mass-market card issuance while expanding around the plumbing that finances what other lenders originate.

Source: Goldman Sachs Research — the outlook for the U.S. consumer

2026-05-31 — PNC's arbitration reaches the assets of failed banks

Status: Occurred Entities: PNC Bank, National Association Track: The Crowd

Individual arbitration; waivers of class, private-attorney-general, representative and joined claims. The definition of who is protected reaches affiliates, predecessors, acquired entities, successors, assigns — and assets acquired from failed banks. Customers get a forty-five-day mailed opt-out, but later amendments do not reopen it unless PNC changes a substantive arbitration clause.

Source: PNC Consumer Account Agreement

2026-06-04 — Three senators write to Bank of America

Status: Occurred Entities: U.S. Senate; Bank of America Track: The Crowd

Senators Blumenthal, Warren and Johnson write to the bank about the newly adopted forced-arbitration provision. The National Consumer Law Center and a Public Citizen coalition letter make the same objection. The independent record confirms what the documents show: this was new, and it was noticed.

Source: Senate letter to Bank of America

2026-06 — Chase writes the AI agent into the access contract

Status: Research Entities: JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A.; J.P. Morgan Securities LLC Track: The Eye

The Digital Services Agreement (last updated June 2026) now defines an "Agent" as "any software or service that takes autonomous, semi-autonomous, or non-autonomous action on behalf of, or at the instruction of, any person or entity." On third-party AI agents the bank says: "we do not provide, control, endorse, nor are responsible for how the Agent operates… we do not assume responsibility for, review, audit or certify its security or privacy policies." And the operative consequence: "we will rely on any instructions or actions taken by your Agent as if they were taken by you personally… we shall have no obligation to investigate the authority or validity of any such instructions."

You will be bound by what your agent does with your money. The bank will not look at your agent.

(The source packet dated this 2025-11-06; the live document says June 2026. The language is verbatim correct; the earlier date is not supported.)

Source: Chase Digital Services Agreement

2026-06 — BofA turns purchases into installments inside the app

Status: Occurred Entities: Bank of America Track: The Hook

Bank of America launches Custom Pay Plan — purchase-to-installment conversion — alongside card referrals and My Credit monitoring. A purchase already made can be converted, after the fact, into a structured payment plan. The continuum is now complete inside one bank: purchase → revolving balance → structured installment → hardship plan → charge-off or sale.

Source: Bank of America — referrals, Custom Pay Plan and My Credit

2026-06-17 — Fifth Third rebuilds the interface on customer interactions

Status: Occurred Entities: Fifth Third Bank, N.A. Track: The Eye

Fifth Third launches an AI-powered mobile interface trained on hundreds of millions of customer interactions, and says later agentic applications may span account opening, service, fraud, disputes and financial advice. The loop is stated openly: engagement and intent data will refine the interface and set the priority of what gets built next.

Source: Fifth Third — AI-powered interface

2026-06-19 — PNC converts FirstBank, and the opt-outs do not survive

Status: Occurred Entities: PNC Bank, National Association; FirstBank Track: The Mask

Systems convert June 19–21. FirstBank customers' opt-outs from discretionary overdraft payment — for checks, automatic bill payments and recurring debit transactions — did not carry over, and PNC states it does not offer an opt-out for that process. Savings and money-market accounts can no longer serve as overdraft-protection accounts. Zelle customers must re-enroll. Secured cards convert to unsecured PNC Cash Rewards cards. The card number stays; the expiration, CVV and possibly the due date change. Certain term-change opt-outs require closing the account and paying it off.

A choice the customer had made — do not pay my overdrafts — was extinguished by a merger they were not party to.

Source: PNC / FirstBank conversion

2026-06-30 — Franklin's tokenized fund keeps its custodian

Status: Research Entities: Franklin Templeton; Franklin Templeton Trust; Franklin Advisers, Inc.; JPMorgan Chase Bank Track: The Owner

Franklin reports $1.79 trillion in AUM, with digital assets around $1.7 billion and growing. But the important document is the OnChain U.S. Government Money Fund's own filing, and it says this: "For wallets that are hosted for an investor by the Fund's transfer agent, the private key associated with the investor's wallet will be held by the Fund's transfer agent." The blockchain-integrated system is "under the unilateral control of the transfer agent," which "has the ability to correct errors."

And the mechanism matters. A blockchain record "can never be deleted" — so the transfer agent corrects an unauthorized or erroneous transaction by appending an offsetting instruction. Not a reversal. An overwrite by addition.

The tokenized share never leaves the conventional registered-fund, transfer-agent and custodian hierarchy. JPMorgan Chase Bank still holds the fund's conventional assets. The token did not remove the intermediary. It renamed it.

Source: Franklin OnChain Fund prospectus / SAI (SEC)

2026-07-11 — The contract stack, as read on the sweep date

Status: Research Entities: Cross River Bank; Affirm; Upstart; BILL / Divvy; Evolve Bank; Stride Bank Track: The Owner

Read across the partner chains, the roles separate completely. For any given consumer loan, record separately who marketed it, who underwrote it, who originated it, who funded it, who services it, who furnishes the credit data, who collects, and who ends up holding the paper. Cross River originates for Affirm; Affirm buys most of the loans and becomes owner of the customer information. The reusable Affirm Card is issued by Evolve or Stride — not Cross River. Upstart's note permits sale without borrower consent and discloses the owner only on written request. The brand on the app is a poor guide to any of it.

Source: Upstart promissory note

The fuse ahead

2026-07-18 — Citi's deposit amendment lands on the GENIUS rules deadline

Status: Scheduled Entities: Citibank, N.A. Track: The Hook

Citi's amendment removes "Enhanced Direct Deposit," broadens what counts as qualifying $250 ACH activity — explicitly including certain Zelle, Venmo and PayPal activity processed through ACH — and waives the Access Checking monthly fee where any owner is twenty-three or younger.

The date is the same day the GENIUS Act's final rules are due, which fixes the statute's ignition date. That is almost certainly coincidence. It is noted because it is the exact seam this whole layer sits on: the definition of a deposit is being loosened at the consumer level in the same week the definition of a dollar is being fixed at the federal level. Calendar fact, not a Ledger call.

Source: Citibank Consumer Deposit Account Agreement + amendment cover

2026-12 — Fifth Third's Comerica conversion

Status: Scheduled Entities: Fifth Third Bank, N.A.; Comerica Bank Track: The Mask

Full customer conversion is scheduled for later in 2026. The account-mapping table, the fee comparison, the arbitration notice and the digital-migration booklet all arrive then. Fifth Third's Federal Reserve submission says consumer checking and savings will be mapped to Fifth Third equivalents and revised terms delivered before conversion. Until then, "Comerica" still appears on the interface of a bank that is already legally Fifth Third.

Source: Fifth Third — customer transition center

2028-01 — Apple Card's customer contract, whenever it comes

Status: Scheduled Entities: JPMorgan Chase; Goldman Sachs Bank USA; Apple Track: The Mask

Roughly twenty-four months from the January 2026 announcement. More than $20 billion of balances expected to move without reapplication. Apple says the details will arrive closer to transition. The window walks toward the Cancer Spine eclipse of 2028-07-22 — the same eclipse that sits under the GENIUS Act's 2028 guillotine.

Source: Apple Card transition FAQ
