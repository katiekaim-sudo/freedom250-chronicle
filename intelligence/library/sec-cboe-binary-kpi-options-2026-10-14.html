What did the SEC approve for Cboe binary KPI options, and which clearing and first-trade gates remain?

SEC approves Cboe options on company performance figures

The SEC approved Cboe options that pay $1 or $0 on reported company performance figures; clearing approval and first trading remain separate.

What we found

  • The October 13 SEC order approved Cboe’s amended exchange rule and designated the contracts standardized options. The rule names 23 eligible stock issuers and their individual company measures, but Cboe has not shown which series actually trade.
  • Each call ordinarily pays $1 if a named measure in the issuer’s SEC earnings filing meets or beats its strike; a put pays $1 below it. The issuer’s press release can set the timing, but the SEC filing sets the settlement figure.
  • A late but expected filing can delay settlement. If the KPI ultimately will not be reported, CCUS may use a reliable last closing option price as an alternative amount; a later restatement does not reopen completed settlement.
  • The limit is 25,000 standard-option equivalents per class and expiration, with 100 binary contracts counting as one. Cboe also plans fully funded positions, OPRA/CAT reporting and insider-trading surveillance, while its usual obvious-error rule will not apply.
  • Cboe wants its affiliate CCUS to clear the contracts even though OCC has an approved binary-options risk model. That would add a second securities-options clearer and requires separate SEC action. MEMX and NYSE American have competing proposals; the SEC confined its jurisdiction finding to Cboe’s particular structure.

Why it matters

The order joins the Observatory’s SEC–CFTC turn, clearing-house transition and official-record story. The larger fight is over who owns a company-event market, who clears it, whether rival contracts can share risk and liquidity, and which issuer filing finally moves cash.

What to watch

  • pending · Cboe post-clearer rule, accepted options disclosure, final series and Robinhood availability
  • pending · First trade, open interest and settlement, including any delayed, missing or restated KPI
  • pending · MEMX and NYSE American dispositions, any OCC KPI-clearing filing, and evidence of cross-venue fungibility or fragmented margin
