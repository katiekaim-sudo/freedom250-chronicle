## Four legal and operating objects—not one

### 1. Original Section 1286 proposal infrastructure

Section 1286 created the research-security initiative, foreign-institution and
talent-program list, proposal policy and continuing-award duties. Its
proposal-specific process gives a proposer suspected of noncompliance at least
30 days to remedy and provides an appeal route for a negative proposal
decision informed by the list.

The August audit/reporting demand is not identified as that statutory proposal
cure. The 14-day/30-day mismatch alone neither waives the proposal protections
nor proves the institutional demand unlawful.

### 2. The FY2025/FY2026 DoW grant-or-contract limitation

[Public Law 118-159 § 238](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PLAW-118publ159),
extended through FY2026 by
[Public Law 119-60 § 215](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PLAW-119publ60),
limits a particular DoW grant or contract for specified fundamental-research
collaboration with a foreign institution or employee on the Section 1286(c)(9)
list. A case-specific § 238 national-security waiver is available.

That is not automatically an institution-wide or government-wide exclusion.

### 3. The separate Confucius Institute restriction

[DFARS 252.209-7011](https://www.acquisition.gov/dfars/252.209-7011-representation-restriction-use-certain-institutions-higher-education.)
implements the institution-level DoW funding restriction for a U.S. institution
hosting a functionally defined covered institute. The definition can reach
rebranded arrangements; the name alone does not decide the test. Amounts paid
directly to students as educational assistance are excepted.

Waiver authority ends October 1, 2026, and no waiver applies on or after that
date. This is a real adjacent clock, but the release does not say the 30
institutions presently host covered institutes or hold waivers.

### 4. The January operating program

The
[January 7 implementation memorandum](https://www.cto.mil/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Fundamental-Research-Security-Initiatives.pdf)
orders annual spot checks of all mitigated fundamental-research awards and at
least 25 percent of the remainder, semiannual enforcement reporting, a shared
risk-review repository, broader support disclosures, damage assessments and
automated-vetting study.

The August action fits that operating architecture. The release does not cite a
specific memo paragraph as the audit authority or publish the audit rubric. The
2026 proposal Decision Matrix is context, not an audit instrument whose
five-year lookbacks can be imported silently.

## Identity firewall

No recipient name enters the cohort without a Department list, recipient
notice, institutional acknowledgment, court or congressional filing, or later
Department disposition directly identifying it.

Do not back-fill the 30 from:

- the press-reported 33-school professional-military-education list—a different
  review whose official memorandum does not publish those 33;

  investigated institutions are a subset of the 21, not an additive route to

- award volume.

## Funding boundary

The release uses broad “future federal research funding” language. The mapped
legal mechanisms directly bind DoW funds. The public record contains no
government-wide debarment, SAM exclusion, OMB/OSTP directive or NSF, NIH or DOE
action automatically propagating this Department decision.

Keep the sequence intact:

`notice → institution response → Department finding → mitigation decision →
eligibility disposition → identified award action → obligation/payment effect
→ review or finality`

No later state may be inferred from the one before it.
