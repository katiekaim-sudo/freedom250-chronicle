Treasury–Fed institutional money state — factual transition timeline

Updated: 2026-08-02 Status: working factual spine, first full institutional sweep
Primary period: 2025–2026
Companion records: Federal Reserve Restructuring — Factual Timeline · Clearing-House Transition — Factual Timeline · Federal Reserve Financial Constitution — Source Document Institutional control map: Treasury and IRS — Entity and Authority Map

Purpose

This timeline records how the U.S. institutions that issue, borrow, spend, collect, regulate, supervise, insure, clear, settle, audit, sanction, or approve money and money-like claims changed during the current transition.

It is deliberately broader than the Department of the Treasury and the Federal Reserve. An entity is in scope if it can materially affect at least one of these functions:

  • sovereign borrowing or debt management;
  • federal receipts, disbursements, or the Treasury General Account;
  • currency, coin, cash, checks, or electronic payment rails;
  • reserve balances, master accounts, payment accounts, or central-bank credit;
  • open-market operations, repo, Treasury-market structure, or collateral;
  • bank, credit-union, stablecoin, securities, derivatives, or insurance regulation;
  • chartering or approving an institution that creates or moves money-like claims;
  • deposit or share insurance;
  • anti-money-laundering, sanctions, financial intelligence, or asset forfeiture;
  • financial-stability monitoring, systemic designation, data standards, or audit oversight;
  • clearing, settlement, depository, or tokenization approvals that connect to the federal money system.

Factual rules

  • Entries state dated public actions, rules, filings, approvals, launches, speeches, meetings, or formally announced deadlines.
  • Occurred means the dated action happened. Proposed means it remained subject to approval. Scheduled is a future deadline. Public position is not adopted policy.
  • Time is included only when the source states it. Otherwise it is not publicly stated.
  • Place distinguishes a legal/regulatory locus from a distributed technical operation. Washington, D.C. is used for a federal rule or order; the relevant Reserve Bank city is used for a regional-bank action; distributed payment launches are identified as such.
  • A proposed charting anchor is a convention, not a historical fact. Exact filing, vote, operation, or speech times should supersede a noon chart.
  • System-wide Federal Reserve actions are not repeated twelve times. Each Reserve Bank receives its own entry when it acts independently, operates a distinct facility, approves an account, or publishes a materially distinct position.
  • Routine rate decisions, ordinary Treasury auctions, ordinary sanctions packages, and ordinary enforcement cases are excluded unless they change institutional authority, operating rails, approved claim types, or the architecture of the transition.
