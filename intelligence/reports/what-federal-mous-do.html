What federal MOUs do, and where their power stops

Question: What does a memorandum of understanding do within or between federal agencies, and what makes its commitments matter?

Answer: An MOU records how offices will use powers they already have together: who leads, supplies staff or data, performs work, pays, keeps records, and resolves disputes. Its title does not tell us enough about its legal effect. Read the authority, operative clauses, signers, funding instrument, and implementation record. A coordination MOU can change daily government work even when it creates no right for an outsider to sue.

Within an agency versus between agencies

Intra-agency means between components of one agency; interagency means between agencies. Treasury–IRS is a department/component relationship; Treasury–DOJ crosses departments. A federal–state agreement adds a separate government and its own authority. These are different institutional relationships even if all use the title MOU.

The Economy Act expressly covers goods or services ordered from a major organizational unit in the same agency or another agency. It requires available funds, a government-interest determination, capacity to perform, and the required commercial-source comparison. An authorized order obligates the ordering appropriation, with deobligation rules for work not timely obligated by the performing agency. 31 U.S.C. §1535.

Five distinct jobs an agreement may do

Job What to read for What the signature alone does not prove
Coordinate work Lead office, responsibilities, referral path, meetings, deadlines, escalation A transferred statutory function or completed work
Supply services or staff Actual service order, personnel authority, costs, period, acceptance A lawful detail or reimbursable obligation merely because cooperation was promised
Share data Permitted records, purpose, disclosure authority, access, security, retention, correction and redisclosure Permission to disclose every record held by either signer
Set operating procedures Technical annex, delivery schedule, data schema, access approval, responsible official A live connection or actual exchange
Move or reimburse money Applicable statutory authority, appropriation, signed order, amount, actual-cost adjustment An appropriation, unrestricted transfer, or payment

For interagency acquisition under the Economy Act, FAR 17.502-2 requires the prescribed determination and findings; more specific statutory authority takes precedence. The FAR does not apply to every interagency business transaction: the cited provision distinguishes transactions that do not result in a contract or order. Intra-agency procedures are addressed by agency regulations. FAR 17.502-2.

USGS's domestic-MOU policy is a useful agency-specific example: it treats its MOUs as formal but non-legally-binding instruments and still requires a clear legal authority. Do not turn that agency's convention into a universal legal rule for every federal instrument. USGS Survey Manual 500.26.

Data permission deserves its own read

An agreement does not override the Privacy Act, tax-return confidentiality, a court order, or program-specific disclosure limits. For a matching program covered by the Privacy Act, §552a(o) prescribes written-agreement terms, including purpose, authority, records, verification, retention and security. Section (p) addresses verification, notice and an opportunity to contest before covered adverse benefit action, subject to its statutory rules. Not every database comparison is a covered matching program; definitions and exclusions must be checked first. This is not a ruling that those provisions govern the California voter case. 5 U.S.C. §552a.

What this adds to our existing fraud work

The September agreement study already found a concrete example: Ohio's published corporate-record MOU provides a data-use framework, leaves schedules and schemas to later instruments, and does not transfer funds. That is a federal–state specimen, not the text of the federal NFDC Charter or an unseen agreement with Florida.

The NFDC Charter study kept signatory, member, data-sharing partner, personnel commitment and operating capability separate. That remains the right test. An MOU can supply a missing handoff between agencies without merging them, and it can enable substantial change without changing their founding statutes.

For each future instrument, record: named parties → legal authority → exact duties → data or money permitted → conditions → implementation → result. GAO's collaboration framework also makes leadership, resources, roles, accountability and written agreements practical tests of whether cooperation works. GAO-23-105520.
