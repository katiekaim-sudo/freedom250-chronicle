Carbon Nanotubes, Batteries and U.S. Sovereignty

Research cutoff: 2026-08-23
State: pending workbench research; not vault canon

Short answer

Carbon nanotubes can help batteries, but not in the way the loudest headlines imply. In the most credible near-term use, a very small amount of nanotubes forms a long-range electronic and mechanical network through an electrode. That network can:

  • reduce the amount of conventional carbon black needed in a cathode and leave more electrode volume for active material;
  • keep silicon particles electrically connected as they swell and contract;
  • improve conductivity through thick or high-loading electrodes; and
  • make some dry-electrode or low-solvent manufacturing routes more plausible.

This is an enabling-material thesis, not a new battery-chemistry thesis. CNTs do not create lithium, replace the cathode or store much of the cell's energy in ordinary lithium-ion designs. Their value is disproportionate to their mass because the connected network can change the usable fraction and manufacturability of the rest of the electrode.

The sovereignty contribution is real but conditional. CNTs can indirectly reduce graphite dependence by helping higher-silicon anodes survive, and they can support denser domestic electrode production. But an imported or foreign-controlled CNT powder and dispersion recipe can simply substitute one choke point for another. The Freedom 250 acceptance test is therefore not "nanotubes made in America." It is:

qualified, origin-compliant CNT material or dispersion, produced under controlled EHS conditions, incorporated into accepted electrodes and delivered to specification on a repeatable clock.

What the evidence establishes

1. The near-term case is already more than speculative

DOE/ORNL researchers demonstrated the most conventional version of the thesis in aqueous NMC532 full pouch cells: an MWCNT network delivered roughly order-of-magnitude-higher electrode conductivity, 99.4-percent capacity retention after 200 cycles versus 94.6 percent for carbon black, an increase in active fraction from 90 to 96 weight percent and a calculated 11.7-percent energy-density gain. It is credible pouch evidence, but still needs current large-format, long-duration, safety, cost and factory-yield confirmation. ORNL publication record

The strongest independent U.S. public evidence in this pass is Argonne National Laboratory's 2024 CAMP benchmarking. It evaluated commercial multi-wall CNT dispersions under a realistic-materials program and reported that 0.1 weight percent MWCNT improved NMC811 rate performance; the same program found that dispersion quality materially changed silicon-electrode initial coulombic efficiency. The tests were USABC-derived 2032 coin cells, so they establish a useful mechanism and formulation result—not automotive cell or pack qualification. Argonne CAMP 2024 Annual Merit Review

Argonne-affiliated work using a commercial OCSiAl single-wall CNT additive also showed that a fraction of a weight percent can create an effective conductive network in high-silicon anodes. A graphite-free SiO design reported higher volumetric energy than the paper's graphite control in coin full cells. The result supports the graphite-substitution mechanism, but its cell format and test conditions do not establish commercial yield, calendar life or pack-level energy. Journal of Power Sources 2020

The use is also present in production. artience reports that its LIOACCUM CNT dispersion has shipped in lithium-ion batteries for Toyota hybrid vehicles and that its North American operation had two customers as of March 2026. The company does not assign that customer state or throughput between Georgia and Kentucky; Georgia production is established while Kentucky remains unresolved. Those are supplier disclosures rather than an independent teardown, but a named vehicle/customer chain is materially stronger than a laboratory claim. 2. Dispersion and qualification are as strategic as the tube

Raw CNTs agglomerate. A nominally excellent powder can underperform or damage electrode yield if the tubes are shortened, bundled, contaminated or poorly distributed during mixing. The commercial object is therefore often a water-based or NMP-based dispersion, not loose CNT powder. The associated recipe controls viscosity, electrode homogeneity, binder compatibility and customer process fit.

This creates a hidden acceptance economy:

hydrocarbon + catalyst
  -> CNT synthesis
  -> purification and morphology control
  -> dispersion / masterbatch formulation
  -> electrode mixing, coating or dry processing
  -> cell validation
  -> customer qualification
  -> recurring accepted delivery

No single upstream capacity number proves that the chain is sovereign. A plant can synthesize powder but lack qualified dispersion. A U.S. formulator can have customer validation while depending on undisclosed foreign powder. A foreign-owned U.S. plant can improve geographic resilience while leaving ownership, IP and governance exposure intact.

3. The United States has pieces of the chain, not yet a proven sovereign stack

The clearest U.S. pieces found in this pass are:

  • artience / LioChem — operating CNT-dispersion production in Georgia, with two North American customers disclosed across the company's Georgia/Kentucky footprint as of March 2026; the Kentucky site's current throughput is not established and the company reported impairment after customer delays;
  • Black Diamond Structures — Austin, Texas dispersion/formulation capacity, with its lithium-ion products described by the company as in final commercial validation; the source of its upstream CNT powder is not publicly resolved;
  • CHASM Advanced Materials — a 50-tonne-per-year CNT pilot reactor in Norman, Oklahoma, with a 1,500-tonne commercial reactor still a future build; and
  • Cnano USA — a large Kansas project intended to make CNT powder and paste, but current commissioned-output and customer-acceptance evidence remained unresolved in this pass; the Chinese parent and a congressional CFIUS request make geography and control visibly different questions.

Cabot's proposed Michigan project is strategically important because the company described it as the first U.S. commercial-scale battery-grade CNT and dispersion facility. But the public object located here is a September 2024 DOE selection for award negotiation, not proof of final award, completed construction, commissioning or customer-qualified output. Cabot's current company material still identifies a CNT plant in Zhuhai, China. Cabot selection announcement Cabot technology centers

By contrast, disclosed operating scale is clearer abroad: LG Chem lists three MWCNT plants in Yeosu, South Korea totaling 2,900 tonnes per year; OCSiAl says its Serbia SWCNT facility has 60 tonnes per year of synthesis capacity; Arkema identifies up to 400 tonnes per year at its French MWCNT facility. Tube type, purity, formulation and usable battery grade are not interchangeable, so those numbers must not be added into one undifferentiated capacity total. Layer U.S. posture at cutoff Sovereignty ruling
CNT science and cell testing strong public-lab and company R&D real competence
battery dispersion/formulation operating U.S. capacity and some named customers useful but powder origin/control can remain opaque
commercial-scale battery-grade powder pilots and projects; complete current domestic scale not proven material gap
cell/electrode qualification company-specific and mostly private pivotal hidden gate

Why this matters to Freedom 250

Focused

The most plausible mission is not "lead the nanotechnology market." It is to qualify CNT networks for specific battery bottlenecks:

  1. high-silicon anodes that reduce graphite intensity;
  2. low-loading cathode conductive networks for higher active-material fraction;
  3. thick or dry electrodes whose yield and conductivity are otherwise poor; and
  4. defense, grid-storage and mobility cells that require a domestically controllable formulation.

Resilient

Resilience requires at least two qualified powder and dispersion routes, with one domestic route and one trusted-allied route, plus interchangeable test methods. A second street address using the same foreign powder, reactor IP or single customer-approved recipe is not full redundancy.

Agile

The transition bottleneck is customer qualification. Argonne CAMP is a useful independent bridge, but public programs need to carry candidates from powder characterization through high-loading multilayer cells, manufacturing yield, calendar/fast-charge/safety testing and named transition users. Laboratory capacity without an electrode-to-customer acceptance route is not agility.

Secure

Security includes ownership, process IP, precursor/catalyst origin, cyber and trade-secret controls, product consistency and worker protection. A policy that funds only nominal tonnes while ignoring these control surfaces can create capacity without technological sovereignty.

Bounded policy path

The best first program is a qualification-and-control demonstration, not a blanket nanotube subsidy:

  1. Select two use cases: a high-silicon anode and a high-loading cathode.
  2. Require two source paths: domestic plus trusted-allied, with powder and dispersion origin disclosed.
  3. Fund the whole acceptance chain: synthesis/purification, dispersion, high-loading electrodes, multilayer cells, safety and production-yield runs.
  4. Measure the right outputs: qualified litres/tonnes delivered, accepted electrode rolls, customer-approved cells, yield, exposure and recurring delivery—not announced nameplate.
  5. Make EHS part of the design: favor contained production and stable dispersions where technically appropriate; use NIOSH's 1 microgram per cubic metre respirable elemental-carbon REL as the federal worker-protection baseline rather than treating nanomaterial handling as ordinary carbon powder. NIOSH CIB 65
  6. Refresh metrology: NIST's former SWCNT reference materials were discontinued in 2021–2022 even though the agency identifies material polydispersity and catalyst impurities as commercialization problems. A battery-relevant reference and interlaboratory protocol would improve procurement and supplier substitution. NIST CNT reference materials

DOE's Battery Materials Processing and Battery Manufacturing and Recycling programs are the most obvious existing industrial route; the 2026 round already uses selections to support silicon-anode and dry-electrode projects. CNTs should be treated as an enabling battery component where a project proves a domestic acceptance object—not assumed to qualify merely because the word "nano" appears in a proposal. What would change the ruling

The U.S. stack moves from pieces to credible sovereign capability only when public evidence can connect:

  • commissioned domestic battery-grade CNT powder output;
  • disclosed domestic or trusted origin for essential precursors and process control;
  • statistically controlled purity, morphology and dispersion specifications;
  • at least two qualified suppliers or demonstrably transferable recipes;
  • full-cell and manufacturing evidence at practical loading and format;
  • named customer acceptance and recurring delivery; and
  • verified exposure controls and waste/recycling handling.

Until then, CNTs are a high-leverage sovereignty opportunity with a real commercial foothold—but not yet a closed U.S. supply chain.
