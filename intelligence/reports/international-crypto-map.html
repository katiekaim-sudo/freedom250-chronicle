Settlement and Control Synthesis

In plain English

Question: In each country, who has authority over crypto, what is legally allowed, which rails actually operate, and who bears the loss when it fails?

Answer: Across 22 jurisdictions, we found no verified cross-border payment route that depends on crypto from end to end. The token layer still settles through ordinary banks and national payment systems.

Evidence checked through: 2026-10-03T09:30:00-04:00

The detailed record below keeps the legal wording, source limits and open questions.

Question

Do the observed systems move crypto-native money, or do they place token and ledger interfaces over conventional commercial-bank and central-bank settlement? Where do the controlling legal persons, authoritative records, final-money anchors and terminal losses actually sit?

Answer

The dominant observed architecture is a token interface over a conventional legal claim and conventional bank or central-bank settlement.

This does not mean the token layer is decorative. It can carry transfer state, programmability, access controls, custody, collateral status and transaction history. But in most mapped systems it does not independently determine all of the following: who owes the holder, what assets back the claim, which record is legally authoritative, when interbank money is final, how an error is corrected or who bears a shortfall.

Three materially different categories remain:

  1. Crypto-native assets such as Bitcoin can settle their own chain state without an issuer redemption promise. They are not thereby sovereign money, a bank deposit or a claim on a reserve pool. Legal ownership, custody, exchange balances, fiat entry and insolvency still depend on persons and non-chain records.
  2. Tokenised private claims—stablecoins, e-money, tokenised deposits and tokenised securities—retain an issuer, bank, trust, custodian or securities debtor. Their token transfer may be technically final while redemption, delivery, registry and cash settlement remain incomplete.
  3. CBDC or tokenised central-bank money can be a direct central-bank liability. The Eurosystem's Pontes interface is now launched for wholesale tokenized-asset settlement in central-bank money, while many other reviewed systems remain pilot, controlled or trial-bounded. Launch and participant readiness do not themselves prove a completed transaction. A technical representation of a reserve balance remains governed by central-bank access, operating and finality rules.

The map therefore cannot use “on-chain,” “tokenised,” “instant,” “settled” or “regulated” as synonyms for final sovereign money.

1 October recertification: three live architectures

The country and major-bank waves add three materially different network architectures. They reinforce, rather than displace, the control model above.

Swift bank-token ledger

Swift says most of its seventeen first-mover banks have used the ledger for 24/7 real-time tokenised-value payments in five currencies. Its July architecture statement is the controlling boundary: participating banks issue tokenised deposits on their own ledgers, the Swift layer orchestrates their movement and final settlement occurs through existing systems. Swift does not thereby become the deposit debtor or the holder of customer funds. Until the transaction-level record identifies the issuing bank, receiving bank, settlement system and beneficiary credit, token moved and bank money became final are separate propositions.

Project Agorá

Agorá's thirty July transactions used real tokenised reserves and deposits, not dummy value. Yet issuance was triggered through dedicated RTGS funding, redemption returned value through external systems, SIX operated predefined test windows and manual approvals and fallback procedures remained material. The approximately eighty-second average therefore measures the controlled prototype interval, not an open production service level. The test shows that central-bank and commercial-bank claims can share programmable execution; it does not merge the two liabilities or eliminate their separate authoritative records.

OUSD and the distribution layer

OUSD is a live private issuer claim, not a bank deposit or Federal Reserve liability. Bridge is identified as issuer; BlackRock, Lead Bank and BNY have stated reserve roles; and Coinbase, Mastercard/BVNK, Stripe and Visa provide integration or distribution paths. The token contract, issuer books, reserve accounts, partner wallets and downstream bank ledgers are therefore distinct records. A partner can distribute or settle with the token without becoming the token debtor. At launch, promised monthly attestations had not yet produced a completed OUSD report, so technical launch did not close the reserve, redemption or stress-evidence gates.

TCH On-Chain Money

The Clearing House has selected Quant to build a future tokenized-deposit network connected to RTP and CHIPS, with availability expected in the first half of 2027. This design would move exact member-bank deposit claims; it would not create one TCH-issued deposit. Legacy RTP or CHIPS operating scale and finality cannot be assigned to the new network until its rulebook defines the authoritative record, discharge point, connection to each existing rail, exception handling and loss allocation.

Where the controlling legal person sits

The token contract is often not the controlling legal person.

  • For a stablecoin, the controlling obligor is the exact issuer named by law and contract. Distributor, exchange, wallet, reserve custodian and group parent remain separate.
  • For a tokenised deposit, the issuing bank is the debtor. The platform, blockchain and technology provider do not replace the bank.
  • For e-money, the e-money issuer owes redemption while the safeguarding bank holds assets under a different relationship.
  • For a tokenised security, the security issuer and legally authoritative registrar, CSD or DLT venue control distinct parts of title and settlement.
  • For a CBDC, the central bank is the monetary issuer, while a commercial bank or PSP may operate the customer interface.
  • For native crypto, there may be no issuer at all. Control can fragment among protocol governance, key holder, custodian, exchange, bridge and court.
  • After insolvency, the controlling person can change: a trustee, liquidator, administrator or court becomes the practical gateway to the claim.

Where finality actually sits

At least four finalities can coexist:

  1. chain finality — the network will not ordinarily reorganize the token transfer;
  2. platform finality — the exchange or intermediary posts an internal balance;
  3. legal title or claim finality — the authoritative register or governing law recognizes ownership, entitlement or debt;
  4. final-money settlement — commercial-bank obligations are discharged in central-bank money or another contractually final asset.

A transaction is not fully mapped until the relevant clocks are joined. A final token transfer can coexist with failed fiat delivery. Final RTGS cash can coexist with a disputed token entitlement. A blockchain receipt can coexist with an insolvent custodian. A court-confirmed claim can coexist with no assets available for distribution.

Terminal loss geography

When all technical and legal corrections are exhausted, loss normally lands in one or more of these places:

  • the holder of an unbacked native asset or inaccessible key;
  • the customer with only an unsecured claim against an exchange or lender;
  • the stablecoin reserve pool and then the holder/issuer estate if the pool is deficient;
  • the issuing bank and its statutory resolution/deposit-protection framework for a true deposit claim;
  • the custodian or safeguarding estate when property cannot be attributed or segregated;
  • the market participant or default waterfall under securities/clearing rules;
  • the payer, payee, intermediary or scheme under payment-error and fraud rules; or
  • the taxpayer/central bank only where a statute, public liability or explicit guarantee actually places the loss there.

No reviewed private stablecoin becomes publicly guaranteed merely because its reserve assets sit at a bank, its token is regulated, or its redemption leg ultimately reaches an RTGS system.
