Fedwire participant payment or securities-transfer instruction

Send and process instructions

Legal basis: Fedwire Funds: Regulation J and OC 6. Fedwire Securities: applicable book-entry securities regulations and OC 7.

When it applies: A Fedwire participant sends a Funds payment order or Securities transfer message under the applicable service’s legal framework.

Named firms: None named yet

Who can use it: Fedwire Funds participant or authorized sender under its service arrangement; Fedwire Securities participant; authorized service provider acting through the participant's applicable Fedwire arrangement

ConditionsIdentify the eligible participant, its Reserve Bank account or securities account, and the exact Fedwire service. Apply the service's authorization/security procedure, message format, account, asset, and cutoff conditions. For securities, establish Fedwire eligibility and any transfer-against-payment conditions; for funds, establish a payment order accepted under the applicable procedure.

The limitThis is a Fedwire-specific authorization route. A wallet signature, API call, or message on another system does not inherit Fedwire authority or establish legal effect for a different claim.

Sources (2)
  • Federal Reserve Operating Circular 6 and Regulation J Fedwire Funds route · effective
  • Federal Reserve Operating Circular 7 Fedwire Securities route · effective

FedLine electronic-access and designated service-provider route

Carry messages across systems

Legal basis: Operating Circular 5 and the relevant Fedwire service terms; the Fedwire form route expressly contemplates designation of a third-party service provider.

When it applies: A Reserve Bank institution or authorized service provider accesses an identified Reserve Bank electronic service through the applicable OC 5 and service terms.

Named firms: None named yet

Who can use it: Reserve Bank service participant; FedLine subscriber; Reserve Bank-authorized API service provider; designated Fedwire third-party service provider

ConditionsIdentify the institution, electronic connection, access credentials, and exact Reserve Bank service. Recover the applicable OC 5/service terms and any third-party service arrangement or API terms. Establish separately the participant that has authority to originate the payment or securities instruction and the service-specific acceptance/finality event.

The limitThis is an authorized Reserve Bank electronic-access route, not a general interoperability license. Electronic transport does not make the provider the account holder, sender, securities participant, title authority, or settlement obligor.

Sources (2)
  • Federal Reserve Operating Circular 5 electronic access and API terms · 2024-10-28
  • Fedwire Services third-party service arrangement and authorization forms · accessed 2

Registered transfer agent using DLT in its official record

Operate networks and validate updates

Legal basis: SEC staff DLT FAQ Q10–Q11, Exchange Act transfer-agent framework, and the transfer agent's existing applicable recordkeeping and safeguarding duties.

When it applies: A registered transfer agent uses DLT for all or part of its official master securityholder file for a covered security.

Named firms: None named yet

Who can use it: SEC-registered transfer agent; bank transfer agent registered with the appropriate federal banking regulator

ConditionsIdentify the registered transfer agent, covered security, issuer appointment, and official master securityholder file. Establish how the on-chain and off-chain components together remain secure, accurate, current, producible, and retained as required. Recover the applicable correction, reconciliation, fork, access, and vendor-control procedures.

The limitThe FAQ does not provide a standalone legal-effect route for a generic network operator, validator, sequencer, bridge, or protocol-governance group. Their legal effect remains a separate arrangement-specific question.

  • SEC staff FAQ on registered transfer agents' use of distributed ledger technology · accessed 2

Technology vendor or outsourced infrastructure under accountable-client controls

Supply software and infrastructure

Legal basis: Service contract plus the accountable client's applicable statutory and regulatory obligations. For supervised banking organizations, SR 23-4 gives risk-management guidance for third-party relationships and explicitly creates no new requirements.

When it applies: A regulated or contractually accountable firm uses a vendor, linked ledger, database, wallet system, or infrastructure provider to perform part of its operating model.

Named firms: Circle Technology Services, LLC (role documented); Arc Network Services LLC (role documented)

Who can use it: software vendor; cloud or infrastructure provider; ledger/database provider; wallet or key-management vendor; systems integrator; recordkeeping transfer agent using linked technical systems

ConditionsIdentify the client that retains the regulated or contractual duty. Recover the vendor agreement, data/control access, security, continuity, audit, incident, correction, and exit provisions. Test the vendor's own activity separately if it also performs a regulated function.

The limitVendor participation does not relocate a regulated client's recordkeeping, reporting, safeguarding, or examination duties, and it does not prove the vendor holds an independent regulatory permission. SR 23-4 is guidance, not a new vendor authorization rule.
