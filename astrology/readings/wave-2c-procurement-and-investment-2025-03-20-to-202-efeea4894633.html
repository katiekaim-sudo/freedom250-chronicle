Wave 2C — Procurement and Investment

Research window: Exact 2025 Aries ingress through exact 2025 Cancer ingress.
Question: Which Wave 1 review leashes became an actual buying or investment carrier, and which remained plans, preferences or promotional aggregates?
Finding: The Aries wave did not create one centralized economic organ. It assembled four different control surfaces: GSA was directed to receive proposals for common-goods procurement; senior procurement executives gained a written approval gate over non-commercial buying; OFPP and the FAR Council began moving the acquisition rulebook from regulation into agency deviations and nonbinding guides; and Commerce received a real office for shepherding investments above $1 billion. The strongest in-window operating receipts were the May 2 FAR implementation memo and the Accelerator's June 12 Micron permitting role. Wholesale GSA transfer, a completed FAR replacement, an identifiable sovereign wealth fund and a publicly reconciled investment ledger all remained unproved.

This packet keeps procurement execution, procurement regulation, investment facilitation and capital deployment separate. A central office is not a fund. A model deviation is not an agency buying rule until adopted. A proposed acquisition transfer is not a completed transfer. A corporate investment announcement is not a draw or construction receipt.

The first chain changes how the United States buys. The second changes how a large investor navigates the United States. Neither automatically supplies money.

2C.1 — March 20: GSA consolidation begins as a proposal architecture

Mover. The President, OMB, GSA, agency heads and agency senior procurement officials.

Institutional change. EO 14240 directed agencies, within 60 days, to propose common-goods and services procurement that GSA could conduct where law permitted. It directed GSA to produce a 90-day government-wide plan and OMB to designate GSA as executive agent for government-wide IT acquisition contracts, while preserving a continuity-based ability to defer or decline particular designations.

Maturity at the Aries boundary. Direction and design only. The order was signed on the ingress civil date, but no public signing time resolves whether it preceded or followed the exact ingress. It therefore remains an unresolved boundary object for strict astrology placement.

Later receipt and handoff. OMB M-25-31, issued June 21, 2025, supplied the consolidation suitability test just after the Cancer boundary: mission-agnostic, standardized and highly commercial requirements were most suitable; complex, customized and mission-critical requirements were less suitable. GSA later established the Office of Centralized Acquisition Services and described agencies as retaining requirements, oversight and performance management while GSA centralizes acquisition execution for designated contracts.

Counterevidence and limit. The order did not transfer every agency buying office, organic authority or contract. The later implementation model itself preserves agency program responsibility. GSA's host or executive-agent role is not a new statutory parent for the agencies it serves.

Plotline. Centralization first appears as a membrane around execution: the mission owner still says what it needs, but the buying hand may move to GSA.

2C.2 — March 31: the Investment Accelerator becomes a Commerce carrier, not a fund

Mover. The President, Commerce Secretary, Treasury Secretary, White House economic-policy staff, an Executive Director, the CHIPS Program Office, investors, permitting agencies, national laboratories and states.

Institutional change. EO 14255 ordered Commerce to establish the U.S. Investment Accelerator within 30 days. The office was assigned legal, transactional, operational and support staff; responsibility for investments above $1 billion; regulatory navigation; lawful burden reduction; access to national resources; national-laboratory collaboration; work with all 50 states; and responsibility for the CHIPS Program Office.

Maturity. A real office and decision path, not a sovereign wealth fund, appropriation, investment account or transaction vehicle. The order moved coordination and casework into Commerce but did not move other agencies' statutory permitting or national-security authorities.

June 12 operating receipt. Commerce announced Micron's approximately $200 billion U.S. manufacturing and R&D plan and said the Accelerator would provide “white glove” service to expedite permitting. The same release noted that up to $6.165 billion in CHIPS direct funding had been awarded on December 10, 2024. That older award is not new Accelerator money. The receipt proves named casework and a live facilitation role; it does not prove permits issued, $200 billion deployed, or a new federal outlay.

Later confirmation and limit. Commerce's FY2027 request places the Accelerator inside Departmental Management with a requested $100 million and 75 FTE. A request is not an appropriation, staffing census or operating capacity. The public claim that the administration had “secured more than $3 trillion” in private investment has no published constituent ledger or reconciliation rule in the research reservoir; it remains a promotional aggregate, not the Accelerator's balance sheet.

Plotline. The state does not become the investor. It becomes the navigator and negotiator standing between capital, permits, public resources and inherited agencies.

2C.3 — April 15: custom buying acquires a written permission gate

Mover. Agency senior procurement executives, contracting officers, OMB and OFPP.

Institutional change. EO 14271 made commercial products and services the presumptive route “to the maximum extent practicable.” Open non-commercial solicitations had to be collected into applications supported by market research and price analysis. The agency's senior procurement executive could return, approve or deny the application in writing and could ask OMB/OFPP for a recommendation.

Maturity at Cancer ingress. An operative approval and reporting instruction, but the packet does not contain the agency application census, written approvals, denials, changed solicitations or award receipts due from that review. The stronger public government-wide implementation memo, M-26-12, arrived in April 2026.

Counterevidence and limit. The order did not forbid every custom system, transfer contracting authority to OMB, select a commercial vendor or prove savings. The senior procurement executive remains inside the agency; OMB's role is advisory when consulted.

Plotline. The new regime makes bespoke government capacity plead its case before it may enter the buying stream. The permission gate favors adaptation of an existing market object over construction of a unique public one.

2C.4 — April 15 to May 2: the rulebook splits into live deviation and future law

Mover. OFPP, the FAR Council, GSA, Defense, NASA, civilian agencies, contracting officers and vendors.

Institutional change. EO 14275 ordered the FAR reduced to provisions required by statute or necessary for usable, effective and secure procurement. It preserved the statutory FAR institution while directing interim deviations and later formal rules.

OMB M-25-26, issued May 2, supplied the operative split:

  • Phase I: FAR Council model deviation text, with agencies generally issuing their own deviations within 30 days; and
  • Phase II: later notice-and-comment rulemaking under 41 U.S.C. § 1707 after the model work.

The same memo created “Strategic Acquisition Guidance”: streamlined regulation plus buying guides. The guides were designed to hold techniques and practical pathways outside the binding FAR.

Maturity at Cancer ingress. The governance and implementation architecture was live. Agency points of contact were due May 1; model text, agency deviations, quarterly reports, training and later rulemaking were the carriers. The research shelf's decisive rule remains: a model is not an agency buying rule until the agency issues its class deviation, and a Companion guide is not a regulation.

Later body. Agencies subsequently adopted class deviations part by part. Those deviations—not the White House announcement, not the GSA-hosted webpage and not the nonmandatory Companion—became the operative rule for covered new solicitations. The first cited 2026 rulemaking package remained proposed, not final, at the evidence cutoff.

Counterevidence and limit. Title 41, the FAR Council's statutory duty, bid-protest forums and agency procurement authority continued. Moving “how-to” out of regulation expands discretion and can reduce public, protestable text; it does not repeal procurement law.

Plotline. The buying constitution is thinned while its interpretive practice moves into a less binding layer. The decision right shifts not only through who signs, but through which text can be enforced.

What changed during the Aries period

  • GSA became the proposed common-goods acquisition executor and government-wide IT-contract rationalizer.
  • Non-commercial buying acquired a senior written approval gate.
  • OFPP and the FAR Council acquired an explicit mandate to separate statutory/essential rules from guidance.
  • The implementation clock split into agency deviation now and formal rulemaking later.
  • Commerce acquired a named office for large-investment casework and responsibility for the CHIPS Program Office.
  • The Accelerator appeared in a named Micron permitting relationship before the Cancer ingress.

What did not change merely because the orders existed

  • Agency missions, statutes and all buying authority did not transfer to GSA.
  • A consolidation proposal was not a completed contract migration.
  • A commercial preference was not a vendor selection, award or savings receipt.
  • A model deviation did not bind an agency before adoption.
  • A Companion guide did not become the FAR.
  • The Investment Accelerator did not become a sovereign wealth fund, bank, permit authority or investment owner.
  • A $200 billion company plan did not prove $200 billion spent.
  • The administration's multi-trillion-dollar investment claim did not become a reconciled capital ledger.

Institutional theorem

Procurement and investment reveal a subtler form of centralization than the trade machine. The regime does not need to own every charter or dollar if it can determine:

  1. where a common purchase is executed;
  2. whether a custom public solution may be solicited;
  3. which rule text is binding and which practice sits outside regulation;
  4. who receives large-investor casework; and
  5. which inherited gate still has the final legal authority.

The result is a routing state. It organizes permission and navigation around legal persons that mostly remain intact.

Both poles

Constructive pole — a clearer route through a fragmented state

  • common purchases can be pooled where standardization genuinely works;
  • custom systems must show why the market cannot satisfy the need;
  • binding rules can be separated from practical guidance;
  • large investors receive an accountable federal navigator; and
  • agencies retain mission, statutory and protestable boundaries while execution becomes more coordinated.

Shadow pole — permission without a fully visible constitution

  • central execution can detach purchasing from mission expertise;
  • commercial preference can hollow public capacity and favor incumbent platforms;
  • mandatory protections can migrate into nonbinding guidance beyond the usual protest hook;
  • “white glove” casework can create unequal access to government; and
  • investment rhetoric can blur facilitation, award, private commitment, financing and deployed capital.

The evidence supports a new routing architecture. It does not support a single procurement corporation or a public investment fund.
