## 7A.1 — June 22–24: the threat becomes an inventory program

[EO 14412](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/25/2026-12909/securing-the-nation-against-advanced-cryptographic-attacks) directs agencies to migrate high-value and high-impact non-national-security systems to NIST-approved post-quantum cryptography. It distinguishes key establishment from digital signature and sets different hard use-targets:

- December 31, 2030 for PQC key establishment; and
- December 31, 2031 for PQC digital signatures.

The order also creates separate clocks for migration leads, agency inventories/plans, a NIST pilot, national-security-system reporting, a cryptographic bill of materials, module validation and FAR rules for covered contractors.

OMB issued [M-26-15](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/M-26-15-Execution-of-the-Migration-to-Post-Quantum-Cryptography.pdf) on June 24. That memo is the immediate operating receipt. It does not wait 90 days to begin the program. It specifies agency migration leadership, FICAM/PQC coordination, planning and implementation work under the Quantum Computing Cybersecurity Preparedness Act and the EO.

The important entity boundary is exact:

- the memo applies to “agency” under 44 U.S.C. § 3502(1), including the Board of Governors for its covered systems;
- it excludes national security systems, which follow the national-security/CNSA route; and
- Federal Reserve Banks and their shared payment-service operation are not silently converted into Board systems by that definition.

So this chain is not:

`PQC order → Fedwire is quantum-safe`

It is:

`covered federal system → inventory and accountable lead → plan → validated component → migration and testing → production cutover`

**Maturity by August 21.** The mandatory civilian migration program is live. Agency leads were due July 22, but no complete public roster was isolated. The FICAM/PQC working-group deadline is August 23, agency migration plans are due in October, and the pilot/FAR/CBOM clocks remain later. No named federal high-value system is proved cut over.

## 7A.2 — June 23: Quantum Genesis gives QC-ADDS a program name

[EO 14413](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/25/2026-12910/ushering-in-the-next-frontier-of-quantum-innovation) establishes the Quantum Computer for Application Development and Discovery Science effort. QC-ADDS is intended to deliver at least one scientifically relevant machine to a DOE facility, make it available to the scientific community where possible, create performance-assessment capability and explore private partnership and advance-market-commitment models.

DOE announced [Quantum Genesis](https://www.energy.gov/science/articles/energy-department-announces-initiative-create-and-deploy-worlds-first) on June 23 as a foundational Genesis Mission element and a step toward QC-ADDS. Its target is a fault-tolerant, scientifically relevant quantum computing capability by 2028.

That target is not a present machine. The correct maturity ladder is:

`initiative → technical specification → partnership / solicitation → award or commitment → integration → acceptance → access and use`

At the cutoff, the initiative and target are named. No final technical specification, selected QC-ADDS performer, signed delivery instrument, accepted machine or scientific use is established by the announcement alone.

## 7A.3 — June 23: quantum sensing receives a transition carrier

The Department of War and DIU announced a multi-phase quantum sensing and timing initiative one day after the EO. It identifies four lines of effort—electric-field sensors and magnetometers, gravity gradiometers, tactical clocks and component insertions—and describes anticipated investment of up to $200 million over the next year.

This is meaningful program intake and transition architecture. It is not a $200 million obligation or three deployed sensor systems. EO 14413 separately requires War to identify at least three next-generation sensor projects within 60 days and aims to field them by September 30, 2028. August 21 is the 60-day boundary; no conclusive public three-project disposition was isolated in this pass.

The War release also mislabels the quantum order as EO 14411. The title and content establish the intended EO 14413 relationship, but the number error must be preserved rather than silently treated as perfect metadata.

**Maturity.** Named transition initiative and capability lanes; future selection, instrument, funding, testing, acceptance and fielding.

## The trust-stack theorem

Quantum development and PQC migration are related but not the same program:

| Layer | Object | Function | Failure of overstatement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Threat / opportunity | scalable quantum compute and sensing | new scientific and security capability | announcement is not delivered capability |
| Standard | FIPS 203/204/205 and later validated modules | defines approved cryptographic destination | standard is not system migration |
| Identity / authorization | FPKI, FICAM, PIV/CAC, system credentials | binds a key and role to a record | signature does not prove the assertion is true |
| Transaction / provenance | award, certifying signature, scientific result, payment schedule | records who authorized what | cryptographic durability does not create legal authority or money |
| Migration | EO 14412 + M-26-15 plans and cutovers | preserves future verifiability | no named rail is covered without its own system evidence |

The connection to the monetary thesis is therefore deep but bounded. Cryptography is how a payment or eligibility system remembers that an authorized actor signed a particular instruction. PQC preserves that memory against a future mathematical break. It does not create the dollar, account, asset, legal entitlement or settlement finality that the signature records.

## Both poles

### Constructive pole — the archive survives the revolution

- each agency names an accountable migration owner and inventories hidden dependence;
- shared standards reduce fragmentation and make future audits possible;
- scientific and national-security systems gain new capability without sacrificing the integrity of old records;
- procurement and module validation turn abstract security into testable requirements; and
- a durable signature preserves who authorized an award, result or payment after the old algorithm is retired.

### Shadow pole — cryptographic certainty impersonates substantive truth

- an intact signature can make a false or unlawful assertion permanently attributable without making it true;
- secret inventories and classified benchmarks can concentrate technical authority;
- migration cost and interoperability failure can split the government into incompatible cryptographic eras;
- “quantum readiness” can become marketing before any system cutover; and
- the quantum/crypto vocabulary can be used to imply a sovereign token or payment system that neither order creates.

Both poles remain hypotheses. Named plans, components, contracts, cutovers, test results, incidents and accepted quantum capabilities determine what matures.
