Federal Reserve Operating Job And Money

Governed Research Desk file · evidence cutoff 2026-07-25

Open source textBack to summary

Federal Reserve — actual job and operating story

Status: pending workbench research; not yet promoted to the vault
Updated: July 25, 2026
Evidence cutoff: official records available through July 25, 2026
Scope: the Federal Reserve's stated mission, legal and operating actors, day-to-day work, public workload measures, staffing proxies and the relationship between functional budgets and audited 2025 uses of money
Dollar convention: every monetary amount is written in full nominal dollars. Amounts expanded from source-rounded figures do not gain additional precision.

This is a factual operating map. It does not assume that every stated function is performed efficiently or that the disclosed expense is necessary. It first reconstructs the Federal Reserve's own institutional story so that later questions about cost, control, duplication and recipient concentration begin from the correct entities and accounting objects.

I. Bottom line

The Federal Reserve's actual claimed job is much broader than setting an interest rate and printing money.

The Fed describes five public functions:

  1. conduct monetary policy;
  2. promote financial-system stability;
  3. supervise and regulate financial institutions and activities;
  4. foster payment-and-settlement-system safety and efficiency; and
  5. promote consumer protection and community development.

Its operating arms also perform a major sixth service lane that is partly nested inside the payment function:

  1. act as banker and fiscal agent for the United States Treasury and other government entities.

Underneath those public functions is a large support structure: economics and market staff, bank examiners, payment operators, cash operations, software and cybersecurity teams, lawyers, accountants, facilities, procurement, human resources, pensions and shared-service organizations.

The cleanest first conclusion is:

The policy decision is small; the implementation, supervision, settlement, fiscal-agent and information infrastructure is large.

But a second conclusion is just as important:

The enormous monetary-balance-sheet flows do not measure what it costs to run that infrastructure.

In 2025, the 12 combined Reserve Banks recognized $167,381,000,000 of interest expense on reserve, deposit and repo liabilities, compared with $9,800,000,000 of operating expenses and assessments. The interest expense was the consequence of the monetary-policy implementation framework. It was not a payroll, building, software, research or grant budget.

II. The three clocks used in this map

The public record does not yet provide one 2025 table that allocates every actual dollar by function. This map therefore keeps three different clocks visible:

The 2025 budget is an authorization ceiling, not proof of spending. The 2025 audited statements are actual accounting records, but their expense classes do not provide the same function-by-function detail as the budget review.

Board of Governors

The Board is a federal agency with separate audited books. It:

  • participates in monetary-policy formulation;
  • writes and administers regulations;
  • supervises large and other covered institutions with the Reserve Banks;
  • oversees the Reserve Banks and major payment systems;
  • approves Reserve Bank budgets and certain major capital projects;
  • assesses the Reserve Banks for Board operations, capital, currency and CFPB funding;
  • orders Federal Reserve notes from Treasury's Bureau of Engraving and Printing; and
  • maintains Board-wide economics, financial-stability, legal, consumer, statistics, technology and administrative staffs.

Federal Open Market Committee

The FOMC is the statutory policy committee, not a conventional operating company with its own public payroll and property balance sheet. Its members decide the stance of monetary policy and direct open-market implementation. Board and Reserve Bank staffs prepare forecasts, market analysis, regional intelligence, briefing material and implementation options for that process.

Twelve Reserve Banks

The Reserve Banks are separately incorporated operating institutions. They:

  • maintain reserve and settlement accounts for depository institutions;
  • lend through the discount window and operate authorized liquidity facilities;
  • supervise and examine covered institutions;
  • operate cash, check, ACH, Fedwire, National Settlement and FedNow services;
  • act as depositories and fiscal agents for Treasury;
  • collect regional economic intelligence and conduct research;
  • maintain local premises and staff; and
  • host national product, technology and employee-benefit functions whose costs may be allocated across all 12 Banks.

New York Fed and the SOMA Desk

The New York Fed conducts open-market operations as agent for the FOMC and all 12 Reserve Banks. It transacts in Treasury and agency securities, agency mortgage-backed securities, repo and reverse repo markets, operates securities lending and handles foreign-official and international-account activity.

The assets and results are participated across the Reserve Banks through the System Open Market Account. New York's operational role therefore should not be mistaken for sole economic ownership of the portfolio.

Shared-service organizations

The System is not simply 12 Banks duplicating every function:

  • Federal Reserve Financial Services coordinates national payment products;
  • Federal Reserve Information Technology, hosted within the Richmond structure, supplies national infrastructure and business-line technology;
  • product and function offices can be hosted at one Bank while serving and charging all Banks; and
  • Atlanta hosts the Office of Employee Benefits for System benefit-plan administration.

These arrangements create inter-Bank charges, reimbursements and accounting ownership that disappear or net in the combined 12-Bank statements.

Treasury and the Bureau of Engraving and Printing

Treasury has multiple, nonidentical relationships with the Fed:

  • issuer of Treasury securities held in SOMA;
  • holder of the Treasury General Account;
  • principal and reimbursing customer for fiscal-agent work;
  • recipient of Reserve Bank residual earnings when remittances are available; and
  • parent department of the Bureau of Engraving and Printing.

The Bureau of Engraving and Printing physically prints Federal Reserve notes. The Board is the issuing authority, orders the notes and pays currency costs through assessments on the Reserve Banks; the Reserve Banks put notes into circulation, distribute, process, authenticate and destroy them.

IV. What the work looks like in practice

1. Monetary policy and implementation

The visible FOMC vote is the end of a recurring production process:

  • collect and revise economic, banking, labor, price and market data;
  • produce national and regional forecasts;
  • interview businesses and communities and prepare regional intelligence;
  • model policy alternatives and financial conditions;
  • brief governors and Reserve Bank presidents;
  • publish statements, minutes, projections, reports and data;
  • implement the directive through interest on reserve balances, repo and reverse-repo facilities, open-market operations and the SOMA portfolio; and
  • maintain lending, collateral, counterparty, settlement and risk systems.

The New York Desk implements the FOMC's directive. The current framework primarily steers short-term rates using interest on reserve balances and the overnight reverse-repurchase facility, with other tools available.

2. Financial stability

The Fed says it monitors four broad vulnerability areas: asset valuations, household and business borrowing, financial-sector leverage and funding risk. Staff perform quarterly assessments, prepare the semiannual Financial Stability Report, contribute to stress scenarios and coordinate with the Financial Stability Oversight Council, the Financial Stability Board and foreign authorities.

This lane overlaps economics, markets and supervision. It is not a separately operated emergency response office whose costs can be read from a single expense line.

3. Supervision and regulation

This is a large recurring field-and-analysis operation. Work includes:

  • examinations and continuous monitoring;
  • capital, liquidity, governance, operational resilience, information technology, cybersecurity, consumer-compliance and anti-money-laundering reviews;
  • applications for mergers, acquisitions, memberships and other corporate actions;
  • rulemaking, guidance and supervisory policy;
  • stress testing and large-firm capital work;
  • enforcement and remediation monitoring; and
  • collection and analysis of regulatory data.

During 2024, the Fed reported:

Those counts do not measure examination depth or staff hours, but they show that supervision is a continuous operating line rather than an occasional policy meeting.

4. Payments, accounts, settlement and cash

The Fed is both overseer and operator. It maintains institution accounts and operates or supports:

  • FedACH;
  • Fedwire Funds;
  • Fedwire Securities;
  • National Settlement Service;
  • FedNow;
  • commercial check processing;
  • FedLine access;
  • currency and coin distribution; and
  • principal, interest and securities settlement for fiscal-agent issuers.

5. Treasury fiscal-agent work

Reserve Banks support Treasury payments, collections, cash forecasting, accounting, debt auctions, marketable and savings securities, retail-investor servicing and government financial systems.

Selected 2024 workload measures were:

Treasury and other government principals reimburse this lane. The 2025 combined Reserve Bank statements reported $934,000,000 of reimbursable-service income. The latest detailed 2024 table showed $859,322,000 of reimbursable cost, including $847,392,000 attributable to Treasury.

The reimbursed cost is real operating work but is not a permanent use of Reserve Bank portfolio income.

6. Consumer protection and community development

Work includes consumer-compliance examinations, fair-lending and Community Reinvestment Act activity, complaints and inquiries, consumer and household research, community-development analysis and outreach.

Federal Reserve Consumer Help processed 34,198 cases in 2024:

  • 17,237 inquiries; and
  • 16,961 complaints.

Complaints against Federal Reserve-regulated entities totaled 9,700. Those counts do not capture community-development research, convenings or policy work, but they identify one concrete operating queue.

7. Internal support

The public-facing functions depend on:

  • information technology, cloud infrastructure and cybersecurity;
  • data acquisition, statistics, modeling and data governance;
  • legal, finance, audit and records work;
  • human resources, compensation and pensions;
  • procurement, travel and communications;
  • facilities, physical security and currency logistics; and
  • national governance, committees and shared-service allocations.

These are not separate congressional missions. They are the support layer through which the five functions and fiscal-agent work operate.

VIII. What it says is driving growth

The Fed's 2025 budget narrative identifies these resource drivers:

  • regional economic research and market-operation monitoring;
  • supervision portfolio growth;
  • payment-infrastructure modernization;
  • FedNow operations;
  • Fedwire Funds migration to ISO 20022;
  • FedLine software and connectivity;
  • cloud migration for Federal Reserve Financial Services;
  • the Accelerate Cloud and Colocation program;
  • the Common Data Platform;
  • Treasury-requested modernization and cloud migration of fiscal-agent applications;
  • NextGen currency-processing machines and sensors;
  • cash-vault and cash-center projects;
  • Board forecasting, modeling, statistics and data infrastructure;
  • cybersecurity and operational resilience; and
  • the triennial Survey of Consumer Finances.

These are the institution's stated explanations. They identify programs and purposes, not proof that the price, staffing level or delivery model is optimal.

IX. The Fed's story, reconciled

Story 1: “We set interest rates”

True but incomplete. The FOMC sets the policy stance. Research, forecasting, regional intelligence, market operations, reserve accounts, collateral, payment systems and public communication make that stance operational.

Story 2: “We print money”

Imprecise in two ways:

  • Treasury's Bureau of Engraving and Printing physically prints notes; the Board is the issuing authority and orders them, while the Reserve Banks put them into circulation, distribute and process them.
  • Balance-sheet “money creation” occurs through reserve-account and asset operations, not through the administrative budget.

Story 3: “The portfolio funds the Fed”

Mechanically true but easy to misread. Reserve Bank earnings ordinarily cover expenses, dividends and remittances. The size of the portfolio is determined by monetary-policy and currency factors, not by adding up the desired cost of employees, buildings and software.

In 2025, the portfolio produced $155,299,000,000 of interest income while the Reserve Banks paid $167,381,000,000 of interest expense. The System did not need a large positive profit to keep operating; instead, its deferred Treasury-remittance asset increased because current earnings were insufficient to cover current expenses and restore prior remittance capacity.

Story 4: “We are a public institution”

The Board is a federal agency. The Reserve Banks are separately incorporated statutory bodies with public functions and limited member-bank stock. The System is federally created and publicly purposed, but it is not one ordinary department, one private corporation or one consolidated legal entity.

Story 5: “We spend mainly on economics and research”

Not supported by the latest functional actuals. Research and policy are material, but Reserve Bank supervision, payments, cash, institution services and fiscal-agent operations together are larger.

The identified 2024 internal research/policy functions totaled $1,146,600,000:

Internal research/policy line 2024 actual
Reserve Bank monetary and economic policy $902,900,000
Board Research and Statistics $117,300,000
Board International Finance $47,700,000

That amount includes employees, data, forecasts, modeling, FOMC support and market analysis. It is not an outside university, nonprofit or grant total.

X. What the public story still does not show

The published record still does not provide:

  • 2025 actual Reserve Bank expense by function;
  • System-wide staff hours or FTE by function and support layer;
  • a complete cost-center ledger connecting every function to payroll, software, data, contractors and facilities;
  • a full vendor and payee ledger;
  • application-level software ownership, users, invoices and inter-Bank allocations;
  • the gross inter-Bank shared-service charges eliminated in combination;
  • a clean split of internal research, purchased data, outside consulting, conferences, visiting scholars, universities, nonprofits and grants;
  • recipient-level interest paid on reserve balances and reverse repos; or
  • actual project-level cash spending for every capital program.

These are disclosure limits, not evidence that the undisclosed relationships are improper.

XI. Working storyline

The institution's own records support this provisional storyline:

Congress created a federated central-bank system whose visible policy committee is only the top of the stack. Under it sits a national information, supervision, settlement, cash, lending and fiscal-agent network. The Reserve Banks do most operating work; the Board sets rules, oversees and supplies central policy staff; New York executes market operations; and shared-service offices distribute technology and product costs across the Banks.

The largest dollars on the financial statements are generated by the monetary balance sheet and flow mainly between Treasury and agency assets, reserve-account holders and repo counterparties. The largest ordinary operating resources go to people, supervision, payment/cash services, software and support. Those two scales intersect, but they are not the same thing.

The next useful audit is therefore not “Why does interest-rate setting cost $177,181,000,000?” It is:

  1. Which functions account for the 24,875 budgeted System FTE?
  2. Which outside vendors and hosted System offices receive the technology, data, professional-services and capital dollars?
  3. Which costs are reimbursed by Treasury or financial-institution fees?
  4. Which inter-Bank allocations vanish in the combined statements?
  5. Which functions, systems or facilities duplicate work already performed by Treasury, other regulators or private payment networks?
  6. What measurable output or risk reduction does each material program buy?
