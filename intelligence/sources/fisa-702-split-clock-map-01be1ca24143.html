FISA 702 — Split Clock Map

Updated: 2026-08-15
Status: factual legal map
Rule: The sunset and the certification are different objects. “702 expired” and “collection continues” can both be true. A failed House bill is not a law. A FISC order not on the public docket is not a date.

The Surveillance subplot stays events. This map does not invent a plotline.

Short answer

Two clocks.

Clock 1 — the statute. Title VII of FISA, including section 702 (50 U.S.C. § 1881a), is repealed effective June 12, 2026, except as provided in section 404 of the FISA Amendments Act of 2008. The repeal sits in FAA § 403(b), last amended by Public Law 119-87 (Apr. 30, 2026). House OLRC prelim, text in effect August 14, 2026, still prints that date. No later enacted reauthorization was retrieved at cutoff.

Clock 2 — the paper already issued. FAA § 404(b)(1) (50 U.S.C. § 1801 note): any order, authorization, or directive issued under Title VII “shall continue in effect until the date of the expiration of such order, authorization, or directive.” Section 702 itself, while live, let the Attorney General and the DNI authorize targeting “for a period of up to 1 year from the effective date of the authorization.”

H.R. 9238 would have moved the repeal from June 12, 2026 to July 2, 2026. The House rejected it on June 11, 2026: 198–218 on a motion to suspend the rules. Tracker: Failed House.

FISC order not retrieved at cutoff. The FISC public docket and ODNI/intel.gov releases do not contain a March 2026 (or nearby) section 702 certification opinion. No grandfather end date is written here.

The charter to start new 702 work is gone as of June 12. Paper already signed, if any still has time on it, is a different object.

2. Legal stack

Layer A — section 702. 50 U.S.C. § 1881a(a): AG and DNI may authorize jointly, “for a period of up to 1 year from the effective date of the authorization,” targeting of persons reasonably believed to be located outside the United States to acquire foreign intelligence information. Certification to FISC under (h). FISC “shall enter an order approving the certification” if the required elements are met ((j)(3)). Intra-year hold ((j)(5)(B)): if they file a replacement certification, the old authorization stays until the Court rules on the new one. That clause is not a congressional extension of Title VII.

Layer B — the sunset. Section 702 has no sunset of its own. The kill switch is FAA § 403(b)(1), as amended through Pub. L. 119-87: except as provided in section 404, effective June 12, 2026, this section is repealed.

Layer C — the grandfather. FAA § 404(b)(1): any Title VII order, authorization, or directive “shall continue in effect until the date of the expiration of such order, authorization, or directive.” § 404(b)(2): Title VII continues to apply to that leftover paper until the later of its expiration or a final judgment. Directives, the no-suit shield, and FISC contempt power ride with it.

Open question, not filled: after June 12, may AG/DNI use leftover (j)(5) to mint a new year? The statute says existing paper continues until its expiration. It does not say “and you may start a new year after repeal.”

H.R. 9238’s operative text named P.L. 119-87 as the last enacted sunset and struck “June 12, 2026” for “July 2, 2026.” Twenty days. It never became law.

4. The certification clock

The statute gives the mechanism, not a 2026 calendar day. The day is on the FISC order.

Retrieved: ODNI released the March 18, 2025 FISC 702 certification opinion (posted Sept. 12, 2025). ODNI’s 2026 transparency report names three 2025 FISC 702 orders. FISC public docket 2026 filings retrieved are Misc. 19-02 accuracy letters, not a 702 cert.

March 2026 (or nearby) FISC 702 certification order: not retrieved. Do not write “collection runs until March 2027.”

What is established without that order:

  • Leftover Title VII paper issued before June 12 continues until its own expiration (FAA § 404(b)(1)).
  • A § 1881a(a) authorization lasts up to 1 year from its effective date.
  • New 702 authorizations after repeal need a live Title VII.
  • Provider directives and the liability shield continue for the life of those directives.
  • FBI query limits ride leftover paper. They do not authorize a new year’s take.

6. Entity Theory read

The federal group does not hold one 702. It holds a title, a court order, a directive to a private company, and a query function inside the Bureau. Congress holds the charter leash. FISC holds the paper leash. Providers hold no charter. The FBI query machine is a downstream use of data already acquired.

“The government is still spying” and “702 expired” can be said in the same week if one speaker means Clock 1 and the other means Clock 2. They are not a contradiction until someone pretends they are the same object.

  • Fate of leftover 2025 paper on June 12 — a fact about those orders’ own dates, not inferred here.
  • Title I individualized orders as the residual path once leftover Title VII paper dies. Named only as the next statute.
