Syracuse housing draws on grants, tax credits and nonprofit developers

HUD gave New York and Syracuse separate HOME grants. At completed Moyer Carriage Lofts, housing and historic credits helped finance the project; a 2022 state-filed chart names one investing member, while final cash and tax claims remain unjoined.

What we found

  • Home HeadQuarters’ original IRS Form 990 describes its Syracuse and Upstate housing mission. For the fiscal year ended April 30, 2025, it reported $25,944,943 in government grants, 728 home-improvement or SHARP cases, 51 rehabilitated or newly built homes, and assistance to about 84 buyers. These are one filer's totals, not named-award results.
  • HHQ's Schedule O describes board contract approval and voucher checks; Schedule R names distinct affiliates. Government records separately show Syracuse CDBG, a New York award, Onondaga's Bayberry fund and a proposed SEDCO loan. Contract, control, award, payment and affiliate are different edges.
  • HUD's FY2024 HOME primes separately name HTFC ($23,907,179 obligated) and City of Syracuse ($1,303,112.35 obligated). The City's final plan expected the smaller amount; its FY2025 audit reports $511,147 in modified-accrual federal expenditures under the City FAIN and a qualified HOME compliance opinion for rental-inspection controls. The audit and City's HHQ CDBG budget do not establish a City HOME payment to HHQ.
  • For state Champion project 20243069, HTFC authorized up to $850,000 for HHQ and five proposed homes. Champion appears on HCR's February 2025 active-program list but not its May 2026 list, and the current USAspending subaward row reports $0; neither source explains the change or proves a project draw, nonprofit receipt or five-home result.
  • At Moyer Carriage Lofts, HCR's later permanent-source report lists $18.26 million federal low-income housing credit financing, $8.45 million federal historic credit financing, $3.50 million state historic credit financing, a mortgage, HCR funds and $500,000 City HOME; it marks 128 units complete. A 2022 chart filed with NY DEC names NEF Fund X LP as the LLC's 99.99% non-managing investing member, and the IRS parent Form 990 lists Housing Visions Consultants as a separate related nonprofit. The source lines are not dated investor cash; HVC's own return, final ownership, credit certifications and City voucher remain unjoined.

Why it matters

Housing tax credits move public support through taxpayers and private project equity, while HOME moves through a grant agreement and draw. The same building may use both, but its nonprofit developer, project owner, investor and public agencies hold different rights and records. A mission statement, funding headline and annual Form 990 cannot substitute for those joins.

Research summary

Syracuse housing reaches nonprofit developers by different routes. HUD made separate HOME grants to New York and the City; neither public prime proves a payment to Home HeadQuarters. At completed Moyer Carriage Lofts, New York's permanent-source report names housing and historic tax-credit financing beside brownfield equity, debt, state funds and City HOME. A 2022 state-filed chart names NEF Investment Partners Fund X LP as a 99.99% non-managing member of the project LLC; a later state certificate confirms distinct property holders. The tax credits support private project equity, not federal grant checks. Investor cash, final tax claims, nonprofit accounts and HOME vouchers remain separate records.
