Federal Reserve Financial Constitution — Source Document

Evidence cutoff: 2026-08-02 ET
Accounting unit: millions of dollars unless a table says otherwise
Rule: legal entity, reporting entity, accounting allocation, cost object, service operator, settlement account, cash transfer and legal right remain separate until an operative record joins them.

This is the factual control note for the Reserve Banks' financial architecture. It does not treat the combined Banks as a thirteenth corporation, a priced- service pro forma as a legal-entity statement, a budget as an accrual-basis actual, or statutory member-bank stock as ordinary residual equity.

1. The reporting perimeters

Record What it reports What it does not establish
Individual Reserve Bank financial statement the Bank's legal-entity books, plus any consolidated VIEs enterprise control of every service, system, contract or participant account
Combined Reserve Banks financial statements aggregation of the twelve Banks and consolidated VIEs after inter-Bank eliminations a separate legal entity or a Bank-by-Bank operating margin
Priced-service pro forma service-line revenue, cost, PSAF and recovery under the Monetary Control Act the legal Bank at which each amount was booked
Reserve Bank budget prospective maximum spending authorization under the Board's budget perimeter an obligation, accrual-basis actual or GAAP/FAM variance
FAM general-ledger accounting recognition, classification, capitalization, depreciation and ISA entries the current cost-driver constitution; FAM assigns cost accounting to CASPR
CASPR cost object benefited activity and internal-support allocation necessarily a legal entity, external cash payment or settlement-finality record

KPMG issued unmodified opinions on the twelve Banks' 2025 and 2024 financial statements and on internal control over financial reporting as of December 31, 2025. The reports are dated March 12, 2026 and use the Federal Reserve Banks' Financial Accounting Manual, a comprehensive basis of accounting other than U.S. GAAP. A clean ICFR opinion is material financial-reporting assurance; it is not an SLA, cyber, availability or transaction-finality opinion for every rail.

Primary record: Combined 2025 financial statements, auditor report pp. 1-2 and statements pp. 3-5 · January 2026 FAM

2. Two monetary-allocation constitutions

The combined statements disclose two distinct allocation bases. They should not be collapsed into one generic "share of the Fed balance sheet."

Monetary object Allocation basis across the twelve Banks Measurement date / mechanism
domestic SOMA securities; repo and reverse-repo balances; securities lending each Bank's annual interdistrict-settlement allocation percentage the second-quarter ISA settlement replaces the trailing-twelve-month average ISA through March 31 with an equal and opposite SOMA reallocation
foreign-currency investments and central-bank liquidity swaps each Bank's share of aggregate capital and surplus prior December 31 capital-and-surplus position

The interdistrict settlement account has two roles: it accumulates inter-Bank payments and positions during the year, and its annual settlement resets the domestic SOMA allocation. It is not payment-service revenue, a bilateral invoice or a solvency ratio.

At December 31, 2025, seven Bank ISA receivables totaled $275,375 and five Bank ISA payables totaled $275,376, a $1 rounding difference. The 2024 sides each totaled $330,069. Large district swings therefore require an ISA/SOMA reading, not a service-profit inference.

The statements prove that the year-end ISA is not the contemporaneous allocation key. Chicago's ISA receivable rose from $10,844 to $98,455 while its domestic Treasury-security allocation share fell from 5.519% to 5.073%. San Francisco's ISA moved from a $92,139 receivable to a $5,358 payable while its allocation share rose from 9.049% to 10.271%. The Q2 calculation uses the prior twelve-month average through March 31; December 31 reports later cumulative state.

Primary record: Combined 2025 financial statements, Note 3, printed pp. 13-15

3. Service accounting is a federated constitution

3.1 External System revenue recognition

Service family Bank recognizing total System revenue 2025 audited presentation
Check and FedACH Atlanta Income from services $256
Fedwire Funds, Fedwire Securities and NSS New York Income from services $182
electronic access / FedLine Chicago Income from services $103
FedNow Boston embedded in Other; not separately stated
combined listed service income combined Banks Income from services $542; FedNow remains in Boston Other

The operating Bank recognizes System revenue when the usage-based performance obligation is satisfied and reimburses provider Banks through Other operating expense. This proves the accounting operator and revenue-recognition location. It does not, by itself, prove system controller, developer, production host, contracting Bank, software-asset owner, incident commander or participant- account liability.

3.2 Legal-Bank and service-line views reconcile differently

For 2024, Atlanta, New York and Chicago recognized $249, $174 and $102, respectively. The Board's priced-service pro forma instead assigned $111.1 to Check, $190.0 to FedACH, $170.3 to Fedwire Funds/NSS and $53.0 to Fedwire Securities. Electronic-access revenue recognized at Chicago is allocated among the benefited services in the pro forma. The Bank statements answer where the external System revenue is booked; the pro forma answers which service bears revenue and cost.

Primary records: 2024 Annual Report, Payment System and Reserve Bank Oversight · Atlanta · New York · Chicago · Boston

3.3 FedNow is outside the mature-service recovery perimeter

The Board forecast $235.1 of FedNow operating expense for 2025 and $230.3 for 2026, but published no FedNow revenue or recovery percentage. Boston's entire Other-income line was $19 in both 2025 and 2024 and contains non-FedNow items. No public official recovery ratio can be computed by dividing those amounts.

Primary record: 90 FR 57052, 2026 Federal Reserve Bank service prices and PSAF

4. Shared-service accounting and eliminations

4.1 Principal/Agent entries

FAM Appendix C requires the Principal Bank to debit service costs incurred and the Agent Bank to credit services provided. The first entry is an accrued estimate through ISA; the following month records the actual and reverses the estimate. An individual Bank can therefore carry a deliberately mirrored, gross internal service entry that disappears on combination. The entry is not necessarily cash settlement, external expense or final service margin.

4.2 National IT at Richmond

Richmond disclosed $1,135 of 2025 System computing and support reimbursements ($1,052 in 2024) as a reduction of Other operating expense. Richmond's Other line was $(513) and total reported operating expense was $850. The identified reimbursements alone exceeded Richmond's reported net operating expense. The recipient Banks bear allocated cost; the combined statements eliminate the inter-Bank mirror while retaining external System cost.

This does not disclose gross National IT cost by application, CASPR driver, software work in process, vendor, cloud, data-center, contract or FRFS service.

National IT's separate capital row also reverses the apparent 2024 System story. District capital was $645.5 against a $784.4 budget, a source-stated $(138.9) variance. National IT was $131.7 against $129.5, approximately $2.3 over budget. Those movements reconcile, subject to source rounding, to the System's $(136.6) total capital variance. The 2025 approved plan then moved the published perimeter to $882.4 of District capital and $60.2 of National IT capital. That is an actual-to-budget presentation shift—not 2025 actual spend, asset-title transfer or proof that owned infrastructure became cloud expense.

The software books likewise reject a Richmond-equals-all-National-IT model. Richmond's net software declined from $35 to $27 in 2025, with $18 of amortization, while combined Reserve Bank net software rose from $597 to $646, with $193 of amortization. The corresponding non-amortization residuals are $10 for Richmond and $242 combined, but can include additions, transfers, disposals, impairments and other adjustments. They are not gross additions or cash paid. Under FAM, the Bank hosting the controlling product or function office generally records shared software; development, physical hosting, contracting and control can reside in different places.

Primary record: Richmond 2025 financial statements, Note 2 printed pp. 9-10 and Note 6 printed pp. 44-45 · 2024 annual-report budget tables D.11-D.12 · FAM Appendix D

5. Budget and audited actual are different bases

The approved 2025 operating budget was $6,382.5, including National IT charges to the Banks but excluding Board assessments, pension cost, currency cost and CFPB assessments. The audited combined operating-expense line was $9,800 and includes those excluded statement objects.

Approximate statement-to-budget bridge 2025

less Board operations and currency (2,702.0)

less System pension service cost (577.0)
approximate statement expense on budget perimeter 6,276.0

approximate favorable difference 106.5 / 1.67%

This is a high-level perimeter reconciliation, not an audited budget variance. Statement rounding, timing, CASPR reclassification, reimbursable claims and function-versus-natural-account presentation remain unreconciled.

Richmond supplies the clearest legal-entity gross-to-net example. Its $850 net operating expense plus the $1,135 inter-Bank computing/support credit reconstructs to $1,985 of gross Richmond-booked expense before reimbursement. Gross Other was $622 before the credit produced the reported $(513). Neither reconstructed amount is a disclosed National IT segment or external-vendor spend.

The same budget authorized $797.4 of fee-based service expense, $532.1 of priced-service revenue, $915.0 of reimbursable claims and $942.7 of 2025 capital spend. It is a maximum authorization, not an obligation to spend.

Primary record: 2025 Reserve Bank budgets, approved December 15, 2024

6. Net-interest architecture

The combined interest result improved sharply, but not uniformly across Banks:

Combined object 2025 2024 Change
interest income 155,299 158,756 (3,457)
repo interest expense 19,719 40,288 (20,569)
depository-institutions-and-others interest expense 147,662 186,478 (38,816)
total interest expense 167,381 226,766 (59,385)
net interest income / (expense) (12,082) (68,010) +55,928

New York's Bank statement accounted for $31,848 of the combined net-interest improvement. Chicago's net interest expense nevertheless worsened by $78, Kansas City's by $191, and Minneapolis net interest income fell from $141 to $75. Allocation, asset runoff, deposit location, liability mix and rate timing therefore matter more than any local operating-margin story.

The public caption is Depository institutions and others. Depository- institution balances accrue interest daily at IORB. Other deposits include foreign central-bank and government deposits, cash collateral, DFMU deposits and GSE deposits; DFMU deposits currently earn a separately Board-set rate equal to IORB. Both interest classes enter the same caption. The audited statements do not publish either component, a DFMU-only amount or a recipient schedule.

6.1 Derived aggregate reserve-interest bridge

A public-series reconstruction materially narrows—but does not audit—the split. The model uses the actual January 1 H.4.1 Wednesday reserve balance because that week's average contains six 2024 days, then uses the 52 WRESBAL week averages ending January 8 through December 31. The four 2025 IORB changes took effect on Thursdays and align with the Thursday-Wednesday reporting weeks. The official day-count convention is 1/360.

IORB window Days Modeled reserve interest
January 1 at 4.40% 1 353.511
January 2-September 17 at 4.40% 259 104,658.119
September 18-October 29 at 4.15% 42 14,336.876
October 30-December 10 at 3.90% 42 13,114.028
December 11-31 at 3.65% 21 6,296.680
modeled reserve-balance interest 365 138,759.214
audited Depository institutions and others — 147,662.000
unallocated residual — 8,902.786

The residual is approximately $8.903 billion of other-deposit interest plus model residue, not DFMU interest. Keeping the cross-year weekly average for January 1 produces an $8.878 billion residual; the defensible range is therefore $8.88-$8.90 billion. Applying IORB to every H.4.1 Other-deposit dollar produces $9.160 billion, about $257 million above the stronger residual, confirming that the broad mixed stock cannot be relabeled as uniformly interest-bearing DFMU balances.

WRESBAL is rounded to $1 million and nets depository-institution overdrafts, whereas actual interest uses positive eligible end-of-day balances. A more granular all-twelve reserve/other interest schedule remains unsupported.

7. Earnings, Treasury remittances and the deferred asset

These are separate clocks:

weekly Bank earnings
  → recovery of that Bank's deferred asset, if any
  → current remittance accrual
  → cash transfer, including settlement of a prior accrual
  → combined deferred-asset movement
  → future district-by-district recovery

The 2025 combined statement reported:

Object 2025
pre-remittance System loss (18,761)
dividends 1,688
cash transferred to Treasury 6,281
increase in deferred asset 27,526
net earnings remittances (21,245)
ending deferred asset 243,481

The cash-transfer/deferred-asset result is not contradictory. Atlanta, Dallas and St. Louis recorded $5,054, $13 and $621 of current/cumulative 2025 remittances, totaling $5,688. Atlanta also settled a $593 remittance payable that existed at December 31, 2024. The sum is the combined $6,281 cash transfer. At the same time, the combined deferred asset increased by $27,526.

Therefore:

  • not all Reserve Bank remittances were suspended throughout 2025;
  • a district cash transfer does not mean the combined deferred asset reversed;
  • one profitable Bank does not cure another Bank's deferred asset;
  • a projection of broad or System-wide resumption is not an audited event;
  • the deferred asset is an accounting claim on future net earnings before remittances resume at the affected Bank; it is not a Treasury receivable, funded asset, cash reserve or conventional intercompany IOU.

Primary record: Combined 2025 financial statements, statements pp. 3-5 and Note 12

8. Member-bank stock, capital and surplus

Reserve Bank stock is a special statutory subscription instrument. The legal word stock is real; the inference to ordinary corporate equity is not.

Feature Instrument term
subscription membership requires subscription tied to the member bank's capital and surplus
paid-in amount one-half of the subscribed $100 par is paid; the remainder is callable
transfer cannot be transferred or hypothecated
dividend cumulative and paid semiannually at the statutory rate/cap
voting one vote per member bank within its class, regardless of shares held, for Class A and B director elections
governance limit member banks elect six of nine Reserve Bank directors; the Board of Governors appoints the three Class C directors
withdrawal/liquidation the member receives the statutory cash-paid amount and bounded accrued dividend, subject to liabilities—not a market-value exit
Reserve Bank dissolution remaining surplus after statutory claims becomes property of the United States
additional exposure unpaid subscribed capital remains callable; the Act also imposes member-bank liability up to twice par under its terms
Combined capital object 2025 2024 Change
capital paid-in 39,152 37,182 +1,970
statutory aggregate surplus 6,785 6,785 —
member-bank dividends 1,688 1,623 +65

The capital schedule reported 39,408,868 additional shares. At $50 paid per $100-par share, those shares tie to the $1,970 increase. Total year-end shares of 783,040,132 similarly tie to $39,152 paid-in capital after rounding. The accounting confirms the half-paid/half-callable statutory structure; it does not convert the instrument into transferable residual equity.

The $6,785 aggregate surplus is reallocated across the Banks according to each Bank's share of paid-in capital. A district surplus increase is therefore not local retained earnings. Richmond's $1,346 capital increase coincided with a $166 increase in allocated surplus; that is the allocation formula operating, not proof that Richmond retained $166 of locally generated earnings.

Use: member banks hold mandatory, nontransferable statutory subscription stock in their district Reserve Bank. Avoid: the member banks are ordinary private owners of the Federal Reserve System.

Primary records: Combined 2025 financial statements, capital statement p. 5; Notes 1 and 3(o) · Federal Reserve Act, Sections 2, 4, 5, 6 and 7

9. Control conclusions

Claim Financial adjudication
New York owns Fedwire because it recognizes its revenue. Unsupported. Revenue recognition proves the accounting operator, not every control layer.
The combined Banks are the legal parent of the districts. False. Combined is an aggregation and elimination perimeter.
Chicago's ISA receivable is service profit. False. ISA is interdistrict settlement state and an annual domestic-SOMA allocation input.
Richmond's net operating expense is National IT's gross cost. False. Large internal reimbursements are netted into Richmond's Other line.
The approved budget can be compared directly with audited operating expense. False without a perimeter bridge.
The $147.662 billion caption is bank-only IORB expense. False. It includes depository institutions and eligible other-deposit interest.
DFMUs earned $8.9 billion in 2025. Unsupported. $8.88-$8.90 billion is a derived other-deposit-plus-model-residue band.
National IT underspent its separate 2024 capital budget. False. National IT was slightly over; Districts drove the total underrun.
The 2025 National IT capital decline proves a move to cloud subscriptions. Unsupported without the application, contract, asset, expense and cash bridges.
The Federal Reserve sent nothing to Treasury after September 2022. False as a blanket claim; 2025 cash transfers occurred while the combined deferred asset grew.
The deferred asset is an IOU from Treasury or an ordinary subsidiary receivable. False. It is a Bank accounting object representing future net earnings retained before later remittances.
Member-bank stock is ordinary transferable equity with a residual claim. False. The statute fixes transfer, dividend, vote, call and dissolution rights.

10. Publicly unresolved objects

No official public record reviewed supplied:

  • the current complete CASPR Manual or 2025 account/driver dictionary;
  • gross inter-Bank provider costs by Bank and payment rail before reimbursement;
  • National IT gross expense, 2025 actual capital and capital reconciliation by application;
  • DFMU-only balances, named holders and interest expense;
  • institution-level reserve interest and district reserve/other expense splits;
  • FedNow revenue, net income, PSAF or official recovery percentage;
  • ISA composition by rail, institution or transaction;
  • service-level software assets, work in process, vendors, clouds or contracts;
  • KPMG materiality, significant-risk or application-scope schedules.

These are bounded public negatives. They are not findings that the records do not exist, and no external request is proposed.
