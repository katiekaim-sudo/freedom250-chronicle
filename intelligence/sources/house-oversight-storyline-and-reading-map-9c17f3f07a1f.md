# House Oversight storyline and reading map

**Coverage:** 2024-11-01 through 2026-07-15, inclusive  
**Committee corpus:** 107 official public meetings — 77 hearings, 15 markups/business meetings, 15 roundtables, 0 forums  
**Status:** factual-first synthesis and retrieval guide; no live-vault edits; no astrology interpretation

## Read this map correctly

This is the cross-event layer over the complete meeting census. It does not replace the chronological registry or the event-level witness records. Its purpose is to show which meetings become more informative when read together and where the committee moved from diagnosis to proposed legislation.

The source boundary is literal: official committee hearings, business meetings/markups, and committee-hosted roundtables with public event pages. It excludes ordinary press releases, letters, subpoenas, transcribed interviews, field appearances and reports issued without a qualifying public meeting. The official archive surfaces are [Hearings](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/), [Markups](https://oversight.house.gov/markup/), [Roundtables](https://oversight.house.gov/roundtable/) and [Forums](https://oversight.house.gov/forum/).

Two institutional periods sit inside the window:

- November–December 2024 is the tail of the 118th Congress, when the body was named the House Committee on Oversight and Accountability.
- January 2025 onward is the 119th Congress, under the name House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform. The January 14, 2025 organizational meeting is therefore a boundary event, not merely another markup.

For roundtables, use the visible date on the official event page and in the detailed registries. The archive's embedded `datetime` metadata is systematically seven days earlier for multiple roundtable records and appears to reflect publication rather than meeting time.

## The four clocks that must stay separate

| Clock | What an event can establish | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Hearing / roundtable clock | Testimony, questions, documents entered, committee framing and policy proposals | A judicial finding, enacted authority, deployed system or verified loss amount |
| Committee clock | Notice, amendment, vote and committee reporting of a measure | House passage, Senate passage or enactment |
| Legislative clock | Introduction, chamber action, enrollment, presidential signature and effective date | Operational deployment unless the law itself makes the change self-executing |
| Implementation clock | Rulemaking, appropriations, contracts, data-sharing agreements, staffing, system launch and adverse-action process | Retroactive proof that the preceding testimony or forecast was correct |

The same separation applies to executive action. An executive order may direct work and set deadlines; it does not prove that every deliverable was completed, that every agency connected its data, or that a production control system is operating.

## Strongest event-to-event chains

### 1. Fraud moves from loss rhetoric to a payment-control architecture

This is the clearest developed sequence in the entire corpus:

1. [The War on Waste — February 12, 2025](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/the-war-on-waste-stamping-out-the-scourge-of-improper-payments-and-fraud/) establishes the majority's scale argument and foregrounds identity verification, unsupported self-attestation and continuing audit.
2. [Shifting Gears — March 11, 2025](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/shifting-gears-moving-from-recovery-to-prevention-of-improper-payments-and-fraud/) states the doctrinal move from post-payment recovery toward prevention, data sharing and modernized systems.
3. [March 25, 2025 business meeting](https://oversight.house.gov/markup/full-committee-business-meeting-77/) advances H.R. 2277, which proposed extending and renaming PRAC as the Fraud Prevention and Accountability Committee. It is an important but distinct precursor to the later proposed Treasury IGFAR function; neither should be treated as created merely because a committee reported a bill.
4. [Safeguarding Procurement — June 4, 2025](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/safeguarding-procurement-examining-fraud-risk-management-in-the-department-of-defense/) applies fraud-risk management to the contractor/acquisition lane; it is about risk controls and procurement governance, not proof that every flagged contract was fraudulent.
5. [Locking in the DOGE Cuts — June 24, 2025](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/locking-in-the-doge-cuts-ending-waste-fraud-and-abuse-for-good/) joins the prevention argument to spending cuts and institutional durability. Waste, fraud, duplication, policy disagreement and workforce reduction remain distinct categories.
6. [Minnesota Part I — January 7, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/oversight-of-fraud-and-misuse-of-federal-funds-in-minnesota-part-i/) and [Curbing Federal Fraud — January 13, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/curbing-federal-fraud-examining-innovative-tools-to-detect-and-prevent-fraud-in-federal-programs/) put a state-administered case record beside PRAC analytics, Treasury screening and GAO data science.
7. [Minnesota Part II — March 4, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/oversight-of-fraud-and-misuse-of-federal-funds-in-minnesota-part-ii/) tests political and administrative responsibility at the state/federal seam.
8. [March 18, 2026 business meeting](https://oversight.house.gov/markup/full-committee-business-meeting-85/) advances program-integrity, exclusions, savings, project-overrun, fiscal-shock and spending-transparency measures. This is committee action only.
9. [Fraud Prevention in State-Run Programs — April 15, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/fraud-prevention-understanding-fraud-in-federally-funded-programs-run-by-the-states/) defines the split control problem: federal money and rules, state records and administration, Treasury payment capability, and audit/enforcement after disbursement.
10. [April 29, 2026 business meeting](https://oversight.house.gov/markup/full-committee-business-meeting-86/) assembles the core legislative back end: Treasury pre-payment data access, payment intervention, government-wide analytics, a proposed permanent fraud-focused inspector-general function, continuous risk controls, workforce training and state-program audits. Committee reporting did not enact the bills.
11. [Universal Basic Fraud — June 3, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/universal-basic-fraud-vulnerabilities-in-medicaid-waiver-programs/) and [Combating Waste, Fraud, and Abuse in SNAP — June 25, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/combating-waste-fraud-and-abuse-in-snap/) test the doctrine against provider enrollment, beneficiary eligibility, duplicate participation, identifiers, state systems and payment suspension.
12. [Emerging Fraud Threats — July 15, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/emerging-fraud-threats-and-the-evolving-fraud-landscape/) supplies the proposed front end: synthetic/stolen identity detection, remote proofing and continuing person/account risk, with privacy, false-positive, exclusion and national-ID concerns inside the same record.

**Control-chain synthesis:**

```text
loss claim / state case
  -> prevention doctrine
  -> data and analytics
  -> identity and eligibility decision
  -> Treasury pre-payment screen
  -> hold / condition / return / pay
  -> referral, exclusion, recovery and appeal
```

**Status boundary:** hearings developed the diagnosis; the March and April markups advanced proposals; later House passage of some measures still did not equal enactment; executive deadlines and agency pilots require their own implementation evidence.

### 2. Identity/data/control is a federation of gates, not one database

Three separate chains should be compared without collapsing their legal authorities:

**Public identity and administrative records**

[Oversight of the U.S. Census Bureau — December 5, 2024](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/oversight-of-the-u-s-census-bureau/) → [April 30, 2025 business meeting](https://oversight.house.gov/markup/full-committee-business-meeting-79/) → September 16, 2025 DoD background-check hearing **postponed; no proceeding** → [December 2, 2025 business meeting](https://oversight.house.gov/markup/full-committee-business-meeting-82/) → [Occurred DoD Background Check System hearing — February 24, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/an-update-on-dods-struggling-background-check-system/) → [Emerging Fraud Threats — July 15, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/emerging-fraud-threats-and-the-evolving-fraud-landscape/).

The April 30 meeting considered resolutions seeking records about DOGE security clearances, AI/personal-data use and federal-records compliance alongside budget-reconciliation text. The Census, personnel-vetting, benefits and fraud-proofing systems involve different statutes, operators, populations, data sources and appeal rules. The recurring architectural question is who may connect which records to which decision.

**Sensitive private data crossing into public power**

[Securing Americans' Genetic Information — June 10, 2025](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/securing-americans-genetic-information-privacy-and-national-security-concerns-surrounding-23andmes-bankruptcy-sale/) → [Using Modern Tools to Counter Human Trafficking — December 10, 2025](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/using-modern-tools-to-counter-human-trafficking/) → [Crimes Against Children in the Digital Age — January 22, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/roundtable/bipartisan-roundtable-crimes-against-children-in-the-digital-age/) → [Privacy Protections and the Second Amendment — May 14, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/privacy-protections-the-second-amendment-examining-atfs-relationship-to-the-tiahrt-amendment/) → [Emerging Fraud Threats — July 15, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/emerging-fraud-threats-and-the-evolving-fraud-landscape/).

Genetic records, platform/device data, investigative leads, firearms trace data and commercial identity-risk signals are not interchangeable. The connector is the public/private boundary: vendor-held or administratively collected data becoming an input to a government decision.

**Identity handoff around unaccompanied children**

[UAC Part I — July 23, 2025](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/catch-and-release-lose-and-forget-addressing-the-crisis-of-unaccompanied-alien-children-part-i/) → [Using Modern Tools to Counter Human Trafficking — December 10, 2025](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/using-modern-tools-to-counter-human-trafficking/) → [Crimes Against Children in the Digital Age — January 22, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/roundtable/bipartisan-roundtable-crimes-against-children-in-the-digital-age/) → [UAC Part II — June 30, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/roundtable/catch-and-release-lose-and-forget-addressing-the-crisis-of-unaccompanied-alien-children-part-ii/).

This is a records-handoff and duty-of-care sequence across DHS, HHS, sponsors, inspectors general and law enforcement. It is adjacent to trafficking analytics, but a control failure, risk indicator or lost contact is not automatically proof of trafficking.

8. [February 4, 2026 business meeting](https://oversight.house.gov/markup/full-committee-business-meeting-84/) advances technology-modernization, acquisition-security, procurement and cybersecurity-workforce bills. This is a proposed machinery layer, not a deployed AI stack.
9. [Artificial Intelligence and American Power — April 16, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/roundtable/artificial-intelligence-and-american-power-leadership-security-and-prosperity/) joins semiconductors, manufacturing, skills, data centers, power and military competition.
10. [Winning the Economic Competition with China — July 14, 2026](https://oversight.house.gov/roundtable/winning-the-economic-competition-with-china-working-families-the-ai-race-and-energy/) closes the window with the most integrated AI/energy/critical-input/industrial framing.

**Strong connector:** AI is not one storyline here. It appears as infrastructure load, cyber target, administrative tool, fraud/adjudication input, investigative lead generator, audit automation, industrial technology and geopolitical capacity. Each use needs its own model, operator, authority, data and decision consequence.

### 4. DOGE becomes a federal-machinery and procurement sequence

### 7. Energy becomes the physical layer beneath AI and industrial policy

### 8. Money, debt and tax appear as fiscal-control and household-cost lanes

This committee's money story is mostly fiscal and administrative rather than settlement-rail or central-bank architecture:

- [Welfare State Part I — February 11, 2025](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/examining-the-growth-of-the-welfare-state-part-i/) and [Welfare State Part II — May 7, 2025](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/examining-the-growth-of-the-welfare-state-part-ii/) frame transfer-program size and design.

## Non-obvious connectors worth preserving

1. **Fraud and AI meet at adjudication, not merely detection.** A model or vendor can produce a risk signal; the consequential question is which public official or system may turn it into a benefit denial, payment hold, provider suspension or referral. 2. **Procurement is the hinge between DOGE rhetoric and state capacity.** Bid protests, acquisition security, software assets, defense innovation and military-contract overruns decide whether “efficiency” means fewer contracts, faster awards, different evaluation rules or stronger audit rights. Pair [Preparing for the Quantum Age](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/preparing-for-the-quantum-age-when-cryptography-breaks/) with [23andMe Genetic Information](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/securing-americans-genetic-information-privacy-and-national-security-concerns-surrounding-23andmes-bankruptcy-sale/).

7. **D.C. is where appointment design, judicial deference, policing and federal override collide.** The September 2025 markup/hearing pair is more revealing than either alone: [business meeting](https://oversight.house.gov/markup/full-committee-business-meeting-81/) plus [D.C. oversight hearing](https://oversight.house.gov/hearing/oversight-of-the-district-of-columbia/).
8. **Secrecy and fraud share an institutional-trust problem but not an evidence standard.** Whistleblower protection, archive access and inspector-general analytics all address whether insiders and records can surface misconduct. That does not allow UAP testimony, fraud allegations and audit findings to be treated as equivalent proof.

## Transcript and document extraction targets

### Universal event template

For every Tier 1 or Tier 2 event, extract:

1. **Object:** person, provider, claim, payment, contract, record, model, facility, asset or legal authority.
2. **Actor:** committee, agency, state, inspector general, Treasury component, contractor, vendor, court or regulated party.
3. **Action:** verify, match, score, hold, pay, deny, refer, suspend, recover, exclude, procure, terminate or disclose.
4. **Authority:** current statute/rule, executive direction, agency policy, contract term, proposed bill or witness recommendation.
5. **Clock:** occurred, proposed, committee-reported, House-passed, enacted, effective, pilot, awarded, deployed or scheduled.
6. **Evidence class:** testimony, GAO/OIG finding, agency record, indictment, conviction, estimate, model output, vendor claim or committee characterization.
7. **Counter-control:** notice, appeal, human review, audit trail, privacy limit, retention rule, false-positive measurement and remedy.

### Fraud / payment-integrity extractions

- Exact definition and denominator behind every dollar, rate and loss claim.
- Whether the speaker means fraud, improper payment, unknown payment, administrative error, unsupported documentation, waste or recovery.
- Data sources named; who owns them; whether access is current, proposed or technically possible.
- Exact pre-payment action available to agency and Treasury; whether it is mandatory, discretionary or proposed.
- Match/risk threshold, false-positive rate, identity-resolution method, adverse-action notice and appeal.
- Handoff among PRAC, GAO, inspectors general, Treasury, program agencies, states and prosecutors.
- Bill number and section for any claimed new authority; then separately track committee, House, Senate, enactment and implementation states.

## Claim and status caveats

1. **Committee titles are framing.** Many titles state a majority conclusion. Preserve the title verbatim but write the factual summary from testimony, documents, agency records and counter-evidence.
2. **Testimony is not adjudication.** A witness allegation is neither a conviction nor an audit finding. A GAO/OIG finding is still not automatically a criminal fraud finding.
3. **Do not convert improper-payment figures into fraud rates.** Improper payment, payment error, unsupported documentation, eligibility error, estimated loss, waste and adjudicated fraud are separate objects.
4. **A risk score is not a fact about a person.** Record the data, model, threshold, decision maker, false-positive process, notice and appeal before describing an account or identity as fraudulent.
5. **Committee approval is not enactment.** Markups create committee records and may report measures. Track later chamber action and implementation bill by bill.
6. **Roundtables are informal policy records.** Participant claims and demonstrations can identify questions and private capabilities; they do not create the procedural or evidentiary record of a hearing or markup.
7. **Scheduled time is not verified gavel time.** Event-page times are filing keys until audible video establishes convening time. This map contains no chart anchors.
8. **Archive metadata requires QA.** For roundtables, the visible event date controls over embedded archive metadata; the latter is seven days early across multiple records.
9. **Agency names and components matter.** Treasury/Bureau of the Fiscal Service, PRAC, GAO, an inspector general, a state agency and a private vendor do not become one “fraud system” because they exchange data or appear at one hearing.
10. **The absence of a meeting is not the absence of committee activity.** Letters, subpoenas, depositions, reports and negotiations were excluded by design and need a separate investigative-action registry if later required.

## Best next build from this corpus

The highest-value next artifact is not another narrative summary. It is a transcript-level control-object table for the Tier 1 set with one row per concrete proposal or claimed operating capability:

```text
event -> speaker -> entity -> object -> data -> authority -> action
      -> status -> evidence class -> counter-control -> next legal/operating clock
```

That table would let the Observatory compare the committee's recurring vocabulary with the actual machinery: who sees the data, who makes the decision, where payment is stopped, what becomes appealable, and which proposal ever moves from hearing to law to production.
