Bottom line

These dossiers turn the cross-sector map into a company-level operating view of twelve exact legal entities. Together they expose the most important places where the scientific build-out can concentrate in a provider, integrator, facility path or acceptance rail without pretending that every diversified company is one technical system.

The twelve divide into distinct evidence states:

  • current cross-sector anchors: Amazon Web Services, SpaceX, Oracle America, Lockheed Martin Corporation, Northrop Grumman Systems, L3Harris, Raytheon Company and BAE Systems IE&SI all have direct current company edges in two or more sector lanes;
  • anchors with an inference-bearing extension: Booz Allen has confirmed cyber and communications work plus a planned HPM-support recompete; Palantir has confirmed Maven work plus an uncontracted DOE opportunity; GE Vernova Operations has two selected pre-operational paths; and Ginkgo has current autonomous-lab and DARPA work plus a historical surveillance position whose future extension is uncontracted;
  • mixed maturity inside a confirmed anchor: a production or installed position never promotes a less mature prototype, field assessment, design agreement or ordering vehicle held by the same company.

The governing company path is:

exact legal entity
  -> canonical company edge
  -> confirmed role or bounded inference
  -> shared rail / acceptance bottleneck
  -> current maturity and adverse state
  -> dated gate
  -> primary monitoring surface

Dossier method

Each dossier applies seven controls:

  1. Resolve the entity before the role. Parent, subsidiary, operating company, brand and business segment are kept separate.
  2. Carry every canonical edge. A dossier does not invent new program work; it organizes existing edges and adds only primary entity or material-risk evidence.
  3. Separate confirmed work from opportunity. Consortium membership, incumbent capability, a planned recompete or a plausible architecture is labeled as inference until a named instrument exists.
  4. Preserve program maturity. Award, design, prototype, delivered, installed, tested, accepted, operating and produced are different states.
  5. Attach the controlling rail. Cloud authorization, power, launch, ground integration, field test, manufacturing qualification and OTA transition explain what must happen after vendor selection.
  6. Retain adverse and contrary evidence. Canceled acquisitions, competition losses, delayed acceptance, missing follow-ons and compliance obligations remain visible.
  7. End on a dated observable gate. Every forward claim has an upgrade, downgrade and monitoring surface rather than an open-ended forecast.

1. Amazon Web Services, Inc.

Entity and parent seam. Amazon Web Services, Inc. is a Delaware subsidiary owned by Amazon.com, Inc. The exact AWS entity recurs on both company edges. Kuiper Government Solutions LLC is a separate Amazon-family communications entity and is not included.

Confirmed roles. AWS is the classified cloud platform for NNSA Secret and Restricted Data scientific workloads (w1-compute-aws-005) and a deployed DISA Olympus/Joint Operational Edge provider (w4-cyber-aws-olympus-006).

Inference-only path. No separate inferred company edge is added. Public records do not expose every NNSA workload, American Science Cloud order, JWCC task or future IL6 transition.

Strongest position and controlling rails. The strongest position is a real exact-entity bridge between classified scientific compute and deployed defense cloud/edge infrastructure. It depends on the DOE/NNSA compute-and-data plane (xd-rail-doe-compute-data-01) and the classified cloud, ICAM and authorization plane (xd-rail-classified-cloud-icam-02).

Maturity and adverse state. Both roles are operating infrastructure, so the open clock is authorization, workload migration, task ordering and accepted performance. Canceled JEDI is not a current edge; JWCC ceilings are not AWS orders; and multi-cloud alternatives prevent an exclusivity claim. Microsoft, Google, Oracle and HPE are the principal registry alternatives.

Next gates. Recheck NNSA scientific-cloud tasking and workload scope by 2026-11-24; recheck DISA IL6, AWS-specific tasks and accepted edge workloads by 2026-12-31; test sustained cross-sector ordering by 2027-06-30.

Monitoring surfaces. Review NNSA and DOE Office of Science releases monthly; DISA DWWCF, JWCC and Joint Operational Edge releases monthly; USAspending and SAM.gov biweekly; and Amazon subsidiary exhibits annually.

2. Space Exploration Technologies Corp.

Entity and parent seam. Space Exploration Technologies Corp. is the exact privately held entity named on the NSSL path. Federal records corroborate the SpaceX brand on the Space Data Network path. The dossier creates no separate Starlink entity and makes no beneficial-ownership inference.

Confirmed roles. SpaceX is a national-security launch provider under NSSL and SDA orders (w3-space-spacex-031) and holds the $2.29 billion Space Data Network prototype delivery order (w4-comm-spacex-001).

Inference-only path. The network is not yet an accepted operational backbone or recurring service. NASA communications demonstrations do not create a NASA service-purchase edge.

Strongest position and controlling rails. The company provides the map's clearest launch-plus-network vertical bridge. Relevant rails are advanced packaging, photonics and optics, launch and checkout, space-ground/relay interoperability and OTA transition (xd-rail-packaging-foundry-04, xd-rail-photonics-optics-05, xd-rail-launch-checkout-10, xd-rail-space-ground-relay-11, xd-rail-ota-transition-13).

Maturity and adverse state. Launch is an executable requirements-and-order lane; the communications backbone is a prototype. A launch mishap, stand-down, mission reassignment or failed network milestone can affect separate clocks, but one does not automatically prove failure of the other. ULA, Blue Origin and Rocket Lab provide launch alternatives; SES, Telesat and Viasat provide communications alternatives, none automatically interchangeable.

Next gates. Recheck clean NSSL/SDA mission execution by 2026-10-31; Space Data Network integration evidence by 2026-11-18; and government acceptance and authorization of the planned operational prototype by 2027-12-31.

Monitoring surfaces. Review Space Systems Command monthly, SDA per mission, NASA SCaN quarterly, and exact-entity SAM.gov/USAspending records biweekly.

Cross-dossier findings

1. Cloud and data concentration is an authorization problem before it is a capacity problem

AWS and Oracle have direct cross-sector infrastructure positions; Palantir has a confirmed operational AI position plus an uncontracted scientific-data path. The common choke point is not a generic claim that science needs cloud. It is the sequence of exact task, identity, security level, ATO, workload migration, data access and accepted operation. Ginkgo's autonomous workflows add a downstream scientific-data consumer, but the record does not assign every workflow to one provider.

The practical implication is to monitor task and authorization objects, not vendor market share. JWCC multi-award status, NNSA's separate path, on-premises systems and other providers create redundancy only after portability and authorization.

2. Launch-plus-network verticality is real but not yet operationally complete

SpaceX is the clearest vertically recurring company: it can place national security payloads in orbit and is building a funded optical-mesh backbone. L3Harris provides a different vertical pattern across tracking satellites, terminals and radios. Neither dossier proves an accepted end-to-end company architecture. Launch delivery, checkout, ground interoperability, cyber authorization and mission-service acceptance remain separate gates.

That distinction keeps a launch anomaly from becoming an automatic network failure and keeps a satellite build award from becoming accepted custody or communications service.

3. Test and qualification integrators sit closer to transition than generic technology vendors

Booz Allen's HPM support, Northrop's QTIME role, Ginkgo's autonomous-platform integration and GE Vernova's industrial validation show a recurring company position between invention and government acceptance. These integrators can shape test data, interfaces, deployment and qualification without owning the underlying weapon, model, biological product or transformer.

Their value to the map lies in the transition surface, but so does their risk: an undefinitized contract, planned recompete, unaccepted inspection method, failed closed-loop workflow or uncommissioned equipment can stop the path even when the core technology is promising.

4. Science-stack recurrence is strongest where physical and digital workflows meet

AWS and Oracle provide compute and data infrastructure; Palantir supplies an operational-data pattern but no confirmed DOE implementation; Ginkgo integrates robotics, experiments and biological manufacturing; GE Vernova connects generative engineering to industrial validation. These are different layers, not one consortium stack.

The high-value research question is whether named work products cross the layers: authorized data into a laboratory, accepted experiment into a model, model output into qualified equipment, or laboratory result into production. Until those bridges are named, recurrence is company-specific rather than a confirmed shared architecture.

5. Defense-prime recurrence is broad, but maturity transfers poorly

Lockheed Martin Corporation, Northrop Grumman Systems, L3Harris, Raytheon Company and BAE IE&SI all recur across strategic mission lanes. Their strongest positions differ:

  • Lockheed has GPS production heritage and installed HELIOS hardware;
  • Northrop has direct PTS-P build, GPI design and manufacturing-qualification work, all before final acceptance;
  • L3Harris bridges satellite construction with terminals and radios;
  • Raytheon combines mature interceptor production with far less mature DE field assessments;
  • BAE combines a production modification with an early receiver design lane.

None of those facts permits technical maturity to transfer from one program to another. The controlling evidence is always the program's own delivered, tested, accepted or produced state.

6. Entity-family hazards are now a first-order monitoring object

The dossiers expose six recurring attribution hazards:

  • AWS is not Kuiper Government Solutions;
  • Ginkgo Bioworks, Inc. is not automatically Ginkgo Biosecurity, LLC;
  • Lockheed Martin Corporation is not Lockheed Martin Aculight Corp.;
  • GE Vernova Operations is not GE Packaged Power, GE Vernova Hitachi Nuclear Energy Americas or Prolec GE;
  • Raytheon Company is not RTX Corporation or another RTX subsidiary;
  • BAE IE&SI is not interchangeable with BAE Systems, Inc., BAE Systems plc or another BAE operating company.

Northrop's abbreviation variants and L3Harris punctuation variants can be normalized because the legal entity is the same. Brand similarity alone is not the rule. Exact signatory, novation, subsidiary exhibit and award paper control.

Criteria for the next dossier expansion

Do not create another dossier tier by company size, public familiarity or stock performance. Add a company only when at least one of the following evidence conditions is met:

  1. the same resolved legal entity holds direct current roles in two or more canonical sectors;
  2. a single company controls a verified shared rail or a documented cross-sector acceptance surface;
  3. an exact-company event could propagate across two or more registry edges through a named facility, authorization, component, eligibility or capacity mechanism;
  4. a portfolio-adjacent node gains a named federal integration object that converts adjacency into shared capability;
  5. a historical node receives a new current instrument that restores executable acquisition or operating proximity;
  6. an unresolved brand or parent edge gains sufficient primary evidence to resolve the exact signatory without collapsing corporate entities.

Microsoft, Boeing, Dynetics and Accenture Federal Services remain useful controls, not automatic additions: their current cross-sector state is portfolio adjacency or historical recurrence. HPE should not become an exact-company dossier until the unresolved DISA Cloud Instance signatory is resolved.

The next work should therefore deepen these twelve dossiers and add only evidence-qualified nodes. It should not become a company ranking or another sector directory.
