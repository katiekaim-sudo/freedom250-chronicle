1. Accountant's answer
The current standing repo is not centrally cleared.
Today the Federal Open Market Committee directs the New York Fed's Open Market
Trading Desk to make overnight secured financing available for the System Open
Market Account. Eligible counterparties submit propositions through the
New York Fed's proprietary FedTrade Plus system. BNY then acts as tri-party
agent, taking custody of collateral, valuing it, applying margin and settling
the transaction on BNY's books.
The Federal Reserve's accounting is straightforward:
the repo is a financing transaction;
the Federal Reserve records an asset, securities purchased under
agreements to resell;
interest income accrues over the overnight term;
the repo asset and related income are participated among all 12 Reserve
Banks using their domestic SOMA shares; and
the transaction reverses at maturity when the counterparty repurchases the
collateral.
The central-clearing proposal would insert a private clearinghouse between the
Federal Reserve and the market counterparty. That can give dealers and banks a
common legal counterparty against which qualifying repo and reverse-repo
positions may be netted. The stated policy benefit is lower intermediary
balance-sheet cost and greater willingness to use the standing operation when
market repo rates rise.
The accounting question is therefore not simply, "Should the Fed modernize the
plumbing?" It is:
Should the Reserve Banks assume new clearinghouse, margin, default-fund,
settlement-bank, software and concentration obligations in order to reduce
the gross balance-sheet exposure of private intermediaries and improve the
Federal Reserve's interest-rate-control tool?
No final public design answers that question as of the cutoff. New York Fed
staff said on 2026-07-09 that the operations remain uncleared and that staff
continue to evaluate the benefits and costs.

Participation among the 12 Reserve Banks
New York executes and holds the repo for SOMA, but the economic asset is not
New York's alone. The Financial Accounting Manual requires repo agreements to
be participated to every Reserve Bank using its designated domestic SOMA
share. The same is true of the related interest income.
That produces two simultaneous records:
operating custody and execution: New York, FedTrade Plus and BNY; and
System economics: an undivided participation allocated across the 12
Reserve Banks.
The participating percentages are recalculated through the annual
interdistrict settlement process. Specific collateral is not assigned as a
standalone security package to each Bank.

5. The membership decision is the real control decision
FICC is the obvious case study because it has the mature Treasury repo service,
but the Federal Reserve has not selected FICC. ICE Clear Credit and CME
Securities Clearing are also registered Treasury clearing agencies. The
accounting consequences differ by clearinghouse and access model.
Model A — New York becomes a direct FICC Netting Member
Structure
Federal Reserve Bank of New York ↔ FICC ↔ dealer/bank member
What it accomplishes
FICC becomes the legal counterparty after novation.
Qualifying private intermediary positions against FICC may enter the same
netting set.
The Federal Reserve avoids using a private dealer as its sponsor or agent.
Unresolved rule and accounting gates
FICC's listed ordinary categories include banks, dealers, futures commission
merchants, interdealer brokers, government-securities issuers, insurance
companies, registered clearing agencies and registered investment companies.
A Federal Reserve Bank is not expressly named as a repo Netting Member
category.
Rule 2A permits an "Other Applicant" to be considered if direct access would
provide material benefit and FICC establishes appropriate standards.
FICC may create another category, but a new category and its standards
require SEC approval.
The rulebook already refers to Federal Reserve Banks in the processing of
Treasury auction purchases. That proves an operational connection, not that
a Reserve Bank is an admitted repo Netting Member.
An ordinary direct Netting Member must be able to meet clearing-fund,
margin, funds-only and other obligations. The public record does not say
whether the Federal Reserve would accept ordinary loss-mutualization duties
or receive a central-bank exception.
Assessment: structurally cleanest, but not ready under a publicly complete
membership and loss-allocation record.
Model B — New York becomes a Sponsored Member
Structure
Federal Reserve Bank of New York
↕ private Sponsoring Member
↕
FICC ↔ dealer/bank member
The sponsor is the processing gateway and assumes defined guaranty,
reimbursement, collateral and indemnity responsibilities. This model could
produce central clearing without direct Federal Reserve membership.
Control problem: a private financial institution would stand between the
central bank's monetary-policy operation and the clearinghouse and would be
paid for doing so. That introduces sponsor credit, pricing, conflict,
portability and concentration questions. No public evidence shows the Federal
Reserve chose this structure.
Assessment: technically plausible but institutionally awkward.
Model C — New York is an Executing Firm Customer of an Agent Clearing Member
Structure
Federal Reserve Bank of New York
↕ private Agent Clearing Member
↕
FICC ↔ dealer/bank member
The FICC rulebook says the Agent Clearing Member acts as agent but remains
fully liable to FICC for all financial and other obligations. FICC's
obligations run to the Agent Clearing Member, not directly to the executing
customer.
Control problem: the Federal Reserve would rely on a private agent that is
the legal obligor to FICC and the recipient of FICC payments and deliveries.
Assessment: available as a general market structure; no evidence makes it
the proposed Federal Reserve structure.
Model D — special central-bank category or bespoke rule
Structure
Federal Reserve Bank of New York ↔ FICC special central-bank account
A dedicated category could specify:
whether Federal Reserve capital or a clearing-fund deposit is required;
whether the Federal Reserve participates in mutualized losses;
whether FICC can assess the Federal Reserve after another member defaults;
whether the Federal Reserve can be suspended or defaulted;
whether margin is posted by the Federal Reserve, its counterparties, or both;
whether funds settle directly in the Federal Reserve's own account rather
than through a private Funds-Only Settling Bank;
what happens when FICC is unavailable; and
how counterparty-level data and two-year Federal Reserve disclosures
interact with clearinghouse reports.
Assessment: most likely to expose the actual policy bargain. It would also
create the clearest public rule-filing trail.
Model E — another Treasury clearing agency
ICE Clear Credit or CME Securities Clearing could compete for the role. That
would change membership, default-fund, settlement-bank and software
interfaces. No public source reviewed here establishes that either was
selected for standing repo.

The key incidence finding
The public argument identifies a private accounting friction: the market
intermediary cannot net an uncleared Federal Reserve repo against centrally
cleared positions. Central clearing may reduce that private gross balance
sheet. The Federal Reserve would incur at least some combination of:
system development;
clearing fees;
clearing-bank charges;
margin and collateral administration;
legal and membership work;
testing and contingency operations; and
concentration and default-management exposure.
That does not prove the proposal is a subsidy or a bad policy. It proves the
cost-benefit analysis must show both sides of the ledger.

Software-accounting rule
The FAM makes the control entity more important than the coding location:
preliminary design, alternatives, vendor selection and proof-of-concept
costs are expensed;
qualifying development, integration, coding and pre-acceptance testing costs
can be capitalized;
internally developed software requires at least $100,000 of qualifying
cost for capitalization;
externally purchased software uses a $25,000 threshold;
maintenance, routine fixes, user training and post-implementation testing
are generally expensed;
a new interface can qualify as a capital improvement;
the Reserve Bank hosting the product/function office that controls the
software generally records the asset, even if another Bank develops it or a
vendor hosts it; and
useful life generally cannot exceed five years without Board staff approval.
Therefore a centrally cleared standing repo project could generate both
capitalized software and current expense. The public budget does not isolate
FedTrade Plus, a future clearing interface, or the controlling host Bank well
enough to calculate either amount.
9. The BNY concentration does not disappear
Current standing repo already relies on BNY for:
custody;
collateral pricing;
margin;
cash and securities settlement; and
the maturity unwind.
FICC's 2026 fee schedule also identifies BNY as its clearing agent bank and
passes actual BNY settlement charges, Fedwire charges and some daylight
overdraft charges to members.
A FICC layer could therefore produce:
New York Desk → FICC → BNY → Fedwire/NSS
instead of:
New York Desk → BNY
Central clearing would add a guaranty and netting layer but may preserve the
same private settlement-bank dependency. The public case for the proposal
should disclose:
whether BNY remains the sole or primary clearing agent;
the backup route if BNY is unavailable;
whether bilateral BNY settlement remains an authorized fallback if FICC is
unavailable;
which event constitutes final settlement for Federal Reserve accounting;
and
who bears extra liquidity cost when FICC, BNY, Fedwire Securities or NSS is
unavailable.
FICC rules permit an extended Fedwire failure to qualify as possible "good
cause," but a clearing-bank problem does not cancel a member's obligation. A
Funds-Only Settling Bank refusal or failure generally leaves the Netting
Member responsible for paying through the prescribed alternate route.
The SEC separately said on 2026-07-20 that a Treasury clearing agency may
permit required market transactions to settle bilaterally during a clearing-
agency outage. Federal Reserve transactions are exempt from the mandate, so
this is not a ready-made Federal Reserve fallback rule. It is evidence that a
bilateral contingency is legally conceivable and should be designed in
advance.

12. What the public story says the project is for
The strongest stated chain is:
repo market rate rises
↓
dealer considers standing repo
↓
uncleared Fed trade cannot be netted against its cleared book
↓
gross balance-sheet cost makes dealer reluctant
↓
standing facility is used less than pure price economics would suggest
↓
market repo pressure can transmit into the federal funds rate
↓
central clearing creates a common counterparty and possible accounting netting
↓
dealer uses standing repo more readily
↓
Fed can control the policy rate with a smaller reserve buffer
This is a coherent monetary-policy explanation. It also reveals that the
proposal is not primarily about making the Federal Reserve's own accounting
simpler. It is about making a Federal Reserve transaction fit the private
intermediaries' centrally cleared accounting and risk architecture.

15. Bottom line
The proposed change is not "the Fed joins a modern platform." It is a choice
about legal counterparty, private netting benefit, default mutualization,
settlement-bank concentration and software ownership.
The clean accounting test is:
name the legal counterparty after novation;
name every cash, collateral, margin, fee and loss object;
identify the entity that books each object;
identify the Reserve Banks that ultimately absorb the expense or exposure;
disclose which private parties receive the netting benefit and service
revenue; and
show the fallback transaction when the clearinghouse or BNY is unavailable.
Until those six items are public, central clearing remains a policy proposal
with a credible rate-control rationale but an incomplete cost and control
ledger.
