## 5A.1 — January 9: Venezuelan oil revenue becomes protected sovereign property

[EO 14373](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/15/2026-00831/safeguarding-venezuelan-oil-revenue-for-the-good-of-the-american-and-venezuelan-people) declares a national emergency concerning judicial attachment of **Foreign Government Deposit Funds**. Its definition covers funds paid to or held by the U.S. Government in designated Treasury accounts on behalf of Venezuela, its agencies or instrumentalities—including the Central Bank and PDVSA—when derived from natural-resource sales or sales of diluents.

The order makes five unusually precise legal findings:

- the funds remain property of the Government of Venezuela, not the United States or a private judgment creditor;
- U.S. possession is custodial and governmental, not market participation;
- the funds are not to be used for U.S. commercial activity;
- State determines public, governmental or diplomatic disposition on Venezuela's behalf; and
- placement in a U.S. account does not waive sovereign immunity.

Attachment, judgment, lien, garnishment and other judicial process are prohibited and deemed null and void unless authorized. Treasury must designate the funds to show their sovereign status, follow State's disbursement instructions and assert immunity with Justice in proceedings.

This is a real decision-right map. Treasury has custody; State has disposition; Justice defends the legal perimeter; Energy can be consulted; Venezuela retains attributed ownership; private creditors are excluded from the account.

### June 10: the legal account gains a usable payment rule

[Venezuela General License 46C](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/935661/download) authorizes qualifying U.S.-entity transactions involving Venezuelan-origin oil and petrochemical products. It requires:

- U.S. or specified foreign contract law and dispute resolution;
- monetary payments to blocked persons—other than local taxes, permits or fees—to be made into the EO 14373 Foreign Government Deposit Funds or another Treasury-instructed account;
- reporting for certain non-U.S.-destination transactions; and
- continued compliance with other Federal authorities.

The license also excludes, among other things, noncommercial terms, gold or Venezuelan-government digital-token payments, blocked vessels and specified Russia, Iran, North Korea, Cuba and China-linked counterparties.

This makes the custody architecture operationally callable. It still does not prove that an eligible cargo moved or a payment entered the account. The same June action amended licenses for U.S. diluents, services, investment-related activity, oil/gas operations, minerals and PDVSA transactions. Authorization is not execution.

**Maturity.** Effective sovereign-property and attachment rule plus a later contract/payment interface.

**Counterevidence and limit.** No public account identifier, balance, deposit, split, creditor adjudication, State disposition order or beneficiary payment was isolated. The United States is custodian, not owner. A license removes sanctions prohibitions for defined conduct; it is not an oil sale, title transfer, cash receipt or Venezuelan recovery program.

## 5A.2 — January 16: the Board of Peace receives international legal personhood

[EO 14375](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/22/2026-01271/designating-the-board-of-peace-as-a-public-international-organization-entitled-to-enjoy-certain) designates the Board of Peace as a public international organization entitled to privileges, exemptions and immunities under the International Organizations Immunities Act. The order does not create all of the Board's governance rules; it recognizes the organization and supplies the U.S. privileges layer.

The Board's [founding Charter was signed January 16 and launched at a January 22 Davos ceremony](https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/01/president-trump-ratifies-board-of-peace-in-historic-ceremony-opening-path-to-hope-and-dignity-for-gazans/). The May 15 report to the UN Security Council, [S/2026/418](https://docs.un.org/S/2026/418), records the institutional structure:

- executive boards;
- Dr. Ali Sha'ath leading the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza;
- Nickolay Mladenov as High Representative for Gaza;
- Major General Jasper Jeffers commanding the International Stabilization Force; and
- the Office of the High Representative as the coordinating interface among the Board, Palestinian administration, security force, guarantors and other parties.

The Board convened its inaugural meeting on February 19. By the reporting cutoff it described itself as a functioning international organization with a charter and **28 member States**; the NCAG was constituted, the ISF had completed a pre-deployment assessment under unified U.S. command, and the High Representative produced the first six-month Security Council report.

### A primary-record correction: $17 billion pledged, not $5 billion

S/2026/418 says **$17 billion had been pledged for reconstruction**. That supersedes the earlier working-ledger shorthand of “more than $5 billion.” The maturity label remains pledge. The report does not supply a receipt, donor-by-donor payment ledger, procurement, outlay or completed recovery project.

The May 21 Security Council meeting supplied important counterevidence. The High Representative and UN participants described a ceasefire that was holding imperfectly, continuing humanitarian crisis and **no tangible recovery** at the necessary scale. NCAG stood ready rather than fully governing Gaza; the ISF had assessed rather than completed deployment; the weapons-decommissioning roadmap remained unresolved.

**Maturity.** Legal recognition, charter, member body, named governance organs, operating coordination office, pre-deployment planning and formal international reporting.

**Counterevidence and limit.** Privileges and immunities are not universal sovereignty or unlimited authority. A U.S. EO cannot by itself settle the Board's status in every jurisdiction. Pledges are not cash; a committee ready to enter is not governing; assessment is not deployment; ceasefire maintenance is not recovery; a report's claims require independent event evidence.

## The vessel-state theorem

The two objects reveal the Capricorn wave's basic institutional move:

`purpose that cannot yet be consummated → legal vessel capable of surviving until consummation`

For Venezuela, the vessel is an account classification with custody, immunity, disposition and licensing rules. For Gaza, it is an international organization with privileges, organs, an office and a reporting relationship.

Neither vessel makes the underlying resource appear. Yet each changes what can happen next:

- a qualifying Venezuelan payment now has a legally protected destination; and
- a peace plan now has named people who can coordinate, report, assess and eventually administer.

The state is building a **carrier of future action**, not proving the future action has occurred.
