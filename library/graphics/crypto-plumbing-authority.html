Freedom 250 · Banks and the dollar · Who is in charge

Who has legal authority over each part of the U.S. securities and payments plumbing?

No single body. The library maps 89 U.S. laws, agencies, market bodies and failure-handling roles, joined by 96 recorded legal links; 37 of them are the market institutions, accounts and settlement services themselves. The agencies with the most supervisory links into market institutions and settlement services are Securities and Exchange Commission (12), Commodity Futures Trading Commission (7) and Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System (5). The Financial Stability Oversight Council designated 8 utilities as systemically important (CLS Bank International, Chicago Mercantile Exchange, DTC, FICC, ICE Clear Credit, NSCC, Options Clearing Corporation and The Clearing House Payments Company); under Title VIII each has a supervisory agency: Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System for The Clearing House Payments Company and CLS Bank International; Commodity Futures Trading Commission for Chicago Mercantile Exchange and ICE Clear Credit; Securities and Exchange Commission for DTC, NSCC, FICC and Options Clearing Corporation. DTC alone answers to 4: Financial Stability Oversight Council (designates), Securities and Exchange Commission (Title VIII supervisory agency), New York Department of Financial Services (state trust supervision) and Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System (state member bank supervision).
