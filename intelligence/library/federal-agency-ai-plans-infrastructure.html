Trump added a super-intelligence team without taking power from agencies

How is federal agency AI and service-delivery infrastructure moving from fragmented systems into shared access, network and triage layers, and which authority, implementation, operation and effect gates remain open?

Trump added a super-intelligence team without taking power from agencies

The Super Intelligence Force adds White House coordination and a reported 120-day review above existing artificial-intelligence programs. It does not yet have a public charter, budget or power transferred from the agencies.

What we found

  • Trump announced the Super Intelligence Force on October 4 with DNI Jay Clayton, FTC Chairman Andrew Ferguson, Defense R&E chief Emil Michael and OPM Director Scott Kupor as its four leaders, reporting to the President and Chief of Staff Susie Wiles.
  • The group is described as coordinating Federal engagement with consumers, public-interest and religious groups, critical-infrastructure providers and AI companies. A reported charter gives it 120 days to assess risks, opportunities and Federal responsibility, but the charter itself was not public at the cutoff.
  • The White House Accord asks frontier-model companies for internal controls, an empowered internal review team, an external auditor or evaluator and an independent board committee. It is voluntary and names no regulator, public reporting duty, test standard, penalty or remedy.
  • The September 29 orders supply the immediate control stack: EO 14434 changes executive-branch wording but borrows the existing legal definition of AI, while EO 14432 directs a shared America.gov front door and expressly leaves records, authority and final decisions with originating agencies.
  • The FBI remains the operating specimen: NERI consolidated more than 600 fragmented networks and TIPS is deployed for AI-assisted tip triage with human review, but whole-enterprise completion and Patel's 140-use, 605% and latency claims still lack reconciled public proof.

Why it matters

The government is rebuilding itself through shared access layers, consolidated infrastructure and AI routing, but it has not become one database or one decision-maker. The legal owner, authoritative record, security authorization, human action and measured result remain separate gates.

What to watch

  • ~2027-01-31 to 2027-02-01 · The SIF report, its exact charter and due date, public recommendations, minority views, and any descended legislation, rule, procurement clause or operating program.
  • pending · A public SIF charter, staff and budget record, records owner, full membership, and formal relationship to OMB, OSTP, NIST and the Chief AI Officer Council.
  • on publication of the DOJ 2026 AI inventory · Whether a row-level FBI inventory reconciles Patel's 140 uses, stages, high-impact status, ATOs and any withheld or classified count.
  • ~2026-12-28 · OMB's America.gov implementation memo and any exact covered-service integration instructions.
  • FY2027 budget and audit cycle · A NERI completion-by-enclave record, TIPS latency and error metrics, funded modernization receipts, and closure or updated disposition of the OIG AI and FISMA recommendations.
