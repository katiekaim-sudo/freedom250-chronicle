What it takes for quantum tech and new encryption to win federal approval

A federal agency does not accept a quantum device or post-quantum encryption because a vendor says it works. It needs a chain of separate proofs ending in a named official's sign-off, and most companies are still partway down that chain.

What we found

  • Executive Order 14413 (signed June 22, published June 25, 2026) sets up an effort to deliver a useful quantum computer to a DOE site, with DOE deadlines at 90 and 180 days and three priority quantum-sensor projects meant to be fielded by Sept 30, 2028. It names no vendor, award or funding.
  • For new encryption, FIPS 203, 204 and 205 are final; FIPS 206 is still in development. The bottleneck is moving from algorithm test (CAVP) to validated module (FIPS 140-3) to an agency's authority to operate. NIST's own example shows post-quantum services can sit inside a validated module but be marked non-compliant.
  • GSA lists only Entrust and WidePoint as federal PKI shared-service providers. Entrust has a FIPS 140-3 module plus separate post-quantum algorithm evidence; WidePoint has no comparable public post-quantum chain.
  • NIST paused new applications for crypto testing labs, so the few existing accredited labs (such as atsec, Lightship and Leidos AT&E) are a scarce choke point.
  • Commerce's nine Quantum CHIPS letters of intent propose $2.013 billion but are not final agreements. NIST's initial $20 million deal with SRI sets up a quantum manufacturing center. No public post-quantum setup for the Genesis system was found.

Why it matters

This is the thesis inside the machinery: the change runs through orders, standards, test labs and sign-off rules rather than a sudden ban, and whoever runs those gates shapes the transition.

What to watch

  • 2026-09-21 to 2026-09-22 · FIPS 140-2 modules move to historical status for new-system purchases; vendors without a 140-3 certificate, such as Crypto4A, hit this cliff.
  • 2026-10-15 · DARPA's QBI independent verification call closes; watch for the named awardee and conflict-of-interest terms.
  • 2026-10-22 · Non-national-security agency post-quantum plans due under OMB M-26-15, including DOE priorities.

Research summary

Quantum and PQC acceptance is an evidence chain: calibration and validation, component and manufacturing evidence, algorithm and module validation, product mapping, integration, and a named authority's authorization remain distinct. A standard, consortium, LOI or validated module is not an operating-system acceptance decision.
