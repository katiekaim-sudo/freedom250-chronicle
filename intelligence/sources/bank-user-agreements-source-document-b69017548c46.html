Source created: 2026-07-12 · Collection evidence cutoff: 2026-07-15

Portable Markdown · Connected Library

Provenance & audit (Claude, 2026-07-12). Delivered by Katie; audited before ingestion by an independent verification pass against live primary sources. The packet's method is unusually strong — it separates source classes (10-K vs newsroom vs survey vs agreement), and it distinguishes CONFIRMED CHANGE (a proven before/after) from CURRENT TERM (what a live document says today). That discipline is why the audit could find its seams. Corrections below are BINDING on the derived layer; the body is reproduced verbatim.

① BofA — the finding holds, but the window is different and the packet's "June 2" agreement could not be found. The §12 arbitration/mass-arbitration insertion is REAL and independently corroborated without the Internet Archive: the May 18 update PDF carries the clause verbatim, its scope does reach Zelle, RTP, ACH, wires, bill pay and Erica, and a June 4 2026 Senate letter (Blumenthal/Warren/Johnson) plus NCLC and Public Citizen all describe it as newly adopted after ~17 years without pre-dispute arbitration. BUT: the live agreement at the packet's own URL today reads effective March 23, 2026, contains the word "arbitration" zero times, and banners the May 18 update as forthcoming. So (a) the insertion window is Mar 23 → May 18, 2026, TIGHTER than the packet's Jan 31 → May 18, and (b) the packet's "2026-06-02 CONFIRMED CHANGE" (transfer clocks, Deals rename) is unverified — demoted. Do not carry the June 2 delta. ② U.S. Bank AI clause — DOWNGRADED to CURRENT TERM. The clause is real and verbatim as quoted ("we may use any data that is input by you to further train or develop AI models"), on a page last modified 2026-02-20. But the packet's proof that it was introduced in February rests entirely on two Internet Archive snapshots, which the audit could not open. Provable today: the TERM exists, strongly dated to Feb 2026. Not provable: the CHANGE. Treat as a current term with circumstantial dating until someone re-verifies the snapshots. ③ Franklin — "reverse" is too strong. The filing says the transfer agent holds the private key for hosted wallets and that its blockchain-integrated system is "under the unilateral control of the transfer agent," which "has the ability to correct errors." But it also says a blockchain record "can never be deleted" — the transfer agent corrects an unauthorized transaction by appending an offsetting instruction, not by reversing it. Use "correct by offset," never "reverse." (The finding survives intact and is arguably sharper: the token cannot be un-written, and the custodian still wins.) Also: the cited text says only "the Fund's transfer agent" — it never names Franklin Templeton Investor Services. The attribution is probably right; it is not sourced. ④ Two dates are wrong. The Chase Digital Services Agreement is labeled "2025-11-06" in the packet; the live document says last updated June 2026. The AI-agent language is verbatim correct — the date is not. Any inference that Chase had AI-agent terms as early as Nov 2025 is unsupported. And the Synchrony "2026-01-01" effective date is not established; the CareCredit form carries a 3/2026 revision code. ⑤ One misattribution. Discover's move to Virginia governing law is real, but it comes from the cardmember agreement, not the merger FAQ as the packet states. VERIFIED CLEAN: Chase's third-party-AI-agent definitions and disclaimer; Capital One N.A. as legal Discover issuer from 2025-05-18; Synchrony's batches-of-fifty mass-filing procedure; the substance and scope of the BofA clause.

Structural note for the next pass: the packet's two headline "confirmed change" proofs were both Internet-Archive-dependent. Where a live primary source or a third-party record (Senate letters, NCLC, CFPB agreement archive) can carry the same weight, prefer it — an audit that cannot open the Archive cannot reproduce the finding.

Bank user agreements — legal-entity and contract-change map

Updated: 2026-07-11
Status: first full agreement sweep; current terms and proven changes are kept separate
Scope: consumer deposit, credit-card, digital-service, open-banking, rewards, portfolio-transfer, and servicing agreements for the entities in the banking website sweep.

Document-retrieval results: Agreement document requests — resolution log

Reading rule

The brand on the card or app is not necessarily the legal counterparty.

For every product, identify separately:

  1. holding company — owns the corporate group;
  2. bank/issuer — owes the deposit or extends the credit;
  3. program/brand company — supplies rewards, merchant access, interface, or marketing;
  4. servicer — bills, collects, furnishes credit data, handles disputes, or runs the account platform;
  5. network/data intermediary — routes payments or data;
  6. successor/assignee — can acquire the account, balance, or agreement.

A current clause is not a recent change. This record uses CONFIRMED CHANGE only where an official amendment, prior agreement, merger notice, conversion notice, or dated comparison establishes the before and after state.

1. Bank of America — new digital-services arbitration constitution

2026-05-18 / 2026-06-02 — 2026 arbitration and mass-claim regime

Status: CONFIRMED CHANGE FROM JANUARY 2026 AGREEMENT
Legal counterparty: Bank of America
Instrument: Online Banking Service Agreement

Bank of America's May 18 replacement agreement and current June 2 agreement contain a binding-arbitration clause, jury and class waiver, and a mass-arbitration process. A preserved official January 31 page lacks the new §12 entirely, proving that this digital arbitration constitution was inserted after the January agreement and by May 18. Its scope is not confined to a checking account. It covers claims tied to online/mobile banking, Zelle, Real Time Payments, bill pay, ACH, wires, internal transfers, alerts, Deals, Erica, advertising, and the application, denial, approval or establishment of an online/mobile ID.

Consumer customers have sixty days from first delivery of the arbitration provision to opt out online or by phone. Small-business users do not receive that consumer opt-out. The provision states that it survives account closure, payment, bankruptcy, and sale or assignment.

The mass-arbitration trigger is twenty-five or more coordinated similar claims. Claims must meet individualized filing requirements; a process arbitrator can screen and dismiss deficient claims; each side initially selects fifteen cases, producing thirty-case rounds. The agreement delays fees on unselected claims and permits sanctions under Rule 11-style standards.

Primary sources: Preserved January 31 official page; May 18, 2026 agreement update; Current agreement — effective June 2, 2026

2026-06-02 — transfer clocks and Deals name change

Status: CONFIRMED CHANGE FROM MAY 18 TEXT

The June agreement changed transfer-limit accounting from a rolling-or-calendar method based on customer profile to fixed calendar clocks: the daily period resets at 12:00 a.m. Eastern and the weekly period resets Monday at 12:00 a.m. Eastern. The disclosed $3,500 daily and $10,000 weekly limits did not change. The agreement also replaced “BankAmeriDeals” with “Deals” in the automatic-enrollment/data-sharing clause and the arbitration scope. The bank may share anonymized transaction information with vendors, merchants, third parties and card networks to present offers and administer rewards.

Legal significance

The digital access layer now has its own dispute constitution reaching multiple payment rails and Erica. The agreement explicitly follows the account through a later sale or assignment. A transfer of the underlying deposit or debt therefore does not necessarily free the customer from this digital arbitration clause.

Comparison resolved

The January-to-May insertion is proven. The May-to-June comparison separately proves the transfer-clock and Deals terminology changes. Deposit, card, Merrill and small-business agreements remain separate contracts and may carry different dispute rules.

2. JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. — arbitration survives assignment and is engineered for mass filings

2024-11-17 — Chase replaces the deposit-account arbitration section

Status: CONFIRMED CHANGE
Legal counterparty: JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A.
Instrument: Deposit Account Agreement

Chase's official 2024 change notice replaced the arbitration section and gave then-current customers until January 16, 2025, or sixty days after account opening, whichever was later, to opt out by phone. The replacement reaches past, present and future claims involving accounts, transactions, products, services, advertising, applications, denials, approvals, employees and agents.

It bars class, representative, consolidated and public-injunctive relief in arbitration. “We” includes Chase affiliates, third-party beneficiaries and agents; if Chase assigns the account to an unaffiliated third party, the definition expands to include that assignee.

Primary source: Chase 2024 agreement and replacement notice

2026-03-15 — current mass-arbitration process

Status: CURRENT TERM; core agreement effective March 15
Legal counterparty: JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A.

The 2026 agreement defines mass arbitration at twenty-five or more coordinated claims. It requires claimant-specific transaction/event facts, damages calculations and authorization; adds a process arbitrator; permits threshold dismissal and sanctions; and sends an initial thirty cases—fifteen chosen by each side—through merits arbitration before later rounds.

Customers may opt out only within sixty days after opening the account. The arbitration provision continues after closure, full payment or bankruptcy. Chase also retains assignment rights, and permitted customer assignments remain subject to Chase setoff rights.

Primary source: Chase Deposit Account Agreement — current 2026 edition

2026-06-14 — incorporated fee schedule, not a new deposit agreement

The June date belongs to “Additional Banking Services and Fees for Personal Accounts.” Compared with the March schedule, it adds Chase Sapphire Checking and Chase Premier Plus Checking to the linked accounts that can waive the $4.95 Chase Secure Checking fee. The three waiver paths remain. Reporting this as a June replacement of the core deposit agreement would be incorrect.

Primary sources: June fee schedule; Preserved March-era schedule

Legal significance

The contract is designed not only to prevent class actions but to manage the later tactic of filing many nominally individual arbitrations. Successors, agents and an unaffiliated account assignee can receive the benefit of the dispute clause.

2025-11-06 digital agreement — third-party AI agents enter the access contract

Status: CURRENT DATED TERM; prior official version not obtained to prove entry date
Named entities: JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A.; J.P. Morgan Securities LLC as applicable; broad parent, affiliate, licensee, successor, agent, contractor, designee and assignee definition

Chase's Digital Services Agreement now defines autonomous, semi-autonomous and non-autonomous third-party AI agents. A customer who connects an agent consents to sharing sensitive account data; Chase says its privacy rules do not govern the third party, disclaims reviewing or auditing the agent's security and privacy, and says it may rely on instructions or actions initiated after the customer supplies credentials or access.

The same digital contract permits Chase to modify terms with continued use as acceptance, suspend or terminate services for any or no reason, assign the agreement within its corporate group or to successors, and delegate work to contractors. Ending the digital agreement does not end the underlying bank or investment account.

Primary source: Chase Digital Services Agreement

3. Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. — online terms change and Bilt splits into three entities

2026-05-21 — Wells updates Online Access Agreement

Status: CONFIRMED CHANGE
Legal counterparty: Wells Fargo Bank, N.A. and other named Wells entities depending on service
Instrument: Online Access Agreement

Wells' current official change notice identifies operational telephone-number updates for brokerage accounts and services. Continued use after the effective date constitutes acceptance. The current agreement also contains exclusive-jurisdiction language for disputes outside enforceable arbitration, but the available change notice does not prove that language was newly added on May 21, so it is retained as a current term rather than promoted as a confirmed amendment.

Primary sources: Wells Fargo — summary of May 21 changes; Current Online Access Agreement

2025-10-15 — Zelle access rules for minors change

Status: CONFIRMED CHANGE
Legal entity: Wells Fargo Bank, N.A.
Network/service: Zelle

Wells added an adult-control regime for customers ages thirteen through seventeen. An adult co-owner may revoke permission; revocation removes the enrolled email or mobile number and cancels pending and scheduled payments. At eighteen, the customer may enroll without co-owner authorization.

Primary sources: Wells Fargo — Zelle update notice; Wells Fargo Zelle addendum

Current Paze entity/data stack

Status: CURRENT TERM

Early Warning Services operates Paze. Wells may send eligible card credentials and identity data—including name, taxpayer identifier, birth date and contact details—to the wallet; the merchant receives tokenized card and identity/contact data. Early Warning may use device, browser and transaction identifiers for fraud, internal research, anonymized reports and compliance. Customers can use Paze's opt-out mechanism.

Primary source: current Wells Online Access Agreement above.

2026-02-07 — legacy Bilt card converts to Wells Fargo Autograph

Status: CONFIRMED PRODUCT/CONTRACT CONVERSION
Old issuer: Wells Fargo Bank, N.A.
Legacy program entity: Bilt
Converted Wells product: Wells Fargo Autograph

Wells' conversion page states that existing Bilt World Elite Mastercard accounts were being converted to Wells Fargo Autograph cards beginning February 7, 2026.

Primary source: Wells Fargo — Bilt conversion

2026 — new Bilt cards use Column N.A. and Cardless

Status: CONFIRMED NEW ENTITY STACK
Issuer: Column N.A.
Servicer: Cardless Inc.
Program/rewards entity: Bilt

Bilt's current offer and referral terms state that Bilt Blue, Obsidian and Palladium cards are issued by Column N.A.; Cardless is the servicer; Bilt supplies the program and rewards layer; Mastercard remains the network.

Primary source: Bilt Card 2.0 Offer Terms

Conversion mechanics recovered

Bilt's transition record proves that choosing Bilt 2.0 opened a new Column/Cardless credit account rather than renaming the Wells account. Customers could preserve the card number, autopay and saved credentials, but a Wells balance moved only through an optional “Special Transfer.” The new agreement treats that transfer as a purchase while excluding it from introductory APR and new-card rewards. A retained Wells account converted separately to Autograph; existing Bilt points remained even if the customer did not open the new card.

The new Column agreement supplies its own forty-five-day mailed arbitration opt-out and reaches Column, Cardless, servicers, agents, affiliates, assignees, successors, related merchants and collectors. It is a new-account agreement, not a proven amendment to the old Wells account.

Primary sources: Bilt transition record; Bilt Obsidian agreement

Legal significance

“Bilt card” is no longer a sufficient entity description. The old credit account stayed with Wells and became Autograph; the new Bilt program is a Column-issued, Cardless-serviced product. Rewards, credit, billing, data and dispute rights can therefore sit in different contracts with different entities.

4. Discover Bank → Capital One, N.A. — counterparty and governing law move under a retained brand

Before 2025-05-18 — Discover Bank is issuer

Status: Former state
Issuer: Discover Bank
Governing state law: Delaware

The December 2024 secured-card agreement named Discover Bank as issuer, used Delaware law, allowed assignment without notice, and contained individual AAA arbitration, a class waiver and a thirty-day opt-out.

Primary source: Discover Secured Cardmember Agreement — December 16, 2024

2025-05-18 — Discover Bank merges into Capital One, N.A.

Status: CONFIRMED LEGAL-ENTITY CHANGE
Successor issuer/counterparty: Capital One, N.A.
Governing state law: Virginia to the extent state law applies

The merger FAQ says Discover Bank merged into Capital One, N.A.; the agreements became agreements with Capital One; federal law and Virginia law now govern. The Discover brand, account number, rewards and interface initially remained.

Primary sources: Discover banking merger FAQ; Discover card merger FAQ

2025-06-30 — post-merger Discover agreement

Status: CONFIRMED POST-MERGER AGREEMENT
Issuer definition: “Discover” means Capital One, N.A.

The post-merger agreement largely retains Discover's arbitration structure, including individual AAA arbitration, class/private-attorney-general waiver, small-claims carveout, better-than-offer award feature, thirty-day mailed opt-out, and survival after sale or assignment.

Primary source: Discover Secured Cardmember Agreement — June 30, 2025

Split inside Capital One, N.A.

Capital One's own general consumer-card agreement publicly available on its site does not contain the Discover arbitration architecture. It does permit unilateral changes subject to law, suspension or closure even without default, credit/income information collection, broad contact, and assignment without customer permission or advance notice.

Primary source: Capital One consumer-card agreement

Post-merger product families remain separate

The legal consolidation did not produce one digital or credit contract. Discover deposits retain their account mechanics under Capital One, N.A.; a later change effective May 17, 2026 gives in-person Capital One checks the same availability treatment as Discover checks. Discover personal-loan proceeds generally may not directly pay another Capital One, N.A. account except select Discover personal loans. Discover wallet terms also remain separate from Capital One Mobile Payments terms, which expressly exclude Discover-by-Capital-One accounts. PayPal and Amazon rewards terms changed entity labels and authorize rewards-balance sharing without proving a change in reward economics.

Primary sources: Discover national deposit agreement; Discover personal-loan terms; Discover wallet terms; PayPal rewards terms; Amazon rewards terms

Legal significance

One legal bank now holds two visibly different inherited contract families. The corporate merger did not instantly harmonize Discover and Capital One arbitration or rewards rules.

5. Synchrony Bank — confirmed mass-arbitration rewrite

2024 agreement state

Status: Former comparison state
Issuer: Synchrony Bank

A June 2024 public agreement provided individual arbitration, class waiver, AAA or JAMS administration, a forty-five-day opt-out, and a less developed mass-arbitration treatment.

Primary source: CFPB archive — Synchrony Preferred Mastercard, June 2024

2026-01-01 — detailed coordinated-claim procedure

Status: CONFIRMED AGREEMENT CHANGE
Issuer: Synchrony Bank
Program partner: PayPal in this example

Current PayPal terms add a mandatory individualized Notice of Dispute, a sixty-day informal-resolution period, Rule 11-style certification, and batching of coordinated claims into groups no larger than fifty, organized by state and alphabetically, with one merits arbitrator and one set of fees per batch. The forty-five-day arbitration opt-out remains.

The agreement makes PayPal and relevant assignees, owners, servicers and collectors intended beneficiaries. It also permits unilateral changes, assignment without prior notice, multichannel contact, broad information sharing, and closure for any reason; closing the linked PayPal account automatically closes the card.

Primary source: Synchrony / PayPal current account terms

CareCredit and OnePay/Walmart comparison

CareCredit's current agreement contains the same sixty-day informal process, individualized certifications and batches no larger than fifty, proving the hardened system is broader than PayPal. A 2024 CFPB agreement supplies the earlier CareCredit state.

OnePay/Walmart is a new Synchrony program launched in 2025, not a conversion of Capital One's former Walmart portfolio. Synchrony Bank is issuer; One Finance Inc. and affiliates govern the platform under a separate contract with its own arbitration/class waiver; rewards have separate OnePay terms.

Primary sources: Current CareCredit agreement; 2024 CareCredit agreement; OnePay card agreement; OnePay platform terms

Legal significance

The merchant brand is not the creditor, but the merchant and downstream account actors can receive the contract's arbitration protection. Synchrony has affirmatively redesigned the process for mass individual claims.

Former bank/brand: Ally
Buyer group: CardWorks
Successor issuer: Merrick Bank
Customer interface: Ollo

Ally sold $2.3 billion of receivables and 1.3 million active cardholders to CardWorks; Ally's annual report says the sale closed April 1. CardWorks had been the servicing partner from the program's inception.

Primary sources: Merrick public baseline agreement; Current Ollo agreement

Limitation

The issuer migration is proven. An account-specific comparison is still needed before claiming that a new arbitration clause was imposed on former Ally customers.

7. Bread Financial → Comenity Bank / Comenity Capital Bank / Comenity Servicing LLC

Current entity stack

Status: CURRENT LEGAL STRUCTURE
Parent/brand: Bread Financial
Issuers: Comenity Bank or Comenity Capital Bank, product-specific
Servicer: Comenity Servicing LLC

Comenity Bank is a Delaware state limited-purpose credit-card bank; Comenity Capital Bank is a Utah industrial bank. Product pages must be checked individually to identify which bank is the creditor.

Primary examples: Comenity Bank terms; Comenity Capital Bank terms

Current consumer dispute and control terms

The Comenity Bank agreement has a thirty-day personally signed mailed arbitration opt-out; a class, private-attorney-general and joinder waiver; a mandatory individualized claim notice and cure period; assignment rights; unilateral term changes; closure, suspension or nonrenewal without notice; broad affiliate/servicer/collector contact consent; and credit, employment and income information rights.

Status note: Current term; entry date into the consumer contract has not yet been proven.

Historical notice-and-cure comparison

November 2018 Comenity forms already required pre-suit/arbitration notice and cure, an individually retained lawyer and personally signed arbitration rejection. They also offered a $5,100 special payment when a claimant beat rejected pre-demand relief. The current form adds express good-faith negotiations, tolling, court power to enjoin noncompliant filings and restrictions on an administrator accepting them; it no longer contains the $5,100 incentive and makes the small-claims exception bilateral. The safe insertion range is between the 2018 and current/Q1-2026 forms, not specifically 2025–2026.

Primary sources: 2018 Comenity Bank form; 2018 Comenity Capital form; Current Comenity terms

2025–2026 servicing-layer changes

Status: CONFIRMED INTERCOMPANY OPERATIONAL CHANGE; not itself a consumer contract change

Bread filings say Comenity Servicing LLC services substantially all loans. Its service agreement was restated and amended several times in 2025. A 2026 amendment describes omnichannel/mobile/account-center engineering, fraud, credit-risk, marketing and loyalty APIs, collection systems, and an AI-driven servicing assistant.

Primary sources: Bread servicing-agreement history; Bread servicing amendment

Legal significance

The brand, creditor and operating machine are three entities. AI-driven servicing is being placed into the affiliate service contract even where the consumer agreement still names one of the Comenity banks as creditor.

12. PNC Bank, National Association

Current agreement

Status: CURRENT EDITION; change not proven
Effective date: May 31, 2026
Legal counterparty: PNC Bank, National Association

PNC provides individual arbitration and waivers of class, private-attorney-general, representative and joined claims. The definition reaches affiliates, predecessors, acquired entities, successors, assigns and assets acquired from failed banks. Customers get a forty-five-day mailed opt-out, but later amendments do not reopen the opt-out unless PNC changes a substantive arbitration clause.

PNC retains setoff, freeze and investigation powers; either side can close at any time and for any reason; the bank may temporarily reopen a closed account for later credits, debits or disputes.

Primary source: PNC Consumer Account Agreement

Conversion watch

Grouped findings — legal structure, not product interpretation

A. Arbitration is being made portable

Chase, BofA, Citi, PNC, Citizens, AmEx, Discover and Merrick use language that reaches successors, assigns, acquired entities, agents or buyers. The contractual dispute shield is designed to travel with an account when the debt or bank changes hands.

B. Banks are hardening against mass arbitration

The post-class-action response is no longer just “individual arbitration.” Chase, Bank of America, Synchrony and Truist now have specific intake, certification, screening, batching, process-arbitrator, or staged-case mechanics for coordinated individual claims.

C. A brand can survive a complete legal-counterparty change

  • Discover remained Discover while Discover Bank disappeared into Capital One, N.A.
  • Ally card customers retained many product terms while Merrick became issuer and Ollo became interface.
  • Bilt remained the customer-facing brand while new cards moved from Wells to Column/Cardless and legacy Wells accounts converted to Autograph.
  • Apple Card remains Goldman-issued while a future Chase transfer has already been agreed.

D. The customer may have several contracts for one apparent product

A card can involve a bank agreement, rewards agreement, digital-access agreement, merchant-program terms, privacy notice, network rules, benefit insurance, and a servicing/data-sharing authorization. Arbitration, forfeiture and data rights can differ across them.

E. Digital-service agreements are becoming operating constitutions

BofA's agreement reaches Erica and multiple payment rails; U.S. Bank's reaches AI model training; Truist's reaches continuous open-banking sharing and instant transfers; Wells binds changes through continued digital use. The digital agreement may now govern more day-to-day conduct than the basic deposit agreement.

F. Closure, freeze and setoff powers remain broad

Most deposit agreements allow closure without cause, freezes for suspected irregularity or disputes, and setoff against customer funds. These powers belong to the named bank entity and may extend to debts owed to affiliates, owners or beneficial owners depending on the agreement.

G. Embedded finance requires transaction-role mapping

Cross River makes the legal chain especially visible: brand, marketing platform, originating bank, processor, servicer, credit furnisher, collector and receivable owner may be different companies. A website sweep must capture each role before treating a partner-branded account as the bank's own retail product.

H. Asset managers require fund-by-fund attribution

Franklin Templeton is not one consumer counterparty. The public parent, distributor, adviser, registered fund, 529 plan, custodian, transfer agent and digital platform can each have a separate legal role. Research and growth statements from the group cannot prove conduct by an individual fund or adviser.
