# Southeast Fraud Enforcement Partnerships — Primary-Source Object Ledger

## Control rule

This is the controlling factual spine for the July 30 announcement. It admits
only official release, official video, official organization/memo, and
official U.S. Attorney/court objects. The release itself is a useful locator,
not proof that each described case resulted from the newly announced State
agreements.

## II. What the Southeast announcement actually announced

### A. Cases and the aggregate

DOJ announced 17 `recent` cases across Alabama, Florida, Georgia, Louisiana,
Mississippi, North Carolina, and South Carolina. It labels their aggregate as
`over $350 million in intended losses`; it separately says that, since July 4,
federal prosecutors charged cases reaching more than $90 million in loss and
12 named defendants.

The release mixes cases at different procedural stages and does not say that
each was opened through, or caused by, a July 30 agreement. The April Division
memo required USAOs to report ongoing taxpayer-fraud matters and significant
expected events to the Division; that sequence makes a regional roll-up
plausible, but this is an inference about the release mechanism, not proof of
case-level causation.

### B. Data-sharing agreements

The release announces agreements with:

| State office group named by DOJ | States named |
|---|---|
| Secretary of State | Alabama, Florida, Georgia, Louisiana, Mississippi, South Carolina |
| State Treasurer | Florida, Mississippi, South Carolina |

DOJ says the agreements provide the Fraud Division access to `publicly
available corporate registration and public benefits payment data held by these
state agencies`, for analysis of links and patterns between entities and
payment activity, including shell companies and layered structures.

**Not published at cutoff:** instrument title or number; named signatories;
signed PDF; date of execution/effectiveness; source agency for each payment
dataset; fields/data dictionary; API, file or portal method; query authority;
frequency; confidentiality/security; retention; correction; audit; user
governance; a record of any actual query, referral, payment hold, or recovery.
North Carolina is not named in the data-agreement group.

### C. Task forces and roundtable

DOJ announced federal-State anti-fraud task forces in North Carolina,
Mississippi, and Florida. The only further public description located is a
Mississippi Attorney General quote calling its body `Joint Task Force
Vigilance`, with the AG's office, two USAOs, and FBI, intended to surge
resources and personnel. No charter, order, membership roster, scope,
delegation, referral protocol, meeting record, or performance measure has been
located for any of the three.

The release says the action followed a recent roundtable of 18 USAOs, seven
State AG offices, five Federal partners, and more than 50 State officials. It
publishes a participant roster, but not the roundtable's date, place, agenda,
minutes, commitments, recording, or written products.

## IV. Strongest supported formulation

> On July 30, DOJ publicly announced a Southeast expansion of the Fraud
> Division's federal-State partnership model: a 17-case mixed-status enforcement
> roll-up; high-level data-sharing agreements for State-held public corporate
> and benefits-payment records; and three announced task-force structures. The
> public record does not yet establish their operative terms, actual data use,
> case-level causation, pre-payment intervention, or recovered aggregate funds.

## V. Case-audit result

The 17 rows are a curated catalogue spanning at least a May 2023 sentence,
2024 conduct/pleas, 2025 indictments, and 2026 pleas/indictments. Their diverse
procedural and amount clocks confirm that July 30 was not one newly filed
regional case, and the current primary record does not establish that the new
State agreements generated any particular listed matter.
