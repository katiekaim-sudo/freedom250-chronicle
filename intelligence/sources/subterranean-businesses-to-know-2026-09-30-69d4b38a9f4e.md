## Short answer

The most important companies are not one clean list of likely winners. They form three different rings:

1. **Proven subterranean ecosystem:** Flyability, ANYbotics, Exyn, Boston Dynamics, Scientific Systems, SNC, Emesent, Coordinated Robotics and the Field AI lineage carry direct program, platform or team evidence from DARPA's Subterranean Challenge.
2. **Defense-transition enabling stack:** Overland AI, Carnegie Robotics, Forterra, Persistent Systems, Silvus, goTenna, IonQ/Vector Atomic, SandboxAQ and Astra Navigation have real U.S. defense paths in autonomy, communications or resilient PNT, but no public Project Meridian or current subterranean award.
3. **Commercial underground scale:** Epiroc and Sandvik show what integrated underground drilling, hauling, traffic management, mapping and connectivity look like in operating mines. Their technical fit is high; their public U.S. defense path is zero in this map.

The strongest near-term architecture is therefore a **system-of-systems team**, not a single prime: a mobile body, a hardware-agnostic autonomy layer, a continually reconciled map, deployable mesh relays, hybrid GPS-free navigation, modular hazard sensors and an integration authority that decides what counts as an accepted mission result.

## The most consequential company seams

### Field AI × Boston Dynamics

This is the clearest current commercial bridge from SubT lineage to a modular product combination. Field AI supplies risk-aware autonomy that can operate without GPS, prior maps or cloud connectivity; Boston Dynamics supplies a commercially supported legged platform. Their 2026 partnership specifically names uncharted underground mines as an example. That still is not a U.S. subterranean award.

### Emesent × heterogeneous fleets

Emesent is unusually important because its SubT evidence joins aerial autonomy, LiDAR SLAM, a common map and robot-deployed wireless networking. The missing U.S. gates are acquisition access, security/export controls and an accepted government performance record.

### Persistent Systems / Silvus / goTenna

These companies occupy different network positions. Persistent and Silvus have stronger Army production or program proximity; goTenna emphasizes low-SWaP, low-bandwidth mesh and store-and-forward behavior. The underground question is not generic radio quality. It is how many relays are required, who deploys them, what survives a cave-in or power loss, and whether the map remains usable after partitions reconnect.

### IonQ/Vector Atomic × SandboxAQ × Astra Navigation

These are enabling PNT bets, not current subterranean suppliers. Optical timing, inertial sensing and magnetic navigation may improve holdover and correction, but the practical solution will almost certainly remain hybrid: inertial + LiDAR/visual/radar SLAM + map matching + cooperative ranging + local beacons. Underground magnetic clutter and calibration are explicit disconfirmers.

### Epiroc × Sandvik

The mining companies are the industrial reality check. They already integrate autonomy, mapping, traffic management, connectivity, access control, drilling and haulage. But commercial mine automation can depend on prepared workings, fixed infrastructure and heavy support. Military utility adds rapid deployment, smaller passages, signatures, contested access, damage recovery and austere logistics.

## Who is not yet visible

The current public field is weakest in four places:

- the integrator that would own the authoritative surface/subsurface operating picture;
- the supplier of a government-accepted robot-deployed relay/PNT node;
- the prime for modular CBRN, air-quality and structural-collapse payload integration; and
- the contractor that can make tactical boring, reinforcement, ventilation, spoil management and underground power deployable rather than mine-scale.

Those gaps are more important than adding dozens of generic robotics names. The next research return should be an instrument, test report, team announcement or accepted field result that resolves one of them.

## Reading rule

`FIT` measures technical relevance. `PATH` measures public proximity to the target acquisition or transition path. `EVID` measures source strength. A company can have `FIT 4 / PATH 0`: excellent underground capability with no public U.S. defense bridge. A direct historical team member can have `PATH 4` while its current product or workshare remains unresolved. Neither condition predicts a Meridian recommendation.

## Bottom line

The businesses most worth following are those that can cross boundaries between lanes. The likely winning architecture needs robots that carry different payloads, autonomy that continues through communications loss, a network that reconstructs itself, PNT that degrades gracefully and industrial tools that can sustain the environment. The decisive company may be the integrator that turns those separate products into one accepted underground mission system—and that company is not yet visible in the public Project Meridian record.
