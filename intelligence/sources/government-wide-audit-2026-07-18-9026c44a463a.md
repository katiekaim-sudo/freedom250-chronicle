# Government-Wide Audit — July 18, 2026

## Executive call

The July 14–18 record does **not** show one government-wide monetary switch being
thrown. It shows four distinct layers moving at different speeds:

1. **Congressional oversight:** five core monetary hearings and one fraud hearing
   occurred. They produced testimony, commitments, draft text and advocacy—not
   enactment or implementation.
2. **Legal and rule clocks:** the GENIUS one-year rule deadline arrived with no
   public final implementing rule located at the cutoff. The Act's outside
   effective date remains January 18, 2027 unless a later final rule accelerates
   it. A CFTC final was published and an NSCC approval order issued, but their
   publication, effective and operating states remain separate.
3. **Operating infrastructure:** DTC used tokenized assets in production-environment
   trades on July 15. That is a real operational milestone, but not the scheduled
   October full participant-service launch.
4. **Control architecture:** HSGAC, GAO and 14 in-window federal OIG reports add
   identity, payment-integrity, spending-traceability and regulator-insider-risk
   evidence. Recommendations, compliance findings, improper payments and fraud
   findings are different objects.

The dominant monetary plotline is therefore a **law-to-operations bottleneck**:
Congress is still contesting institutional perimeters while regulators and
market utilities move through proposal, approval, publication, test, production
milestone and future-launch clocks.

## Scope and method

- **Formal audit window:** July 15–18, 2026 ET.

- **Clock rule:** hearing, introduction, approval, publication, effective date,

### Deadline state

Public Law 119-27 required the primary federal payment-stablecoin regulators,
Treasury and state regulators to promulgate implementing regulations within one
year of enactment. The official Federal Register inventory located proposals and
notices but no final GENIUS implementing rule by the July 18 cutoff. The precise
call is: **deadline date arrived; no public final located as of cutoff**. Because
July 18 was a Saturday, the Federal Register public-inspection list must be
replayed Monday, July 20 before closing the late-posting check.

This missed/unfinished rule clock does not itself make the entire Act generally
operative. The outside effective date remains January 18, 2027 unless final
regulations trigger the alternative 120-day clock first.

## Integrated monetary analysis

### 1. The Fed is auditing its operating system, not replacing it yet

Warsh's five workstreams make the internal design review unusually legible:
communications; balance-sheet size/composition and ample reserves; data and
methodology; productivity/employment; and inflation frameworks. His hearing
commitments create a discovery and public-communication sequence through
year-end 2026. They do not start a new reserve-framework or balance-sheet
implementation clock.

### 2. Market utilities are moving ahead of the legislative perimeter

DTC's production-environment tokenized trades are the strongest operational
delta in the window. The SEC/NSCC order and CFTC final add real rule-state
movement. Meanwhile, congressional market-structure objects remain unreconciled.
This is the current architecture: utilities can advance within existing
authority and approvals while Congress continues to negotiate the statutory
SEC/CFTC perimeter.

### 3. CFPB change remains an administrative, judicial and legislative contest

The hearings showed an agency leadership posture and a committee draft, not a
completed structural transfer. Appropriations, enforcement standards,
supervision, section 1071 and open banking must continue to be tracked by their
own rule, case, personnel and legislative clocks.

### 4. The fraud plotline now includes control of the overseers

The evidence chain is now:

1. identity proofing;
2. eligibility/program decision;
3. payment gate and destination;
4. measurement and classification;
5. award/subaward traceability and watchdog review;
6. investigation, recovery and prosecution; and
7. access controls inside the regulators and watchdog institutions themselves.

GAO's Login.gov testimony connects identity theft to redirected benefit
payments. VA's approximately $3.9 billion in FY2025 improper and unknown
payments coexists with compliance on all six PIIA requirements; PBGC's separate
below-threshold posture shows why “compliant” never means “zero.” The Fed and SEC
OIG items add the insider-access layer. HSGAC's witness claims remain attributed
claims and should not be promoted to audit findings without independent support.
