3 · THE LEGAL SPINE (the deep dig — what the law actually says)

3a · The parent node is RIGHT — and the government agrees in writing

The sovereign has corporate legal personality in the oldest, most respectable sense — Chief Justice Marshall, 1823: "The United States is a government, and, consequently, a body politic and corporate." And an 1886 case goes further: the United States is "a great corporation … ordained and established by the American people." The root node stands on two centuries of case law.

Born 1776, re-chartered twice. Under the continuity doctrine the state dates from the Declaration; the Articles of Confederation (in force 1781-03-01) and the Constitution (effective 1789-03-04, government operational 1789-04-30) are successive governments of the same state. The proof is in the paper itself: Article VI carries the Confederation's debts forward "as valid against the United States under this Constitution, as under the Confederation" — the oldest assumption-of-liabilities clause in our story — and the 1783 Treaty of Paris bound the new government without renewal. In our language: same reporting entity, two charter restatements.

3b · The accountant's canon exists, and it is SFFAS 47

The government writes GAAP for itself, and the standard answering "is this entity the government?" is FASAB SFFAS 47 — Reporting Entity (2014, effective FY2017). Its scheme:

  • In the group if ANY of: in the Budget, majority-owned, or controlled with risk of loss / expectation of benefit — plus a misleading-to-exclude backstop. Control indicators: appoint/remove executives, approve budgets, veto board decisions, finance deficits, rescind the framework. And explicitly: regulatory power and economic dependency are NOT control. The hinge of the whole Entity Theory is stated as black-letter accounting standard.
  • Once in, sorted consolidation vs disclosure: consolidation entities have governance "vertically integrated" with elected officials; disclosure entities have autonomy-protecting governance or temporary relationships — the standard says outright that conservatorships are generally disclosure entities. Third tier: related parties (significant influence, outside the group).

What the audited Financial Report of the US actually does:

Treatment Who
Consolidates (~150+ entities) Three branches, all departments, USPS, TVA, PBGC, FDIC, SEC, NCUA, Ex-Im, MCC, DFC, RRB, FCSIC — and the Smithsonian
Discloses (the complete list — five names) Federal Reserve System · Fannie Mae · Freddie Mac · Amtrak · the COVID-era SPVs
Related parties Federal Home Loan Banks · IMF · the multilateral development banks · PEFCO
Outside entirely Farm Credit System banks (only their insurer FCSIC consolidates), Red Cross, National Academies, SIPC, the FFRDC operators

So the government's own audited books draw our dashed lines — Fed dashed ✓, Fannie/Freddie dashed ✓ — and then correct us three times: Amtrak should be dashed too (we drew it solid), Smithsonian should be solid (we parked it as ambiguous quasi), and the chartered-but-private bodies shouldn't be inside at all.

Two glorious footnotes from the standard itself: - FASAB refused to classify the Fed in the rule text — and spelled out in its basis-for-conclusions what consolidation would mean: SOMA's ~$4T of Treasuries would eliminate against the public debt, and Federal Reserve notes would land on the government balance sheet as a liability. The standard-setter looked straight at our question and filed the answer where it wouldn't bind. The disclosure classification is what keeps "monetary" and "fiscal" as separate books. - CFPB is statutorily inside the Federal Reserve System, yet consolidated directly into the government-wide report. One limb of the Fed is on the books while the body is off. (And the entity trying to kill the CFPB keeps losing precisely because the funding runs through the off-books body.)

3c · The 1871 / “USA Inc.” claim — the legal distinction

  • What the DC Organic Act of 1871 actually did: merged Washington City, Georgetown, and Washington County into one municipal government — "a body corporate for municipal purposes." A city charter, replaced in 1874. It says nothing about the nation, and no court has ever read it otherwise.
  • What 28 U.S.C. § 3002(15) actually does: sits inside the 1990 Federal Debt Collection Procedures Act, opens "As used in this chapter," and defines "United States" to include federal corporations, agencies, and instrumentalities — so the government's debt-collection machinery works for money owed to TVA or Ex-Im. It treats federal corporations as arms of the United States — the opposite of declaring the United States a corporation.
  • What IS true nearby — the honest ground: the sovereign has body-politic-and-corporate personality (1823, 1886, above); DC genuinely is a municipal corporation; the US charters corporations at will and today operates dozens, some privately owned. The interesting claim was never "the US secretly incorporated in 1871." It's that the corporate form has been the sovereign's operating instrument since Hamilton — which is this track's actual thesis, and it's simply, boringly true.

3d · The Fed seam — our drawing is half right

The law, confirmed: Reserve Banks are private corporations for some purposes (the 1982 Ninth Circuit tort case), instrumentalities for others; member-bank stock is not equity — can't sell it, can't pledge it, dividend capped, elects only 6 of 9 directors, and on liquidation the surplus goes to Treasury, not the members. No residual claim = not ownership, in any accounting sense. Meanwhile the Board of Governors is unambiguously a federal agency — and the 2025–26 removal fight (SCOTUS blocked the Cook removal ~29 Jun 2026: structural change to the Fed "must come from Congress") was litigated on exactly that premise.

So dashing the whole System off the sovereign understates the very real federal control chain. The true seam is at the Board–Banks joint, not the sovereign–System joint. The money overlay also needs district-level accounting clocks. The 2025 audited statements show $6.281B of cash transferred to Treasury while the combined deferred asset increased $27.526B to $243.481B. Atlanta, Dallas and St. Louis recorded current/cumulative remittances, and Atlanta also settled a prior-year payable. The defensible statement is therefore not that every remittance was suspended after September 2022; it is that affected Banks retain future net earnings until their own deferred assets are recovered, while Bank earnings, accrual, cash transfer and the combined deferred-asset balance can move differently. See Federal Reserve Financial Constitution — Source Document.

3e · The GSE seam — dashed is right, anchor is wrong

Conservatorship since Sep 2008; Treasury holds senior preferred + warrants for 79.9% of the common; the 2021 SCOTUS case blessed the net-worth sweep and courts can't restrain the conservator. "Release" needs no statute — the FHFA Director can end it — BUT the 2 Jan 2025 PSPA amendment restored Treasury's consent right over any release. As of July 2026: still in conservatorship, IPO talk cooling (the FHFA director now double-hatted as DNI; analysts say 2026 spin-out likely off), a June 2026 House bill package sketching a statutory release framework.

The accountant's split-screen, documented: Treasury's audited books treat Fannie/Freddie as disclosure entities (off-balance-sheet); CBO treats them as governmental and ON the budget. The two official scorekeepers disagree about whether $7T of balance sheet is inside the group. Under ordinary corporate GAAP a controlling conservator holding 79.9% warrants would consolidate, full stop. The government's exemption from its own logic is the seam.

3f · The third seam we missed: Amtrak

Statute says Amtrak "is not a department, agency, or instrumentality of the United States." The Supreme Court (2015) says the opposite: "the practical reality of federal control and supervision prevails over Congress' disclaimer" — Amtrak IS governmental for constitutional purposes. And the Financial Report lists it as a disclosure entity — the only operating company on that five-name list, sitting beside the Fed and the GSEs.

3g · A fourth, quieter seam: the Federal Home Loan Banks

Congress lists the FHLBanks as "mixed-ownership Government corporations" in the Government Corporation Control Act — while Treasury's Financial Report calls them mere related parties, outside the group. Two arms of the canon disagree in print.
