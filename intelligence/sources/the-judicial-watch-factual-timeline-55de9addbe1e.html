The Judicial Watch — factual timeline

Updated: 2026-07-11 Status: working factual spine Scope: every court case that controls, restrains, or defines the monetary transition — Fed structure and removal, Reserve-account gates, charters and preemption, Treasury-market rules, sanctions and emergency power, sovereign assets, seizure and digital custody, agency funding and rescue money, payment data and AML.

Inclusion rules

  • One entry = one legally or operationally meaningful case event (not one docket filing), imported from the audited Judicial Money Map. Descriptions state procedural effect, then institutional effect; no motives.
  • Case IDs (JM-…) and event IDs (E-…) refer to Judicial Money Map — Source Document, which carries the dossiers, crosswalks, party ledgers, and verification notes. The registry grows with each map revision — never trust a count written in prose; the seed builder prints the live totals.
  • Eight tracks: Throne (removal & independence) · Purse (funding, rescue money, conservators & review bars) · Gates (Reserve accounts) · Perimeter (charters, preemption, market lines) · Sword (sanctions & emergency power) · Hoard (sovereign assets) · Vault (seizure, forfeiture & digital custody) · Ledger (payment data, privacy & AML).

Charting convention (Katie's rulings, 2026-07-11 and 2026-08-09)

  • Anchor: HH:MM — a verified clock time preserves the event's true instant. A non-D.C. source clock is converted to that instant, then displayed in Washington Eastern time and cast with Washington angles.
  • Anchor: daytime — released or heard during the day, no verified clock → noon Washington time.
  • Anchor: date-only — nothing about the clock is public (e-filings, signed orders, effective dates) → midnight Washington time.
  • Anchor place "unlocated" — the source site remains flagged as unlocated; the chart is still Washington.
  • Scheduled deadlines keep the standing rule: midnight Washington time, day of.

JM-01 — Cook

Caption: Trump v. Cook — SCOTUS No. 25A312; below D.D.C. and D.C. Circuit
Question: May the President immediately remove a sitting Fed Governor "for cause," without notice and a chance to answer — and is the Governor's tenure protection constitutional?
State: Stay denied 2026-06-29. Fed Governor for-cause protection held constitutional; removal requires notice and an opportunity to respond. Cook keeps her seat and votes while the merits continue below.
Stakes: One Governor's chair is one FOMC vote — and the ruling drew the constitutional fence around the Fed itself: the one agency the removal revolution doesn't reach.

JM-02 — Slaughter

Caption: Trump v. Slaughter — SCOTUS No. 25-332; below D.D.C.
Question: Does the FTC's for-cause removal protection survive Article II?
State: Reversed 6–3 on 2026-06-29: FTC protection unconstitutional; Humphrey's Executor no longer shields the modern commission. The Fed was expressly treated as a distinct historical arrangement.
Stakes: Every independent financial commission except the Fed is now removable at will — the President's hand reaches SEC-style bodies, and only the central bank keeps its carve-out.

JM-03 — CFPB funding

Caption: CFPB v. Community Financial Services Ass'n — SCOTUS No. 22-448 (2024)
Question: Is the CFPB's capped draw from the Federal Reserve a valid appropriation?
State: Upheld 7–2 (2024): a standing source-and-purpose appropriation satisfies the Appropriations Clause.
Stakes: Self-funding through the Fed is constitutional in principle — which made the later fight (JM-04) about starving the channel in practice, not its legality.

JM-04 — NTEU v. Vought

Caption: NTEU v. Vought — D.D.C. 1:25-cv-00381; D.C. Cir. 25-5091 & 26-5220 (en banc)
Question: May the executive stop work, refuse funding, cancel contracts, and RIF an agency Congress created — and can courts stop it?
State: Preliminary restraints partly hold; panel vacatur wiped by en banc grant; 2026-06-19 limited remand with jurisdiction retained. No final rule on how small the CFPB may lawfully become.
Stakes: The live test of whether an agency can be killed by management rather than statute — the purse fenced but not filled.

JM-05 — Consumers' Research

Caption: FCC v. Consumers' Research — SCOTUS No. 24-354 (2025)
Question: Did Congress unconstitutionally delegate revenue power over the Universal Service Fund, or the FCC subdelegate it to a private administrator?
State: Upheld 6–3 (2025): adequate guidance, agency retains final say, private role advisory only.
Stakes: The public-regulator/private-administrator funding machine survives — the closest analogue blessing hybrid money plumbing run through private bodies.

JM-06 — Custodia

Caption: Custodia Bank v. Federal Reserve Board & FRB Kansas City — D. Wyo.; 10th Cir. 24-8024; SCOTUS No. 26-62 Question: Does statutory eligibility give a state-chartered bank a right to a Reserve Bank master account?
State: Tenth Circuit: no — Reserve Banks keep discretion to deny eligible institutions. Cert petition filed one day before the extended deadline; disposition pending.
Stakes: THE gate case. Every crypto bank, fintech trust, and state money experiment needs the Fed's rails; whether access is a right or a favor is now before the Court — or settled for a generation if cert is denied.

JM-07 — Banco San Juan

State: Affirmed 2026-05-13: no statutory right to continued service; FRBNY's termination authority stands.
Stakes: The gate swings both ways — an account once granted can be taken. Second and Tenth Circuits now converge on Reserve Bank discretion.
