# Wave 3F — Personnel Perimeters and Institutional Name

**Research window:** Exact 2025 Cancer ingress through the eve of exact 2025 Libra ingress.  
**Question:** When this wave changed the federal body's people and public identity, did it replace institutions, or did it redraw appointment, bargaining and naming perimeters inside legal persons that largely survived?  
**Finding:** The three orders in this packet alter three different institutional layers. EO 14317 creates a centrally reviewed noncareer appointment box for policy-making and policy-advocating roles. EO 14343 removes named agencies and subdivisions from the chapter 71 labor-management system. EO 14347 authorizes “Department of War” as a secondary executive-branch title while expressly preserving the statutory Department of Defense. These are real changes in who can be placed, who must be bargained with and how an institution presents itself. They are not one general conversion of the civil service, one mass firing, or one new cabinet department.

The common chain is:

`institutional identity claim → legal classification → central review or exclusion → personnel / bargaining / naming practice → later record → contest or statutory gate`

The crucial distinction is that **office, employee, bargaining unit, public title and statutory person are five different objects**.

## Three perimeter changes, three clocks

| Order | Immediate object | Decision right that moved | Operating body found | What did not follow automatically |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EO 14317 · Schedule G** | Noncareer policy-making or policy-advocating position | Agency use routes through OPM intake and White House Presidential Personnel review | OPM's July 29 appointment code and central approval workflow | No named position, appointee, conversion or firing is proved by creation of the schedule |
| **EO 14343 · labor exclusions** | Named agency, subdivision or operating unit | Chapter 71 bargaining and exclusive-recognition duties are switched off for covered units | Later OPM instructions to terminate affected CBAs, petition FLRA and recode employees | The employee, agency and statutory mission do not disappear; unaffected units retain bargaining rights |
| **EO 14347 · War secondary title** | Public and non-statutory name | Defense leadership may use and require specified secondary titles | October 10 implementation memo, title list, signage/reference work and a renaming tiger team | The legal Department of Defense is not replaced; Title 10 and statutory references remain controlling |

## 3F.1 — July 17: Schedule G separates political-policy advocacy from the career box

[EO 14317](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/07/23/2025-13925/creating-schedule-g-in-the-excepted-service) created Schedule G for **noncareer** positions that are policy-making or policy-advocating and normally change with a presidential transition. It defines that transition character to include positions whose appointments require the assent of the White House Office of Presidential Personnel.

That places Schedule G between two existing concepts without collapsing either one:

- Schedule C covers noncareer confidential or policy-determining roles;
- Schedule Policy/Career covers career policy-influencing positions; and
- Schedule G covers noncareer policy-making or policy-advocating roles.

The order amended Civil Service Rule VI directly. It also provides that ordinary civil-service rules and regulations do not govern removal from listed Schedule G positions except where statute requires otherwise. For Department of Veterans Affairs appointments, the Secretary must consider whether an appointee is a suitable exponent of presidential policy while not considering political affiliation or political activity.

### July 29: the classification acquires an operating intake path

[OPM's July 29 guidance](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/latest-memos/guidance-on-executive-order-creating-schedule-g-in-the-excepted-service/) made the paper category administrable:

- agencies record a Schedule G appointment under the EO 14317 authority and code `ZLM`;
- use of the authority goes through OPM's Executive and Schedule C System;
- the employing agency's White House liaison coordinates the request; and
- approval or withdrawal requires White House Office of Presidential Personnel review.

**Institutional change.** A policy-advocacy appointment is no longer only an agency-local staffing choice. It has a named federal personnel box and a White House-linked intake and approval route.

**Maturity by Libra ingress.** Operative classification plus a documented approval workflow.

**Counterevidence and limit.** Neither the order nor the guidance lists a position or names an appointee. The reviewed record does not provide a public government-wide Schedule G census. The new code proves that the route can be used; it does not prove how many chairs traveled through it. It is also not Schedule Policy/Career and should never be used as evidence that a career employee was converted or removed.

## 3F.2 — August 28: the labor order changes the representative perimeter, not the legal person

[EO 14343](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/03/2025-16924/further-exclusions-from-the-federal-labor--management-relations-program) determined that specified agencies and subdivisions perform intelligence, counterintelligence, investigative or national-security work and that chapter 71 cannot be applied consistently with national-security requirements.

It added or revised coverage for:

- Bureau of Reclamation units primarily responsible for operating, managing or maintaining hydropower facilities;
- the International Trade Administration;
- the Patent and Trademark Office's Commissioner-for-Patents units;
- NOAA's National Environmental Satellite, Data, and Information Service and National Weather Service;
- NASA; and
- the U.S. Agency for Global Media.

It also reopened a 15-day window for Defense and Veterans Affairs to publish qualifying restoration orders under EO 14251's exception route.

**Immediate legal result.** For the named units, the chapter 71 bargaining regime and the unions' exclusive-recognition status no longer supply the ordinary statutory bargaining route. The scope must be read at the subdivision level: the order does not exclude all of Interior or Commerce merely because a component is named.

### The later operating body is a labor-record conversion

OPM's [February 12, 2026 implementation FAQ](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/frequently-asked-questions-executive-order-14251-exclusions-from-federal-labor-management-relations-programs-updated-february-12-2026.pdf) treats EO 14251 and EO 14343 together. It tells covered agencies to:

- terminate or modify affected collective-bargaining agreements;
- file decertification or unit-clarification petitions with FLRA;
- change affected employees' bargaining-unit status code to `8888`;
- disregard certain union grievances where recognition has ended; and
- implement changes in conditions of employment without the prior notice and negotiation otherwise supplied by chapter 71.

Those instructions are a genuine operating conversion of the **labor-relations record**. They do not terminate employment.

### Litigation changes the contest clock, not the merits into certainty

The principal federal-union litigation challenged EO 14251, the earlier and much broader exclusion order. A district court preliminarily enjoined that order; the Ninth Circuit stayed the injunction in August 2025 and, on June 17, 2026, [vacated the preliminary injunction](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2026/06/17/25-4014.pdf). The panel stated that unions had not shown likely success on the present preliminary record and acknowledged that agencies had begun terminating some CBAs.

**Counterevidence and limit.** That was preliminary-relief review, not a final merits judgment adjudicating every agency application or every unit added by EO 14343. The court expressly left room for a fuller record. Unaffected bargaining units retain their agreements and representation. Employees remain employees; statutory agencies remain agencies; and an exclusion from chapter 71 is not a removal, furlough or conversion of the position itself.

## 3F.3 — September 5: “Department of War” changes the public body before the legal body

[EO 14347](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/10/2025-17508/restoring-the-united-states-department-of-war) authorized the Secretary of Defense, Department of Defense, Office of the Secretary of Defense and corresponding subordinate officials to use “War” secondary titles in official correspondence, public communications, ceremonial contexts and non-statutory executive-branch documents.

The order simultaneously states the boundary:

> Statutory references to the Department of Defense, Secretary of Defense and subordinate officers and components remain controlling until changed by law.

It required a 30-day congressional notification identifying offices or commands that began using a secondary designation and a 60-day recommendation describing legislative and executive steps for a permanent rename.

### The name acquires administrative body

On October 10, 2025, the Secretary issued a [secondary-titles memorandum](https://pclt.defense.gov/Portals/140/Documents/Regulatory/Department%20of%20War%20Secondary%20Titles%20OSD010837-25%20RES%20FINAL.pdf). It:

- made the listed secondary titles effective immediately for specified senior offices;
- instructed components to update signage and references;
- required cost data to be sent to the Comptroller and a Department of War Renaming Tiger Team;
- directed the tiger team to provide transition guidance and milestones; and
- required, within 45 days, an implementation plan and legislative proposal for a legal name change.

By 2026, the department's public materials, historical office, regulatory pages and strategic plan used the War secondary label. That is an observable public-administrative naming regime, not merely a speech flourish.

### Congress remains the legal-person gate

Congress had its own live clock. S. 2685 was introduced on September 2, before the EO, and H.R. 5522 was introduced on September 19, after it. Both proposed a statutory redesignation. Neither proposal, by itself, changed 10 U.S.C. §111.

**Maturity.** Secondary title authorized in the Cancer wave; later implemented across named offices, references and public surfaces.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The Department of Defense remains the same statutory person. “Department of War” is an alias attached to that entity, not a second cabinet node. Contracts, appropriations, treaties, inspector-general authorities and other legal objects continue to join to the Department of Defense wherever the controlling statute says so. A tiger-team plan, signage change or introduced bill is not enacted redesignation.

## What changed during the Cancer period

- A new noncareer policy-advocacy appointment category became legally and administratively available.
- White House-linked approval became part of the Schedule G appointment route.
- Named national-security and operational units lost the ordinary chapter 71 collective-bargaining perimeter.
- The executive branch acquired a sanctioned martial secondary vocabulary for the Defense Department and its senior officers.
- Personnel classification, labor representation and institutional presentation became centrally legible records.

## What did not change merely because the perimeter moved

- Schedule G did not convert career employees into political appointees.
- Creation of a personnel authority did not prove a named appointment.
- A labor exclusion did not fire an employee or abolish NASA, USAGM, NOAA, PTO, ITA or Reclamation.
- The whole departments of Commerce and Interior were not excluded.
- A preliminary-injunction ruling did not finally adjudicate every merits claim.
- A public War title did not repeal Title 10 or create a second department.
- An introduced renaming bill was not an enacted statute.

## Institutional theorem

This packet shows the regime changing **the membrane around an institution before changing its charter**.

It asks three upstream questions:

1. Who may be installed in a policy-bearing chair?
2. Which workforce units must still be negotiated with as collective actors?
3. What name tells insiders and outsiders what the institution believes it is for?

These decisions can materially change behavior while the underlying statutory person survives. The legal continuity is not evidence that nothing happened; the practical change is not evidence that the charter disappeared.

## Both poles

### Constructive pole — legible roles and accountable authority

- political-policy roles are honestly classified as noncareer instead of obscured inside career structures;
- central review can produce consistent appointment records;
- national-security units can move rapidly where bargaining procedure genuinely conflicts with mission;
- unaffected workers and statutory protections remain distinguishable;
- a secondary title can communicate purpose while the order openly preserves the legal name; and
- Congress remains visible as the gate for permanent redesignation.

### Shadow pole — loyalty, silence and image substitute for institution

- “policy advocacy” can become a loyalty membrane around public administration;
- central personnel approval can narrow plural expertise;
- national-security language can be stretched across operational units to remove collective voice;
- management may acquire practical freedom before courts finish reviewing the legal boundary;
- a martial public identity can normalize a changed mission without a statutory debate; and
- aliases can contaminate data joins, contracts and accountability if the legal person is not preserved underneath them.

The evidence supports a real appointment workflow, an operating labor-record conversion and an implemented secondary-title regime. It does not support mass Schedule G placement, automatic employee removal, dissolution of the named agencies or a completed statutory Department of War.

## Handoffs

- Schedule G returns only with a named position, appointment action or reliable government-wide census; the authority itself is already mapped.
- Schedule Policy/Career remains a different 2026 career-personnel story and must not be merged with Schedule G.
- EO 14343 implementation should be tracked by named CBA termination, FLRA unit petition, bargaining-unit code change and final merits ruling.
- The Department of War alias must always resolve to the Department of Defense statutory entity in Observatory joins.
- Any permanent rename returns with enacted text, not a bill, recommendation, website label or strategic plan.
- The next packet stays inside the same administrative-body question but moves from enduring personnel and name perimeters to temporary commissions, task forces, boards and public-design organs.
