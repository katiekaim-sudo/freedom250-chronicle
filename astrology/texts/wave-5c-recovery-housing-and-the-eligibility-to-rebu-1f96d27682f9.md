# Wave 5C — Recovery, Housing, and the Eligibility to Rebuild

**Research window:** Exact 2025 Capricorn ingress through exact 2026 Aries ingress.  
**Evidence cutoff:** June 20, 2026, 11:59 p.m. EDT. Later facts are quarantined as handoffs.  
**Question:** When winter policy promised homeownership, wildfire reconstruction, addiction recovery, mortgage access and cheaper construction, which promises changed an actual eligibility, enforcement, funding or permitting route?  
**Finding:** The deepest in-wave conversion was procedural, not physical. EO 14377 became a two-document SBA loan-amendment route allowing qualifying Los Angeles disaster borrowers to use builder self-certification after a 60-day local delay; the underlying $3.2 billion and 12,600 approvals predated the new route, and no attributable draw or completed rebuild was isolated. EO 14379 became an operating privacy-enforcement channel and a sequence of announced funding opportunities, but not selected awards or treatment outcomes. EO 14394 produced HUD's May best-practices report in the later Aries period, not a State adoption or house. EO 14376 did not produce a public federal-sale restriction by the cutoff, and EO 14393 did not reach even its first public RFI until after the cutoff. The packet's thesis is **the recovery state first changes who may proceed and on what paperwork; capital, care and construction remain separate clocks**.

The shared ladder is:

`social diagnosis → eligible person / property → application or disclosure rule → agency approval → obligation / draw → service or construction → outcome → audit / contest`

## 5C.1 — January 20: ownership policy searches for an executable definition

[EO 14376](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/23/2026-01424/stopping-wall-street-from-competing-with-main-street-homebuyers) states that large institutional investors should not buy single-family homes that could otherwise be purchased by families. But the order does not itself identify either class. Treasury must first define “large institutional investor” and “single-family home.” Agriculture, HUD, VA, GSA and FHFA are then to issue guidance for federal and GSE sales, approval, insurance, guarantee, securitization and facilitation, with owner-occupant first looks, anti-circumvention and narrowly tailored build-to-rent exceptions.

This is an eligibility constitution with several legal holders. Treasury defines; housing and asset agencies administer federal channels; DOJ and FTC review acquisitions and coordinated rental conduct; HUD gathers beneficial-ownership information in assisted rentals; Congress would be asked to codify the policy.

No public definition, final guidance, restricted federal disposition, denied investor transaction or completed owner-occupant sale attributable to the EO was isolated by June 20. That keeps the order at directed architecture during this packet's cutoff.

**Maturity.** Policy, assigned definitions, review and future guidance.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The order does not ban every institutional purchase or change private title law by itself. Build-to-rent exceptions are expressly contemplated. A later HUD asset-sale attestation surfaced after the cutoff and belongs to the next evidence snapshot, not this wave's proved maturity.

## 5C.2 — January 23 and 29: a disaster borrower receives a new route

[EO 14377](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/01/29/2026-01871/addressing-state-and-local-failures-to-rebuild-los-angeles-after-wildfire-disasters) asks FEMA and SBA to consider preemption and builder self-certification where local approval delays block use of federal disaster funds. It also orders expedited federal reviews, named senior officials, an HMGP audit and possible grant conditions, recoupment or technical assistance.

On January 29, SBA issued [regulatory guidance](https://legacy.sba.gov/article/2026/01/29/sba-issues-new-regulatory-guidance-cut-california-red-tape-expedite-los-angeles-wildfire-rebuild) for disaster borrowers who had waited at least 60 days for local permits or approvals. The route requires:

1. a Disaster Loan Modification application;
2. a Builder's Certification;
3. proof that required applications were submitted at least 60 days earlier;
4. certification that delay is governmental; and
5. continuing compliance with substantive building, health, safety, inspection and certificate-of-occupancy requirements.

Once SBA approves the two documents, they amend the borrower's Loan Authorization and Agreement and replace the local-permit prerequisite for use of the SBA-financed rebuilding path. This is a real change in an individual borrower's administrative gate.

The release says 12,600 loans totaling $3.2 billion had already been approved and less than 25 percent drawn. Those balances are the preexisting problem the new route addresses; they are not EO-created obligations or proof that self-certification unlocked a particular draw. The agency release also mistakenly cites “EO 14277.” The official order is **EO 14377**; the matching title and action make the press-release number a source typo, not a different instrument.

**Maturity.** Operating eligibility and loan-amendment procedure.

**Counterevidence and limit.** No borrower-level modification, new draw, builder start, inspection, occupancy certificate or completed home attributable to the route was isolated. Self-certification removes a procedural condition; it does not remove substantive safety duties or prove preemption will survive challenge.

## 5C.3 — January 29 onward: recovery acquires a coordinator, complaint channel and solicitations

[EO 14379](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/02/03/2026-02249/addressing-addiction-through-the-great-american-recovery-initiative) creates the Great American Recovery Initiative with HHS and a Senior Advisor as co-chairs, an executive director, a broad interagency membership and advisory relationships across health, justice, labor, education, housing, faith and private systems.

The EO's direct power is coordination. Agency heads retain program authority. Its strongest descendants therefore need to be typed separately.

### February 2–16: announced program and live enforcement channel

HHS [announced a $100 million STREETS concept](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/secretary-kennedy-announces-100-million-investment-great-american-recovery.html) on February 2, alongside a $10 million Assisted Outpatient Treatment opportunity, block-grant allocations and a child-welfare medication-matching policy. The headline “investment” is not one homogeneous transaction; it joins a proposed initiative, funding opportunities, existing block-grant allocations and a match-eligible service rule.

On February 16, HHS's [civil enforcement program for substance-use-disorder patient records](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-announce-civil-enforcement-program-sud-patient-records.html) became operative. OCR began accepting complaints and breach notifications under 42 CFR part 2, with resolution agreements, corrective action, settlements and civil penalties available. The underlying CARES Act and 2024 rule predate GARI, but HHS expressly identifies the new channel as supporting EO 14379. This is an inherited legal authority activated as a recovery-system interface, not a newly invented privacy statute.

### March–June: money is offered before it is awarded

Before the evidence cutoff, SAMHSA announced:

- [$69.1 million in three funding opportunities](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/samhsa-grants-serious-mental-illness-suicide-prevention.html) on March 6;
- [$40 million in eight funding opportunities](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/samhsa-announces-funding-opportunities-prevent-addiction-child-trauma-suicide-mental-illness.html) on June 11; and
- on June 17, a [$96 million four-year STREETS opportunity plus $612 million in additional opportunities](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/secretary-kennedy-announces-new-funding-mental-illness-addiction-homelessness.html).

These create application markets and expected program ceilings. They are not recipient selections, grant awards, obligations, disbursements, patient encounters or recovery outcomes.

**Maturity.** Operating coordinating body, live privacy complaint/enforcement route, service and matching policy changes, and multiple solicitations.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The public record did not supply an initiative-wide objective dashboard, award list, obligation ledger, delivered treatment count, housing placement or sustained recovery measure by the cutoff. Civil enforcement protects and governs records; it is not treatment.

## 5C.4 — March 13: mortgage-credit reform remains an instruction to consider

[EO 14393](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/18/2026-05384/promoting-access-to-mortgage-credit) asks independent and executive regulators to consider a broad series of changes: ATR/QM and TRID tailoring; HMDA thresholds; correction-first supervision; capital and liquidity alignment; FHLB facilities and possible discount-window interface; appraisal and digital closing; servicing; enforcement; and licensing.

The repeated phrase “consider, as appropriate and consistent with applicable law” matters. The President sets policy and asks the relevant legal holders to open proceedings; he does not amend Regulation Z, change a bank's capital requirement or grant a mortgage through the EO.

No public proposal, guidance revision, FHLB facility, completed report, bank product or mortgage transaction attributable to the order was isolated by June 20. CFPB's first clearly titled [Request for Information](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/notice-opportunities-comment/open-notices/request-for-information-regarding-promoting-access-to-mortgage-credit/) was published July 9—after the cutoff—and is quarantined for the next snapshot.

**Maturity.** Directed regulatory consideration.

**Counterevidence and limit.** A future RFI is intake, not a proposed or final rule. No evidence yet supports claims of lower mortgage rates, added bank participation, cheaper closings or expanded borrower access.

## 5C.5 — March 13 and May 20: construction policy reaches guidance

[EO 14394](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/18/2026-05388/removing-regulatory-barriers-to-affordable-home-construction) assigns federal water, environmental, energy, housing-finance, historic-preservation and opportunity-zone reviews. It also requires HUD to develop State and local best practices for cost, land and time.

HUD delivered the [State and Local Best Practices for Home Construction](https://www.hud.gov/sites/default/files/Main/documents/State-and-Local-Best-Practices-for-Home-Construction.pdf) in May. The report recommends permitting clocks and fee caps, by-right rules, third-party inspections, code and mandate review, modular/manufactured-housing neutrality, land and growth reforms, and digital administration. The [HUD announcement](https://www.hud.gov/news/hud-no-26-036) explicitly presents it as EO 14394 implementation.

This is a completed federal deliverable, but it matures after the Aries ingress. Its own date belongs to the 2026 Aries period even though it fulfills a Capricorn-wave seed. It remains guidance to jurisdictions, not a change in their law.

**Maturity.** Federal report and recommended operating model.

**Counterevidence and limit.** No State or locality adoption, revised permit, Opportunity Zone transaction, construction loan, building start, completion, sale or measured affordability change was isolated by June 20.

## The eligibility-to-rebuild theorem

The packet's five objects all speak of tangible ends—homeownership, rebuilding, recovery, credit and housing supply. Their proved effects occur earlier in the chain:

- define who counts;
- alter which paperwork lets a borrower proceed;
- open a complaint or grant-application channel;
- invite regulators to reconsider their rules; and
- publish a model for other governments.

The institutional structure is not simply removed. Authority is redistributed among Treasury definitions, agency sale channels, SBA loan agreements, builder certifications, OCR complaints, SAMHSA NOFOs, independent regulators and local adoption decisions. **The regime changes the interface before it changes the house, loan or life.**

## Both poles

### Constructive pole — procedure makes trapped resources usable

- families gain first-look and anti-circumvention principles;
- delayed disaster borrowers receive an alternative path that preserves substantive safety duties;
- recovery is treated as longitudinal service rather than episodic crisis;
- privacy enforcement can make patients safer entering coordinated care;
- community banks and small builders are invited back into housing finance; and
- jurisdictions receive concrete cost, land and time reforms.

### Shadow pole — eligibility becomes conditional discipline

- ownership categories can become blunt political classifications;
- self-certification can shift risk from public permitting bodies to borrowers and builders;
- recovery systems can join treatment, housing, courts and police in coercive ways;
- grant ceilings can be advertised as delivered care;
- lighter mortgage and appraisal rules can relocate rather than eliminate risk; and
- deregulation can lower visible cost while externalizing safety, environmental or infrastructure burdens.

Both poles remain live. Definitions, borrower files, certifications, complaints, awards, obligations, services, loans, permits, buildings and long-term outcomes—not the archetypal rhyme—show which matured.
