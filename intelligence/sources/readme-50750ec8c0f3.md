## Controlling ruling

Blockchain and smart contracts can change the **recording, reconciliation,
control and publication architecture** of accounting without eliminating the
economic questions that accrual accounting answers.

The most defensible transition model is not:

> cash basis replaces double-entry bookkeeping.

It is:

> signed business events become the durable record; cash, U.S. GAAP, tax,
> regulatory, budgetary and management accounts become reproducible views
> generated by versioned rules; estimates and exceptions remain separately
> signed judgment objects.

That architecture could make accounting simpler for operators and more legible
to outsiders. It does not make recognition, measurement, entity boundaries,
valuation, legal rights, performance, collectibility or audit evidence
automatic.

## Boundaries

- “Modified cash basis” is not U.S. GAAP.
- “Modified cash basis” is not GASB’s modified accrual basis for governmental
  funds.
- Tax accounting, internal management accounting and external GAAP reporting
  are different rule systems.
- A blockchain entry proves that a recorded state transition occurred under
  the network’s rules. It does not by itself prove delivery, legal ownership,
  collectibility, fair value, allowability, beneficiary eligibility or absence
  of collusion.
- Open-source code, open standards, open data, a publicly verifiable
  transaction record and open governance are five different transparency
  objects.
- Treasury’s federal grant blockchain work was a prototype. It did not process
  real grant payments.
