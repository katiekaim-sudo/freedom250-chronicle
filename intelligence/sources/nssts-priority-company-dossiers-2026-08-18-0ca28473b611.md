## Bottom line

These dossiers turn the cross-sector map into a company-level operating view of
**twelve exact legal entities**. Together they expose the most important places
where the scientific build-out can concentrate in a provider, integrator,
facility path or acceptance rail without pretending that every diversified
company is one technical system.

The twelve divide into distinct evidence states:

- **current cross-sector anchors:** Amazon Web Services, SpaceX, Oracle America,
  Lockheed Martin Corporation, Northrop Grumman Systems, L3Harris, Raytheon
  Company and BAE Systems IE&SI all have direct current company edges in two or
  more sector lanes;
- **anchors with an inference-bearing extension:** Booz Allen has confirmed
  cyber and communications work plus a planned HPM-support recompete; Palantir
  has confirmed Maven work plus an uncontracted DOE opportunity; GE Vernova
  Operations has two selected pre-operational paths; and Ginkgo has current
  autonomous-lab and DARPA work plus a historical surveillance position whose
  future extension is uncontracted;
- **mixed maturity inside a confirmed anchor:** a production or installed
  position never promotes a less mature prototype, field assessment, design
  agreement or ordering vehicle held by the same company.

The governing company path is:

```text
exact legal entity
  -> canonical company edge
  -> confirmed role or bounded inference
  -> shared rail / acceptance bottleneck
  -> current maturity and adverse state
  -> dated gate
  -> primary monitoring surface
```

## Dossier method

Each dossier applies seven controls:

1. **Resolve the entity before the role.** Parent, subsidiary, operating
   company, brand and business segment are kept separate.
2. **Carry every canonical edge.** A dossier does not invent new program work;
   it organizes existing edges and adds only primary entity or material-risk
   evidence.
3. **Separate confirmed work from opportunity.** Consortium membership,
   incumbent capability, a planned recompete or a plausible architecture is
   labeled as inference until a named instrument exists.
4. **Preserve program maturity.** Award, design, prototype, delivered,
   installed, tested, accepted, operating and produced are different states.
5. **Attach the controlling rail.** Cloud authorization, power, launch,
   ground integration, field test, manufacturing qualification and OTA
   transition explain what must happen after vendor selection.
6. **Retain adverse and contrary evidence.** Canceled acquisitions, competition
   losses, delayed acceptance, missing follow-ons and compliance obligations
   remain visible.
7. **End on a dated observable gate.** Every forward claim has an upgrade,
   downgrade and monitoring surface rather than an open-ended forecast.

## Cross-dossier findings

### 1. Cloud and data concentration is an authorization problem before it is a capacity problem

AWS and Oracle have direct cross-sector infrastructure positions; Palantir has a
confirmed operational AI position plus an uncontracted scientific-data path.
The common choke point is not a generic claim that science needs cloud. It is
the sequence of exact task, identity, security level, ATO, workload migration,
data access and accepted operation. Ginkgo's autonomous workflows add a
downstream scientific-data consumer, but the record does not assign every
workflow to one provider.

The practical implication is to monitor task and authorization objects, not
vendor market share. JWCC multi-award status, NNSA's separate path, on-premises
systems and other providers create redundancy only after portability and
authorization.

### 2. Launch-plus-network verticality is real but not yet operationally complete

SpaceX is the clearest vertically recurring company: it can place national
security payloads in orbit and is building a funded optical-mesh backbone.
L3Harris provides a different vertical pattern across tracking satellites,
terminals and radios. Neither dossier proves an accepted end-to-end company
architecture. Launch delivery, checkout, ground interoperability, cyber
authorization and mission-service acceptance remain separate gates.

That distinction keeps a launch anomaly from becoming an automatic network
failure and keeps a satellite build award from becoming accepted custody or
communications service.

### 3. Test and qualification integrators sit closer to transition than generic technology vendors

Booz Allen's HPM support, Northrop's QTIME role, Ginkgo's autonomous-platform
integration and GE Vernova's industrial validation show a recurring company
position between invention and government acceptance. These integrators can
shape test data, interfaces, deployment and qualification without owning the
underlying weapon, model, biological product or transformer.

Their value to the map lies in the transition surface, but so does their risk:
an undefinitized contract, planned recompete, unaccepted inspection method,
failed closed-loop workflow or uncommissioned equipment can stop the path even
when the core technology is promising.

### 4. Science-stack recurrence is strongest where physical and digital workflows meet

AWS and Oracle provide compute and data infrastructure; Palantir supplies an
operational-data pattern but no confirmed DOE implementation; Ginkgo integrates
robotics, experiments and biological manufacturing; GE Vernova connects
generative engineering to industrial validation. These are different layers,
not one consortium stack.

The high-value research question is whether named work products cross the
layers: authorized data into a laboratory, accepted experiment into a model,
model output into qualified equipment, or laboratory result into production.
Until those bridges are named, recurrence is company-specific rather than a
confirmed shared architecture.

### 5. Defense-prime recurrence is broad, but maturity transfers poorly

Lockheed Martin Corporation, Northrop Grumman Systems, L3Harris, Raytheon
Company and BAE IE&SI all recur across strategic mission lanes. Their strongest
positions differ:

- Lockheed has GPS production heritage and installed HELIOS hardware;
- Northrop has direct PTS-P build, GPI design and manufacturing-qualification
  work, all before final acceptance;
- L3Harris bridges satellite construction with terminals and radios;
- Raytheon combines mature interceptor production with far less mature DE field
  assessments;
- BAE combines a production modification with an early receiver design lane.

None of those facts permits technical maturity to transfer from one program to
another. The controlling evidence is always the program's own delivered,
tested, accepted or produced state.

### 6. Entity-family hazards are now a first-order monitoring object

The dossiers expose six recurring attribution hazards:

- AWS is not Kuiper Government Solutions;
- Ginkgo Bioworks, Inc. is not automatically Ginkgo Biosecurity, LLC;
- Lockheed Martin Corporation is not Lockheed Martin Aculight Corp.;
- GE Vernova Operations is not GE Packaged Power, GE Vernova Hitachi Nuclear
  Energy Americas or Prolec GE;
- Raytheon Company is not RTX Corporation or another RTX subsidiary;
- BAE IE&SI is not interchangeable with BAE Systems, Inc., BAE Systems plc or
  another BAE operating company.

Northrop's abbreviation variants and L3Harris punctuation variants can be
normalized because the legal entity is the same. Brand similarity alone is not
the rule. Exact signatory, novation, subsidiary exhibit and award paper control.
