When the Fed is not the expert owner

The Fed OIG turned one construction failure into an agency-wide rule: for major work outside its core expertise, the Board needs imported delivery skill, independent challenge and controls that survive into the next project.

What we found

  • The Eccles-1951 finding is a capability boundary, not a claim that the Federal Reserve is generally incompetent: monetary, regulatory and supervisory expertise does not automatically create construction or enterprise-technology delivery expertise.
  • The OIG's seventh recommendation calls for an agency-wide framework with full-scope budgets, stated cost limits, KPIs, accountability, materiality-based escalation, oversight criteria and independent senior members.
  • Chairman Warsh accepted the program and directed GSA project leadership, an independent awarded-cost audit, GMP negotiations, fixed metrics, contract reconciliation and permanent standards, but those are directions rather than completed results.
  • The independent audit can connect contractor and subcontractor awards to invoices, cost codes, construction in process and eventual fixed assets; the public financial statements currently show only aggregate construction in process.
  • The decisive durability test is first reuse: whether the framework actually governs planned IT modernization or another named major initiative before costs and schedule drift become irreversible.

Why it matters

This could change how an unusually autonomous, self-funded institution governs work outside its core mandate. The Board would keep legal authority while deliberately separating ownership, specialized execution, independent assurance and senior oversight—a portable control constitution rather than a one-project repair.

What to watch

  • 2026-12-31 or earlier on publication · An operative GSA project-executive instrument, named independent auditor, audit protocol or completed cost-validation result.
  • pending · A signed GMP or enforceable substitute, fixed original/current baselines, fee cap, service reconciliation and any reimbursement or project credit.
  • 2027-03-31 or earlier on publication · An issued agency-wide significant-initiative policy and the Board's 2026 construction-in-process and placed-in-service accounting.
  • pending · First documented use of the framework on IT modernization or another major non-core initiative, followed by measurable outcome evidence.

The Fed is many legal bodies, and its building cost doubled

The Federal Reserve has no single public-or-private status: the Board, the FOMC and twelve separately chartered Reserve Banks each have their own legal footing, records and bosses, and only Congress can rewrite the setup. Separately, the Fed's inspector general found on Sept. 29 that the Board badly managed its headquarters project, whose construction budget rose from $921 million to $2.018 billion.

What we found

  • The inspector general (Sept. 29) closed the criminal and misconduct claims it reviewed, but found the Board never agreed a price ceiling for the Eccles project after more than $2 billion was awarded, and let 84 small work packages set prices with no overall cap.
  • The marble, fountains and garden terrace were not big cost drivers, per the report. Chairman Warsh agreed and ordered fixes, including GSA project leadership, an independent cost audit and price-ceiling talks. These are ordered steps, not yet a final cost or money recovered.
  • The Board is a federal agency and the FOMC is an agency under the APA and FOIA, but each Reserve Bank is a separate federally chartered corporation whose legal status changes from one law to the next. Records are sorted by function and control, not by which building holds them.
  • Congress defines the dollar and has expanded and cut Fed powers many times, including in the 1930s, 1977, 1980, 2008, 2010 and 2025–26. Executive orders, regulations and operator contracts cannot rewrite the Federal Reserve Act.
  • The study finds the Fed's jobs are being split up by function, with private clearing taking a bigger middle role, but not abolition, merger or private capture. DTCC's tokenization, 24x5 clearing and settlement upgrades are separate projects, each with its own approval step.

Why it matters

This is the thesis close up: the dollar unit and the crisis backstop stay with Congress and the Fed, while daily clearing and record work move into modernized private utilities under existing law. The study finds contested unbundling and less day-to-day visibility, not a takeover.

What to watch

  • pending · Whether the Fed's ordered fixes produce an agreed price ceiling, an audited final cost for the Eccles project, and any money recovered.
  • 2026-12-31 · SEC compliance date for central clearing of cash Treasury trades (repo follows on 2027-06-30).
  • pending · Whether any DTCC tokenization or 24x5 clearing project passes its own approval step and goes live.

No single emergency switch turns crypto into federal money

Federal emergency powers can speed up, prioritize, fund or restrict a private payment or crypto service, but only one that is already licensed, contracted and connected. An emergency is usually the last step, not the first.

What we found

  • The study lays out separate legal routes: emergency buying under FAR Part 18, the Defense Production Act, FEMA tasks after a Stafford Act declaration, Federal Reserve emergency lending under section 13(3), and IEEPA controls after a declared national emergency. Each has its own test, and none makes a token legal tender or a Treasury payment method.
  • The 2025 GENIUS Act has real emergency tools for stablecoins: a Treasury safe harbor in "unusual and exigent" times, emergency brakes that can limit redemptions, and a duty that issuers be able to freeze, seize or burn tokens on a lawful order.
  • Those GENIUS tools are enacted but not yet in force. The law takes effect January 18, 2027, or 120 days after final rules, whichever is first. As of July 15, 2026, no final rule was found; the OCC and FDIC rules were still proposals.
  • The March 2025 order set up a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve from forfeited assets. It is custody of a reserve asset, not a power to spend or lend Bitcoin in a crisis.
  • Crypto's most developed federal role today is as something to control (sanctions, freezes, seizures), not as an emergency payment rail. The closest likely route is a stablecoin or tokenized-deposit backend behind an existing bank, card network or Treasury financial agent, with the government still paying in dollars.

Why it matters

This fits the thesis: new dollar-token rails are being built into existing law so they can be governed and controlled, and old emergency powers would act on them through the same agencies. It shows a legal path being prepared, not that it has been used.

What to watch

  • January 18, 2027 · GENIUS Act effective date, unless final rules arrive first and start the earlier 120-day clock.
  • pending · Final GENIUS rules from the OCC, FDIC or other regulators, and any Treasury rule defining the emergency safe harbor.
  • pending · Any real activation: a federal contract, DPA order, FEMA task or disbursement rule that actually uses a stablecoin or tokenized-deposit rail.

The court cases deciding who gets inside the money system

A map of 72 case families and 159 dated events on who can hold a Fed account, which agencies survive and how far emergency money powers reach. So far the courts mostly leave gatekeeping power with the Fed, Treasury and bank receivers.

What we found

  • Custodia Bank lost in the Tenth Circuit: being eligible does not give a bank an automatic right to a Fed master account, and Reserve Banks keep discretion. Its Supreme Court petition (No. 26-62) was docketed July 14, 2026, with responses due Sept 11; no grant, ruling or account access has followed.
  • In Banco San Juan, the Second Circuit held on May 13, 2026 that the Federal Reserve Act does not force a Reserve Bank to keep an account open.
  • On June 29, 2026 the Supreme Court held FTC for-cause removal protection unconstitutional (Slaughter, 6–3) but treated the Fed as a separate case. In Cook it denied the President a stay, upheld for-cause protection for Fed Governors and required notice and a chance to respond before removal.
  • In 2026 the Supreme Court held that IEEPA does not let the President impose tariffs. That limits emergency economic power but does not disable IEEPA blocking sanctions.
  • Agency and payment fights are still open: court restraints still govern parts of the planned CFPB contraction (NTEU v. Vought, en banc appeal live), and a modified injunction limits DOGE-team access to Treasury payment systems holding personal data (New York v. Trump, appeal pending).

Why it matters

It shows the fight over the money system running through the courts under existing law: who gets in, which agencies survive and how far emergency power goes are settled case by case. So far the rulings mostly keep control with the Fed and Treasury.

Not a new currency: a fight over the points where money changes form

As of July 2026, the money system looked less like one coin replacing another and more like a linked, programmable set of national systems. Power sits with whoever controls the points where deposits become collateral, collateral becomes cash, and losses get assigned.

What we found

  • The study traces one chain: funds become a regulated claim, then an eligible asset, then collateral and cash, then a final settled payment, then a cross-border link, then insurance, and last a legal split of losses. No country has finished every link.
  • Banks mostly keep the customer. Korea's deposit-token tests, Japan's trust-money setups, Europe's tokenized deposits and China's revised e-CNY all keep a bank or trust claim on top and central-bank money underneath for final settlement.
  • The real choke point is collateral eligibility: who owns the asset, which record proves title, what discount applies, and whether a central bank will lend against it. Examples: Europe showed one blockchain-native EIB instrument could pass through regulated custody into Bundesbank financing, and South Korea lining up corporate-loan receivables for a separate emergency facility.
  • BRICS gives outside support, not a shared currency. The CRA is a callable but undrawn arrangement, the NDB makes loans that create debt rather than reserve money, and the guarantee mechanism is only proposed.
  • The study expects a crisis, possibly outside the U.S., to show the new structure first. The U.S. would then matter through dollar funding, Treasury collateral, sanctions, reinsurance and its courts.

Why it matters

This is the thesis at the plumbing level: existing central banks, clearinghouses, insurers and courts are writing new rails into their own rulebooks, not being replaced. The study says plainly that this is a reading of separate developments, not proof of one coordinated plan.

What to watch

  • pending · A real emergency draw against newly pre-positioned collateral, such as the Korean facility, or tokenized assets pledged for actual central-bank or repo cash.
  • pending · Whether temporary emergency powers used in a future crisis stay on as permanent infrastructure after it passes.
  • pending · Signs the reading is wrong: pilots that stay isolated demos, and BRICS support tools that stay unused.

The short version (draft, not yet reviewed by Katie)

States still decide much of how banks and credit unions work

The U.S. runs two overlapping federal-and-state charter systems, one for banks and one for credit unions. Federal law sets common floors, but states still control charters, public deposits, taxes and some digital-asset powers, and Florida, Wyoming, New York and Texas each use that power differently.

What we found

  • A stock bank is owned by shareholders (mutual savings banks, owned by depositors, are the exception). A credit union is a nonprofit cooperative where members usually get one vote each. Charter, insurer (FDIC or NCUA's fund) and supervisor are three separate facts to check.
  • Florida (since July 1, 2024, with 7%/7%/10% caps), Wyoming (since July 1, 2025; ten credit unions on the Treasurer's June 4, 2026 approved list) and Texas let credit unions hold public deposits. New York's ordinary local-deposit law still names only banks and trust companies; bills S6050 and S3066 to change that were still in Senate committee at the cutoff.
  • Wyoming has no current state credit-union law, so its credit unions are federally chartered. Its special digital-asset custody powers belong to state banks and special-purpose depository institutions (SPDIs), not to credit unions.
  • Digital-asset permission follows the legal entity. Florida's 2026 stablecoin law keeps insured banks and credit unions out of its separate state-qualified issuer lane; New York requires DFS prior approval; Texas has 2021 custody guidance for state banks, but no matching credit-union instrument was found.
  • Being eligible for public deposits is not the same as holding them, and a tax exemption does not prove better rates for members. The study says those claims need more data.

Why it matters

States are changing who may hold public money and who may touch digital assets by using powers they already have, without touching federal charters. That is change made inside existing law, one state statute at a time.

What to watch

  • pending · Whether New York's S6050 or S3066 moves out of the Senate Banks Committee, which would let credit unions take local public deposits.
  • pending · First real public-deposit balances at credit unions in Florida, Wyoming and Texas, and any sign they change lending or pricing.
  • 2027-09-10 · NCUA's 18% loan-rate ceiling for federal credit unions runs through this date; the default is 15% unless the NCUA Board acts again.

Rating firms paid $49M+ for lost messages; the clean-up is still private

In September 2024 six credit-rating firms admitted they failed to keep required work messages and paid more than $49 million. Four had to hire outside consultants to fix it, but those reports are private, so the public cannot confirm the fix is done.

What we found

  • On September 3, 2024 the SEC entered six settled orders. All six firms admitted the facts and the violations, accepted censures and cease-and-desist orders, and paid penalties totaling more than $49 million.
  • Penalties: Moody's and S&P $20 million each, Fitch $8 million, A.M. Best $1 million, HR Ratings $250,000 and Demotech $100,000.
  • The missing records were messages about rating decisions. In the Fitch order the SEC found long and widespread use of texts, WhatsApp and WeChat, including by senior staff, and found the gaps likely hurt its own exams and investigations.
  • Fitch, HR Ratings, Moody's and S&P must use independent consultants on a set schedule: review, report, adopt fixes, a second check a year later, and written certification. A.M. Best and Demotech were not required to, because the SEC credited their earlier efforts and cooperation.
  • A search of the SEC site through September 25, 2026 found no public consultant report and no public SEC finding that the four firms finished. The orders say those reports stay nonpublic.

Why it matters

The regulator used ordinary enforcement to force the rating firms to keep a record it can check. The results of that clean-up stay out of public view, so the public sees that a fix was ordered but not whether it worked.

What to watch

  • pending · Any public SEC statement, certification or later action showing whether Fitch, HR Ratings, Moody's and S&P completed the required fixes.
  • pending · Any court order or SEC decision that makes a consultant report public.

The ownership-reporting rule now reaches only foreign companies

The December 2024 court order paused the federal ownership-reporting rule, but it was Treasury and FinCEN, not the courts, that shrank it. Since August 14, 2026 the final rule covers mainly foreign companies registered to do business in the U.S., and U.S. owners are exempt.

What we found

  • On December 3, 2024 a Texas federal court (Texas Top Cop Shop) blocked the Corporate Transparency Act's reporting rule nationwide, just before the January 1, 2025 deadline. This was preliminary relief, not a final ruling on the law.
  • The courts then went back and forth: the Fifth Circuit stayed the order, then restored it, and the Supreme Court stayed it on January 23, 2025. A second Texas order (Smith) kept reporting paused until February 17, 2025.
  • Treasury stopped enforcing the rule against U.S. citizens and domestic companies on March 2, 2025. FinCEN's interim final rule of March 26, 2025 then removed domestic companies from the definition of reporting company.
  • FinCEN made the narrowed rule final on August 14, 2026 (91 FR 52508) and added more exemptions for U.S. persons. Foreign companies registered after March 26, 2025 generally get 30 days to file.
  • The Act itself was never repealed. Congress has not changed it, and no court has issued a final merits ruling on whether it is constitutional.

Why it matters

This is a clear case of a federal law being reshaped from inside: the statute stayed on the books while the agency used its own exemption power to turn a broad domestic ownership registry into a narrow foreign-company filing. Courts paused it; the agency redesigned it.

What to watch

  • pending · Whether Congress amends the Corporate Transparency Act or a court issues a final merits ruling on FinCEN's power to keep the narrowed rule.
  • pending · Any FinCEN source on what happens to ownership reports already filed by now-exempt U.S. companies (kept, deleted or used).

A CPA license may become a private-market passport

The SEC has asked for comment on treating an active U.S. CPA license as an accredited-investor credential; this is a live notice, not a final designation order.

What we found

  • The proposed route would let a state professional license do double duty as a federal private-offering access credential. It would cover any active U.S. CPA license in good standing, not just audit partners, securities specialists or wealthy accountants.
  • NASBA reported 650,667 active CPAs as of August 27, 2026 based on 53 of 55 U.S. licensing jurisdictions. That is a gross credential count, not the number of newly eligible investors; overlap with existing income, net-worth and securities-license routes is unknown.
  • The person must hold the license, keep it active and remain in good standing. Passing the Uniform CPA Examination or IQEX alone would not qualify, and an inactive or suspended license would not qualify.
  • The discipline boundary is unusually narrow: a CPA barred by the SEC for lacking qualifications would not qualify, but some other SEC practice bars would not automatically defeat the route if the state license remained active and in good standing.
  • Public license lookup could make Rule 506(c) verification easier and less invasive than producing tax or wealth records. It would not register the offering, require an issuer or fund to accept the investor, eliminate minimum investments, or supply public-market disclosure and liquidity protections.

Why it matters

The Commission is considering importing state accounting licensure into federal securities law as an investor-access gate. That is a striking form of credential portability: a license designed to authorize accountancy practice could also unlock participation in private offerings.

What to watch

  • pending Federal Register publication · Publication of Release 33-11446 and the resulting exact 60-day comment deadline.
  • pending · Any final Commission order designating the CPA license, and its effective date and conditions.
  • comment period · Evidence on state-to-state licensing variation, discipline, investor sophistication, issuer verification and how many CPAs would actually become newly eligible.
  • pending · FINRA's specifications and administration plan for the separate proposed accredited-investor examination.

What big banks are building, and the fine print behind it

Big banks are growing by tying checking, cards, installment loans, payments and customer data into one relationship, and using AI to approve and personalize. The fine print is moving too: new arbitration and AI-data terms, and card balances changing hands between issuers.

What we found

  • Chase agreed to take over more than $20 billion of Apple Card balances, with about a 24-month transition from January 2026. Goldman Sachs is leaving mass-market card lending as a result.
  • Capital One bought Discover, then agreed to buy Brex for $5.15 billion (closed in 2026), joining cards, deposits, a payment network, merchant acceptance and business spend software. Capital One, N.A. has been the legal Discover issuer since May 18, 2025.
  • Bank of America added pre-dispute arbitration and mass-arbitration rules to its account agreement, reaching Zelle, RTP, ACH, wires, bill pay and its Erica assistant, after about 17 years without it. The audit pins the change to between March 23 and May 18, 2026.
  • U.S. Bank's terms say it may use data you type in to train or develop AI models. The term is real, but when it was added is not proven. Chase's digital agreement, updated June 2026, now defines third-party AI agents.
  • Citi says new AI risk models raise approval rates by 100 basis points. Chase reports approving more than 76,000 people with no credit history by using their deposit data instead.

Why it matters

The consumer money system is being reworked inside existing banks: merged networks, installment products, AI approvals and new contract terms shift who controls disputes and data without any new law. These are separate business decisions, not a coordinated plan.

  • pending · The Apple Card move from Goldman to Chase over the roughly 24-month transition.

Banks are moving card debt to firms willing to hold it, not forgiving it

Big lenders are selling or buying whole debt books and moving more struggling cardholders into low-rate fixed payment plans. Very little of this is forgiveness: a sale, a charge-off or a new servicer does not wipe out what the borrower owes.

What we found

  • Discover sold about $10.1 billion of private student loans to Santiago Holdings, a Carlyle and KKR affiliate, in late 2024, with Nelnet's Firstmark as servicer. Capital One bought Discover in May 2025 and sold its home-loan book for $8.8 billion in Nov. 2025.
  • Goldman agreed in Jan. 2026 to move the Apple Card program, over $20 billion of balances, to Chase after a roughly 24-month transition, subject to approvals. It was agreed but not closed at the cutoff. Ally sold its $2.3 billion card book to CardWorks and Merrick Bank in April 2025.
  • Chase's hardship changes on cards rose from $648 million in 2023 to $2.084 billion in 2025, with most rates cut from about 22.9% to about 3.5%, and $853 million more in Q1 2026 alone. Its core card book still grew 7% to $239.1 billion.
  • Capital One, Synchrony and Citi also report steep rate cuts, fixed plans and some partial forgiveness: Capital One reported $70 million of principal reductions and Synchrony forgave $11 million in Q1 2026. American Express says its 2025 changes did not cut principal.
  • Across the system, card balances were $1.25 trillion in Q1 2026 and bank card delinquency eased slightly to 2.92%. But 10.3% of student-loan balances were 90+ days late, up from 9.6%.

Why it matters

Consumer debt is being re-sorted inside the existing banking system: books move to consolidators and private-equity buyers, and hardship terms are rewritten loan by loan, with no new law and no broad cancellation. Who holds the debt, and the loss, shifts through sales and accounting choices.

Credit approval rules are shifting, but not all toward easier lending

Following one mortgage through the books to the Fed

One $300,000 mortgage can show up as several real, separate claims on different books (the household's loan, Fannie's mortgage asset, an MBS, and bank reserves and deposits) without the household owing more than $300,000.

What we found

  • The walkthrough uses made-up examples at par, not real journals. A nonbank lender funds the loan with $294,000 of warehouse borrowing plus $6,000 of its own, sells it to Fannie for $300,000 and gets its cash back without booking a profit.
  • When Fannie puts the loan in a trust and sells the MBS to an outside investor, cash comes back but the mortgage stays on Fannie's consolidated balance sheet, matched by $300,000 of 'debt of consolidated trusts'. Fannie's 2025 cash-flow statement shows $255.5 billion of proceeds from issuing that trust debt.
  • Add a repo and a Fed purchase and the example chain holds $1.2 million of selected gross claims: four linked $300,000 positions, not four times the household's debt and not a money multiplier.
  • When the household pays $500 of principal, each link falls by $500. Under the Fed's July 29, 2026 instruction, paid-down agency MBS principal is now reinvested in Treasury bills.
  • Each reporting boundary (one company, Fannie's group, the combined Reserve Banks, an analyst's chosen view) cancels out different things. Two independent firms cannot net matching positions in their own published statements.

Why it matters

This is the plumbing behind the thesis: the same loan feeds Fannie's books, the bond market and the Fed's balance sheet, so a change at one gate, like the Fed's switch to reinvesting MBS principal into bills, reshapes the system without touching the household's loan.

What to watch

  • pending · Real balances, fees and dates attached to a specific MBS pool, the study's named next step.
  • pending · Any change to the Fed's July 29, 2026 instruction to reinvest agency MBS principal into Treasury bills.
  • pending · Production and performance data on credit-score reform, which changes who enters this chain.

Big insurance is part of the monetary and governmental constitution

U.S. insurance runs through many separate regulators, very large insurer balance sheets, reinsurance and public loss programs that together work as one system. Each authority, company, contract and payment still keeps its own legal identity and timing.

What we found

  • The United States has no single insurance ministry. State commissioners are the main supervisors, NAIC coordinates model laws and accounting, federal bodies such as FIO, FSOC and the Federal Reserve have limited roles, and IAIS adds international standards.
  • FIO's year-end 2025 tables put life-sector assets at $9.902 trillion and property/casualty assets at $3.223 trillion. Insurers are major buyers of bonds, mortgages, structured assets and private credit, so their investment choices reach governments, companies and housing.

Why it matters

Insurers turn long-term promises into large demand for bonds, mortgages and private credit, and they move defined risks across companies and borders. Public money enters only through specific programs with named triggers, so the retail policy is the legal starting point, not the whole system.

Gold can swing a country's trade numbers without moving its currency

Gold can cause big swings in a country's trade accounts and reserves, but whether it moves the exchange rate depends on who owns the gold, who gets paid, whether that money is converted, and what the central bank does. None of the eight cases studied cleanly isolated a gold-driven currency move.

What we found

  • U.S. nonmonetary gold went from a net -$63.025 billion in 2025 Q1 (imports of $78.486 billion) to +$33.552 billion in Q2, a $96.577 billion swing in one quarter. The effect on the dollar is unresolved because payment and conversion details are not published.
  • Australia ran a A$41.092 billion gold surplus in 2025. Switzerland's precious-metals trade lowered its 2025 balance by CHF16.2 billion. In both, currency conversion and central-bank absorption were not observed.
  • Ghana is the strongest case: gold dominated exports inside a current-account surplus of 7.9% of GDP, but gold's exact share of the cedi's rise was not isolated. Uganda bought nearly US$3.5 billion of foreign currency to slow appreciation; its gold link is weak.
  • Two control cases: Sierra Leone counts gold as an eligible reserve but reported none, and India in 1991 used US$600 million of gold-backed loans during a US$9.7 billion current-account-deficit crisis, as a bridge, not the sole fix.
  • Higher gold prices raise the value of reserves without any trade or cash, and pledged gold is not fully usable for intervention. The study rules both 'gold price equals reserve build-up' and 'pledged gold is fully available' false as general claims.

Why it matters

Part of any rebuild of the money system runs through reserve and trade accounting. This study gives the steps to check before saying gold actually did work, rather than only changing values on paper.

What to watch

  • pending · Shipment-level invoice, payment and hedge currency records, and central-bank intervention records that could show gold-related currency conversion.
  • pending · A full Ghana chain from mined ounce to export to foreign-currency receipt to reserves, which would move it from aggregate to transaction-level proof.
