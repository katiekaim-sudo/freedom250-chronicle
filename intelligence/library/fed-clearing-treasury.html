Federal Reserve, Clearing and Treasury

Who may access, clear, settle or control public money and collateral across the Federal Reserve, Treasury and private clearing utilities?

Research through 2026-09-30

Home room: Banking & the DollarAlso in: Government Changes
The short version (draft, not yet reviewed by Katie)

The Fed is many legal bodies, and its building cost doubled

The Federal Reserve has no single public-or-private status: the Board, the FOMC and twelve separately chartered Reserve Banks each have their own legal footing, records and bosses, and only Congress can rewrite the setup. Separately, the Fed's inspector general found on Sept. 29 that the Board badly managed its headquarters project, whose construction budget rose from $921 million to $2.018 billion.

What we found

  • The inspector general (Sept. 29) closed the criminal and misconduct claims it reviewed, but found the Board never agreed a price ceiling for the Eccles project after more than $2 billion was awarded, and let 84 small work packages set prices with no overall cap.
  • The marble, fountains and garden terrace were not big cost drivers, per the report. Chairman Warsh agreed and ordered fixes, including GSA project leadership, an independent cost audit and price-ceiling talks. These are ordered steps, not yet a final cost or money recovered.
  • The Board is a federal agency and the FOMC is an agency under the APA and FOIA, but each Reserve Bank is a separate federally chartered corporation whose legal status changes from one law to the next. Records are sorted by function and control, not by which building holds them.
  • Congress defines the dollar and has expanded and cut Fed powers many times, including in the 1930s, 1977, 1980, 2008, 2010 and 2025–26. Executive orders, regulations and operator contracts cannot rewrite the Federal Reserve Act.
  • The study finds the Fed's jobs are being split up by function, with private clearing taking a bigger middle role, but not abolition, merger or private capture. DTCC's tokenization, 24x5 clearing and settlement upgrades are separate projects, each with its own approval step.

Why it matters

This is the thesis close up: the dollar unit and the crisis backstop stay with Congress and the Fed, while daily clearing and record work move into modernized private utilities under existing law. The study finds contested unbundling and less day-to-day visibility, not a takeover.

What to watch

  • pending · Whether the Fed's ordered fixes produce an agreed price ceiling, an audited final cost for the Eccles project, and any money recovered.
  • 2026-12-31 · SEC compliance date for central clearing of cash Treasury trades (repo follows on 2027-06-30).
  • pending · Whether any DTCC tokenization or 24x5 clearing project passes its own approval step and goes live.

Research summary

The September 29 Federal Reserve OIG evaluation closes the criminal and administrative-misconduct allegations within its scope but finds a serious management failure: the Board did not effectively execute the Eccles-1951 construction-manager-at-risk contract, had no agreed project-level guaranteed maximum price after more than $2,000,000,000 was awarded, underused its owner representative, allowed 84 limited-scope packages to set price without an overall ceiling and repeatedly reported the project as on track against rebased benchmarks. Construction rose from a $921,000,000 February 2020 budget to a $2,018,000,000 December 2024 budget; four MEP awards alone rose from a $206,000,000 estimate to $694,000,000. Inflation and site/design conditions mattered, but the OIG found the criticized marble, fountains, garden terrace and related features were not material cost drivers. Chairman Warsh concurred and directed GSA project leadership, an independent cost audit, GMP negotiations, fixed metrics and contract remedies; these are directed corrective actions, not yet a ceiling, validated final cost or recovered dollar. The September 29 American Express feedback dispute exposes a resolution-planning classification seam: the Federal Reserve and FDIC identified significant uncertainty in the feasibility of a one-buyer sale across an FDIC bank receivership and two Chapter 11 estates, yet found no deficiency or shortcoming. Regulation QQ permits additional feedback, but its shortcoming definition uses closely related feasibility language; Gould's dissent therefore targets the opacity and structural leverage of unclassified feedback, not the statutory existence of living wills. The September 20 legal-status and records-routing matrix establishes that the Federal Reserve has no single public/private status: the answer varies by actor, function, statute, record custody and remedy. The Board is a federal agency; the FOMC is an APA/FOIA agency; each Reserve Bank is a separate federally chartered corporation whose agency, instrumentality, employer, person and liability status changes by legal regime. Records likewise route by function and control rather than physical host: Bank-held material can be an FOMC record, a current-rule Board record, or a Bank record subject to the Banks' voluntary transparency policy and other lawful access routes. The companion appointment, removal and succession chain maps five distinct personnel constitutions from Governors and Reserve Bank directors through FOMC officers, SOMA management, the Inspector General and Bank general auditors; it also restores the August whole-government FRFS/National IT committee constitution to the direct package's discovery routes.
