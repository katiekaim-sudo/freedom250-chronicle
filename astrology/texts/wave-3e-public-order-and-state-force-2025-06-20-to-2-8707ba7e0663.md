# Wave 3E — Public Order and State Force

**Research window:** Exact 2025 Cancer ingress through the eve of exact 2025 Libra ingress.  
**Question:** Did the Cancer-period public-order orders transfer local legal authority to a new federal sovereign, or did they build temporary service, funding, custody, prosecution and rapid-deployment overlays around surviving courts and local institutions?  
**Finding:** The wave built a real federal enforcement surge and tried to move several practical decisions upstream: which homelessness programs receive preference, when civil commitment should be encouraged, which police services must be supplied for federal purposes, which conduct receives federal prosecution priority, which defendants should be pursued federally for detention, and which jurisdictions may lose federal support over bail policy. But the legal result was bounded and contested. In D.C., the President invoked an unprecedented temporary Home Rule Act service power; the Attorney General's attempt to replace the local police chief and assume all MPD powers was challenged and rescinded within a day. MPD's chief and local legal identity survived while federal agencies, National Guard personnel, prosecutors and narrower MPD service directives created an operating overlay. The homelessness, nationwide bail and flag orders were strongest as grant, litigation, charging and future-policy directions; their major implementing bodies matured later or remained publicly unverified by Libra ingress.

The controlling chain is:

`public-order claim → emergency or policy authority → command / grant / charging route → arrest or custody request → prosecutor → court → detention, treatment or release → contest and review`

No order collapses that chain into one presidential decision.

## Command, custody and adjudication are different clocks

| Clock | Holder or carrier | What changed in the wave | What remained separate |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Police service** | Mayor/MPD under D.C. Home Rule Act §740 | President requested MPD services for federal purposes; AG designated federal requests | MPD legal identity, local chief and ordinary municipal authority |
| **Federal surge** | DOJ components, Park Police, Guard, other agencies | Personnel and coordinated operations were deployed or ordered | One arrest is not a charge, conviction or causal crime-reduction proof |
| **Charging** | U.S. Attorney / prosecutors | Federal charges and detention were prioritized where law allowed | Prosecutorial choice, probable cause, grand jury and judicial ruling |
| **Pretrial custody** | Court under governing bail law | Executive sought federal custody and funding pressure against cashless-bail policy | Judge's detention decision and D.C./state substantive law |
| **Civil commitment** | State/federal court and treatment system | DOJ/HHS directed toward flexible standards, litigation and grants | Individual findings, due process, available bed and actual treatment |
| **Homelessness grants** | HUD/HHS/DOJ/DOT | Priority, treatment, data and enforcement criteria were ordered for review | Appropriation, NOFO, award, recipient compliance and housing outcome |
| **Expression/prosecution** | DOJ and courts | Enforcement priority for independently unlawful conduct involving flag desecration | Protected expression, elements of an offense and First Amendment review |

## 3E.1 — July 24: homelessness is reclassified as custody, treatment and grant eligibility

[EO 14321](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/07/29/2025-14391/ending-crime-and-disorder-on-americas-streets) directed DOJ and HHS toward broader civil-commitment, institutional-treatment and step-down standards for people meeting specified danger or incapacity criteria. It also directed federal agencies to review discretionary grants and consider preference for jurisdictions enforcing drug, camping, loitering and squatting laws or using assisted outpatient treatment and civil commitment.

The order connects five different institutional levers:

- litigation against precedents or consent decrees seen as obstacles;
- technical assistance and discretionary grants;
- federal evaluation of detainees under existing commitment law;
- housing/homelessness-program terms and data sharing; and
- prosecution of independently unlawful drug-site or property conduct.

**Maturity by Libra ingress.** Presidential direction and agency review route.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The order did not itself commit one person, create a treatment bed, change a state's legal standard, cancel a housing award or adjudicate a grant recipient unlawful. Civil commitment still requires an applicable statute, individual evidence, process and a court or other authorized decision-maker. A grant preference is not a custody order.

**Later body.** HUD's later FY2025 Continuum of Care competition expressly invoked EO 14321 and changed competition, treatment and provider priorities. That later NOFO is a real program carrier, but it remains a solicitation/award architecture rather than proof that a particular person received effective treatment or housing.

## 3E.2 — August 11: the President invokes a temporary D.C. police-services power

[EO 14333](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/14/2025-15550/declaring-a-crime-emergency-in-the-district-of-columbia) invoked §740 of the D.C. Home Rule Act for the first time. The President found emergency conditions requiring MPD services for federal purposes and directed the Mayor to provide those services for the maximum statutory period. The President delegated his authority to direct the Mayor to the Attorney General.

The statutory architecture matters. Section 740 permits a presidential request for MPD **services** for federal purposes for up to 30 days unless Congress extends the period. It does not on its face dissolve MPD, repeal home rule or turn every local offense into a federal crime.

**Maturity.** Legally invoked temporary services authority plus an operating multi-agency surge.

**Counterevidence and factual contest.** The emergency characterization was disputed by the District and by earlier federal data. DOJ had reported in January that 2024 violent crime was down 35 percent from 2023 and at a more-than-30-year low. That does not prove zero public-safety need; it does show that “rising crime” and the baseline trend were contested factual propositions rather than neutral givens.

## 3E.3 — August 14–15: attempted command transfer meets the legal-person boundary

Attorney General Order 6370-2025 attempted to appoint DEA Administrator Terrance Cole as MPD “Emergency Police Commissioner,” grant him all powers of the local chief, require local leaders to obtain his approval and suspend MPD directives.

D.C. sued on August 15, arguing that §740 authorized temporary services for federal purposes rather than seizure of the local department. After an emergency hearing, DOJ issued [Order 6372-2025](https://www.justice.gov/ag/media/1411361/dl?inline=). The revised order:

- rescinded Order 6370;
- left the local MPD chief in command;
- made Cole the Attorney General's designee for directing the Mayor to provide specified services; and
- requested MPD assistance with federal immigration enforcement, locating and detaining certain noncitizens, federal database inquiries and unlawful-public-space enforcement.

**Entity result.** `temporary federal service overlay → attempted command transfer → immediate legal contest → narrower service directive`.

This is neither “nothing changed” nor “MPD became a federal agency.” The federal center acquired a temporary, contested service-request route; the local chief and department survived.

## 3E.4 — the surge becomes an operating body, but attribution stays open

Federal law-enforcement agents and National Guard personnel joined the D.C. operation. MPD's public August 31 surge report recorded arrests, firearm recoveries, calls and crime counts during the period. Those are operating receipts.

The report also showed that year-to-date violent crime had already been declining before the surge. Consequently:

- an arrest is attributable to an agency operation when the record says so;
- a charge, detention, conviction and sentence retain their own clocks;
- a short-period decline can be described as observed; but
- the entire decline cannot be assigned to the order without a method that separates preexisting trend, policing, prosecution, seasonality and other factors.

The 30-day §740 service window ended unless Congress extended it. Federal agencies retained their own preexisting authorities, so a continuing federal surge after that date is not identical to continued presidential control of MPD.

## 3E.5 — August 25: a temporary surge is designed as a reusable force architecture

[EO 14339](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/28/2025-16614/additional-measures-to-address-the-crime-emergency-in-the-district-of-columbia) ordered:

- additional Park Police and D.C. federal prosecutors, subject to law and appropriations;
- a public recruitment portal for experienced applicants;
- specialized federal law-enforcement units for D.C. and possible use in other cities;
- a specialized D.C. National Guard public-order unit;
- state Guard readiness and a standing rapid-deployment force;
- HUD compliance investigations; and
- transit safety inspections and possible remedial action.

**Institutional change.** The D.C. event becomes a template. The regime attempts to convert an emergency surge into standing readiness, trained units, recruitment, housing-contract review and a repeatable federal/local coordination model.

**Counterevidence and limit.** “Create and begin training” is not a certified, staffed or deployed unit. A quick-reaction-force instruction is not a lawful activation in every future case. Guard status, deputation, mission, rules for use of force, Posse Comitatus constraints, governor/adjutant-general roles and each deployment order remain separate.

## 3E.6 — August 25: bail policy is targeted through federal forum and funding

[EO 14340](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/28/2025-16615/measures-to-end-cashless-bail-and-enforce-the-law-in-the-district-of-columbia) instructed participating federal actors to pursue federal charges and federal pretrial detention whenever legally possible for D.C. arrestees viewed as public-safety threats. It also directed the Attorney General to determine whether D.C. retained a cashless-bail policy and allowed agencies to consider funding, services and approvals as pressure.

[EO 14342](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/28/2025-16618/taking-steps-to-end-cashless-bail-to-protect-americans) broadened the idea nationally. It ordered a 30-day list of jurisdictions judged to have substantially eliminated cash bail for specified offenses and an agency/OMB inventory of federal grants and contracts that might be suspended or terminated.

**Maturity by Libra ingress.** Charging/detention preference and future list/funding-review architecture. The nationwide list was not yet due at the September 21 boundary, and no public completed list or attributable suspension belongs inside this wave.

**Counterevidence and limit.** Cash bail and preventive detention are not synonyms. The executive cannot itself order a judge to detain a defendant, manufacture federal jurisdiction or terminate funds contrary to statute and award terms. A federal charge pursued to increase detention exposure remains subject to ordinary charging and judicial review.

## 3E.7 — August 25: the flag order preserves the offense/expression distinction

[EO 14341](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/28/2025-16616/prosecuting-burning-of-the-american-flag) directs DOJ to prioritize content-neutral criminal or civil laws when flag desecration also involves independently unlawful violence, property destruction, breach of peace, discrimination or related conduct. It also contemplates litigation over First Amendment boundaries and immigration remedies after an appropriate legal determination.

**Maturity by Libra ingress.** Enforcement and litigation priority.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The order does not create a federal crime of protected flag burning. Texas v. Johnson remains governing law. Immigration or prosecution consequences still require a statutory ground, facts, process and review. The reviewed record does not identify an in-window prosecution created by this order.

## What changed during the Cancer period

- Homelessness policy was routed toward civil commitment, treatment conditions, enforcement and discretionary-grant preference.
- The President invoked a temporary D.C. police-services authority for the first time.
- Federal personnel and Guard support acquired an operating coordination body in the capital.
- The attempted replacement of MPD's chief was quickly narrowed after litigation.
- D.C. became the prototype for specialized units and standing Guard readiness.
- Bail policy became a federal charging, detention and funding-review object.
- Flag-related unlawful conduct became an express prosecution and immigration-review priority.

## What did not change merely because force was ordered

- MPD did not become a permanent federal agency.
- The Attorney General did not retain the full powers of the local chief after the August 15 revision.
- An emergency declaration did not eliminate Home Rule Act time limits.
- An arrest did not establish guilt, detention or sentence.
- A decline in crime did not by itself prove the surge caused the decline.
- A civil-commitment policy did not commit an individual.
- A bail order did not replace the judge.
- A flag order did not outlaw protected expression.
- A funding review did not terminate a grant or contract.

## Institutional theorem

The public-order state grows through a **service-and-custody overlay**, not through the instant abolition of local law.

Its recurring move is:

1. declare a class of danger;
2. attach federal money, personnel, records or charging authority;
3. request or pressure a surviving local institution to provide service;
4. seek custody through an existing legal route; and
5. preserve the executive's ability to repeat the model elsewhere.

The courts, local department, mayor, statutes, individual rights and appropriations remain active counterweights. The regime can change the practical balance before it changes the legal charter, but the difference remains observable.

## Both poles

### Constructive pole — coordinated safety with visible legal gates

- federal and local agencies can share personnel and focus on violent offenders;
- treatment, court and housing systems can be joined instead of leaving people without support;
- grant programs can reward measurable public-safety and recovery outcomes;
- federal charging can reach conduct that genuinely belongs in federal court;
- service requests and short statutory clocks can make emergency power inspectable; and
- litigation can correct an overbroad command claim without dissolving useful coordination.

### Shadow pole — care, order and belonging become coercive classifications

- homelessness can be converted from a housing problem into involuntary custody;
- temporary service authority can be stretched toward local command;
- federal charging can be chosen to increase detention leverage;
- grants and contracts can pressure jurisdictions to adopt executive policy;
- a standing rapid force can normalize emergency deployment;
- public-space and immigration enforcement can merge; and
- symbolic dissent can acquire immigration or prosecution scrutiny at the edge of constitutional protection.

The evidence supports an operating federal enforcement overlay and a reusable public-order design. It does not support permanent federal ownership of MPD, executive control of judges or an already completed nationwide bail/civil-commitment regime by September 21, 2025.
