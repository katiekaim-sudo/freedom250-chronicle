## Method

The package treats “start” as six different clocks:

| Clock | Test |
|---|---|
| **Stress** | Did a market, currency, collateral pool or balance sheet force adaptation? |
| **Law** | Did an enacted or effective rule change what may legally be issued, held or settled? |
| **Pilot** | Did a controlled experiment use real or simulated participants/value? |
| **Production** | Can eligible users move live value under operating service terms beyond a bounded experiment? A closed-account service can be production. |
| **Interoperability** | Does the rail cross systems, currencies or borders? |
| **Control** | Who can admit, freeze, sanction, reverse, collateralize or confer settlement finality? |

Every regional record separates:

- the legal entity from the political brand;
- the tokenized asset from the settlement asset;
- messaging/orchestration from final settlement;
- tokenized central-bank reserves, retail CBDC, tokenized commercial-bank deposits and stablecoins;
- `proposed`, `law passed`, `effective`, `PoC`, `pilot`, `controlled go-live`, `production`, `scheduled` and `unverified`.

## Scope boundary

This is a factual-first research layer for later synthesis. It does not predict a single replacement currency, treat BRICS as a supranational issuer, or infer production from a proof of concept. Market-stress interpretation is kept separate from the legal and operational record.

## The question

What has the IMF released for the Legal Department's 80-year conference,
*From Bretton Woods to the Digital Age*; what is on the agenda; what research
and legal materials sit behind it; and what, if anything, does the event itself
change?

## Short answer

The IMF released a full two-day agenda and three substantial anniversary
publications: a department brochure, a special edition of the Articles of
Agreement, and an 88-page anniversary magazine. Day 1 is organized around IFI
legal departments, digital money and technology, the IMF mandate, governance
and financial integrity, and sovereign debt. Day 2 moves into smaller technical
sessions on program law, payment law, AI, AML/CFT, dispute resolution,
governance diagnostics, regional financing arrangements, insolvency, and
central-bank law.

Five complete official Day 1 replays are now public, totaling about 5 hours 36
minutes. The IMF player exposes no caption track and no official transcript was
located, so the package holds complete local machine transcripts strictly for
search and timestamp navigation. The replays—not the local text—control what
was delivered.

The package is important evidence about how the IMF Legal Department describes
its own operating role. It is not an Executive Board decision, a treaty
amendment, a new digital-money regime, or proof of any token, stablecoin, CBDC,
SDR, payment-rail, or sovereign-debt implementation. No public Day 2 replay,
official transcript, panel deck or conference communique was located.
