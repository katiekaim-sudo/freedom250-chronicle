Five crypto trust banks are active; most of the line is still waiting

Circle's national trust bank opened July 24 as a nondeposit custody bank, while most crypto-facing OCC applicants remain conditionally approved or pending.

What we found

  • The OCC's August 31 active trust-bank list includes five plainly crypto-facing legal persons: Anchorage Digital Bank, BitGo Bank & Trust, Fidelity Digital Assets, First National Digital Currency Bank and Paxos Trust Company.
  • Circle's First National Digital Currency Bank crossed the missing legal clock: OCC CAS marks it consummated/effective July 24 and Circle's SEC filing says it opened that day.
  • Circle National Trust does not issue USDC, accept deposits or make loans. It opened with custody for Circle and affiliates; reserve management remains a separate future transfer.
  • A selected thirteen-institution group has conditional or preliminary approval but still needs final approval and opening proof; the OCC's separate public digital-assets page lists eleven pending applications.
  • An OCC charter does not itself supply FDIC insurance, Federal Reserve account access, interest on Reserve Bank balances, discount-window credit, stablecoin-issuer approval under effective GENIUS rules or operating scale.
  • On October 13 the Kansas City Fed filed its Supreme Court opposition in Custodia No. 26-62. The Court has not disposed of the petition, and the filing does not change Custodia's account status.

Why it matters

The crypto-to-bank shift is real, but it is happening through narrow legal persons and staged approvals. The charter brings custody, reserve and payment functions under federal supervision without turning stablecoins into insured bank deposits or giving every crypto group direct access to central-bank settlement.

What to watch

  • On the next OCC active list or CAS completion · Which conditionally approved crypto-facing trust bank first reaches final approval, consummation and opening.
  • On an exact Fed database record · Whether any named national trust bank receives or requests its own Reserve Bank account/services access.
  • On a Circle filing or customer disclosure · First unaffiliated custody client, USDC reserve-management transfer or change in the bank's nonissuer role.
  • On Supreme Court action · Certiorari disposition in Custodia Bank v. Federal Reserve Board, No. 26-62.
