Data Centers, Power & Law

Separate executive direction, legislation, site selection, permitting, construction and actual operation in the data-center buildout.

Federal data-center legal spine, 2025–2026

Short answer

The federal contest is not presently one nationwide legal “war” with two operative codes. It is an asymmetric field:

  • the administration has an operative acceleration directive and a growing set of returned agency and project-stage instruments;
  • members of Congress have proposed materially different moratorium, ratepayer, disclosure, emissions, local-control and acceleration regimes, but the direct commercial bills located remain in committee; and
  • ordinary environmental, utility, land and project-approval law still applies unless an issued instrument changes a particular procedure or a competent agency grants a particular authorization.

The strongest public evidence is therefore not a political statement. It is an exact object: a final rule, guidance document, solicitation, lease, right-of-way decision, covered-project posting, permit, enacted section or official bill action.

1. What is law now

Operative executive direction

EO 14318, signed July 23, 2025 and published at 90 FR 35385, defines a “Data Center Project” as a facility requiring more than 100 MW of new load for specified AI workloads. Its “Qualifying Project” routes include at least $500 million in capital expenditure, more than 100 MW of new load, national-security protection, or a Secretary-designated project. It directs:

  • Commerce and OSTP financial-support coordination;
  • agency categorical-exclusion inventories and possible new or adopted CEs;
  • FAST-41 transparency and covered-project routing;
  • EPA work under the CAA, CWA, CERCLA and TSCA;
  • ten-year ESA programmatic consultation after federal sites are identified;
  • Army nationwide-permit review under CWA section 404 and RHA section 10; and
  • Interior, Energy and Defense land authorizations or leases.

The order is an operative instruction to executive agencies. It is not itself an appropriation, private grant, project permit, lease, pollution waiver or construction approval. It also expressly preserves applicable law and appropriations.

Primary authority: EO 14318, 90 FR 35385.

Narrow enacted congressional provisions

The FY2026 NDAA, Public Law 119-60, contains two material enacted provisions:

  • Section 321 requires the Navy by December 18, 2026 to initiate a ten-year nuclear-energy pilot at one or more naval installations. Possible data-center co-location is a site-selection consideration; the law does not approve a commercial data center.
  • Section 1531 requires installation-specific electricity, water, footprint, community-effect, mitigation and resilience estimates for every data center built or expanded on a military installation in the DOD HPC roadmap. Updates are due March 1, 2027 and every three years through March 1, 2033.

Primary authority: P.L. 119-60.

These are planning and pilot duties, not a general commercial siting, water, emissions or rate code.

4. Environmental-law ruling

The issued returns chiefly alter process, not the substantive existence of environmental law:

  • CEQ removed its binding government-wide NEPA regulations, while NEPA and agency procedures remain; its April 2026 CE guidance is nonbinding and is not a data-center CE.
  • EPA's CERCLA/Brownfields document is reuse guidance, not a cleanup waiver or site approval.
  • TSCA priority handling does not alter substantive review standards.
  • NWP 39 is a final permit framework but not a blanket permit for every data center.
  • EPA's islanded-generator memo addresses only Acid Rain Program applicability under stated facts and expressly says it does not address other CAA programs.
  • The public record reviewed did not reveal an EO-specific CWA effluent rule or an EO-specific ten-year ESA programmatic consultation/biological opinion.

The correct sentence is therefore: some federal review pathways have been accelerated or clarified; ordinary project-level environmental gates remain unless a competent authority has issued the relevant instrument.

5. Current public gaps

The following were not located as public official records by the cutoff:

  • a consolidated Commerce/OSTP EO §3 financial-support initiative or agency support inventory;
  • a public compilation of the ten-day agency CE inventories submitted to CEQ;
  • an EO-specific FAST-41 transparency-project listing or project-specific EO §6 designation;
  • a nationwide DOI site list or Secretary-level Qualifying Project designation;
  • an EO-specific EPA CWA rule or omnibus final CAA/CERCLA/TSCA amendment;
  • a public list of EPA-designated Brownfield/Superfund data-center sites;
  • a posted ten-year EO-specific ESA programmatic consultation or biological opinion; and
  • final public selections or executed leases for several DOE and Air Force solicitations.

These are no-public-record findings, not proof that an internal submission never occurred or that an agency violated the order.

6. Research consequence

The next unit of evidence should always be the object that changes a row:

  • bill action or enacted text;
  • final rule or signed guidance;
  • dashboard authorization completion;
  • executed lease or right-of-way instrument;
  • FERC/state PUC tariff or order;
  • state statute, local ordinance, rezoning/CUP or court judgment; or
  • construction/commissioning/COD evidence for a named project.

That is the factual spine on which later public-opposition, ratepayer, resource, national-security and related analytical layers can safely be joined.
