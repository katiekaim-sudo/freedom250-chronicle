## Phase breathing in the first half

From January through June 15, New Moons repeatedly carry a much larger empty arc
than the intervening Full Moons. The New-Moon values are 187.48°, 198.36°,
199.78°, 198.20°, 194.13° and 183.22°. The corresponding Full-Moon values are
161.32°, 170.67°, 141.70°, 112.88°, 112.33° and 135.98°.

The geometry is astronomically intelligible. At New Moon, the Moon doubles the
solar side of the wheel and can deepen an already large gap. At Full Moon, the
Moon moves to the anti-solar side and often occupies some of that gap. The first
half therefore breathes between a more concentrated seed field and a more
distributed revelation field without requiring a named shape family.

The March 19 New Moon and March 20 Aries ingress are the most concentrated
registered pair, with empty arcs of 199.78° and 199.77°. Their root circuits are
different—Jupiter–Moon versus Mars–Jupiter–Moon—but both begin the Aries year
with only about 160° occupied.

## The July break

The Cancer ingress begins a five-chart corridor:

`Cancer ingress → Jun 29 Full Moon → Jul 14 New Moon → Jul 29 Full Moon → Aug 12 eclipse`

The crucial change is July 14. Its New Moon leaves only a 149.40° largest gap,
far below the 183.22°–199.78° New-Moon range of January through June. The old
phase alternation does not return. The Moon is final in Cancer, but the planetary
field remains substantially more distributed than at the previous seed phases.

This means the midyear redistribution begins before the August eclipses. The
eclipses do not create it from nothing; they continue a transition already
visible after the Cancer ingress.

## The late-year low-gap corridor

August 28 is the first registered chart whose largest empty arc falls below
104°. Every subsequent registered chart through December 24 remains below 93°.
This is the defensible boundary: a measured low-gap corridor, not an automatic
family classification.

The astronomy explains the transition. At the August 12 eclipse, the largest
gap is 118.01° from Venus in early Libra to Pluto in early Aquarius. By August
28:

- Venus has advanced through Libra, shrinking that gap;
- the Full Moon occupies Pisces instead of doubling the solar cluster; and
- Mars, Jupiter, Sun and Mercury establish separated Cancer–Leo–Virgo centres.

The September 11 New Moon cannot restore the earlier concentration because
Venus has moved into Scorpio while the occupied centres remain distributed. The
slow outer frame, inner-planet elongations and lunation geometry jointly limit
how large an empty arc is available.

Low-gap geometry does not mean chaos, plurality or cooperation by itself. It
means only that no very large unoccupied arc organizes the ten-planet set.
Aspects, houses, roots and human Judgment decide what the distribution means.
