Fisa 702 Split Clock State

Current saved study

Two clocks. **Clock 1 — the statute.** Title VII of FISA, including section 702 (50 U.S.C. § 1881a), is repealed **effective June 12, 2026**, except as provided in section 404 of the FISA Amendments Act of 2008. The repeal sits in FAA § 403(b), last amended by **Public Law 119-87** (Apr. 30, 2026). House OLRC prelim, text in effect August 14, 2026, still prints that date. No later enacted reauthorization was retrieved at cutoff. **Clock 2 — the paper already issued.** FAA § 404(b)(1) (50 U.S.C. § 1801 note): any **order, authorization, or directive** issued under Title VII “shall continue in effect until the date of the expiration of such order, authorization, or directive.” Section 702 itself, while live, let the Attorney General and the DNI authorize targeting “for a period of **up to 1 year** from the effective date of the authorization.” **H.R. 9238** would have moved the repeal from June 12, 2026 to July 2, 2026. The House **rejected** it on June 11, 2026: **198–218** on a motion to suspend the rules. Tracker: Failed House. **FISC order not retrieved at cutoff.** The FISC public docket and ODNI/intel.gov releases do **not** contain a March 2026 (or nearby) section 702 certification opinion. No grandfather end date is written here. The charter to start **new** 702 work is gone as of June 12. Paper already signed, if any still has time on it, is a different object.

Title 10 Guard And Insurrection Act Authority State

Current saved study

The President federalized Guard under **10 U.S.C. § 12406**. He did **not** invoke the Insurrection Act, **10 U.S.C. §§ 251–255**. No § 254 proclamation to disperse was retrieved. *Trump v. Illinois*, No. 25A443 (Dec. 23, 2025), **denied** the Government’s stay. The unsigned order held that “regular forces” in § 12406(3) “likely refers to the regular forces of the United States military.” Because the President had not invoked a statute that excepts the Posse Comitatus Act, 18 U.S.C. § 1385, the Government had not carried its burden to show that § 12406(3) lets him federalize the Guard to protect federal personnel and property in Illinois. The Court did **not** decide the Insurrection Act. Justice Kavanaugh said so in footnote 2 of his concurrence. Vault notes that say “SCOTUS greenlit the Insurrection Act” name a different object. The order leaves that statute unused and the Illinois deployment barred at the stay stage.

Iran War Powers Authority State

Current saved study

No 2026 Iran declaration of war. No enacted Iran AUMF. **H.J. Res. 176** (Barrett) is introduced only — referred to House Foreign Affairs 2026-05-07. It is not law. The President directed force on **2026-02-28** and sent Congress a written report dated **2026-03-02** (printed as **H. Doc. 119-139**). That letter rests on **Article II** (Commander in Chief and Chief Executive). It does **not** cite the 2001 AUMF, the 2002 AUMF, or any other statute as the force grant. Later 2026 letters (June 12, June 29, July 10, August 3) again cite Article II and say they are “consistent with” the War Powers Resolution. They do not enact authority. **H. Con. Res. 86** is the only 2026 Iran instrument **agreed by both chambers**. House **215–208** (2026-06-03, Roll 199). Senate **50–48** (2026-06-23, Vote 184). Enrolled the same day. It is a **concurrent** resolution under WPR **§ 5(c) / 50 U.S.C. § 1544(c)**. A concurrent resolution is **not presented** to the President. It is **not** a public law. It cannot be vetoed because it never reaches the desk. No later **joint** resolution directing removal was presented, vetoed, or enacted. Senate discharge votes on the joint-resolution path (**S.J. Res. 172, 180, 181, 184**; motion on **S.J. Res. 185**) all **failed**. **H.J. Res. 156** (clock-compliance joint resolution) is introduced only. **Claim cut.** “Congress ended the war” is **not established**. What is established: both chambers **expressed** a § 5(c) direction, without a veto-proof statute, without presentment, and without an enacted AUMF. **The 60-day clock result is not computed here.** § 1544(b) runs only from a report **submitted or required under § 1543(a)(1)** (hostilities). The March 2 letter’s **text** does not name a subsection. The **GPO/House header** on H. Doc. 119-139 classifies the communication under **§ 1543(a)(3)** (substantial enlargement) and **§ 1543(b)** — not (a)(1). Barrett’s **unenacted** recitals say the sixty days have expired. Those recitals are not a court finding and not a statute.

Ieepa Emergency Powers Tariff And Blocking State

Current saved study

*Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump* and *Trump v. V.O.S. Selections, Inc.*, Nos. 24-1287 and 25-250 (Feb. 20, 2026), 607 U.S. ___: > **Held:** IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs. The Federal Circuit judgment against the tariffs (No. 25-250) is **affirmed**. The D.D.C. action (No. 24-1287) is **vacated and remanded with instructions to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction**. The tariff challenges belong on the CIT / Federal Circuit path. Roberts, C.J., announced the judgment. Parts I, II–A–1, and II–B (the statutory holding that “regulate … importation” does not include the power to tax) are the opinion of the Court, joined by Sotomayor, Kagan, Gorsuch, Barrett, and Jackson. Parts II–A–2 and III (major-questions overlay) are Roberts, Gorsuch, and Barrett only. Kagan, joined by Sotomayor and Jackson, concurred in the judgment on ordinary statutory tools and would not have invoked major questions. Thomas dissented. Kavanaugh dissented, joined by Thomas and Alito. **Judgment 6–3.** The list in 50 U.S.C. § 1702(a)(1)(B) is investigate, block, regulate, direct and compel, nullify, void, prevent or prohibit — acquisition, holding, use, transfer, importation, exportation, dealing in property. **“Absent from this lengthy list of specific powers is any mention of tariffs or duties.”** Had Congress meant the tariff power, it would have said so, as it does in the statutes that actually grant duties (Trade Expansion Act § 232; Trade Act § 301). **Same day, operating paper.** EO 14389 (Feb. 20, 2026; 91 FR 9437): the additional **ad valorem duties imposed pursuant to IEEPA** in the listed 2025–26 emergency orders “shall no longer be in effect and, as soon as practicable, shall no longer be collected.” **The national emergencies remain.** Actions that are not those IEEPA duties remain. Section 232 and section 301 duties are **unaffected**. EO 14388 (de minimis suspension) is **unaffected**. **Same day, different statute.** Proclamation **11012** (Feb. 20, 2026; 91 FR 9339): a 10 percent ad valorem surcharge under Trade Act § 122, 19 U.S.C. § 2132. The statute lets the President proclaim a surcharge **not exceeding 15 percent** for a period **not exceeding 150 days** unless extended by Act of Congress. The proclamation itself: goods entered or withdrawn for consumption on or after 12:01 a.m. EST **February 24, 2026**, through 12:01 a.m. EDT **July 24, 2026**, unless earlier ended or extended by Act of Congress. **No extending Act retrieved at cutoff.** That is not IEEPA. A later CIT judgment on 11012 is a different object, named below. **Claim cut.** “IEEPA is dead” is **not established**. The tariff use is dead. The block/regulate/nullify machine is the statute Congress wrote. OFAC still runs on it.

Universal Relief After Trump V Casa

Current saved study

On June 27, 2025 the Supreme Court, per Justice Barrett, held that **universal injunctions likely exceed the equitable authority Congress gave the federal courts in the Judiciary Act of 1789.** The Court granted the government's applications for partial stays of three preliminary injunctions against EO 14160, "but only to the extent that the injunctions are broader than necessary to provide complete relief to each plaintiff with standing to sue." The applications "do not raise — and thus the Court does not address — the question whether the Executive Order violates the Citizenship Clause or Nationality Act." That sentence is load-bearing. *CASA* did not decide birthright. *Barbara* did, a year later. What remains open, because the Court said so: APA vacatur, nationwide class relief under Rule 23, and how wide "complete relief" runs when the plaintiff is a State. A single district judge no longer freezes the whole country for non-parties. That changes the operating clock of every later order on the EO spine.
