# Project Meridian: The Future of Warfare — Authority, State and Return Gates

**Status:** `current`  
**Research ID:** `military-modernization`  
**Factual cutoff:** 2026-09-30  
**Scope:** the signed commissioning memorandum, same-day Department release, the 2026 National Security Science and Technology Strategy it invokes, and the public governance record available on the announcement date  
**Source priority:** signed memorandum → Department release → cited strategy → governing advisory-committee law and guidance → later implementation receipts

## Short answer

Project Meridian is real. Secretary Pete Hegseth signed a one-page memorandum effective immediately on September 30, 2026 directing Department of War Chief Technology Officer Emil Michael to commission, **through an unnamed partner organization**, a 120-day study of the future battlefield. Its function is to map the trajectory of warfare, identify capability gaps, and recommend weapons, technologies and actions the Department could begin developing, testing and fielding.

The Department's same-day release supplies details not present in the signed memorandum: Elon Musk, Palmer Luckey and Newt Gingrich are to co-direct the project; the team will include academic, policy and industry experts; and the result is intended to include a public unclassified report plus a classified annex.

The current state is therefore **commissioned study and recommendation process**. It is not yet a combatant command, acquisition program, appropriation, contract award, approved capability requirement, test result, fielding decision or operational capability. Project Agincourt and the announced Autonomous Warfare Command are separate initiatives in the same six-initiative release.

The memorandum says the findings and recommendations are due **January 28, 2027**, 120 days after the announcement. The due date is a return gate, not proof that a report will be delivered publicly on that date or that any recommendation will be adopted.

## What the remit covers

The memorandum names rapid advances in artificial intelligence, autonomous systems, directed energy, robotics and biotechnology. It asks Meridian to:

1. study the character of future battlefields;
2. identify weapons and technologies likely to matter;
3. identify capability gaps;
4. propose actionable solutions for development, testing and fielding; and
5. connect the Joint Staff's and services' existing future-warfare research to the project.

The cited National Security Science and Technology Strategy makes the likely policy vocabulary more concrete. Its battlefield priorities include the undersea domain, space from very-low Earth orbit through cislunar space, and AI/autonomy, with air, long-range strike and C5ISR also prioritized. Its enabling technologies include advanced manufacturing and materials, communications and networking, directed energy, future computing, hypersonics and missiles, cyber and information management, nuclear energy, positioning/navigation/timing, semiconductors, sensing/signature management and space technology. Its appendices also include robotics and embodied intelligence, multi-agent and swarm systems, autonomous command and control, biotechnology and neurotechnology.

That strategy alignment is a research map, not a forecast that Meridian will recommend every listed field.

## The institutional seam

The most consequential sentence may be the least dramatic one: the CTO is to commission the project **through a partner organization**. The public documents reviewed here do not name that organization or say whether the relationship is a grant, contract, cooperative agreement, other transaction, nonprofit sponsorship, informal convening arrangement or government-established advisory committee.

That missing instrument controls several questions:

- who selects, pays and can remove participants;
- who owns the work product and underlying data;
- whether meetings, records and membership are public;
- which ethics, procurement-integrity, security and conflict rules apply;
- whether the government or the outside co-directors controls agendas and recommendations;
- how classified information will be handled; and
- who formally accepts, rejects or routes the recommendations.

## Advisory-committee and conflict boundary

The Federal Advisory Committee Act can apply when a committee, panel or task force is established or utilized to obtain advice or recommendations for the President or a federal agency. Covered advisory committees ordinarily require an authorized basis, a formal determination where applicable, notice and a charter; their function remains advisory, and government officials retain decision authority. The legal outcome depends on the actual constitution and control of the group. A body created and managed by an independent contractor can present a different analysis from one established or tightly controlled by the Department.

No Project Meridian charter, Federal Register establishment notice or public FACA database listing was located by the same-day cutoff. That is an unresolved-status finding, not a conclusion that FACA applies or that the project is noncompliant. The controlling follow-up is the partner instrument and the facts of government selection, management and control.

Primary legal sources: [5 U.S.C. § 1001 definitions](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title5-section1001); [5 U.S.C. § 1008 establishment and purpose](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title5-section1008); [GSA FACA guidance](https://www.gsa.gov/policy-regulations/policy/faca-overview/advice-and-guidance).

The three announced co-directors also make conflict architecture a substantive rather than cosmetic question. Musk and Luckey are associated with firms holding or seeking defense, space, communications, AI and autonomy work. That does not establish improper influence. It does mean the eventual instrument should be tested for disclosure, recusal, data access, intellectual-property treatment, procurement-sensitive information and whether recommendations are capability-neutral or vendor-specific.

## Separation from Project Agincourt

The Department announced six initiatives together. Meridian is the **study and recommendations** lane. Project Agincourt is the separate lane associated with an Autonomous Warfare Command and autonomous-system development and fielding. Their subject matter can overlap, and Meridian recommendations could later feed Agincourt or other programs, but neither project is evidence that the other has completed its work.

Keep this sequence explicit:

> commission → constitute team → conduct study → deliver report → government disposition → requirement/budget/solicitation → award → test → field → operational result

Skipping from the first step to the last would turn an institutional design exercise into an unproved capability claim.

## Research judgment

Meridian is important because the Department is not merely buying an individual technology. It is inviting prominent private technologists, an industry founder and a former senior policymaker into a concentrated exercise to describe the future battlefield and propose an actionable development agenda across multiple domains.

The plausible significance is architectural: this process may help define which problems become requirements, which technologies become priorities and which interfaces connect service research to future programs. The formal government decision, funding, acquisition and operational chains still remain separate until later instruments move them.

## Return gates

1. **Partner identity and instrument** — named organization, legal vehicle, scope of work, funding and custody terms.
2. **Governance** — charter, membership, selection, meeting rules, records, FACA determination and government control.
3. **Conflict controls** — disclosures, recusals, procurement-sensitive information, proprietary data and vendor-neutrality rules.
4. **Work process** — requests for information, workshops, service/Joint Staff inputs and interim products.
5. **January 28, 2027 deliverable** — receipt, public report, classified annex and any missed or revised clock.
6. **Disposition** — who accepts, rejects, ranks or routes each recommendation.
7. **Implementation** — named requirements, appropriations, budget lines, solicitations, contracts, tests and fielding decisions.
8. **Results** — delivered capability, operational acceptance, commander use and measured performance.
