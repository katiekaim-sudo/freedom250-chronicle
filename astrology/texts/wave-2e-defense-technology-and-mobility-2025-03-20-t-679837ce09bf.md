# Wave 2E — Defense, Technology and Mobility

**Research window:** Exact 2025 Aries ingress through exact 2025 Cancer ingress.  
**Question:** Did the defense, cyber, maritime, aviation and drone commands become deployed capability before Cancer, or did they chiefly change the route by which capability could be selected, approved and scaled?  
**Finding:** This sector makes the Aries regime's method unusually visible. Defense acquisition acquired a commercial-first preference, program-review clocks and a stronger ability to cancel or redirect slow programs. The Army translated the broader posture into an April 30 transformation memorandum, a May 1 public program and a May 20 Maven contract modification. Yet the software-acquisition shift had already begun with a March 6 Defense memorandum, and the underlying programs and vendors predated the April order. Foreign military sales, maritime policy, supersonic flight, airspace security, cyber standards and drone integration mostly remained lists, plans, rulemaking assignments and future systems at the Cancer boundary. Their later rules, program selections and operating receipts belong to later waves.

The controlling ladder is:

`policy preference → program office or review → requirement/list/rule → solicitation or agreement → award/authorization → test → fielding/operation → validated effect`

An order may move the first three rungs quickly. It does not turn a contract ceiling into expenditure, a proposed rule into permission, a pilot into a fleet or a software output into a validated military decision.

## The chain in one view

```text
commercial-first / speed / sovereignty doctrine
    ↓
program, partner, item and vendor categories are reclassified
    ↓
review, approval, cancellation, export and access rights are reassigned
    ↓
program office, contract modification, list, rulemaking or pilot intake
    ↓
test, authorization, fielding and operation
    ↓
validated military, cyber, aviation or logistical effect
```

The Aries change is **a new selection and acceleration grammar**, not a completed autonomous, maritime or aviation system.

## 2E.1 — March 6 to April 9: defense software reform begins before the acquisition order

**Mover.** Secretary of Defense, service acquisition executives, program executive offices, Defense Innovation Unit, commercial vendors and operational commands.

The Defense Department's [March 6 modern-software memorandum](https://www.defense.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/4114775/modern-software-acquisition-to-speed-delivery-boost-warfighter-lethality/) directed use of the Software Acquisition Pathway, Commercial Solutions Openings and Other Transaction authority before EO 14265 was signed. That earlier move matters: the April order consolidated a live direction rather than creating commercial and iterative software acquisition from nothing.

[EO 14265](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/15/2025-06436/modernizing-defense-acquisitions-and-spurring-innovation-in-the-defense-industrial-base) then widened the method across major acquisition:

- commercial solutions and rapid-capability pathways were to become the preference where appropriate;
- an acquisition-transformation plan was due in 60 days;
- internal acquisition regulations and guidance were to be reviewed;
- a workforce plan was due in 120 days;
- major programs more than 15 percent late or over budget, failing key performance parameters or no longer aligned with mission were to be reviewed for cancellation; and
- the Joint Capabilities Integration and Development System was to be reviewed within 180 days.

**Maturity at Cancer ingress.** Effective decision criteria, review clocks and an acceleration preference. The order did not itself cancel a named program, execute an award or validate a capability.

**Counterevidence and limit.** “Commercial-first” remained bounded by mission, law, security, testing, appropriations and the phrase “where appropriate.” The March 6 memo proves continuity; it prevents us from crediting the whole reform to April 9.

**Plotline.** The regime tries to replace a requirements-first bureaucracy with a problem-and-performance hearing: what can be bought, adapted and fielded now, and which inherited program no longer justifies its place?

## 2E.2 — April 11 to May 20: the Army gives the preference an operating carrier

The Army lane produced the clearest in-window descendants:

- **April 11:** the Next Generation Command and Control effort acquired an official program-office carrier. Its proximity to the April 9 order is notable, but the program's prior development means the date does not prove the order originated it.
- **April 30 / May 1:** the Secretary of Defense's [Army Transformation Initiative](https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/4171008/secretary-of-defense-directed-review-of-army-transformation-and-acquisition-ref/) directed force-structure changes, acquisition reform, right-to-repair review, performance-based contracting, multiyear procurement where economical, and priority for long-range fires, air and missile defense, cyber, electronic warfare, counter-space, AI-enabled command and unmanned systems.
- **May 20:** the Army issued a **$795 million contract modification ceiling** for the Maven Smart System through 2029.

**Maturity.** Program office, department-level implementation direction and contract capacity. These are more mature than an EO instruction but remain short of validated, force-wide fielding.

**Counterevidence and limit.** A ceiling is not an obligation or outlay of the full amount. A program office is not a deployed network. An exercise or field test is not fleet-wide adoption. A model output is not a validated target, and none of these sources changes the separate legal requirements for human command, rules of engagement or lawful force.

**Plotline.** The institutional mover is the program office: doctrine becomes durable when a named organization can own requirements, contracting, integration and iteration.

## 2E.3 — April 9: foreign defense sales become a partner-and-item sorting problem

[EO 14268](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/15/2025-06438/reforming-foreign-defense-sales-to-improve-speed-and-accountability) ordered:

- identification of priority partners and priority defense items;
- review of the list of items limited to the Foreign Military Sales channel;
- metrics for speed and accountability;
- earlier exportability design and consolidated approval processes; and
- a plan for a single electronic tracking system.

**Maturity at Cancer ingress.** List formation, process review and system design. The order did not approve a particular sale, create a Letter of Offer and Acceptance, issue an export license, deliver equipment or settle payment.

**Later body.** On October 15, 2025, [DSCA Policy Memorandum 25-66](https://samm.dsca.mil/policy-memoranda/dsca-25-66) renamed and revised the FMS-Only list as the Government-to-Government Only List after a zero-based review. That is a real later rule/procedure receipt: sensitive transfers remained in a government channel while more items could potentially use direct commercial sales. It belongs to the Libra wave, not the Aries maturity column.

**Counterevidence and limit.** A policy that permits a commercial channel is not a completed commercial export. State still supervises arms-transfer decisions, and sensitivity, treaty, legal, end-use and source-of-supply gates survive.

## 2E.4 — April 9: maritime dominance is designed as a future public-capital and logistics organism

[EO 14269](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/15/2025-06439/restoring-americas-maritime-dominance) asked for a Maritime Action Plan and a broad set of reports, assessments and legislative proposals covering shipyards, financing, harbor fees, workforce, mariners, sealift, the reserve fleet, commercial-vessel acquisition, shipbuilding incentives, prosperity zones and a possible Maritime Security Trust Fund and Strategic Commercial Fleet.

**Maturity at Cancer ingress.** Planning, assessment and legislative design. Existing Ready Reserve Force, Maritime Security Program and Tanker Security Program capacity continued under their pre-existing authorities; it should not be relabeled as newly created by the order.

**Later body.** The Maritime Action Plan was published in February 2026. Even then, proposed trust funds, fleets and financing structures remained distinct from enactment, capitalization, vessel acquisition and ship construction.

**Counterevidence and limit.** A fleet named in a plan is not a legal person or a hull. Prepared access is not deployment; capital mobilization is not an outlay; a shipyard policy is not a delivered vessel.

**Plotline.** The maritime order reveals the same Aries instinct at a larger physical scale: build a coordinating plan able to connect trade rules, capital, labor, industrial sites and military logistics, then wait for each material carrier to acquire law and money.

## 2E.5 — June 6: mobility sovereignty moves into rules, lists and data

Four orders signed on June 6 form one political cluster but four different machines.

| Order | Object being reorganized | State at Cancer ingress | Later receipt that must keep its own date |
|---|---|---|---|
| [EO 14304](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/06/11/2025-10800/leading-the-world-in-supersonic-flight) | Overland supersonic prohibition, noise certification, federal R&D and international standards | Rulemaking and R&D coordination only; no repealed rule, certified aircraft or commercial overland service | FAA proposed an overland supersonic noise framework on June 30, 2026—after the 2026 Cancer ingress cutoff used by this project |
| [EO 14305](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/06/11/2025-10811/restoring-american-airspace-sovereignty) | Fixed-site restrictions, Remote ID access, threat detection, enforcement and counter-UAS coordination | Task force, data/rule assignments and future training architecture | FAA proposed a petition process for critical-infrastructure restrictions on May 6, 2026; still proposed, not a universal no-fly rule |
| [EO 14306](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/06/11/2025-10804/sustaining-select-efforts-to-strengthen-the-nations-cybersecurity-and-amending-executive-order-13694) | Secure software, patch controls, post-quantum readiness, AI vulnerability management and machine-readable cyber policy | Amended policy plus deadlines; almost all named deliverables were due after Cancer | NIST formed its software-security consortium and issued draft/final control work beginning July–August 2025; later federal PQC and national-security-system actions retain their later clocks |
| [EO 14307](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/06/11/2025-10814/unleashing-american-drone-dominance) | BVLOS operations, waiver review, eVTOL pilots, domestic vendor preference, export finance and military procurement | Proposed-rule, metric, list, pilot and report assignments; no routine BVLOS permission, eVTOL project or export transaction | FAA proposed the BVLOS framework August 6, 2025; launched eIPP in September; selected eight projects March 9, 2026 |

### The cyber order edits inheritance rather than starting over

EO 14306 did not create a blank cyber constitution. It amended EO 14144, removed some provisions, narrowed the cyber-sanctions language from “any person” to “any foreign person,” and retained or retimed selected secure-software, patching, post-quantum, AI-vulnerability and rules-as-code work.

The result is an administrative chain:

`software practice → published control → machine-readable policy → procurement requirement → agency implementation`

At Cancer, the chain was still mostly assigned. [NIST's implementation page](https://www.nist.gov/itl/executive-orders/strengthening-and-promoting-innovation-nations-cybersecurity) records later work including the DevSecOps consortium, SP 800-53 patch guidance and the SSDF update. None of that should be backdated to June 20.

### The drone orders create two complementary sovereignty poles

EO 14305 asks who may enter sensitive airspace and who may see the identifying record. EO 14307 asks who may routinely operate, manufacture, export, finance and train with a trusted UAS.

Together they define the access problem:

- secure the perimeter and make the operator legible;
- normalize approved operations through performance-based rules;
- privilege domestic or approved suppliers;
- use lists and procurement rules to exclude supply-chain risk;
- use test ranges and pilots to produce safety evidence; and
- scale only after a rule, authorization or agreement exists.

**Counterevidence and limit.** Remote ID access is not guilt. A Covered Foreign Entity List is not a domestic contract. A proposed BVLOS rule is not general operating authority. An eVTOL selection is not a certified aircraft or passenger flight. Export finance priority is not a loan or shipment.

## What changed during the Aries period

- Defense acquisition acquired a stronger commercial/rapid-path preference and explicit program-review criteria.
- The Army gave that preference an organization, transformation directive and contract vehicle.
- Foreign defense sales were reframed around priority partners, priority items and channel eligibility.
- Maritime policy acquired a whole-system planning mandate spanning capital, labor, shipyards, trade and sealift.
- Supersonic, drone and airspace policy acquired new rulemaking and integration agendas.
- Cybersecurity acquired a selective inheritance, machine-readable-policy ambition and post-quantum/AI vulnerability clocks.
- Drones became simultaneously a commercial-mobility, military-capability, industrial-base, export and airspace-identity object.

## What did not change merely because the orders existed

- No reviewed source proves EO 14265 itself originated the March software reform or every later Army program.
- No order automatically cancelled a named major acquisition program.
- A contract modification ceiling was not the same as full obligation, outlay or validated fielding.
- A priority partner/item list was not a foreign military sale.
- A Maritime Action Plan was not a trust fund, strategic fleet or ship.
- A rulemaking command was not a final rule or operating permission.
- A cyber standard was not agency compliance or network security.
- A UAS list, waiver tool or pilot was not routine national deployment.
- No autonomous lethal decision authority is inferred from acquisition or AI language.

## Institutional theorem

Aries does not abolish the acquisition state. It tries to make it **iterative, selective and machine-hearable**.

The common move is to relocate delay and judgment upstream:

1. define the approved category, partner, vendor or operational risk;
2. give one program office, list owner or regulator the first hearing;
3. prefer a rapid, commercial or performance-based route;
4. preserve a security, safety, export or legal gate at the point of use; and
5. learn from tests and operations fast enough to revise the rule.

Constructively, this can shorten the distance between a real need and a useful capability. In shadow, it can make speed, vendor proximity and executive priority substitutes for independent testing, interoperability, public accountability and lawful restraint.

## Both poles

### Constructive pole — capability learns faster

- program offices can iterate with users rather than freeze a requirement years early;
- commercial and nontraditional suppliers can enter the hearing;
- partner and item criteria become more legible;
- tests and pilots can generate evidence for regulation;
- cyber standards can become machine-readable and procurement-aware; and
- domestic industrial capacity can be joined to military, civil and export demand.

### Shadow pole — acceleration outruns adjudication

- executive urgency can preselect the answer before testing;
- vendor ecosystems can become de facto governance;
- commercial speed can obscure lifecycle cost, lock-in and interoperability;
- operator identity and airspace data can expand surveillance without proportionate remedy;
- autonomous or AI-assisted systems can acquire institutional momentum before their outputs are validated; and
- proposed fleets, aircraft and cyber controls can be narrated as present capability.

The evidence supports a new route to capability. It does not support a completed autonomous warfare, maritime, supersonic, drone or cyber state by June 20, 2025.
