Permanent Magnets — the hidden motor stack
In plain English
Question: What is the current ore-to-qualified-part state for NdFeB and SmCo, and which public supports are building a domestic market rather than merely proposed capacity?
Answer: China still controls most steps of the rare-earth magnet chain. The U.S. has restarted real magnet production at a few plants, and Washington is building a protected home market through equity stakes, price floors, loans, tariffs and defense buying rules. Most promised U.S. capacity is still plans.

Short answer
The permanent-magnet field is in a real industrial transition, but the public story is usually one layer too shallow.
China still controls the system because it combines separation, metal and alloy production, magnet manufacturing, equipment, process knowledge and scale. The United States has restarted genuine commercial magnet production through Noveon, MP Materials and eVAC, with USA Rare Earth commissioning a fourth platform. But the flood of proposed American capacity for 2027–2030 is not yet equivalent to qualified, continuous output.
The decisive change is the role of the state. Federal policy has moved beyond grants into equity, warrants, conditional and final loans, price floors, guaranteed offtake, tariff protection, strategic inventory finance and contractor supply-chain mandates. MP Materials is the clearest national-champion model; USA Rare Earth, Vulcan/ReElement and Energy Fuels show variants of the same structure.
The near-term bottleneck is therefore not simply geology. It is the conversion and qualification chain:
separated NdPr/Dy/Tb/Sm oxide → metal → alloy/flake → sintered or bonded magnet → coating and machining → motor or component qualification → sustained deliveries

What is actually live in the United States
Commercial magnet production and shipments
Noveon Magnetics, San Marcos, Texas: established commercial sintered-NdFeB production. GM deliveries began in July 2025; ABB monthly deliveries began in August 2025; a five-year Nidec agreement contemplates more than 1,000 total tonnes. Its facility is commonly described as having 2,000-tpa capacity, but a public audited sustained-output series was not located. GM agreement ABB agreement Nidec agreement
MP Materials, Independence, Texas: commercial metal production began in 2024; first alloy flake and finished magnets on commercial equipment followed in 2025. MP said 2026 would be the GM ramp year. The planned 10X campus would bring total U.S. nameplate to roughly 10,000 tpa, with commissioning targeted for 2028. 2025 results 10X site
eVAC Magnetics, Sumter, South Carolina: shipped its first U.S.-made commercial NdFeB magnets in December 2025. The company said the new plant would scale to 2,000 tpa in Q1 2026 and could expand further; sustained 2026 throughput has not yet been independently established in the reviewed public record. eVAC first shipment
Commissioning, planned scale or adjacent output
USA Rare Earth, Stillwater, Oklahoma: commissioned Phase 1a in March 2026 and targeted customer orders in Q2, a 600-tpa run rate by year-end 2026 and 1,200 tpa in Q1 2027. Those are separate clocks. Its June 2026 Commerce agreement supports eventual 10,000-tpa magnet and 10,000-tpa metal/alloy capabilities across Stillwater, Blacksburg and Round Top. Commerce final agreement
Vulcan Elements, North Carolina: operates a small commercial/R&D facility; its proposed Benson campus is a 10,000-tpa project. The $620 million Office of Strategic Capital loan commitment is conditional, not a disbursed final loan. ReElement's related $80 million commitment supports the refining side. OSC announcement
Energy Fuels, Utah: commercially produces separated NdPr oxide and is developing heavy-rare-earth separation and metallization. It is a midstream supplier, not presently a U.S. finished-magnet producer. Its $725 million OSC financing announced in June 2026 is conditional. OSC announcement
Niron Magnetics, Minnesota: samples rare-earth-free iron-nitride magnets and is developing pilot/Plant 1 plus a proposed 10,000-tpa site. It is strategically interesting substitution technology, not yet a bulk substitute supply source. Niron manufacturing plan
The China control mechanism
China's leverage is not merely its mine share.
Industrial concentration: separation, metallization, alloying and magnet production are more concentrated than mining.
Export licensing: China's April 4, 2025 controls cover seven medium/heavy rare-earth families, including specified permanent-magnet materials containing controlled elements. A license requirement is not a total ban, but it creates transaction-level permission and delay risk. MOFCOM Announcement No. 18
Multiple legal clocks: broader October 2025 controls were later suspended to November 10, 2026, while the April controls remained in force. General licenses for selected exporters eased some flows without ending the control architecture. SCIO suspension explanation April 2026 status
Equipment and knowledge: production equipment, recipes, yield learning, patents and a skilled workforce are part of the moat.
Price power: excess capacity and low prices can make non-China projects unfinanceable even while physical concentration makes them strategically necessary.

The clock that matters now
The July 20, 2026 executive order moves the defense market from general sourcing preference toward supplier-level proof.
Starting January 1, 2027, routine non-availability waivers under 10 U.S.C. 4872 are to cease unless a contractor accepts an approved mitigation plan.
Within 180 days, the Department of War is to develop policy for mapping critical supply chains through all contractor tiers; implementing regulations follow within 90 days.
The order defines an “indentured Bill of Materials” back to raw-material origin and orders accelerated testing and qualification.
This turns qualification records, bills of material, foreign ownership/control, feedstock origin and funded mitigation plans into immediate corporate variables. July 20 order
Demand: much wider than the EV headline
Defense: actuators, guidance, radar, aircraft, submarines, drones, satellites and many motors.
Industrial/data centers: ABB's Noveon agreement explicitly ties magnets to motors for cooling towers, pumps, building systems, HVAC and data-center growth.
Semiconductor fabrication: motors and magnetic components in lithography, deposition, etch and vacuum equipment; Commerce used this as the CHIPS nexus for magnet awards.
Automotive: propulsion, steering, pumps, speakers and auxiliary systems in both combustion and electric vehicles.
Robotics and automation: compact high-torque motors.
Consumer electronics and storage: speakers, haptics, cameras and hard-disk drives.
Energy: direct-drive wind, generators and efficient industrial motors.
Substitution and recycling
There are three different substitution claims:
reduce heavy rare earths inside NdFeB through grain-boundary diffusion and microstructure control;
replace NdFeB in selected motor designs with ferrite, wound-field or reluctance systems;
replace rare-earth magnets as material with candidates such as iron nitride.
They should not be combined. Proterial and Shin-Etsu are advancing heavy-rare-earth-free or saving NdFeB formulations; this reduces Dy/Tb exposure but still uses NdFeB. Niron is a genuine rare-earth-free material program but remains pre-scale. Proterial technology Shin-Etsu magnet business
Recycling also has two clocks: factory scrap can return quickly and predictably; end-of-life recovery depends on collection, disassembly, chemistry and product design. Noveon, MP/Apple and Shin-Etsu demonstrate meaningful routes, but recycling cannot be counted as free new supply.
Strongest Freedom 250 joins
1. The state is becoming the market maker
The magnet stack joins the Observatory's broader state–private seam: public capital, private factories, price support, offtake guarantees, tariffs and mandatory qualification are being assembled into one industrial control system.
2. Project Vault is a strategic-liquidity mechanism
The reserve sits between Treasury/EXIM-style public finance, commodity traders and industrial users. It is inventory finance for supply continuity, not simply a pile of rocks.
3. The “processing” claim is correct but incomplete
The vault's Bessent and Burgum events correctly identify processing. The deeper version is: separation, metallization, alloy, magnet-making yield and customer qualification are different choke points.
4. Data-center growth has a physical motor layer
The stack links Technology/AI to mines and industrial motors through cooling, pumps, robotics, storage and semiconductor tools. “AI infrastructure” is not only chips and electricity.
5. Capacity announcements may outrun feedstock and customers
If every announced U.S. plant reaches nameplate, the late-2020s market could contain duplicated capacity, feedstock competition and dependence on price floors. The correct watch is qualified tonnes delivered by specification—not ribbon cuttings or aggregate nameplate.
Bottom line
This is a real reindustrialization story, not just rhetoric. But it is still early, state-shaped and vulnerable to category errors. The most defensible current reading is:
China retains system control; the United States has restarted real production; Washington is constructing a protected domestic market; and the decisive proof will be qualified, continuous, origin-traceable deliveries through 2027–2029.
