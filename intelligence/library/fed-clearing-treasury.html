Research summary

The September 20 legal-status and records-routing matrix establishes that the Federal Reserve has no single public/private status: the answer varies by actor, function, statute, record custody and remedy. The Board is a federal agency; the FOMC is an APA/FOIA agency; each Reserve Bank is a separate federally chartered corporation whose agency, instrumentality, employer, person and liability status changes by legal regime. Records likewise route by function and control rather than physical host: Bank-held material can be an FOMC record, a current-rule Board record, or a Bank record subject to the Banks' voluntary transparency policy and other lawful access routes. The companion appointment, removal and succession chain maps five distinct personnel constitutions from Governors and Reserve Bank directors through FOMC officers, SOMA management, the Inspector General and Bank general auditors; it also restores the August whole-government FRFS/National IT committee constitution to the direct package's discovery routes. The Wave 4 reconciliation confirms that the prior committee map was already substantial and classifies its bodies by legal effect: statutory Board/FOMC/Bank-board decisions, Board advisory and delegated routes, PSPAC policy advice, COP/Payments Committee/AFRC/ITOC enterprise governance, CPE/FRFS management, Richmond/National IT hosting and independent assurance. It distinguishes PCSC, PSPAC and the presidents' Payments Committee and preserves the unpublished charters, votes, materiality tests and application RACI as proof gaps. Wave 5 separates inspection and recommendation from approval, conditioning, direction, injunction, removal and institutional suspension. It finds extensive but nonuniversal audit, preserves GAO's monetary-policy exclusions and Dodd-Frank exceptions, defines the clean 2025 outside-audit limits, and locates actual stopping power in Board/Bank-board/FOMC/Treasury/court/congressional instruments rather than in the audit label itself. Wave 6 finds no unitary Federal Reserve crisis commander: insured-bank resolution, daylight and Discount Window credit, §13(3), FOMC/SOMA operations, payment and cyber incidents, Buddy Reserve Bank continuity, §248(h) intervention, cash logistics and Treasury fiscal-agent recovery have distinct legal principals, operators, books, finality and exit rules. It separates participant buddy banks from inter-Reserve-Bank continuity and service restoration from transaction reconciliation. Wave 7 reconstructs the twelve corporations' foundation: the Federal Reserve Act is the congressional charter; filing a district organization certificate created the body corporate; the Comptroller's separate certificate authorized business; and bylaws govern internally beneath federal law. It inventories all twelve current-posted bylaws and twenty-four Branches, distinguishes statutory member stock from ordinary equity, and identifies the direct St. Louis certificate plus exact missing constitutive, election, seal and title records. The September 20 congressional-oversight map shows why the Federal Reserve is not legally a fourth constitutional branch while preserving the real accountability critique: Congress created the System, controls its statutes and institutional design, confirms Governors through the Senate, mandates testimony, reports, audits and disclosures, and can amend or repeal the architecture; yet self-funding, long protected terms, nonconfirmed Reserve Bank presidents, GAO exclusions, OIG jurisdictional seams and monetary-policy exceptions deliberately insulate immediate policy operations. The vault-canonical factual spine and current Workbench extensions show how the Fed can retain the dollar unit and crisis backstop while private clearing moves into the middle and the twelve Reserve Banks specialize inside a common operating machine. The whole-site evidence supports contested functional unbundling and reduced daily visibility, not established castration, abolition, merger, private capture or removal. The September 20 dollar-issuance constitution shows that the Federal Reserve's role is a congressional statutory operating arrangement, not a private agreement or transfer of U.S. sovereignty. Congress defines the unit and legal-tender system; Treasury issues coins, retains capped United States-note authority and prints Federal Reserve notes; the Board orders and authorizes those notes; Reserve Banks issue notes into circulation and maintain central-bank balances; and private deposits and stablecoins remain legally distinct claims. Congress can restructure or replace that architecture by ordinary federal law, while executive orders, regulations and operator contracts cannot rewrite the statute. The companion congressional change history proves this is not merely theoretical: from 1927 corporate succession and the 1930s governance and gold rewrites through the 1977 objectives, 1980 monetary-and-payment reach, 2008 interest on reserves, 2010 emergency-credit limits and 2025–26 digital-money boundaries, Congress has repeatedly expanded, constrained and reallocated Fed powers. It also separates legislative dissolution from violation-based franchise forfeiture and the public dollar unit from Federal Reserve notes and private redeemable dollar claims. The Oklahoma City hearing audit shows that Congress was hardening the digital-public-money boundary, but the June 12 exchange did not establish illegal CBDC issuance or a §11(h) forfeiture case: no current Fed policymaker admitted ongoing work, the enacted §16A prohibition postdated the hearing, the House text predated it, and the public post-order projects reviewed do not establish creation of a U.S. central-bank liability. The September 20 payment-system mandate map shows that fostering safety and efficiency is a major Federal Reserve function but not a unitary third mandate parallel to Federal Reserve Act §2A. Congress supplied tool-specific powers that the Board, twelve Reserve Banks, private operators and co-regulators exercise through operation, access, intraday liquidity, rules, supervision, oversight and coordination; safety, efficiency and accessibility must therefore be tested at each lifecycle layer. The September 16 interest-rate constitution adds the missing rate-setting process: the FOMC target, administered tools, transaction benchmarks, auctions, traded yields and customer contracts are separate objects, and private, tax, insider, public-credit and credit-union below-market lanes have different authority and consequence. Its overnight-money companion follows the actual reserve-account, Fedwire, unsecured-credit, rollover and ON RRP mechanics beneath the policy rate and distinguishes Federal Reserve balances from commercial nostro/vostro claims. The September 18 DTCC whole-site refresh supports a regulated modular-conversion reading: DTCC is rebuilding legal submission, machine-readable reporting, operating hours, fund distribution, asset records and settlement interfaces while preserving incumbent membership, account, risk, correction and finality controls. AnnounceDirect, CMU CSV conversion, Fund/SERV tokenization access, 24x5 clearing, DTC tokenization and settlement modernization remain distinct objects with distinct proof gates rather than one common launch. The September 25 Treasury Market Conference replay adds the missing operating-design layer: possible TGA-repo deployment links public cash to reserve volatility and dealer liquidity; multi-CCP competition remains constrained by netting and liquidity location; and stablecoin effects depend on source funds, redemption velocity, digital/traditional rail synchronization and the cash-finality route. The replay is a delivered audiovisual record with local-STT discovery custody, not an official transcript, TGA authorization, clearing completion or tokenized-settlement launch.
