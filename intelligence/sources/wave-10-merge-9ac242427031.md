## Controlling answer

The strongest movement of the lost week is not a single policy theme. It is a
set of institutions making their control points more explicit:

- Treasury now checks payment identity/account fields before disbursement;
- bank supervisors reached the holding company, service company and national
  bank through separate orders;
- GAO drew legal lines around when programme termination becomes unlawful
  impoundment;
- the President created a hearing-and-findings process around statutory cause
  for a Federal Reserve governor's removal;
- regulators and health agencies changed which machine-readable records must
  exist and who must attest to them; and
- energy and sanctions bodies named counterparties and assets while leaving
  later transaction and operating clocks open.

That confirms the Library's existing **inch-by-inch institutional refactoring**
thesis. It does not prove a coordinated transfer of the federal state, one
shared data platform or one command chain.

## Ranked substantive changes

### 1. Treasury payment verification — operating plus reported effect

Treasury says its verification process became fully operational September 30
and screened 1.1 billion FY2026 payments totaling about $3.7 trillion. It says
13,500 payments totaling $175 million were returned because they would have
gone to deceased people. This moves the public record from Do Not Pay access
and programme design into government-wide operation and an agency-reported
effect. It still does not supply an independently audited, publicly joined
obligation-to-payment-to-correction spine.

### 2. American Express — coordinated supervision without entity collapse

The Fed reached American Express Company and American Express Travel Related
Services Company through enterprise and OFAC duties; OCC reached American
Express National Bank through bank-level BSA/AML duties and a $350 million
penalty. The orders expose the exact group seam: parent oversight, service-
company sanctions compliance and national-bank customer/transaction controls
are joined but not interchangeable. Ninety-day plans and later quarterly
reports are implementation gates, not completed remediation.

### 3. GAO impoundment trilogy — same doctrine, different outcomes

GAO found unlawful impoundments at EPA and AHRQ, but no FY2025 CCAMPIS
violation at Education. The decisions make the operative difference factual:
which funds were available, what the agency did, whether a special message was
sent, and whether obligations occurred. A budget proposal or programme dislike
does not itself create a violation; actual withholding can. GAO's findings are
not court judgments or restored cash.

### 4. Lisa Cook inquiry — removal gate formalized

The President created a committee, closed hearing, evidence process and written
recommendation route for deciding whether statutory cause exists. The November
5 hearing and possible November 10 post-hearing statement are real clocks. No
finding, removal or vacancy exists at the cutoff.

### 5. Health-price data — final rule, future machine obligations

The interdepartmental Transparency in Coverage rule standardizes files,
location, context, attestation and cadence. It is a genuine final legal change,
but its effective/applicability clocks extend months forward. Its root text
locator and homepage link are especially relevant to the Observatory's own
machine-readable practice without proving that insurers' future data will be
accurate or useful.

### 6. Energy transaction stack — three different money states

- SPR: named authorization for ExxonMobil and BP America, with premium-barrel
  return due in 2027;
- Alaska Railbelt: intent to use up to $150 million of DPA funds in a planned
  $418 million project; and
- Vistra nuclear fleet: conditional commitment up to $4.2 billion, subject to
  conditions before definitive documents or funding.

These objects must not be added as comparable spending. Authorization,
intent, conditional commitment, obligation, draw, construction and delivered
power remain different.

### 7. Foreign-control and trade gates

Treasury announced the first OISP penalty, exposing a clean parent → controlled
foreign fund → Chinese AI company chain. USTR separately invoked the USMCA
Rapid Response Labor Mechanism and suspended liquidation of unliquidated GLM
Components entries. Both are completed enforcement/procedure steps; neither is
a final merits or operating-effect finding.

### 8. Sanctions list and fleet actions

OFAC removed 125 entries and updated 22, then designated more Iran shadow-
fleet nodes. Removal and designation are exact legal list movements. Treasury's
claim that the fleet was effectively neutralized goes beyond what list changes
alone prove about cargo, payment, seizure or operation.
