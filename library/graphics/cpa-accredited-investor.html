Can a CPA license make you an accredited investor?

Not yet. On September 30, 2026 the SEC issued Release 33-11446, a notice and request for comment on a possible designation order under Rule 501(a)(10) of Regulation D: a natural person holding an active U.S. CPA license in good standing would qualify personally as an accredited investor, without the income or net-worth tests. It is not a final order; comments are due 60 days after Federal Register publication, and that date was not yet set when the library last checked (October 5, 2026). What a CPA can do today is give a Rule 506(c) written confirmation that another investor is accredited.

What's still uncertain

  • The Commission has not issued the final orders. At this cutoff, the SEC-issued versions still showed Federal Register date placeholders, so there is not yet a reliable calendar comment deadline to carry.
  • 650,667 is the number of actively licensed CPAs, not the number newly eligible: some already qualify by income, net worth or another credential.
  • Commissioner Peirce asked what happens when a person cannot maintain a credential and later wants to make a follow-on private investment. The notices do not supply a final answer.
  • The notice also preserves eligibility for some CPAs subject to SEC practice bars so long as their licensing status remains active.
  • A CPA uses the license to buy a tokenized private-company investment end to end: The license check, offering, issuance, ownership record, payment, books, report, audit and correction records (no named case yet).
  • The library has not checked the Federal Register since its October 5, 2026 cutoff.
