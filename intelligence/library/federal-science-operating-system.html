How Washington plans to turn science strategy into funded work

Seventeen countries endorsed the White House science-reform blueprint in Kyoto, but implementation still belongs to each national system and no shared institution or fund was created.

What we found

  • The October 4 Kyoto Vision moves the July White House science-reform design from U.S. doctrine to a seventeen-country endorsement covering funding methods, metascience, AI-enabled discovery and technical talent.
  • The declaration tells countries to realize the vision in their national systems. It creates no international organization, common fund, shared compute or laboratory access, implementation deadline, metric or reporting duty.
  • Kyoto does not amend CERN, ITER, Horizon Europe or another incumbent regime. The plausible power shift is functional bypass: national funders could route new grants, compute, facilities and talent programmes around older institutions while leaving their charters intact.
  • The tools that do carry force are separate: NSTM-5 / OMB M-26-16 gives FY2028 budget guidance and 90-day agency implementation-plan instructions, and Executive Order 14363 charters the Genesis Mission and its American Science and Security Platform.
  • The security layer the strategy describes covers research security, screening of people and capital, export and data controls, supply-chain checks and cryptography. The study treats it as strategy until a specific legal or technical tool applies it to a specific object.
  • DOE's finalized Fusion Science and Technology Roadmap (June 9) is the first worked example. The eight Milestone companies are one federal funding group, not eight power plants, and the net-electricity and grid-delivery gates are still unmet.
  • The study finds no federal blockchain, no federal science token, no unrestricted spending by AI agents, no blanket preemption of opposition and no plan to eliminate universities.

Why it matters

The American science redesign has now become an international coalition idea. The next question is whether endorsers build compatible national grant, metascience, AI-access and talent machinery—or leave Kyoto as a one-page signal.

What to watch

  • 2026-10-06 or on publication · The STS Forum closing statement or meeting summary, and any named ministerial signature or country-specific commitment.
  • pending · The technology-specific plans the NSSTS says OSTP and the NSC will issue later.
  • pending · Agency implementation plans under OMB M-26-16 and how they shape the FY2028 budget.
  • 2027 · The fusion framework deadline that traces to NEIMA (2019) as amended by the ADVANCE Act; watch the NRC fusion-machine rulemaking against it.

Research summary

The doctrine-to-operation map for an AI-native scientific-industrial economy now includes an international endorsement layer. On October 4, the United States and sixteen other countries endorsed the Kyoto Vision's portfolio of long-duration and rapid grants, prizes, new research institutions, government metascience capability, Super Intelligence for closed-loop discovery, access to data/compute/facilities and technical-talent pathways. This confirms international uptake of the July U.S. design doctrine, but the one-page declaration sends implementation back into national systems and creates no common institution, fund, programme, access right, deadline or reporting mechanism. The August NSSTS security membrane and the fusion implementation gates remain separate U.S. layers.
