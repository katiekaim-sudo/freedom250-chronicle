Stablecoin Laws Compared: Who May Issue, What Backs the Coin, How It Settles

Cards comparing the new stablecoin laws that sit behind familiar ways to pay

Store payment terminals and stablecoin laws are changing in the same way. Both separate what the customer pays with from who owes the money, what backs it, who routes it, and how it finally settles. The laws let more companies connect stablecoins to existing payment systems. They do not make every contactless (NFC) terminal accept crypto directly.

The common adapter stack

Each law assigns a different constitution to the same six jobs. The terminal touches only one edge.

30 Jun 2024
Law in force European Union EUR / referenced fiat in force

European Union: stablecoins need a license as e-money or asset-backed tokens

Europe regulates by legal type of token. It is the clearest category-first model.

IssuerOnly a credit institution or an electronic-money institution may issue an e-money token. Issuers of asset-referenced tokens need a separate authorization.
ReserveDuties on reserves, liquidity, keeping assets separate, custody and redemption at any time differ by token type. E-money token holders can redeem at face value. Paying interest on them is prohibited.
Terminal joinA strong legal base for connecting services. We do not claim contactless card software (EMV) reads MiCA tokens directly.
open legal object + rail reading
Legal clock

The stablecoin parts of MiCA, the EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation, apply from 30 Jun 2024. The rest of MiCA applies from 30 Dec 2024.

Distribution

Crypto-asset service providers need an EU-wide authorization and follow EU-wide conduct rules.

Settlement boundary

The token may move on a blockchain. Issuer redemption and bank or central-bank money settle separately, each final at its own time.

Currency Watch join

The euro system favors one equal-value euro (monetary singleness) and regulated private claims over an unlimited token market.

Counterweight

MiCA authorization is not a European Central Bank (ECB) guarantee, deposit insurance, or acceptance by every merchant.

31 Aug 2024
Law in force United Arab Emirates AED / registered foreign payment tokens in force

United Arab Emirates: stores may accept only licensed dirham tokens

The UAE shows that legal routing and merchant permission decide stablecoin acceptance, not contactless (NFC) capability.

IssuerA UAE company may be licensed to issue a Dirham Payment Token; a bank issues through a subsidiary or affiliate. Issuing, converting, holding and transferring foreign tokens each need their own permission.
ReserveBy default, reserves are 100% cash in the same currency, held in a separate escrow account at an unrelated UAE bank. A qualifying bank-owned issuer may hold at least 50% cash and the rest in short UAE government or CBUAE instruments, with extra capital and controls.
Terminal joinThis is the clearest comparison for paying in stores. An ordinary UAE merchant may accept only a licensed Dirham Payment Token. Registered foreign tokens may be used only to buy virtual assets.
open legal object + rail reading
Legal clock

The Payment Token Services Regulation of the Central Bank of the UAE (CBUAE) has been in force since 31 Aug 2024.

Distribution

Issuing, holding and transferring, and converting tokens are separate regulated services.

Settlement boundary

The wallet transfer is the customer's part. Reserve cash and redemption are separate parts, at a commercial bank or, when directed, in central-bank custody.

Currency Watch join

The law protects the dirham's role in payments by controlling which legal tokens merchants may accept, not by controlling the contactless hardware.

Counterweight

A terminal that supports contactless payment does not meet the rules on token licensing, conversion, custody, merchants or redemption.

18 Jul 2025
Enacted · not yet in force United States USD enacted · not yet generally in effect

United States: the GENIUS Act sets who may issue and what they must hold

Of these laws, GENIUS most directly links more stablecoins to more government-debt purchases for reserves. It also shows most clearly that stablecoin law is banking law too.

IssuerPermitted issuers are subsidiaries of insured depository institutions, federal qualified issuers, or state qualified issuers, divided by the law's federal–state split and size threshold.
ReserveReserves must match coins one-to-one: cash, central-bank balances, insured deposits, Treasury bills of 93 days or less, qualifying repurchase agreements (repo), government money funds and related liquid government assets. Issuers report monthly. Holders get no interest and no FDIC insurance.
Terminal joinGENIUS supplies the lawful issuer and reserves that a card, wallet or merchant processor (acquirer) can route. It does not update any terminal.
open legal object + rail reading
Legal clock

Public Law 119-27 was signed 18 Jul 2025. Final implementing rules were due 18 Jul 2026. We found none on official final-rule pages through the 28 Jul cutoff. The latest general effective date is still 18 Jan 2027. It moves earlier only if final rules start the 120-day countdown.

Distribution

Wallets, card networks, processors and distributors can be separate companies from the legal issuer. A restriction on service providers starts in 2028, setting a later deadline for market access.

Settlement boundary

Blockchain transfer, card-network obligation, minting and redemption, bank-deposit movement and central-bank final settlement stay separate.

Currency Watch join

The reserve rules directly create demand for short-term Treasury bills. The dollar stays the unit, and private companies still distribute the coins.

Counterweight

Enacted does not mean in effect. A proposal, a charter application or a conditional approval from the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) is not a live GENIUS issuer authorization.

1 Aug 2025
Law in force Hong Kong HKD / referenced fiat in force

Hong Kong: issuing a stablecoin requires a license from the Hong Kong Monetary Authority

Hong Kong uses licensing by the regulator: a strict issuer license in an international financial center.

IssuerIssuing an in-scope stablecoin tied to a government currency (fiat-referenced) in Hong Kong requires a license. So do specified issuing or offering activities linked to Hong Kong.
ReserveHKMA supervision focuses on reserve management, redemption, risk management, governance, anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing (AML/CFT) controls, and disclosure.
Terminal joinA licensed stablecoin can fund or settle payments behind an existing wallet or card. The legal link still needs an authorized distributor and processor.
open legal object + rail reading
Legal clock

The Stablecoins Ordinance and the supervisory framework of the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) took effect 1 Aug 2025.

Distribution

Only specified regulated institutions may offer in-scope stablecoins to retail investors. The issuer's license and the permission to distribute are separate.

Settlement boundary

The Ordinance covers the issuer and the token's promise, not the merchant's card processor (acquirer) or the final bank-money payment.

Currency Watch join

Hong Kong protects its monetary system by licensing issuers and supervising them locally. It does not treat every token as bank money.

Counterweight

A sandbox place, a license application or a public announcement is not a license.

Feb 2026
Enacted · not yet in force United Kingdom GBP / qualifying referenced fiat law made · regime scheduled

United Kingdom: issuing stablecoins becomes a licensed activity; the full regime is expected to start in late 2027

Britain is the best example of a license requirement already in law whose operating rules are still being written.

IssuerIssuing a UK qualifying stablecoin becomes a regulated activity. For systemic payment stablecoins, the Bank of England adds oversight to FCA supervision.
ReserveQualifying stablecoins are tied to a government currency and hold backing assets. Detailed FCA rules are expected to provide full backing and redemption rights.
Terminal joinPromising law for connecting services, but the payment-services rules are not finished.
open legal object + rail reading
Legal clock

The 2026 cryptoasset regulations make issuing and safeguarding regulated activities. The wider regime is expected to begin in late 2027. The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) still has to make rules and set application steps.

Distribution

Safeguarding and other crypto activities need separate permissions. The government is still settling what the dealing and payment-services rules cover.

Settlement boundary

The UK is explicitly designing stablecoins as both cryptoassets and possible payment instruments, with separate oversight of systemic ones.

Currency Watch join

The UK goes in order: what the law covers first, payments integration second, tax treatment third.

Counterweight

Do not call the late-2027 regime live. Current policy notes explicitly describe more rules and payments reform still to come.

Under discussion · not law South Korea KRW under policy discussion · not enacted

South Korea: a bank-led won stablecoin is under discussion, not law

Korea is the comparison case: strong technical readiness does not mean settled law.

IssuerA bank-centered model, including a debated consortium with banks holding 50%+1, was discussed, not adopted.
ReserveNot final.
Terminal joinKorea runs advanced payment and token pilots, but they have not led to an enacted law for paying merchants directly in stablecoins.
open legal object + rail reading
Legal clock

The Digital Asset Committee of Korea's Financial Services Commission (FSC) discussed the government review draft. Legislation and the final design for issuers are not settled.

Distribution

Not final. Existing bank-token and wholesale-settlement pilots are separate from any won-stablecoin law.

Settlement boundary

The Bank of Korea's work puts central-bank and commercial-bank money first, with stablecoins as a supplementary option.

Currency Watch join

What institutions are actually working on is tokenizing bank deposits and testing wholesale settlement. The rules for stablecoin issuers remain open.

Counterweight

A committee discussion or a bank proof of concept is not a won-stablecoin law.

1 Jun 2026
Law in force Japan JPY / qualifying referenced fiat in force

Japan: trust-issued stablecoins may hold up to half their reserves in short government bonds

Like GENIUS, Japan lets reserves fund government debt, but more narrowly: only trust stablecoins may hold limited short government bonds.

IssuerBanks, registered funds-transfer providers, and trust companies or trust banks issue different legal claims. A lighter intermediary that holds no customer assets may broker for a full provider.
ReserveCovered trust stablecoins may hold up to 50% in qualifying government bonds of three months or less, or in term deposits that can be withdrawn early without losing principal. At least 50% stays in demand deposits. Rules on daily fair-value measurement and fixing shortfalls apply.
Terminal joinJapan is setting the legal roles for issuers, intermediaries and redemption. A payment service provider could keep the tap while changing funding or payout behind it.
open legal object + rail reading
Legal clock

Japan's 2022 rules for electronic payment instruments (EPIs) have applied since 1 Jun 2023. The main parts of Act No. 66 of 2025 and its final implementing rules took effect 1 Jun 2026.

Distribution

Full EPI providers handle sale, exchange, brokering or custody. The lighter intermediary cannot hold customer assets.

Settlement boundary

Trust redemption, deposit-token settlement, bank reserves and a public-blockchain transfer are different things. Each settles on its own timeline.

Currency Watch join

Yen Currency Watch grades for stability and payment use: underlying order B; exchange order B; current-payment usability A; systemic service A. The yen is absorbing the change in an orderly way, not showing a broken payment system.

Counterweight

JPYSC running on controlled venues is not open-wallet circulation at merchants. Tokenized bank deposits are not trust stablecoins.

Related payment and market law Japan Crypto market perimeter enacted · not yet in effect

Japan: crypto trading moves under securities law; stablecoins stay under payments law

The split is the key point. Japan keeps ‘crypto,’ ‘stablecoin’ and ‘bank deposit’ as separate legal things.

IssuerThis is not a new stablecoin issuer category. Stablecoins, as electronic payment instruments, stay under the PSA.
ReserveThis card covers no change to stablecoin reserves.
Terminal joinThe law helps institutions classify crypto investment activity. It does not make crypto a means of contactless payment.
open legal object + rail reading
Legal clock

The Diet (Japan's parliament) passed the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act (FIEA) and Payment Services Act (PSA) reform 15 Jul 2026. Its main parts start on a Cabinet-set date within the legal window.

Distribution

Rules on trading, offerings, lending, management, advice, disclosure, intermediary duties and unfair trading move under the FIEA.

Settlement boundary

The law deliberately keeps market regulation separate from the settlement of payment money.

Currency Watch join

Japan is building two legal paths: payments law for money claims that can be redeemed, and market law for cryptoassets held as investments.

Counterweight

Do not say Japan made every cryptoasset a security, legal tender or a bank deposit.

Japan: paper checks and bills to stop clearing from April 2027 (a plan, not a stablecoin law)

Japan is changing both ends at once. It is retiring paper claims while making new tokenized claims legal.

IssuerBanks still owe the payment; only the form of the payment instruction changes.
ReserveNo stablecoin reserve rule applies.
Terminal joinIt is the closest historical parallel in this set: the paper instrument disappears while the bank relationship stays.
open legal object + rail reading
Legal clock

The plan aims for zero paper bills and checks through the electronic clearinghouse by the end of FY2026, and an end to paper exchange from the start of FY2027.

Distribution

Electronically recorded monetary claims and internet transfers replace presenting paper.

Settlement boundary

This is a direct move from cash and checks to electronic payment systems, separate from stablecoin law.

Currency Watch join

A mature currency system can retire an old payment instrument without changing the unit of account.

Counterweight

This is an official transition plan. It is not a stablecoin law, and it does not prove every check disappears on one day.
