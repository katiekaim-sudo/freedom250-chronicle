Federal Reserve System entity, transaction and software map

Updated: 2026-07-25
Status: pending workbench research; not yet promoted to the vault
Evidence cutoff: official records available through 2026-07-25
Scope: legal entities, control relationships, interdistrict accounting, System-wide allocations, shared services, software, technology, research, external reimbursements and international counterparties. This is an accounting/control map, not a finding of waste, unlawful coordination or beneficial ownership.

Every monetary amount is written in full nominal dollars with commas and no letter-suffix scaling. When a source uses a $1,000,000 or $1,000 reporting unit, the displayed source amount is expanded to full dollars. Added zeros do not create precision beyond the source.

Short answer

The Federal Reserve does much more than set an interest-rate target and order paper currency:

  • it operates the reserve-account layer for depository institutions;
  • it settles high-value, automated-clearing-house, check, securities and instant payments;
  • it lends to depository institutions and operates emergency facilities when authorized;
  • it acts as Treasury's bank and fiscal agent;
  • it issues, distributes, authenticates and destroys currency;
  • it supervises bank holding companies, state member banks, savings-and-loan holding companies, certain foreign-bank offices and designated financial market utilities;
  • it conducts monetary-policy, financial-stability, supervision, payments, consumer and regional-economic research;
  • it maintains foreign official accounts, foreign-currency investments, central-bank swap lines and foreign-official repo services; and
  • it runs a large national software, data, cyber, payments and accounting infrastructure.

But the user's accounting instinct is still right:

A full map must ask which legal entity books each cost, which entity directs the work, which entity receives the service, who reimburses it, which allocations are eliminated in the combined statements, and which outside party ultimately bears or benefits from the economics.

The most important correction is:

Gross interest income is not the Fed's operating-funding requirement.

The portfolio exists to implement monetary policy. Its interest income and the interest paid on reserve and repo liabilities are balance-sheet consequences of that policy. Operating costs are much smaller and are funded inside the same earnings mechanism, but they are not the reason the securities portfolio produces its gross income.

In 2025, the combined Reserve Banks recorded:

So the Banks did not produce excess 2025 interest income because they "needed" it for software, buildings or research. They actually paid more interest on monetary liabilities than they earned on monetary assets.

This is not a parent-subsidiary chart:

  • the Board is not consolidated with the Reserve Banks;
  • the FOMC is a statutory committee, not a holding company;
  • member banks do not hold ordinary residual equity;
  • the New York Fed executes SOMA transactions as agent, but the economic participation is allocated across all 12 Banks;
  • shared organizations such as Federal Reserve Financial Services and Federal Reserve Information Technology are operational structures hosted within the Reserve Bank network, not necessarily separately incorporated companies; and
  • Treasury is outside the Reserve Bank combined reporting entity even though it is customer, counterparty, recipient of remittances and ultimate public residual claimant.

IV. Income is generated by the monetary balance sheet, not sized to expenses

Keep the two funding engines separate

The Reserve Banks' monetary-portfolio earnings and the Board's assessments are different accounting systems:

This table explains an accounting point that can otherwise look like surplus income. The Board assessment explicitly funds both current operations and capital expenditures. Cash spent on a building or capitalized software is not fully charged to operating expense immediately; it first becomes an asset and then reaches expense through depreciation or amortization. The Board therefore reported $454,511,223 of net income while also spending $527,426,113 of cash on capital expenditures.

Those are the Board's separate public-agency books, funded by mandatory assessments rather than ordinary congressional appropriations. They are not private shareholder funds and should not be mixed with the 12 Reserve Banks' SOMA interest-income and Treasury-remittance waterfall.

This bridge answers “what do they need the income for?”:

  1. The interest-income line is generated principally by securities acquired to implement monetary policy.
  2. The interest-expense line is generated principally by reserve balances and repo liabilities used to implement policy and provide monetary control.
  3. Operating expenses sit below that spread.
  4. Service fees and Treasury reimbursements offset defined operating functions.
  5. Excess earnings, when present, go to Treasury after expenses, dividends and limited surplus.
  6. When costs and interest exceed earnings, remittances stop and a deferred asset records the earnings hurdle that must be recovered before remittances resume.

The portfolio is therefore not economically comparable to an endowment sized to cover a nonprofit's annual budget. Its scale reflects the amount of central bank assets and liabilities used to operate monetary policy.

The deferred asset is not cash, a loan to the System or an enforceable receivable from Treasury. It is the cumulative amount of future net earnings the Banks must retain before ordinary Treasury remittances resume.

V. Interdistrict and related-party transaction lanes

1. Interdistrict Settlement Account

Each Reserve Bank carries an Interdistrict Settlement Account representing the cumulative amount due to or from other Reserve Banks. The centralized accounting system posts the settlement entries.

The account captures:

  • cross-District payment activity;
  • automated-clearing-house, funds and securities transfers;
  • monthly Federal Reserve note clearings;
  • priced-service reimbursements between principal and processing Banks;
  • shared-service transfers; and
  • the annual settlement through gold-certificate accounts.

The 12 individual Interdistrict Settlement Account balances net across the System. The combined statement therefore removes the Bank-to-Bank geography that is visible in the individual statements.

Settlement can also be effected through changes in each Bank's SOMA participation. An ISA movement can therefore change an individual Bank's allocated securities position without that District having made a cash purchase or sale. ISA is a clearing/allocation mechanism, not an inter-Bank loan.

2. SOMA domestic participation

The New York Fed executes and holds domestic SOMA transactions, but each Reserve Bank books an undivided participation:

  • domestic securities, repo, reverse repo and related income, premium, discount, gain and loss activity are allocated using percentages derived from the annual Interdistrict Settlement Account settlement;
  • specific securities are not assigned security-by-security to individual Banks; and
  • the allocation is recalculated annually.

This is why an individual Bank can report a large securities-income or interest-expense result even though the New York Desk executed the trades.

3. Foreign portfolios and central-bank swaps

Foreign-currency investment and central-bank-swap activity is allocated by each Reserve Bank's share of aggregate capital and surplus at the preceding year-end, rather than the domestic Interdistrict Settlement Account formula.

This is a different allocation key and must remain a different accounting lane.

4. Priced payment services

Federal Reserve Financial Services manages Check, FedACH, Fedwire Funds, Fedwire Securities, National Settlement, FedNow and electronic access across the Banks.

The FAM says:

  • principal/host Banks reimburse processing/agent Banks;
  • estimated interbank transfers are recorded monthly through the Interdistrict Settlement Account and trued to actual cost the following month;
  • income from customers is recorded gross, not netted against expense or payments to other Banks; and
  • the uniform cost-accounting system allocates direct, internal-support, nationally provided support and corporate-overhead costs to benefiting activities.

The reciprocal Bank-to-Bank receivables, payables and service-cost reallocations disappear or net in the combined Reserve Bank presentation. The underlying external payroll, vendor and operating cost remains. These flows should therefore be called cost allocations or reimbursements—not loosely called intercompany income.

6. Board, currency and CFPB assessments

The Board assesses the Reserve Banks for:

  • Board operations and capital;
  • currency costs, including Bureau of Engraving and Printing costs; and
  • CFPB funding.

These payments are related-System transactions but are not eliminated in the Reserve Bank combined statements because the Board and CFPB are outside the combined Reserve Bank reporting entity.

Other external flows that survive the 12-Bank combination include Treasury fiscal-agent reimbursements, depository-institution service revenue, member bank dividends, Treasury remittances, vendor and payroll spending, external benefit obligations, and transactions with foreign official and market counterparties.

Richmond is an especially important host entity. Its 2025 audited statement says:

  • computing and System-support costs were redistributed to the other Reserve Banks;
  • Richmond received $1,135,000,000 of reimbursement for those costs in 2025, compared with $1,052,000,000 in 2024; and
  • costs for the Currency Technology Office and System Financial Planning & Management were major System services for which Richmond was not reimbursed by the other Banks.

The $1,135,000,000 is an inter-Bank cost redistribution, not a donation, loan or additional external System expenditure. It is also not a complete measure of National IT's external vendor spend. It is evidence that Richmond's own expense presentation contains a large national shared-service layer.

The FAM adds a procedural detail: Richmond processes each Reserve Bank's Board assessment through an inter-Fed accounting entry, and the Board maintains its operating deposit accounts at Richmond.

7. Employee-benefit and interagency allocations

Atlanta recognizes the System retirement-plan net asset/liability on behalf of participants. The service cost is allocated among the Reserve Banks, Board and other participating entities. The Office of Employee Benefits also initiates inter-Bank transfers for health-plan premiums.

This is a classic host-entity issue: the legal book holder is not the sole economic beneficiary.

The FFIEC is a separate interagency example. Its five participating agencies include the Board and CFPB, but its financial data is not consolidated into the Board. The Board can both be assessed by the council and earn reimbursement for services it performs for the council.

8. Emergency-facility VIEs

Main Street is managed through Boston with Treasury preferred equity. The Reserve Bank loan to the VIE and other qualifying intercompany amounts are eliminated when the VIE is consolidated.

That is a true accounting consolidation lane, unlike the Board-Reserve Bank or Treasury-Reserve Bank relationships.

Where the software asset sits

The FAM's software-control rule is unusually important:

A software project may be developed by one Reserve Bank, installed on hardware at another Bank or an outside vendor, and controlled by a product or function office hosted at a third Bank.

The controlling or hosting Bank generally records the whole software asset, including amortization and disposal, even when all 12 Banks use it.

Control indicators include:

  • deciding requirements;
  • setting implementation and retirement dates;
  • operating and supporting the software; and
  • granting rights to use it.

Capitalizable costs paid to another Reserve Bank for software development can be included in the owner's asset. Shared-project costs are transferred to the books of the Bank that owns or controls the software.

This means a bank-by-bank software balance is not a clean measure of that Bank's local technology consumption. It may identify where the System has placed accounting ownership.

For every material application, the audit therefore needs four separate fields:

  1. contracting or invoice-paying Bank;
  2. controlling product/function office and its host Bank;
  3. Bank or vendor that developed and physically hosts the application; and
  4. Banks, agencies or business lines to which the cost is ultimately allocated.

Under the current FAM, internally developed software is ordinarily capitalized when the total project cost is $100,000 or more. Externally purchased software is ordinarily capitalized at $25,000 or more. Meeting a threshold does not override the underlying asset-recognition and project-stage rules.

The available budget says 2025 Reserve Bank growth included staffing for regional economic research. It does not establish that most of this money is passed through to outside nonprofit research organizations.

The public financial statements also do not provide:

  • a recipient-level research-grant ledger;
  • outside university or nonprofit contract totals;
  • sponsor concentration by research center;
  • beneficial-interest or board-overlap schedules for funded organizations; or
  • a clean split between employee research, purchased data, outside consulting, conferences and grants.

Therefore:

Internal research spending is verified. A broad claim that the Fed mainly funds outside research arms is not yet supported.

That hypothesis should be tested through contracts, grants, conference sponsorships, visiting-scholar programs, data licenses and personnel overlap.

VIII. International layer

The New York Fed is the main operating node:

  • it provides accounts and services to foreign official and international account holders;
  • it holds foreign-currency deposits with foreign central banks and the Bank for International Settlements;
  • it invests in selected foreign government obligations;
  • it operates the Foreign and International Monetary Authorities repo facility;
  • it executes foreign-exchange operations; and
  • it maintains central-bank liquidity swaps as authorized by the FOMC.

IX. Relationship-closeness test

The nonprofit/high-donor analogy is useful if it is treated as an influence test rather than an ownership conclusion.

For every organization touching the Fed, score these separately:

Lens Question
Legal control Can one party appoint, remove, approve, direct or veto?
Funding dependence What share of the recipient's budget comes from the relationship? Is payment mandatory, contractual or discretionary?
Operational dependence Does one party supply the other's staff, platform, premises, accounting or critical service?
Data dependence Does one party control essential data, models, credentials or access?
Personnel overlap Are there shared officers, directors, fellows, secondees or revolving-door patterns?
Risk absorption Does one party guarantee, capitalize, indemnify or absorb losses for the other?
Residual economics Who receives surplus, bears deficits or captures asset value on wind-down?
Exclusivity and duration Is the relationship recurring, concentrated and difficult to replace?
Direction of work Does the funder set the questions, deliverables, methods, publication rights or implementation path?
Accounting treatment Is it consolidated, eliminated, grossed up, reimbursed, allocated or left off the public entity map?
