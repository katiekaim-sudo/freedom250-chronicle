Crypto Infrastructure Hub — 2026-09-01

Purpose

This package owns the governed source registry and generated projection for the Observatory's first-class Crypto Hub reference view. Tokenization remains a major use case, but the governed scope is broader: who steers, builds, operates, regulates, connects and settles each crypto infrastructure stack?

The hub's central finding is that the relationship web is dense while actual corporate control is sparse. Ownership, minority investment, board influence, registration, commercial partnership, operating structure, policy advocacy, technical integration, asset flow, historical origin and proposed-rule overlap are therefore separate edge classes. Proposal overlap marks where a current capability touches the SEC's proposed architecture without turning that overlap into approval or a final-rule compliance finding. Ripple, XRPL, RLUSD and XRP remain separate objects; Securitize's affiliates remain separate regulated legal entities; and Wormhole remains an interoperability layer rather than an issuer, transfer agent, custodian or title authority.

For the FCA's bounded 2026 UK market description, use the UK cryptoasset actor-and-flow baseline. It maps the FCA's role taxonomy, consumer access and custody co-location, settlement clocks, cross-border structure and October 2027 regime without creating new Hub entity relationships or treating brand-level survey responses as legal-entity market shares.

The registry currently contains 280 nodes, 338 typed relationships, 318 source objects, 20 governed ecosystem profiles, three governed process maps, sixteen governed asset traces with process-role references, eight analytical lenses and eight operational clocks. The profiles cover Ripple/XRPL, Securitize, Ethereum, Hedera, Stellar, Canton, Solana, Avalanche, Chainlink, Bitcoin, DTC/DTCC tokenization, cash legs and tokenized bank money, Circle/USDC/CPN/Arc/Tazapay, Coinbase/Base/Tokenize, Algorand, XDC, IOTA/TWIN and bounded Quant orchestration, with Wormhole now visible as its own interoperability stack. Each follows a common walkthrough: steering, operation, execution, native asset, external asset authority, access, technical finality and legal settlement or remedy.

The schema-v4 process layer supplies the shared evidence for Process, Trace and Compare: for each lifecycle stage, which exact ecosystem controls, operates, integrates or depends on the relevant rail? The three maps are Tokenization (14 stages), Stablecoins (13), and FX & cross-border (12), backed by the deeper claim, authority, admission, funding, instruction, execution, clearing, technical-state, authoritative-record, cash, finality, posting, servicing, correction, default, backstop and proof families. Every visible role references existing governed nodes, relationships and primary sources. Operating clock, scale evidence and stress evidence remain separate dimensions.

Ethereum appears in all three process maps, but its role is not the same in each. It operates live execution and consensus for tokenized assets and native USDC state and transport. In the FX map it appears only as a transport layer: the registry does not assign Ethereum the currency quote, conversion, funding, clearing, payout, issuer, reserve or redemption functions.

Ripple's tokenization view now makes three different collateral ideas visible without treating them as one capability. Fubon Bank's 2023 e-HKD pilot is a historical external dependency in Underlying & collateral: the tokens represented bank charges over property on a private ledger built with XRPL technology, not title deeds or assets controlled by Ripple on the public XRPL. Licuido remains admission, issuance and market-access buildout because its collateral-mobility announcement does not prove custody or control of the underlying asset. Evernorth and Pathfinder are separately identified XRP treasury and custody actors, not tokenized-asset controllers; their pending combination, restricted pre-close XRP and proposed XLS-66 participation do not fill a tokenization process cell.

Hedera, Solana, Avalanche, Stellar, Algorand and XDC now receive the same native-USDC treatment in the Stablecoins map: each operates token-state execution and network transport, while Circle and its named counterparties remain external dependencies for issuer authority, reserves, liability records, direct redemption and remedy. Hedera also appears in FX only for one completed Aberdeen-Lloyds-Archax pilot in which tokenized fund units and gilts moved as collateral. Canton separately records one completed Treasury-for-USDCx cash-leg event while leaving USDCx issuer and redemption terms unresolved. Neither single-event receipt establishes a production corridor or general service.

Circle's September 4 definitive agreement to acquire Tazapay is mapped as pending control, not current ownership. The live operating relationship came first: Tazapay was already a CPN Beneficiary Financial Institution converting received stablecoin into local fiat and paying through local bank rails. The map now separates Circle Internet Group, the actual purchaser Taurus Acquisition Inc., CTS-operated CPN, Tazapay Pte. Ltd., Tazapay Canada Corp.'s exclusive stablecoin on/off-ramp role and Tazapay's local payout stack. If the transaction closes, Circle would internalize a regulated last-mile adapter; it would not collapse the exact licences, bank books, local payment systems, USDC issuer or beneficiary-finality obligations into one entity. The $400 million all-stock base price is adjustable and the expected 2027 close still requires MAS approval and other conditions. Circle's reported $25 billion annualized volume, 60-plus partners, 100-plus payout markets and roughly 60% stablecoin-including volume are aggregate company claims as of July 31, not audited USDC-only or corridor-level measurements.

The SEC proposal-overlap view makes the proposed accountability sequence inspectable from issuer and recordkeeping transfer agent through the authoritative master securityholder file, linked systems, wallet records, governance, reporting, corrections and downstream services. The network view separates protocol governance, operators, execution layers, native-token economics and named product connections rather than collapsing a company, network and token into one object.

Question-led Hub home and Transaction X-ray — September 15

The generated Hub now opens on a question-led home instead of treating ten different instruments as a flat room list. The existing rooms are grouped by the job they perform:

  • Follow something moving: Walk, Transaction maps, Steps & rights and Debits & credits.
  • See the whole system: Current stack map, Crypto map and Whole hub.
  • Prove authority or control: Permissions & players (the former Evidence label) and Legal process.
  • Cross-cutting lens: Liquidity & collateral.

Share, Dollar and Fund are now persistent route contexts across the compatible rooms. Their existing route and step IDs already aligned; the interface carries the selection rather than creating a new transaction model.

Transaction X-ray is the joined transaction instrument in this wave. At each existing route step it joins five independently owned layers: action and movement; right and authoritative record; books and cash; authority and permission; and correction, failure and remedy. The optional crypto comparison uses the existing Steps & rights classification and books overlay. X-ray is a presentation join, not a transaction receipt, new source owner or claim that technical movement, legal title, accounting entry, cash discharge and remedy share one clock.

Tracked Systems Field Guide — September 15

The generated Hub now includes a plain-language Field Guide for all 20 tracked technology ecosystems. It is a reading layer over the same governed registry and sources, not a second crypto research store. Each profile answers:

  • what the object actually is;
  • why it matters to this investigation;
  • how it works in a short sequence;
  • what it controls and does not control;
  • where it attaches to banks, markets, records and cash;
  • what makes it different, with a required boundary on that distinction;
  • its observed state, next evidence to watch and a Do not confuse list.

Differentiators are labeled meaningful, contextual or not established. Those labels describe the strength of the architectural or operating distinction in the saved evidence; they are not rankings, investment views or proof of adoption, legal authority, production scale or token value capture. The entrance preserves three especially important separations: Ripple company / XRPL / XRP / RLUSD; Securitize's regulated operating entities versus Ethereum execution; and Solana network versus SOL native asset.

The stable Observatory route is obs-ch. The Workbench registry remains the editable evidence owner; the app view is a generated projection and must not be edited independently.

The Hub opens in Process, with the selected lifecycle's numbered stages in registry order. The Follow the process strip shows sourced-role counts and evidence gaps; click a stage or use Exact stage to inspect that step across systems. All steps restores the full lifecycle. Its arrows show analytical sequence, not a verified transaction route or movement of money.

Process, Trace and Compare use the same stage order, role records, research cards and flow renderer:

  • Process aligns all mapped ecosystem rows for the selected process.
  • Compare shows only the systems checked in Systems to compare. A shared object does not automatically add other ecosystems. Select the process and compare the same exact step, or retain all steps for a whole-process view.
  • Trace shows only the curated coverage records referenced by the selected asset or claim path. It follows process stages rather than structural lanes. Where a trace spans several processes, the Process selector offers its mapped choices. The sidebar retains the walkthrough, mapping limits and documented relationships; a trace is not proof of one continuous live transaction.

Follow a technology or object narrows these three modes to records that explicitly reference that object. It does not assign every ecosystem function to its coin, software or network. The selected process and exact stage persist between compatible views; explicit comparison choices are retained. An object filter resets to all objects when the new scope has no role for it. Rows retain No mapped role or Hidden by filters cells so an evidence gap can be distinguished from a filtered result.

Explore and Full map remain structural relationship views. Explore opens one ecosystem's organizations, authorities, operators, products, networks and market rails; Full map shows the whole relationship field. These columns classify objects and do not represent transaction order. Compare this system opens explicit system comparison; Compare this step on an object's researched role opens its exact process stage. Process cards can return to the relevant ecosystem structure without creating a second version of the evidence.

Accounting remains a separate grouped lens over those same process records, with the illustrative journal walkthrough. Its seven broad questions are not interchangeable with exact lifecycle steps. Use See process role to open the matching process and stage for precise comparison.

The structural view menu isolates the SEC proposal overlap, legal control, products and flows, cash-leg comparison, network architecture, capital and influence, or all relationships. Operational clock distinguishes proposed, announced, planned, pilot/test, limited production, live production, current operating and historical records. Find and the clock filter apply to the visible mode. Refine & key explains structural lanes, relationship lines, process roles and clock dots. Select a structural relationship to inspect its claim, primary sources and overclaim boundary; open a process card's Research for its finding, evidence limits and sources.

Boundaries

  • Evidence cutoff: 2026-09-09 for SWIFT; other evidence retains its existing dates.
  • Circle and Taurus do not control Tazapay before closing. The signed agreement remains subject to MAS and other approvals and closing conditions; it does not transfer Tazapay licences, bank relationships, customer contracts or local payment-system control. Tazapay's existing CPN role remains a separately live partnership.
  • Tazapay Pte. Ltd.'s Singapore MPI licence does not include Digital Payment Token service. Tazapay identifies Tazapay Canada Corp. as the exclusive group entity providing stablecoin payment and conversion services. Neither entity issues USDC or controls its reserves or redemption terms.
  • SEC Release 34-106246 / S7-2026-30 is a proposal, not a final rule.
  • A registration describes the named legal entity and permitted role; it is not approval of every affiliate, product, chain or transaction.
  • A minority investment or board seat is not control unless a source proves control.
  • A technical bridge moves messages or supported representations; it does not itself determine beneficial ownership, legal title, custody or finality.
  • A chain's native fee, burn, staking or governance economics describe network utility; they do not determine the authoritative securityholder record or establish value capture from a tokenized product.
  • Technical finality, the authoritative legal record and the cash or redemption remedy are separate endpoints. A DTC token, Base wallet, trade credential or bank-money representation does not inherit legal title merely by settling on a chain.
  • DTC limited production in July 2026 does not backdate the expected October general-service launch; Arc private mainnet does not make the planned September 16 public mainnet or its announced founding-validator cohort live; Circle National Trust's effective charter does not prove reserve-management activation or operating volume; Ripple and Coinbase preliminary charter approvals do not make those proposed trust banks open.
  • Coinbase's minority Circle stake and USDC distribution/reserve-income economics are material company relationships, but they do not make Coinbase the USDC issuer, reserve obligor or sole stablecoin-network governor.
  • Fubon Bank's 2023 e-HKD pilot proves one historical home-equity-lending test in which tokens represented property liens or bank charges on a private ledger built with XRPL technology. It did not tokenize title deeds, use the public XRPL mainnet, establish a current production service or transfer the authoritative property record to Ripple or XRPL.
  • Licuido's announced collateral-market mobility supports Ripple's admission, issuance and market-access buildout, but it does not prove custody, immobilization or duplicate-issuance control for an underlying asset.
  • Evernorth and Pathfinder remain legal entities separate from Ripple. Their reported pre-close XRP holdings and BitGo custody arrangements describe a corporate treasury path; the pending combination and proposed Single Asset Vault/XLS-66 participation do not prove a completed listing, operating lending volume, collateral control or a tokenization or FX route. XLS-66's documented protocol design is uncollateralized lending, not an underlying- asset collateral system.
  • Company announcements support the stated relationship and status only; they do not prove scale, universal production use or token value capture.
  • A process cell is not a yes/no capability score. It records one exact stage-specific role and its evidence ceiling. An operator or integrator does not inherit the controller's authority, the cash system's finality or a public institution's backstop.
  • Ethereum confirmation can establish USDC token state and transport without establishing Circle's liability record, reserve position, direct redemption, an FX conversion, cross-border purpose or recipient bank payout.
  • Native USDC support on Hedera, Solana, Avalanche C-Chain, Stellar, Algorand and XDC likewise proves a supported token-state and transport rail, not issuer authority, use volume, FX conversion, bank payout or stressed-redemption performance.
  • The Aberdeen-Lloyds-Archax event proves one Hedera-based tokenized-collateral pilot for FX trades; the collateral movement is not either currency leg and does not establish an FX quote, conversion, clearing or production corridor.
  • The completed Canton Treasury-for-USDCx event proves one synchronized tokenized-security/tokenized-cash exchange. It does not establish the exact USDCx issuer or redemption chain, recurring volume or bank cash payout.
  • Blank process cells mean that no governed primary-source receipt is mapped; they do not prove that a capability does not exist.
  • The map is Workbench evidence and an Observatory projection, not investment advice, a price forecast or a vault doctrine owner.
