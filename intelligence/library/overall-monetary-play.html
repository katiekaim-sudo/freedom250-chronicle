Not a new currency: a fight over the points where money changes form

As of July 2026, the money system looked less like one coin replacing another and more like a linked, programmable set of national systems. Power sits with whoever controls the points where deposits become collateral, collateral becomes cash, and losses get assigned.

What we found

  • The study traces one chain: funds become a regulated claim, then an eligible asset, then collateral and cash, then a final settled payment, then a cross-border link, then insurance, and last a legal split of losses. No country has finished every link.
  • Banks mostly keep the customer. Korea's deposit-token tests, Japan's trust-money setups, Europe's tokenized deposits and China's revised e-CNY all keep a bank or trust claim on top and central-bank money underneath for final settlement.
  • The real choke point is collateral eligibility: who owns the asset, which record proves title, what discount applies, and whether a central bank will lend against it. Examples: Europe showed one blockchain-native EIB instrument could pass through regulated custody into Bundesbank financing, and South Korea lining up corporate-loan receivables for a separate emergency facility.
  • BRICS gives outside support, not a shared currency. The CRA is a callable but undrawn arrangement, the NDB makes loans that create debt rather than reserve money, and the guarantee mechanism is only proposed.
  • The study expects a crisis, possibly outside the U.S., to show the new structure first. The U.S. would then matter through dollar funding, Treasury collateral, sanctions, reinsurance and its courts.

Why it matters

This is the thesis at the plumbing level: existing central banks, clearinghouses, insurers and courts are writing new rails into their own rulebooks, not being replaced. The study says plainly that this is a reading of separate developments, not proof of one coordinated plan.

What to watch

  • pending · A real emergency draw against newly pre-positioned collateral, such as the Korean facility, or tokenized assets pledged for actual central-bank or repo cash.
  • pending · Whether temporary emergency powers used in a future crisis stay on as permanent infrastructure after it passes.
  • pending · Signs the reading is wrong: pilots that stay isolated demos, and BRICS support tools that stay unused.
