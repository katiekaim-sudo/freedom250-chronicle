Research summary

There is no single U.S.–China relationship. It is an instrument stack and operating system spanning political direction, diplomacy, trade, industrial dependencies, technology permissions, money, Taiwan and military risk, global institutions and people-to-people ties. China’s strongest industrial leverage often sits in concentrated midstream processing, components and production scale, while U.S. and allied leverage is strongest in frontier technology permissions, aerospace systems and market access. Each lane has different actors, legal objects and substitution clocks. The orientation maps those lanes, preserves publisher perspective and routes detailed claims to the closest issue package.
