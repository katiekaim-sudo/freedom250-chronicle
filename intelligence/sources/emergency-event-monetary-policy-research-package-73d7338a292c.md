## The answer this pass produced

Emergency monetary policy is the moment when money's apparent unity breaks apart. Bank deposits, central-bank reserves, CBDCs/tokens, sovereign debt, payment messages, guarantees and insurance claims stop behaving as if they were interchangeable.

The real activation chain is:

`operational continuity -> settlement liquidity -> collateral conversion -> market/FX stabilization -> guarantee or insurance -> resolution and loss allocation -> external support -> exit and deferred-loss recognition`

The central bank controls only part of that chain. It can create its own settlement liability and lend against acceptable collateral. It cannot by that act reconstruct a corrupted ledger, make an insolvent institution solvent, pay an uninsured claim, restore inaccessible foreign reserves, authorize a sanctioned payment, or replace destroyed physical assets.

## Strongest factual findings

### 1. Emergency policy often begins before the interest-rate decision

The first binding act can be a system isolation, cut-off extension, alternate-site activation, collateral revaluation, capital-transfer restriction, moratorium or resolution order. If settlement data or legal finality is uncertain, adding reserves cannot safely solve the event.

### 2. Japan has three distinct central-bank emergency routes

- **BOJ Act Article 33:** ordinary collateralized credit;
- **Article 37:** narrow uncollateralized liquidity for an unexpected, temporary payment shortage caused by an accident such as an electronic-processing failure;
- **Article 38:** systemic special action requiring a Prime Minister/Finance Minister request and a separate BOJ Policy Board necessity decision.

Deposit insurance, DICJ resolution and MOF fiscal/earthquake-reinsurance exposure remain separate.

### 3. Europe distributes emergency authority—and therefore propagation risk

National central banks normally provide ELA at their own risk. The ECB Governing Council controls interference with the single monetary policy. TARGET is a Eurosystem operating stack; bank resolution sits with SRB/national authorities; deposit protection remains national; fiscal support is Commission-controlled; ESM support is conditional. The intended ESM common backstop to the SRF was still not in force at the cutoff.

The February 2025 TARGET outage is a useful real precedent: T2 contingency processed critical payments, while a concurrent T2/T2S outage delayed collateral mobilization. Continuity and collateral can therefore diverge inside the same emergency.

### 4. Korea is preparing collateral for a digital-speed run

From 2026-01-02, qualifying corporate loan receivables can be reviewed and legally perfected in advance for a separately activated BOK Article 65 facility. The collateral infrastructure is active; no automatic draw exists. Article 65 requires at least four Monetary Policy Board members and can address both a bank funding mismatch and a temporary payment-fund shortage caused by electronic-processing failure or accident.

### 5. Indonesia is combining a crisis authority chain with an FX reservoir

BI supplies settlement and short-term liquidity; OJK assesses bank condition; LPS insures deposits and executes resolution; the finance minister chairs KSSK. PLJP/PLJPS is a collateralized liquidity object, not capital. The DHE SDA export-proceeds rule is a separate standing FX-capture system. During a commodity/currency shock, that rule can affect the domestic FX reservoir before any bank enters resolution.

### 6. China moved the e-CNY emergency run onto operator balance sheets

Under the framework effective 2026-01-01, a commercial-bank e-CNY wallet is that bank's deposit liability with deposit-insurance treatment and reserve-base inclusion. A run therefore initially reaches the operator bank, its collateral and PBOC access; it is not automatically a direct run on PBOC. Nonbank-provider balances follow a separate 100%-guarantee-fund structure.

### 7. Russia shows what happens when reserves exist but cannot be used normally

After the 2022 reserve freeze, Bank of Russia said ordinary FX-intervention capacity was impaired. The response combined domestic liquidity, market/valuation relief, payment continuity and capital-movement restrictions, with later partial easing. Capital controls ration convertibility; they do not create foreign currency.

### 8. Brazil has made collateral pre-positioning operational

Brazil's Immediate Liquidity Line can be accessed automatically within a pre-established limit against eligible, valued collateral. Its Term Liquidity Line can run up to one year and use a broader pool. The decisive design variable is therefore not only central-bank discretion—it is whether collateral is already perfected, deposited, valued and inside an approved limit.

### 9. BRICS adds outer support rings, not a common central bank

The original USD100 billion CRA is treaty-based, operative and callable, but no participant has drawn it. Its original access is 30% de-linked and 70% IMF-linked. A proposed revision including eligible payment currencies and new-member onboarding remained under national review in the latest located official record.

NDB demonstrated rapid emergency sovereign lending during COVID-19. That is budget/recovery finance creating a sovereign debt claim, not national reserve money or automatic currency defense.

## What actually happens by event type

| Event | First monetary pressure | First institutional response | Escalation hinge | Terminal question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mobile bank run | Deposit conversion drains bank reserves | Intraday/standing liquidity against collateral | Is the bank solvent and is collateral usable? | Transfer, payout, bail-in, recapitalization or repayment |
| Cyber/payment outage | Missing receipts and uncertain finality cause reserve hoarding | Isolation, alternate site, queue/finality decision, liquidity bridge | Can the authoritative ledger and collateral record be reconstructed? | Which payments stand and who bears operational loss? |
| Sovereign-bond seizure | Collateral values fall; bank/sovereign loop tightens | Repo/ELA, market-function tools, fiscal/ESM/IMF route where eligible | Is repricing a liquidity/transmission event or a solvency event? | Who absorbs sovereign and bank losses? |
| FX/sudden stop | Foreign-currency demand exceeds accessible supply | Reserves/swaps/repos, rate action, prudential or flow measures | Are reserves usable and is external support callable? | Depreciation, controls, restructuring or program finance |
| Sanctions/war | Assets, correspondents, custody and legal permissions fragment | Domestic continuity, liquidity/forbearance, legal transfer controls | Can settlement asset and FX route operate legally? | Permanent rail redesign, trapped claims or resolution |
| Earthquake/flood/blackout | Cash, telecom, branch and insurance access break | BCP/cash logistics, payment continuity, temporary credit treatment | Do claims, collateral damage and fiscal costs impair institutions/sovereign? | Insurance/reinsurance/public-backstop loss allocation |

## Where a non-U.S. initiation could plausibly begin

1. **Japan:** earthquake/payment interruption plus bank/JGB collateral stress, or a rapid digital deposit run testing pre-existing BOJ/DICJ boundaries.
2. **Europe:** TARGET/collateral disruption, national ELA event, sovereign spread shock, national DGS liquidity strain or uninsured catastrophe crossing into fiscal capacity.
3. **South Korea:** mobile run or cyber event that tests the newly pre-positioned corporate-loan collateral and BOK Article 65.
4. **Indonesia:** commodity/FX shock moving through DHE state-bank balances, FX sovereign instruments, BI liquidity and KSSK before a U.S. policy reaction.
5. **China:** operator-bank e-CNY run or regional-bank stress testing the deposit-insurance/restructuring/PBOC hierarchy.
6. **Russia / cross-border:** sanctions or reserve-access shock forcing capital controls, alternative FX liquidity and payment routing.
7. **BRICS:** first CRA request with a non-dollar eligible payment currency—only if the revised treaty becomes effective—would be a genuine international monetary event.

America can enter later through dollar swap access, correspondent and sanctions jurisdiction, Treasury collateral, dollar funding markets and the Federal Reserve's balance sheet. It does not have to supply the first shock or first legal activation.
