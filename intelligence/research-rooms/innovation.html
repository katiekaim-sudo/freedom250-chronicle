Genesis: a federal AI science platform, mostly still being built

Genesis, launched by executive order in November 2025, links national labs, federal data, AI models and tech companies into one science platform run through DOE. A few pieces work now, but most of the big computers, models and awards are still announced, selected or under construction.

What we found

  • Executive Order 14363 launched Genesis in November 2025. The White House coordinates it, DOE builds the shared platform, and Under Secretary for Science Darío Gil directs it at DOE. The stated goal is to double the productivity and impact of American science and engineering within ten years.
  • The study covers 33 official science challenges and all 278 projects on DOE's July 2026 selection list. The White House cites more than $5 billion in federal commitments.
  • One workflow is shown working: an Oak Ridge plant-imaging campaign of 360 plants over 40 days, with routine data queries taking 3 to 10 seconds. That result comes from the authors' own preprint.
  • Most big systems are not running yet. Lux at Oak Ridge targets October 2026; Discovery is expected in 2028; Argonne's Solstice (100,000 GPUs) and Equinox (10,000 GPUs) are announced designs, not installed. An NNSA secret cloud with AWS is authorized, but Genesis work on it is not yet shown.
  • Named partners include NVIDIA, Oracle, HPE, AMD and AWS; consortium members include Palantir, CoreWeave, Scale AI and Intel. Membership is not proof of signed project deals, and some money figures conflict (GridFM: $11.5 million per DOE, $14.2 million per Brookhaven).

Why it matters

Federal science is being rebuilt as a shared AI and computing platform run through DOE and its labs, with private chip, cloud and AI firms built into the working layer. The agencies keep the legal mandate while the operating machinery is new.

What to watch

  • October 2026 · Whether Lux at Oak Ridge actually goes into operation.
  • pending · Release and testing of the Genesis-Science-1 open science model, and signed award agreements for the selected projects.
  • 2027–2028 · Delivery and acceptance of Discovery and the Los Alamos Mission and Vision systems.

How Washington plans to turn science strategy into funded work

Seventeen countries endorsed the White House science-reform blueprint in Kyoto, but implementation still belongs to each national system and no shared institution or fund was created.

What we found

  • The October 4 Kyoto Vision moves the July White House science-reform design from U.S. doctrine to a seventeen-country endorsement covering funding methods, metascience, AI-enabled discovery and technical talent.
  • The declaration tells countries to realize the vision in their national systems. It creates no international organization, common fund, shared compute or laboratory access, implementation deadline, metric or reporting duty.
  • Kyoto does not amend CERN, ITER, Horizon Europe or another incumbent regime. The plausible power shift is functional bypass: national funders could route new grants, compute, facilities and talent programmes around older institutions while leaving their charters intact.
  • The tools that do carry force are separate: NSTM-5 / OMB M-26-16 gives FY2028 budget guidance and 90-day agency implementation-plan instructions, and Executive Order 14363 charters the Genesis Mission and its American Science and Security Platform.
  • The security layer the strategy describes covers research security, screening of people and capital, export and data controls, supply-chain checks and cryptography. The study treats it as strategy until a specific legal or technical tool applies it to a specific object.
  • DOE's finalized Fusion Science and Technology Roadmap (June 9) is the first worked example. The eight Milestone companies are one federal funding group, not eight power plants, and the net-electricity and grid-delivery gates are still unmet.
  • The study finds no federal blockchain, no federal science token, no unrestricted spending by AI agents, no blanket preemption of opposition and no plan to eliminate universities.

Why it matters

The American science redesign has now become an international coalition idea. The next question is whether endorsers build compatible national grant, metascience, AI-access and talent machinery—or leave Kyoto as a one-page signal.

What to watch

  • 2026-10-06 or on publication · The STS Forum closing statement or meeting summary, and any named ministerial signature or country-specific commitment.
  • pending · The technology-specific plans the NSSTS says OSTP and the NSC will issue later.
  • pending · Agency implementation plans under OMB M-26-16 and how they shape the FY2028 budget.
  • 2027 · The fusion framework deadline that traces to NEIMA (2019) as amended by the ADVANCE Act; watch the NRC fusion-machine rulemaking against it.

No single pass lets federally funded science through

Federally funded science is routed through many separate checks on identity, disclosure, ownership, access, security, data and investment, each run by its own authority. No single credential, signature, list or score gives blanket permission or proves the science is true.

What we found

  • Each gate answers a different question. A CFIUS clearance, DOE research-security review, DCSA facility clearance, BIS license, FASCSA order, CHIPS award condition, identity credential or valid signature is not a universal green light.
  • The study maps the chain: identified people, organizations and artifacts, then disclosures and certifications, then agency-specific eligibility decisions, then scoped access, then logged work and provenance, then independent scientific checks, then ongoing monitoring, correction and appeal.
  • Its main new finding is that security gates do not only keep people out. Known-investor profiles, routing through cleared-investment vendors and federal capital programs can also steer approved money toward trusted capabilities.
  • The study sets hard limits: proving identity is not permission, permission is not scientific validity, a record of origin is not truth, and monitoring without notice, correction and appeal is not a complete trust system.

Why it matters

Federal science is being re-plumbed through existing agency powers over investment, export, security clearance and grant conditions, not one new law. The result is a patchwork of authorities, not one central platform.

What to watch

  • pending · Any move to fold these separate gates into one shared credential, list or trust score, which the study says does not exist today.
  • pending · Signs that security screening is steering federal or private investment toward approved science projects.

Which companies could build the national-security tech plan, and who signs off

This study maps which exact companies could supply, test, finance or run the projects behind the National Security Science and Technology Strategy across twelve tech sectors. Its main point: the headline supplier often does not make the final call; testers, operators, permit holders and the government's acceptance step decide whether a project moves.

What we found

  • DOE selected 278 Genesis Mission projects for award negotiations, including 19 company-led selections and 157 participating companies. Selection for negotiation is not an issued award.
  • DOE reported more than $800 million in partner commitments, but the total mixes computing, credits, models, expertise and direct money. It is not a company-by-company award list.
  • NNSA's AWS secret-level cloud environment is authorized and meant to host the first Genesis workloads, but the notice does not show those workloads are running.
  • Natcast holds the final operator award for the advanced packaging pilot facility planned for ASU Research Park in Tempe, but the site rests on a nonbinding letter of intent, and construction and qualification are open. Commerce terminated the SMART USA award.
  • For 30 'pivotal third parties' (such as lender engineers, certifiers, insurers and commissioning firms) and 40 more candidates, no public award tied to the strategy was found at the cutoff. The study says that does not prove they are not involved.

Why it matters

The national-security tech buildout runs through ordinary contracting, testing, permitting and acceptance steps, not a new agency. Real leverage sits with whoever holds the sign-off and the test evidence, which is easy to miss if you only follow the big-name vendors.

What to watch

  • pending · Whether Genesis selections become signed awards, and whether the NNSA cloud environment shows Genesis work actually running.
  • pending · A final site contract for the Natcast packaging facility at ASU Research Park, replacing the nonbinding letter of intent.

Winning a federal tech award is not the same as being accepted

Across ten federal technology programs, the study finds that awards, production and prototypes still fall short of the government actually accepting a working system. Being near a program is not being in it.

What we found

  • The map covers ten federal programs, 37 company positions, 13 facility records and four past test cases. Five of the 13 facility records are unresolved or only company sites with no direct program link.
  • SpaceX holds a $2.29 billion fixed-price prototype order for the Space Force's Space Data Network Backbone, with an operational prototype planned for end of 2027. Other relay providers are possible candidates, not disclosed competitors.
  • Natcast holds the final $1.1 billion operator award for the advanced-packaging pilot facility planned at ASU Research Park in Tempe. The site letter is nonbinding; construction, tools and qualified output are still ahead.
  • For the Joint Laser Weapon System, nLIGHT and Lockheed Martin Aculight hold parallel prototype agreements. First systems target about 150 kW, and the $86 million initial value and $847 million ceiling are combined program figures, not per-company amounts.
  • AWS runs the NNSA Secret/Restricted Data cloud that is meant to host early Genesis work, but running Genesis workloads is not established. The Argonne Genesis supercomputing center is still market research with no solicitation or award.

Why it matters

The thesis says change runs through the government's own machinery; this shows that machinery's gates in detail. Federal demand turns into real capability only after named offices, authorizations and acceptance tests sign off, so announcements alone do not mark change.

What to watch

  • 2026-10-31 · Recheck date for most programs: DBPC selection in Hawaii, JLWS design reviews and 150-kW data, Natcast site contract, GPS IIIF delivery.
  • 2026-11-18 · Space Data Network Backbone recheck for reviews, deliveries and acceptance steps.
  • 2026-11-24 · NNSA classified cloud recheck for named tasks and actual Genesis workloads.

What it takes for quantum tech and new encryption to win federal approval

A federal agency does not accept a quantum device or post-quantum encryption because a vendor says it works. It needs a chain of separate proofs ending in a named official's sign-off, and most companies are still partway down that chain.

What we found

  • Executive Order 14413 (signed June 22, published June 25, 2026) sets up an effort to deliver a useful quantum computer to a DOE site, with DOE deadlines at 90 and 180 days and three priority quantum-sensor projects meant to be fielded by Sept 30, 2028. It names no vendor, award or funding.
  • For new encryption, FIPS 203, 204 and 205 are final; FIPS 206 is still in development. The bottleneck is moving from algorithm test (CAVP) to validated module (FIPS 140-3) to an agency's authority to operate. NIST's own example shows post-quantum services can sit inside a validated module but be marked non-compliant.
  • GSA lists only Entrust and WidePoint as federal PKI shared-service providers. Entrust has a FIPS 140-3 module plus separate post-quantum algorithm evidence; WidePoint has no comparable public post-quantum chain.
  • NIST paused new applications for crypto testing labs, so the few existing accredited labs (such as atsec, Lightship and Leidos AT&E) are a scarce choke point.
  • Commerce's nine Quantum CHIPS letters of intent propose $2.013 billion but are not final agreements. NIST's initial $20 million deal with SRI sets up a quantum manufacturing center. No public post-quantum setup for the Genesis system was found.

Why it matters

This is the thesis inside the machinery: the change runs through orders, standards, test labs and sign-off rules rather than a sudden ban, and whoever runs those gates shapes the transition.

What to watch

  • 2026-09-21 to 2026-09-22 · FIPS 140-2 modules move to historical status for new-system purchases; vendors without a 140-3 certificate, such as Crypto4A, hit this cliff.
  • 2026-10-15 · DARPA's QBI independent verification call closes; watch for the named awardee and conflict-of-interest terms.
  • 2026-10-22 · Non-national-security agency post-quantum plans due under OMB M-26-15, including DOE priorities.

The emergency communications backbone: who owns it and who switches it on

Public-safety radio, satellites, fiber, airwaves, tribal rights and continuity plans form one emergency communications layer. The study keeps the assets, the rights, the operators, everyday access and emergency activation as separate things.

What we found

  • The study's scope covers public safety, satellite, fiber, spectrum, tribal sovereignty, continuity of government and government access.
  • Its core rule: owning a network, holding the right to use it, running it, using it day to day and taking it over in an emergency are five separate states.
  • A dated September 29, 2026 map refreshes only one seam: how states and the federal government coordinate emergency communications through NCSWIC. It does not update the rest of the package.

Why it matters

Emergency communications are a quiet place where federal, state, tribal and private control meet. Who can switch a network into emergency use is a procedural power, not a visible one.

What to watch

  • pending · A source refresh of the full package; its main findings stop at July 15, 2026.

NSA made the post-quantum migration public, but the real proof is still downstream

NSA's new hub gives national-security and defense-industry operators one public entrance, while the actual migration still has to move from policy and approved algorithms through validated products, registered solutions and observed system use.

What we found

  • The October 1 hub is an education and routing surface, not a new rule. The 2027 acquisition/support gate and 2030 phase-out gate come from earlier CNSS Policy 15 and CNSA 2.0 transition material.
  • The live hub showed three recommended articles at the cutoff: a primer, a threat-and-migration explainer, and a reaffirmation that NSA does not recommend quantum key distribution or quantum cryptography for NSS unless their implementation limitations are overcome.
  • NSA now makes two risks explicit: Harvest Now, Decrypt Later threatens confidentiality, while Trust Now, Forge Later threatens authentication, signatures and certificate-based attribution. The second branch reaches federal identity, signed claims, software provenance and payment authorization directly.
  • Approved March 2026 CSfC packages already include ML-KEM-1024 and ML-DSA-87, but generally as objective requirements that can still map to classical threshold alternatives where the objective setting is infeasible. Approved algorithm, product support, validation, solution registration and deployed use are different receipts.
  • The launch page compresses the clock and contains visible defects: it says 'Executive Order XX,' omits the separate 2031 use mandate and 2035 end state, and uses inconsistent wording for the Trust Now threat. Those are publication-quality issues, not proof that the underlying policy changed.

Why it matters

This is the missing public bridge between abstract federal PQC policy and the systems that must preserve identity, authority and durable signatures. It gives the Observatory a clean migration ladder and keeps support, compliance and operation from being collapsed into one claim.

What to watch

  • source-triggered · NSA correction or expansion of the hub, including a named EO, consistent threat terminology, technical profiles, white papers, migration tools or retirement of stale legacy text.
  • 2027-01-01 and the 2027 acquisition cycle · A named NSS solicitation, acquisition, waiver or rejection and the first applicable CNSA 2.0-supporting components on the CSfC list; product support, FIPS validation, NIAP evaluation and procurement remain separate.
  • 2028 capability-package cycle · Whether Mobile Access, Campus WLAN, Multi-Site Connectivity, Data at Rest and Key Management actually convert the March 2026 objective settings into required threshold settings.
  • 2030-12-31 through 2031-12-31 · Evidence of incapable-equipment phase-out, registered-solution mitigation and then mandatory CNSA 2.0 use, including any exception or waiver.
  • source-triggered · A named FPKI, PIV/CAC, Login.gov, Treasury SPS, software-signing or certificate/revocation cutover with integration, authorization, operation and recovery evidence.

New York's factory push is real and spread across the state, not just Micron

Yes, New York has a real, spread-out industrial base along the I-90 and CSX rail line, fed by Great Lakes water, hydropower and specialized ports. Micron is the biggest future anchor, but it is still being built, with production targeted for 2030.

What we found

  • Micron's first fab in Clay began vertical construction in July 2026, with production targeted for 2030. Its $20 billion first phase, $51.5 billion local incentive scope, up-to-$100 billion New York vision and $250 billion U.S. plan are different amounts, not one number.
  • Micron's environmental impact statement models fresh-water use rising from 7.85 million gallons a day for the first fab to 48 million at four fabs, plus new water mains, wastewater, a 345-kV power line, roads and a rail spur. Water and wastewater for later fabs still need building and permits.
  • Already operating: GlobalFoundries in Malta, Wolfspeed in Marcy, Albany NanoTech research (High-NA tools being installed), TTM in DeWitt (opened June 2026), IBM Poughkeepsie, Alcoa Massena, Norsk Titanium Plattsburgh, GE Vernova Schenectady and Lockheed Martin Owego.
  • Water does four separate jobs: Lake Ontario supplies Micron's process water, the Niagara and St. Lawrence supply hydropower, ports at Oswego and Albany move special cargo, and old waterfronts offer sites. No record reviewed shows Port Oswego, the Erie Canal or the Port of NY/NJ as a Micron supply route.
  • Many items are still only proposals or awards, such as STAMP Project Double Reed, the NCCS/NYDIG Massena expansion and a later GlobalFoundries fab. Large proposed data-center loads in the North Country are not proven energized.

Why it matters

Federal chip awards, state hydropower programs and public utility building are being used to grow a new industrial base on top of old rail, water and power systems. It is a rebuild through existing agencies and permits, with most of the payoff still years away.

What to watch

  • 2030 · Micron's targeted start of production at the first Clay fab.
  • pending · Permits and construction for water, wastewater and transmission for the later Clay fabs.
  • pending · Whether site awards such as Corning Canton and FAST NY / POWER UP sites turn into occupied, energized plants.

The short version (draft, not yet reviewed by Katie)

U.S. is making rare-earth magnets again, but China still runs the chain

China still controls most steps of the rare-earth magnet chain. The U.S. has restarted real magnet production at a few plants, and Washington is building a protected home market through equity stakes, price floors, loans, tariffs and defense buying rules. Most promised U.S. capacity is still plans.

What we found

  • A DOE assessment put China's 2020 shares at about 58% of rare-earth mining, 89% of separation, 90% of metal refining and 92% of magnet-alloy and magnet making. China's April 4, 2025 export licensing on seven rare-earth groups is still in force; broader October 2025 controls are suspended to November 10, 2026.
  • Real U.S. output: Noveon in Texas began GM deliveries in July 2025 and ABB deliveries in August 2025; MP Materials in Texas has made metal since 2024 and magnets since 2025; eVAC in South Carolina shipped its first U.S. magnets in December 2025. USA Rare Earth commissioned its first Oklahoma phase in March 2026.
  • Federal tools: a DoD equity-and-warrant deal with MP plus a $110/kg price floor for ten years; a final Commerce agreement with USA Rare Earth signed June 3, 2026 (up to $277 million in incentives and up to a $1.3 billion loan); conditional loan commitments of $700 million to Vulcan/ReElement and $725 million to Energy Fuels; EXIM approval of up to a $10 billion loan for the Project Vault stockpile; a 25% tariff on Chinese magnets in 2026.
  • A July 20, 2026 executive order says routine defense waivers for non-domestic materials are to stop January 1, 2027 unless a contractor accepts an approved mitigation plan, and orders supply-chain mapping back to raw-material origin.
  • MP's planned 10X campus would bring U.S. capacity to about 10,000 tons a year, with commissioning targeted for 2028. Announced capacity is not the same as approved, steady deliveries.

Why it matters

The government is acting as owner, price-setter, buyer and lender in one industry, working through existing agencies like DoD, Commerce and EXIM rather than a new law. It is a clear case of a market being rebuilt from inside the current machinery.

What to watch

  • November 10, 2026 · Whether China's suspension of its October 2025 rare-earth controls ends or is extended.
  • January 1, 2027 · End of routine defense waivers for non-domestic materials under the July 20 order.
  • 2027–2029 · Customer-approved, steady magnet deliveries from U.S. plants, versus announced capacity (MP's 10X targeted for 2028).

The short version (draft, not yet reviewed by Katie)

Nanotubes help batteries, but the U.S. lacks a full home supply chain

A tiny amount of carbon nanotubes can make battery electrodes work better, especially silicon anodes and thick electrodes. But the U.S. has only pieces of a supply chain, not a proven mass-scale chain from domestic nanotube production to finished cells.

What we found

  • Nanotubes are a helper material, not a new battery chemistry. In ORNL pouch-cell tests a nanotube network kept 99.4 percent of capacity after 200 cycles versus 94.6 percent with carbon black, with a calculated 11.7 percent energy-density gain.
  • Argonne's 2024 tests found 0.1 weight percent of multi-wall nanotubes improved NMC811 rate performance, but these were coin cells, not car-scale cells.
  • U.S. pieces exist: artience/LioChem runs dispersion production in Georgia with two North American customers as of March 2026; CHASM has a 50-tonne-per-year pilot reactor in Oklahoma, with a 1,500-tonne reactor still to be built; Cnano USA's Kansas project has a Chinese parent and a congressional CFIUS request.
  • Cabot's Michigan plant, billed as the first U.S. commercial-scale battery-grade nanotube facility, was only selected for DOE award negotiation in September 2024. That is not a final award, construction or output.
  • Larger proven capacity is abroad: LG Chem lists 2,900 tonnes per year in South Korea, Arkema up to 400 tonnes in France and OCSiAl 60 tonnes in Serbia.

Why it matters

It shows that rebuilding industrial supply chains at home is not just about building plants. A U.S. address can still depend on foreign powder, recipes or ownership, so real control depends on qualification, origin and customer acceptance.

What to watch

  • pending · Whether Cabot's Michigan project moves from award selection to a final award, construction and customer-qualified output.
  • pending · Proof of commissioned output and customer acceptance from Cnano USA in Kansas and CHASM's planned 1,500-tonne reactor.
