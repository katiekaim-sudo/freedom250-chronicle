Wave 6B — The Admissibility Record

Research window: Exact 2026 Aries ingress through exact 2026 Cancer ingress.
Evidence cutoff: June 20, 2026, 11:59 p.m. EDT.
Question: When the Aries-year regime decides who may vote, save, clear payments, use the financial system or import, does it create one universal identity machine—or place different documentary gates in front of legally distinct doors?
Finding: These five orders do not merge elections, retirement accounts, Reserve Bank services, Bank Secrecy Act reporting and customs law. They do share a governing technique: move admissibility upstream into a formal record. Citizenship lists and ballot-mail identifiers condition an election interface; a Treasury-curated provider list conditions future Trump Account and Saver's Match routing; existing legal eligibility and Reserve Bank review still condition a proposed Payment Account; red flags and a named SAR term organize financial-intelligence reporting; and importer status is tied to identity, domestic assets, bonding, ownership and recurrent vetting. The packet's thesis is the regime becomes selectively permeable by specifying which record allows an actor to approach each door, while the legal holder at each door remains separate.

The institutional ladder is:

person / institution / shipment → qualifying record → screening or list → gatekeeper decision → access / report / denial → correction, appeal or contest

6B.1 — March 31 through June 2: citizenship becomes a proposed list-and-mail interface

EO 14399 directs Homeland Security to assemble a State Citizenship List from federal citizenship and naturalization records, Social Security Administration data, SAVE and other lawful databases. The contemplated route is not merely a one-way federal verdict. The order also calls for procedures through which individuals may access and correct their records and through which states may supplement the federal list. Federal transmission is due at least 60 days before an election.

The order separately directs the Postal Service to propose uniform ballot-mail requirements, including Official Election Mail markings, automation compatibility, a unique Intelligent Mail barcode, design review and state participation information. USPS published the proposed rule on June 2. At the cutoff it remained a proposal, not a final mail standard or completed participation list.

The legal chain is therefore:

federal source records → proposed citizenship compilation → access/correction and state supplement → state election administration

and, separately:

state ballot-mail choice → proposed USPS participation record and barcode → mail tracking → state receipt and counting law

Counterevidence and limit. A State Citizenship List entry is not the same object as a valid state voter registration. The EO does not itself amend the National Voter Registration Act, enact the SAVE Act or displace state election administration. The 90-day DHS infrastructure clock extended beyond the Cancer ingress. A June 22 district-court vacatur affecting the SAVE bulk/SSN overlay belongs to the next wave's contest clock, not this packet's cutoff state.

6B.2 — April 30: Treasury is assigned a future retirement doorway, not the account assets

EO 14403 directs Treasury to establish TrumpIRA.gov by January 1, 2027. The portal is intended to list qualifying private IRA providers that accept federal Saver's Match payments, charge no account minimum and satisfy the order's low-expense standard.

The proposed topology is:

worker / child beneficiary → qualifying private provider → Treasury listing and portal → private IRA → contribution → later federal match

Treasury is the curator of the doorway, not necessarily the custodian or owner of each account's assets. The order does not create a new Treasury investment fund or make the government the counterparty to every portfolio transaction.

Maturity. Direction, deadline and provider conditions only. No live portal, qualifying-institution list, funded account, contribution or Saver's Match existed by June 20. IRS Notice 2026-48 and the later contribution/match clocks are post-wave handoffs.

6B.3 — May 19–20: access is widened as a question while credit remains fenced

EO 14405 directs federal financial regulators to review barriers to fintech integration. Because the Federal Reserve Board has its own statutory position, the order separately requests Board review of access by uninsured depositories, nonbanks and digital-asset firms; the roles of the twelve Reserve Banks; consistency controls; and possible procedures where existing law permits.

On May 20 the Board proposed a special-purpose Payment Account. The proposal is a real institutional object, but it preserves the critical boundaries:

  • only institutions already legally eligible for Reserve Bank accounts or services could request one;
  • an individual Reserve Bank would decide an application under Board policy;
  • the account would be prefunded and protected by automated overdraft controls;
  • it would pay no interest and provide no intraday credit or discount-window access; and
  • it would offer a narrower service set than a normal master-account relationship.

The correct ladder is:

existing statutory eligibility → application and complete record → Reserve Bank risk decision → account agreement and service limits → technical opening → first production settlement

At the Cancer ingress, only the proposal and policy-review stages had matured. No general legal perimeter had expanded; no applicant had acquired an EO-created entitlement; and no Payment Account had opened or settled a transaction.

Important tension. The same period asks whether innovative firms can enter central-bank payment services while insisting that the entrance remain conditioned by identity, risk and illicit-finance controls. Openness and screening are not contrary stories here. They are the two sides of selective permeability.

6B.4 — June 5: financial integrity acquires a reporting vocabulary

EO 14406 directs Treasury to address financial-system risk associated with non-work-authorized populations and complicit employers through a BSA advisory, customer-due-diligence review and later regulatory guidance. FinCEN's June 5 joint advisory is a direct implementation receipt: it says it supports EO 14406, identifies 18 red-flag indicators and requests the SAR key term FINANCIALINTEGRITY-2026-A002.

This makes a previously general policy concern machine-readable inside an existing reporting system:

customer / employer activity → institution observes pattern → red-flag analysis → SAR with named key term → FinCEN intake → possible investigation or referral

The advisory did not turn a red flag into a dispositive identity ruling. It did not command automatic account closure, deny credit, determine immigration status, prove a crime or recover money. A financial institution still evaluates facts and applicable law; investigators and adjudicators remain separate holders.

Maturity. Operating signal-and-reporting channel. Later CDD rulemaking, examination, named enforcement and case outcomes remain distinct clocks.

6B.5 — June 3: customs moves the gate before the border transaction

EO 14411 directs DHS and CBP to harden the Importer of Record perimeter through eligibility and good-standing rules, domestic presence and asset requirements, stronger bonds, beneficial-ownership information, recurrent vetting, supply-chain certification, audit, penalties, seizure and disposal procedures. It also distinguishes domestic and foreign importers rather than treating an IOR number as a permanent passport.

The architecture reaches further upstream than an individual tariff rate:

person / firm → verified identity and beneficial owner → domestic assets / presence / bond → IOR registry and recurrent review → entry filing → examination / release → duty, penalty or seizure

Many implementing instruments carried 90- and 180-day clocks beyond the ingress. At June 20 the order had created a detailed decision design, not a completed new registry or a proved denial, collection, seizure, title transfer or sale.

Continuity. CBP remains the customs administrator; bonds remain risk security rather than automatic forfeiture; an adverse importer decision still requires its applicable legal and review path; and a shipment does not become unlawful merely because the order directs future risk tiers.

The shared regime form is not a single database. It is a repeated proposition: before the transaction, decide whether the actor is admissible and make the decision legible in a record.
