# U.S.–China People-to-People Relationship Infrastructure — 2026

**Wave:** China deepening wave 8
**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-09-24
**Question:** What is the real operating condition of travel, study, research, journalism, youth and subnational exchange?

## Bottom line

The human relationship is open, large and highly asymmetric—but it does not operate as one exchange system.

- **Education remains the deepest operating bridge.** Open Doors counted 265,919 students from China at U.S. institutions in academic year 2024/25. Only 1,749 U.S. students received academic credit for study in China in 2023/24. The clocks differ, but the scale difference is still unmistakable.
- **Youth visits have become a substantial Chinese-sponsored lane.** President Xi's November 2023 invitation was for 50,000 young Americans over five years, not 100,000. Chinese official sources reported nearly 15,000 visits in the first year and more than 50,000 by May 2026. Those reports establish a claimed aggregate and functioning visa/program machinery; they do not yet provide a participant-level reconciliation.
- **Air rights have recovered faster than service.** Each side may operate up to fifty scheduled roundtrip passenger flights a week, but U.S. carrier filings show that authorized frequencies have gone unused. A bilateral cap is capacity permission, not a schedule, seat or passenger receipt.
- **Ordinary travel is possible but frictioned.** Chinese nationals can receive long-validity U.S. visitor and student visas, while U.S. nationals generally still require a Chinese visa except for bounded transit and other narrow exemptions. A visa permits travel to a port of entry; it does not guarantee admission.
- **Government science cooperation survives in a narrower form.** The December 2024 protocol extended the bilateral Science and Technology Agreement for five years but limited it to basic research and added data-reciprocity, transparency, intellectual-property and researcher-security guardrails. It is a framework for eligible federal cooperation, not permission for every institution, person, dataset or technology.
- **Journalism is the most visibly constrained lane.** China maintains a formal journalist-visa, press-card and permanent-office system. The latest Foreign Correspondents' Club of China survey nevertheless reported routine obstruction, source intimidation and expanding red lines. Credential availability and practical reporting access are separate facts.
- **Subnational agreements remain real but bounded.** The 2023 California–NDRC climate memorandum names owners, cooperation fields and an implementation process, but expressly creates no binding rights and supplies no funding. It proves an institutional route, not completed projects.

The correct operating chain is:

`eligibility -> invitation/admission document -> visa/credential -> funded place or transport capacity -> departure -> admission -> participation -> completion -> repeat or durable institutional result`

Every public claim should stop at its last evidenced state.

## 2. Travel and admission

### A visa is not an admission receipt

The U.S. reciprocity schedule allows qualified PRC applicants multiple-entry B-1/B-2 visas valid for 120 months, and F-1 and J-1 visas valid for 60 months. It gives PRC journalists an I visa valid for twelve months with a reciprocity fee. Those are maximum validity conditions for visas that are actually issued. They are not approval rates, admission guarantees or authorized duration of stay.

The corresponding proof chain is:

`application -> adjudication -> visa issuance -> carrier boarding -> CBP inspection -> admission/class/duration -> departure or status compliance`

Appointment wait time is another separate clock. State's published wait time is an estimate for the next available interview at a post; it is neither total processing time nor an assurance of issuance.

### U.S. nationals still generally need a Chinese visa

China's current visa-free expansion should not be summarized as general visa-free entry for Americans. The United States is not among the nationalities covered by China's unilateral thirty-day visa waiver. U.S. passport holders may use the 240-hour visa-free transit policy when they meet its conditions: transit through an eligible port and area to a third country or region, an ordinary passport and a confirmed onward ticket. That is a maximum ten-day transit permission, not ordinary bilateral visa-free travel.

China's own guidance also excludes work, study and news coverage from the ordinary visa-waiver routes. Each requires the proper visa or permit.

The U.S. travel advisory for mainland China was Level 2 as of September 4, 2026, citing risks including arbitrary enforcement of local laws, exit bans and wrongful detention. An advisory is a risk statement, not a prohibition on travel.

## 3. Aviation: four clocks, not one

The bilateral passenger-air system has at least four measurable clocks:

1. **entitlement** — the maximum frequencies the governments allow;
2. **allocation/schedule** — frequencies assigned and filed by carriers;
3. **operation** — flights actually flown and seats supplied; and
4. **use** — boarded passengers, load factors, origin/destination and purpose.

A 2025 Department of Transportation order states that the United States and China then permitted each other's carriers up to fifty scheduled roundtrip combination operations per week. Carrier waiver and allocation filings show that U.S. airlines were not using the full U.S. entitlement. They cite route economics and the competitive effect of Russian airspace remaining closed to U.S. carriers while Chinese carriers can use it.

This means “fifty flights a week” cannot be used as an actual-service count without a carrier schedule and completed-flight return. Nor does restored capacity prove restored tourism. The National Travel and Tourism Office reported about 491,000 arrivals from China by air in the third quarter of 2025; that is a passenger-flow receipt for a defined quarter, not a bilateral annual total or a count of unique people.

## 4. Education: the deepest bridge and the largest asymmetry

### Chinese students in the United States

The State Department-funded Open Doors census counted **265,919** students from China at U.S. higher-education institutions in 2024/25, down 4 percent from 277,398 the previous year and second among places of origin. The level breakdown was:

| Level/status | 2024/25 | Year-on-year change |
|---|---:|---:|
| Undergraduate | 78,583 | -10.2% |
| Graduate | 120,229 | -2.1% |
| Nondegree | 5,126 | -7.1% |
| Optional Practical Training | 61,981 | +0.7% |
| **Total** | **265,919** | **-4.1%** |

The total is an enrollment/OPT census, not new arrivals or visas issued. ICE reported 329,541 active SEVIS records associated with China in calendar 2024, but that population and definition are not interchangeable with Open Doors: SEVIS includes different record and program states, and its count is dynamic.

Presidential Proclamation 10043 also remains part of the permission layer for a bounded subset of PRC nationals seeking F or J status for graduate study or research who are or were associated with entities supporting the PRC's military-civil fusion strategy. It does not bar Chinese students generally. A current case therefore needs program level, institutional association, visa basis and adjudication—not nationality alone.

### U.S. students in China

Open Doors counted **1,749 U.S. students receiving academic credit in China in 2023/24**, up from 469 in 2022/23 but still far below **11,639 in 2018/19**. The recent series makes the discontinuity visible:

| Academic year | U.S. credit-bearing study-abroad students in China |
|---|---:|
| 2018/19 | 11,639 |
| 2019/20 | 2,481 |
| 2020/21 | 382 |
| 2021/22 | 211 |
| 2022/23 | 469 |
| 2023/24 | 1,749 |

This is not a count of every American physically studying in China. It omits some full-degree, non-credit, secondary, language and short-visit participants. It is nevertheless the cleanest consistent U.S. series for university-sponsored, credit-bearing mobility.

The bilateral education relationship is therefore not merely “large.” It is **large in one direction and institutionally thin in the other**. That matters for language expertise, field access, professional networks and the number of future officials or researchers with lived experience in the other country.

## 5. The 50,000 youth initiative

### What was actually promised

In November 2023, President Xi announced that China would invite **50,000 young Americans** for exchange and study over five years. The Young Envoys Scholarship program is one implementation route, led by the China Education Association for International Exchange. China later offered qualified YES participants a fee-free, five-year, multiple-entry F visa when they hold the program-specific invitation.

The initiative is broader than YES alone: Chinese descriptions include school exchanges, short visits, summer and winter programs, and other host-organized travel.

### What has been reported

Chinese official sources reported:

- nearly **15,000** young Americans visited under the initiative in its first year, covering all fifty states and the District of Columbia; and
- more than **50,000** had come to China for visits and exchanges by May 2026, which China described as meeting the five-year target two and a half years early.

Those figures are materially stronger than a bare invitation because they purport to count participation. They still need a common accounting table before they can be treated as a reconciled outcome.

Minimum return fields are:

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| unique person ID or deduplicated cohort | prevents one participant or multi-entry visa from being counted repeatedly |
| age/eligibility rule | defines “young American” consistently |
| program and host | separates YES from other exchanges and ordinary travel |
| invitation and visa class | establishes program admission route |
| funding source and amount | distinguishes invitation, subsidy and fully funded place |
| arrival/departure and duration | distinguishes attendance from invitation or visa issuance |
| activity and completion | shows what the exchange actually delivered |
| U.S. institution/state | tests geographic and institutional reach |
| later follow-up | measures durable ties rather than a single visit |

Until that ledger exists publicly, the correct formulation is: **China reports that the aggregate target was exceeded; the public evidence does not yet permit independent cohort reconciliation.**

## 6. Science and research

The December 13, 2024 protocol amended and extended the 1979 U.S.–PRC Science and Technology Agreement for five years. The U.S. description says it:

- covers only basic research;
- excludes cooperation in critical and emerging technologies;
- strengthens intellectual-property protection;
- adds transparency and data-reciprocity provisions; and
- adds guardrails for researcher safety and security.

The protocol keeps a government-to-government legal framework alive through 2029. It does not itself:

- appropriate funds;
- create a grant or project;
- waive export controls or sanctions;
- authorize release of controlled technology, personal data or proprietary material;
- admit a student or researcher;
- override Proclamation 10043 or institution-specific research-security rules; or
- prove that an implementing protocol is still active.

Each research claim needs a named implementing agency, counterpart, project/protocol, appropriation or grant, participant eligibility, subject area, data/material route, intellectual-property terms, security review, activity and output.

## 7. Journalism: credential route versus reporting reality

China's published media rules establish a formal operating structure:

- permanent foreign-media offices register with the Ministry of Foreign Affairs;
- resident correspondents require a J-1 visa and Press Card (R);
- short-term correspondents require a J-2 visa;
- interview subjects must give prior consent;
- Chinese assistants are hired through designated service organizations; and
- reporting without the required credential may be stopped and sanctioned.

China also publicly hosts foreign correspondents and described about 800 journalists, family members, diplomats and other guests at its January 2025 New Year reception. That establishes an operating foreign-media community, not the freedom or practical reach of its reporting.

The Foreign Correspondents' Club of China's survey of reporting conditions in 2025 received responses from 89 of its 176 correspondent members. According to the published survey account:

- 94 percent said conditions usually did not or never met international reporting standards;
- 64 percent reported obstruction by officials or police at least once;
- 23 percent said sources faced questioning, intimidation or other repercussions; and
- 77 percent reported sources declining or cancelling interviews because they lacked permission to speak.

These are membership-survey results, not an administrative census of every journalist or incident. They are still primary evidence of correspondents' reported operating experience and should sit beside—not be erased by—the formal visa and press-card rules.

On the U.S. side, Chinese nationals working as journalists use the I visa lane. The reciprocity schedule permits multiple entries for twelve months and adds a $397 reciprocity fee. Visa validity does not establish how many Chinese journalists are accredited, employed, admitted or reporting in the United States. A full reciprocity audit needs both governments' current resident-correspondent rosters, outlet/foreign-mission designations, visa issuances and renewals, accreditation, staffing rules, expulsions and denials.

## 8. Subnational and institutional ties

The October 2023 memorandum between China's National Development and Reform Commission and California creates a flexible framework for climate, clean energy, pollution, electric vehicles, storage, hydrogen, grid reliability, resilience and local-level exchanges. It names NDRC and California agencies, designates contact points and contemplates an action plan, meetings, research, workshops, training and pilot projects.

Its legal and financial boundary is unusually clear:

- it is voluntary and creates no legally binding rights, obligations or remedies;
- each party retains discretion;
- it exchanges no funds and obligates no funding or personnel;
- activities remain subject to available resources and applicable law; and
- either party may withdraw.

Therefore the MOU is a **live institutional route**, not evidence that any specific project was funded or completed. The return chain is:

`MOU -> named action plan -> agency appropriation or partner funding -> project/research/training agreement -> participants/delivery -> output -> policy or environmental result`

The same rule applies to sister-city, university and museum agreements. An affiliation list proves a relationship claim; a current contact, funded program, completed exchange or joint output proves operation.

## 9. What this changes in the China orientation

The human infrastructure supplies three kinds of strategic capacity that do not appear in tariff or military tables:

1. **interpretive capacity** — language skill, field knowledge, trusted contacts and the ability to distinguish central policy from local practice;
2. **resilience capacity** — institutional relationships that can continue during high-level political friction; and
3. **verification capacity** — journalists, scholars, students, firms and local officials who can produce operating evidence rather than rely entirely on national statements.

Those capacities are not evenly distributed. The United States hosts a very large Chinese student population while sending comparatively few students to China; China has rapidly expanded short youth visits while foreign correspondents report a more restrictive professional environment. The relationship can therefore become socially busy yet epistemically thinner at the same time.

2. Obtain route-level 2026 operated U.S.–China flight frequencies, seats, passenger counts and cancellations.
3. Add 2024/25 U.S. study-abroad-to-China data when Open Doors releases the same-clock result.
4. Obtain a public 50,000-initiative cohort ledger or program-level annual totals with deduplication and completion fields.
5. Inventory current U.S.–PRC Science and Technology Agreement implementing protocols and delivered outputs.
6. Build a reciprocal journalist roster and visa/accreditation decision ledger.
7. Inventory live state/province, city, university and cultural agreements, then attach annual operating receipts.
8. Separate mainland China, Hong Kong and Macao for every visa, student, journalist and travel statistic.

## Reuse rule

Never use “exchange resumed” without naming the lane and receipt. Use one of:

- permission available;
- visa or credential issued;
- capacity scheduled;
- participant admitted;
- activity begun;
- activity completed;
- institution still operating; or
- durable output observed.

The broader relationship should not inherit a stronger state than its underlying people, flights, programs and institutions can prove.
