## Answer first

**2020 squeezed the slow planets into one corner of the sky. 2026 fans them back
out into a network. The decade asks who owns the network.**

Three facts carry that sentence:

1. **The compression (2020–2022).** Saturn–Pluto, Jupiter–Pluto (three passes)
   and Jupiter–Saturn all perfected within eleven months at the end of Capricorn
   and the top of Aquarius. Barbault's cyclic index hit its deepest trough since
   1983–85. The next comparable trough is in the 2340s–50s.
2. **The sign change (2023–2026).** Every outer planet changed sign in three
   years. The result is **Uranus in Gemini, Neptune in Aries and Pluto in
   Aquarius at the same time**. The last time all three stood in those signs
   together was around **1400 BCE**. Two of the three pairs last overlapped
   during **1778–1781** (Uranus–Gemini with Pluto–Aquarius, during the
   Revolution) and **1862–1865** (Uranus–Gemini with Neptune–Aries, during the
   Civil War).
3. **The reset and the circuit (2026).** Saturn and Neptune began a new cycle at
   **0°45′ Aries**, the nearest a Saturn–Neptune conjunction has come to the
   Aries Point in about 3,700 years. In July, Jupiter, Uranus, Neptune and Pluto
   locked into a six-edge circuit — all six relationships within 0.25° at once.
   **Between 1800 and 2400 that happens exactly once: July 19–20, 2026.**

For a Chronicle built around the republic's 250th year, the sky could hardly be
more on-theme. It is replaying, at once, the outer-planet signatures of both
founding crises — while opening a brand-new 36-year cycle on the world axis.
