Retail debt — separate entity factual spine

Updated: 2026-07-11
Status: first factual pass; entities and debt books remain separate
Scope: U.S. credit cards, private student loans, consumer installment debt, and adjacent retail-credit books. This record tracks what each lender, buyer, servicer, or regulator has actually done with debt—not what a headline implies it may do.

Project position: This is a derived storyline from the broader banking website full sweep, not the frame for that sweep. Growth, research, payments, AI, footprint, partnerships, and capital actions remain in the parent entity record until a specific debt event belongs here.

Reading rule

A portfolio sale is not debt forgiveness. A charge-off is an accounting act and does not by itself extinguish a borrower's duty to pay. A servicing transfer changes who administers a loan, not necessarily who owns it. A hardship modification may reduce interest, waive fees, extend time, or settle principal; each concession must be stated separately.

The entity clocks are:

  • credit: delinquencies, charge-offs, reserves, modifications, settlements;
  • capital: portfolio purchase, sale, securitization, or held-for-sale classification;
  • customer: line closure, fixed-payment conversion, servicing transfer, hardship enrollment;
  • regulatory: consent order, restitution, penalty, or rule;
  • retail: merchant-card partnership gained, lost, renewed, or moved.

System baseline — what is and is not rising

2026-05-12 — New York Fed publishes first-quarter household-debt data

Status: Occurred
Entity acting: Federal Reserve Bank of New York
Clock: System credit

Credit-card balances fell seasonally by $25 billion in the first quarter to $1.25 trillion. Credit-card limits rose by $60 billion. The annualized transition rate into early card delinquency edged down from 8.7% to 8.6%; the transition into serious delinquency was mostly unchanged. By contrast, 10.3% of student-loan balances were 90+ days delinquent, up from 9.6% in 2025 Q4. These are different series and must not be merged into a single “90+ rate.”

Primary source: New York Fed — Household Debt and Credit Report, 2026 Q1

2026-05-19 / 2026-05-27 — Fed and FDIC update bank-level credit series

Status: Occurred
Entities acting: Federal Reserve Board; FDIC
Clock: System credit / bank balance sheets

The Fed reported a seasonally adjusted 2.92% credit-card delinquency rate at commercial banks in 2026 Q1, down from 2.94% in 2025 Q4 and 3.06% in 2025 Q1. The FDIC reported that credit-card past-due and nonaccrual loans fell seasonally by seven basis points to 3.08%, while still remaining elevated relative to the pre-pandemic average. The broad banking system therefore showed strain but not a first-quarter acceleration in card delinquency.

Primary sources: Federal Reserve Board — Delinquency Rates, All Banks; FDIC — Quarterly Banking Profile, 2026 Q1

A. Discover Financial Services → Capital One → Santiago Holdings / Carlyle / KKR / Firstmark

2023-11-29 / 2024-02-01 — Discover decides to leave private student-loan origination

Status: Occurred
Clock: Capital / customer
Debt book: Private student loans

Discover's board authorized management to stop accepting new private-student-loan applications, explore a sale, and transfer servicing. Discover stopped accepting applications on February 1, 2024. It had already signed a servicing agreement with Nelnet on January 18.

Primary source: Discover SEC filing — portfolio-sale history

2024-07-17 / 2024 Q4 — Discover agrees to and completes sale of the private student-loan portfolio

Status: Occurred
Clock: Capital / customer
Debt book: Private student loans

Discover agreed to sell a portfolio with about $10.1 billion of principal to Santiago Holdings, an affiliate of Carlyle and KKR, for a price expected to be up to about $10.8 billion. Firstmark, a Nelnet division, became servicer as loans transferred. Discover's 2024 annual filing says the sale was completed in the fourth quarter.

The official filings establish an exit, sale, and servicing transfer. They do not quantify a broad cancellation of the loans excluded from or affected by the sale. Borrower reports of cancellations are a research lead until a Discover accounting note, loan tape, consent-order report, or borrower notice establishes the amount and rule.

Primary sources: Discover SEC filing — July 17 agreement; Discover 2024 Form 10-K — sale completed

2025-05-18 — Capital One completes acquisition of Discover

Status: Occurred
Clock: Entity / capital / payments network

Capital One completed its acquisition of Discover. Customer accounts initially remained unchanged. The deal joined a large card lender to the Discover payment network; it did not itself cancel card debt.

Primary source: Capital One — acquisition completed

2025-11-24 — Capital One sells the Discover home-loan portfolio

Status: Occurred
Clock: Capital
Debt book: Closed-end mortgages and home-equity loans

Capital One sold the Discover home-loan portfolio for $8.8 billion cash and recorded a $483 million pretax gain. This was another whole-book exit, separate from cards and student loans.

Primary source: Capital One 2025 annual report

2026 Q1 — Capital One expands modifications after absorbing Discover

Status: Occurred
Clock: Credit / customer
Debt book: Credit cards

Capital One modified $1.235 billion of credit-card balances during the quarter, including $1.103 billion of domestic-card balances. The domestic-card interest-rate reductions averaged 15.25 percentage points. The company reports $70 million of principal reductions and $64 million of interest and fees forgiven in the financial effects of domestic-card modifications. It also says settlements may accept partial payment in full satisfaction and waive principal, interest, or fees.

At March 31, the combined card book was $270.6 billion; 30+ delinquency was 3.67%, 90+ delinquent card loans accruing interest were $4.962 billion or 1.83%, and the quarterly card net charge-off rate was 5.05%. All three rates were lower than their disclosed comparison points, while the absolute loan book was much larger because Discover was included.

Primary sources: Capital One 2026 Q1 Form 10-Q, pp. 20, 43–47, 93–98; Capital One — 2026 Q1 results

Current state

Discover's private student-loan and home-loan exits are confirmed. Capital One is keeping and integrating the card franchise while using large-scale rate reductions, term conversions, and some principal/fee forgiveness as loss mitigation. The unverified piece is the size and rule of any Discover student-loan cancellations outside the sold portfolio.

B. Goldman Sachs → JPMorgan Chase / Apple Card

2026-01-07 — Goldman agrees to transfer the Apple Card program to Chase

Status: Announced; closing conditions remain
Clock: Capital / customer
Debt book: Credit cards

Goldman agreed to transition Apple Card accounts and an over-$20-billion card portfolio to Chase. Goldman said the transaction substantially completes the narrowing of its consumer business. It expected to release $2.48 billion of loan-loss reserves, offset by $2.26 billion of markdowns and contract-termination obligations plus $38 million of expenses.

This is a seller exit and buyer expansion, not borrower relief. Account terms and the actual transfer date remain customer-clock watches.

Primary source: Goldman Sachs — Apple Card transition agreement

Current state

Goldman is leaving mass-market card lending; Chase is taking the book. Watch for regulatory approval, closing, servicing notices, portfolio marks, and any change to hardship terms or credit lines during conversion.

C. Ally Financial → CardWorks / Merrick Bank

2025-01-20 / 2025-04-01 — Ally approves and closes sale of its credit-card business

Status: Occurred
Clock: Capital / customer
Debt book: Credit cards

Ally sold its credit-card business to CardWorks and Merrick Bank. At December 31, 2024, the book contained $2.3 billion of receivables and 1.3 million active cardholders. Ally described the move as a way to simplify, focus on core businesses, and improve returns. The sale closed April 1, 2025; Ally recorded a $305 million goodwill impairment tied to the held-for-sale classification.

Primary sources: Ally — sale agreement; Ally 2025 Q1 Form 10-Q — closing and accounting

2025 Q2 — Ally ceases new consumer-mortgage originations

Status: Occurred / run-off
Clock: Capital
Debt book: Consumer mortgages

Ally's filing says consumer-mortgage originations would cease in the second quarter of 2025 and the remaining book would run off. This is a second retail-credit retreat, distinct from the card sale.

Primary source: Ally 2025 Q1 Form 10-Q

Current state

Ally exited cards and stopped adding new mortgages while staying centered on auto finance. CardWorks/Merrick became the card-book owner. Watch the buyer's performance reports, customer conversions, line closures, and charged-off-debt sales rather than treating Ally's exit as extinguishment.

D. Synchrony Financial

2026 Q1 — Synchrony modifies $619 million of financially distressed loans

Status: Occurred
Clock: Credit / customer
Debt book: Mostly retail and co-brand cards

Synchrony enrolled $398 million of card balances in long-term modifications and $218 million in short-term modifications during the quarter. Long-term modifications reduced weighted-average interest rates by 97%. For borrowers who successfully exited short-term programs, Synchrony forgave $11 million of unpaid balances. Sixty percent of short-term-program borrowers completed and exited successfully; $101 million of recently modified card loans defaulted and charged off during the quarter.

The bank recorded $1.233 billion of card net charge-offs, a 5.36% annualized rate, down from 6.36% a year earlier. Its $9.704 billion card allowance was nearly unchanged from year-end.

Primary source: Synchrony 2026 Q1 Form 10-Q, pp. 39–43

2026-04 — Synchrony completes purchase of Lowe's commercial co-brand portfolio

Status: Occurred
Clock: Capital / retail
Debt book: Commercial co-brand cards

Synchrony acquired about $700 million of outstanding Lowe's commercial co-brand receivables. The same entity modifying and charging off distressed consumer balances is still buying selected retail credit books.

Primary source: Synchrony 2026 Q1 Form 10-Q

Current state

Synchrony is not leaving retail credit. It is buying selected merchant books while using steep rate cuts, short-term programs, limited balance forgiveness, and charge-offs on distressed accounts.

E. Citigroup

2025 Q4 — Cards produce most U.S. personal-banking credit losses

Status: Occurred
Clock: Credit / retail

Citi reported that Branded Cards and Retail Services represented about 75% of U.S. Personal Banking end-of-period loans and about 94% of that segment's net credit losses for the quarter. Retail Services covers more than twenty retailer and dealer partners, including home improvement, consumer electronics, specialty retail, and fuel.

Primary source: Citi 2025 Form 10-K, U.S. Personal Banking section

2026 Q1 — Citi keeps cards and restructures distressed balances

Status: Occurred
Clock: Credit / customer

Citi says its long-term card modifications generally reduce the rate, place the borrower on a fixed plan of no more than 60 months, and cancel the open credit line. Citi also works through third-party debt-renegotiation agencies and may forgive part of a balance when the borrower pays a required amount. Its 90+ card delinquency rates rose seasonally from the prior quarter but were broadly stable year over year; general-purpose-card losses improved year over year.

Primary source: Citi 2026 Q1 Form 10-Q, consumer credit and modification sections

Current state

Citi is keeping and reorganizing its card business, not exiting it. The relevant debt acts are fixed-payment conversions, line cancellations, negotiated settlements, and the credit performance of its retailer-specific book.

F. American Express

2025 — American Express reports $3.255 billion of modified loans and receivables

Status: Occurred
Clock: Credit / customer

American Express says its relief programs may reduce rates as low as zero and put customers on fixed plans of no more than 60 months; purchase ability is limited, canceled, or suspended during the program. At year-end, balances associated with current- and prior-year modifications still in program or already defaulted totaled $3.255 billion, including $1.868 billion of consumer card-member loans and $738 million of small-business card-member loans. The company says these modifications did not reduce principal. Modified balances that defaulted within twelve months totaled $133 million in 2025.

Primary source: American Express 2025 annual report, pp. 125–127

Current state

American Express is using large rate cuts and fixed plans, coupled with spending-line suspension or cancellation. Unlike Capital One and Synchrony disclosures above, this filing says the reported 2025 modifications did not reduce principal.

G. Bank of America

2026-06-10 — Bank of America rolls out Custom Pay Plan

Status: Occurred / rollout
Clock: Customer / product
Debt book: Credit cards

Bank of America introduced a feature that lets eligible cardholders convert qualifying purchases into structured payment plans in its app or online banking. This is a product-level conversion tool, not a hardship modification or debt cancellation.

Primary source: Bank of America — Custom Pay Plan announcement

Current state

The public customer pages confirm that Bank of America offers assistance for card and unsecured-loan stress, but the first pass has not yet isolated a clean 2026 entity table comparable to Capital One, Synchrony, Citi, or American Express. Keep product installment plans separate from loss-mitigation plans.

Primary source: Bank of America — credit-card debt assistance

H. JPMorgan Chase & Co. → Chase Card Services

2025 — Chase more than doubles the card balances entering financial-difficulty modifications

Status: Occurred
Clock: Credit / customer
Debt book: General-purpose and co-brand credit cards

Chase modified $2.084 billion of retained card loans for borrowers experiencing financial difficulty in 2025, up from $926 million in 2024 and $648 million in 2023. Of the 2025 amount, $1.8 billion was placed into term extensions with interest-rate reductions: the weighted-average contractual rate fell from 22.88% to 3.48%. Another $284 million received other modifications, mainly rate reductions, lowering the weighted-average rate from 22.75% to 8.09%.

Chase generally places modified accounts on fixed-payment plans lasting about 60 months and, in most cases, does not restore the borrower's line of credit. It reported $111 million of modified card balances defaulting within twelve months during 2025. Modified fixed-payment accounts are generally charged off no later than 120 days past due, compared with no later than 180 days for unmodified card accounts.

Primary source: JPMorganChase 2025 annual report, pp. 267–280

2025-12-30 / 2026-01-07 — Chase commits to acquire the Apple Card portfolio

Status: Agreed; not closed
Clock: Capital / customer / retail
Debt book: Apple Card

JPMorgan Chase entered into the forward purchase commitment on December 30 and announced it on January 7. Chase expects to become Apple Card's issuer after a transition of approximately 24 months, subject to regulatory approvals. The purchase is expected to add more than $20 billion of card balances to Chase. Mastercard is expected to remain the network.

JPMorgan recorded a $2.2 billion allowance for lending-related commitments for the expected Apple Card purchase. Management said about 15% of Chase's existing portfolio was already subprime and that it did not expect Apple Card to increase that share materially. Until closing, Goldman remains the issuer and Apple Card customers continue using their accounts normally.

Primary sources: Chase — Apple Card issuer announcement; JPMorganChase 2026 Q1 Form 10-Q, pp. 19–20; JPMorganChase — February 2026 company update

2026 Q1 — Chase modifies $853 million of card balances while the core book grows

Status: Occurred
Clock: Credit / customer / capital
Debt book: Credit cards

Chase modified $853 million of retained card loans in the quarter, more than twice the $381 million modified in 2025 Q1. Of the 2026 amount, $689 million entered term extensions with the weighted-average contractual rate cut from 22.76% to 3.39%; $164 million received rate reductions from 22.77% to 8.23%. Recently modified balances totaling $99 million defaulted during the quarter.

The retained card book was $239.1 billion, up 7% year over year before the Apple Card acquisition. The Card Services net charge-off rate was 3.47%, down from 3.58% a year earlier. The 30+ delinquency rate was 2.17%, down from 2.21% year over year but up one basis point from year-end; the 90+ rate was 1.15%, down from 1.16% year over year but up from 1.10% at year-end. Chase held a $15.563 billion card allowance, up 4% year over year.

Primary source: JPMorganChase 2026 Q1 Form 10-Q, pp. 19–20 and 130–131

Current state

JPMorgan is a consolidator, not an exiting lender. It is growing its existing card book, committing capital to buy Apple Card, and sharply increasing the volume of distressed balances moved from revolving credit into low-rate fixed-payment plans. The key tension is visible inside the same entity: ordinary card balances keep growing while hardship modifications have risen from $648 million in 2023 to $2.084 billion in 2025 and $853 million in the first quarter of 2026 alone.

First grouped finding — still factual

The bank actions sort into four distinct buckets:

  1. Whole-book exit: Discover student loans; Discover home loans; Ally cards and new mortgages; Goldman Apple Card.
  2. Book acquisition/concentration: Capital One buys Discover; Chase agrees to take Apple Card while growing a $239.1 billion retained card book; CardWorks/Merrick takes Ally cards; Synchrony buys Lowe's commercial cards.
  3. Internal loss mitigation: rate cuts, fixed-payment conversion, line cancellation, fee waiver, limited principal settlement, and re-aging after successful payments.
  4. Accounting and collection: reserves, charge-offs, recoveries, securitization, and possible later debt sales. None alone proves legal discharge.

The early story is therefore not “banks are all canceling debt.” It is a sorting of retail debt toward the firms and funding machines willing to hold it, while incumbent issuers quietly modify a measurable slice before charge-off.
