Credit approval standards: what is changing, and who sets the rules

Research ID: mortgage-credit-score-reform. Evidence checked September 7, 2026, America/New_York. This extends the Pulte score-reform investigation into approval standards, with mortgages as the main case and bounded comparisons to other consumer credit. It is not an individual eligibility determination or a complete review of every lender, state law or mortgage product.

Finding: The reforms change accepted models, access to assessment, evidence of repayment behavior and some operating requirements. These are consequential standards changes. They do not all represent a decision to accept greater default risk. Legal duties, program eligibility, automated risk assessment, pricing and lender-specific approval policies remain distinct.

Judgment and administration follow-up: Section 10 examines income classification, employer-level validation, QC sampling discretion, fewer repeat checks and conditional contractual relief.

Retail and business extension: Cross-market judgment and administration comparison covers named retail deployments, SBA repayment analysis, commercial supervision and the separate 2026 regulatory changes.

2. Separate the three major credit changes

Accepted scoring models. The 2018 law and 2019 validation framework preceded approval of VantageScore 4.0 and FICO 10T in October 2022. Pulte’s 2025 lender-choice policy and 2026 rollout change the operating route and competition. In the current July GSE playbook, participating lenders can deliver using Classic FICO or VantageScore 4.0; FICO 10T remains future. Tri-merge reporting remains, one model is used consistently across borrowers on a delivered loan, and pricing differs by model. These are separate dimensions: model approval, operational delivery, bureau count and pricing. Joint GSE playbook

Fannie’s numerical eligibility gate. The 620 minimum was removed for new DU casefiles created November 16, 2025 onward. DU applies its proprietary risk assessment. Fannie says DU does not use third-party scores to assess credit risk and anticipated negligible change in Approve/Eligible volumes from the gate removal. Scores still have required uses under the guide. This is a distinct change from VantageScore acceptance. Exact release boundary, Fannie’s explanation of its risk standard

Evidence used in underwriting. Rent histories, trended credit information and qualifying account cash-flow data can support an assessment that a conventional score alone does not capture. These pathways began before the September announcement. Fannie’s rent-history offering and Freddie’s separate cash-flow rules must be evaluated under their own requirements, not treated as a single new nationwide program.

The September 3–4 all-lender VantageScore direction remains corroborated by reporting and the vendor announcement. Public GSE/FHFA operating materials inspected September 7 still contain earlier limited-rollout wording. That is a documentation/implementation gap; it establishes neither universal lender readiness nor failure of the direction. FHFA current initiative, Fannie current initiative, September vendor announcement

3. The models themselves must pass standards

Under 12 CFR Part 1254, validation includes initial application review, a credit-score assessment and an Enterprise business assessment. Accuracy concerns ranking repayment risk; reliability concerns performance through the economic cycle; integrity concerns relevant underlying data. Further assessment examines operation inside Enterprise systems, fair-lending effects, competition, vendor qualifications, industry effects and risk management. FHFA reviews proposed determinations and may impose use conditions. Approval is not simply permission to substitute a different brand name. Current Part 1254, particularly sections 1254.6–1254.9

Our analytical test for a changed approval standard is to compare decisions at comparable mortgage risk, controlling for model/version, available data, borrower and loan characteristics, and performance horizon. A 620 under one model is not sufficient evidence of equivalent risk to 620 under another. The joint GSE playbook explicitly provides for separate pricing. Better recognition of creditworthy applicants, a different price for risk, and a greater willingness to accept risk are three different outcomes.

4. Current underwriting requirements are channel-specific

Fannie DU No numerical minimum score; follow score-request and delivery rules General maximum total DTI 50%, with transaction exceptions; DU evaluates the whole file
Fannie manual Published general minima 620 fixed-rate / 640 ARM; matrix conditions can be stricter and exceptions exist General DTI 36%; up to 45% with prescribed credit/reserve conditions
Freddie LPA Accept No minimum Indicator Score under the general guide LPA assesses qualifying ratios; its general section does not state a universal 50% ceiling

Sources: Fannie score requirements, Fannie DTI, Freddie scores, Freddie DTI, Freddie Exhibit 25, VA eligibility toolkit.

The Fannie manual figures are its published general score rules; they are not a substitute for the participating lender’s model-specific VantageScore instructions. Freddie’s no-minimum Accept treatment should not be described as a simultaneous result of Fannie’s November reform. Its manual 45% limit must not be silently applied to LPA Accept.

DTI is qualifying monthly obligations divided by qualifying monthly income. LTV is the loan amount relative to the applicable property value. Reserves are eligible assets remaining after closing, expressed under the program’s rules. These dimensions interact with credit history, occupancy, loan purpose, property type and amortization structure. A ceiling is not an approval promise. Fannie expressly allows consistently applied more-conservative lender calculations. Newly discovered debt or reduced income can require re-underwriting before closing. Fannie DTI and re-underwriting rules, DU risk factors

5. No score, limited history and poor credit are different cases

A person may lack a score because the file is too sparse or inactive; another person may have an established record of missed payments. These are not interchangeable underwriting problems. Fannie’s nontraditional-credit rules generally do not allow a lender to replace an existing poor score or derogatory credit with selected alternative references. Manual and DU no-score pathways have their own property, occupancy, debt-ratio and documentation conditions. Fannie nontraditional-credit eligibility

Fannie’s current positive-rent feature can use credit reports or borrower-authorized asset reports. At least one qualifying borrower must have rented for 12 months, pay at least $300 monthly, and have no mortgage reported, limited credit history, or no credit score. The specific feature is positive-only: missing observed rent payments are not treated as negative because the system cannot tell whether another payment method was used. That limited rule does not mean all adverse rental information must be ignored everywhere. Fannie rent-history FAQs

There is a measurable underwriting result: Fannie reports more than 21,000 applications improved their DU recommendation using rent payments in permissioned bank statements between September 2021 and May 2026. This is an institutional application-recommendation measure, not additional closed loans, unique new homeowners, or an effect of September 2026 score competition. Fannie reported rent-history outcomes

Freddie’s cash-flow assessment separately uses at least 12 months of account data and can apply to borrowers without scores. Its rent-specific and all-borrowers-unscored pathways have different eligibility and reference requirements. Do not transplant Fannie’s positive-rent conditions into Freddie. Freddie credit assessment rules, Freddie cash-flow explanation

6. Verification is another standard being modernized

Fannie’s DU validation service can validate eligible income, employment and assets using authorized electronic reports. Validated components may receive specified representation-and-warranty enforcement relief. This changes acceptable proof and allocation of lender responsibility; it is not permission to assume income exists. Participation is optional, borrower authorization is required, and lenders must investigate conflicting information. DU validation-service requirements

This distinction is significant for access and cost. A rule can preserve its required economic fact—stable qualifying income—while accepting a different method of establishing it. Similarly, removing a redundant form or appraisal-review procedure changes process standards without necessarily changing borrower-risk tolerance.

7. FHA and the broader 2026 agenda have separate operating clocks

HUD announced the intention to permit VantageScore 4.0 and FICO 10T on April 22. Its May 21 FHA INFO 2026-11 explicitly says implementation dates and further guidance will follow, and lenders should continue existing Handbook policy until then. The later INFO and Mortgagee Letter indexes inspected did not establish completed score-model implementation as of this research. The April press release alone therefore cannot be used as operating instructions. HUD April announcement, FHA INFO, May 21 and later updates, Mortgagee Letter index

The March 13, 2026 executive order, Promoting Access to Mortgage Credit, is a broader policy agenda. It directs agencies to consider changes involving ATR/QM, smaller-bank regulation, mortgage/servicing/warehouse capital treatment, appraisal methods, digital documentation and housing-finance liquidity. These provisions generally require subsequent agency action; the order is not itself a blanket replacement of existing underwriting regulations. This pass identifies that authority and checks current rules below, but does not complete an agency-by-agency implementation audit. Executive Order 14393

8. The legal floor and other consumer credit

For covered dwelling-secured transactions, Regulation Z requires a reasonable, good-faith ability-to-repay determination. Its general framework considers income/assets, employment when relied upon, mortgage and simultaneous-loan payments, mortgage-related obligations, other debt/support obligations, DTI or residual income, and credit history, with prescribed verification and payment rules. Its credit-history interpretation does not mandate a consolidated score or numerical minimum. General QM has its own criteria, including pricing; the old blanket assertion that every QM requires DTI at or below 43% is not the current general rule. Neither changing score suppliers nor obtaining a higher number repeals repayment duties. Scope exclusions and special QM categories matter. Current Regulation Z, section 1026.43

Credit cards have a separate federal ability-to-pay provision: issuers must consider ability to make required minimum payments before opening an account or increasing its limit, under section 1026.51. Auto and personal-loan approval criteria depend on the lender/product and applicable law; FHFA’s mortgage score initiative does not establish a national approval score for these markets. Credit-card ability to pay

ECOA/Regulation B constrain credit evaluation and adverse-action notices across covered credit. Current section 1002.9 requires specific reasons or the prescribed notice of the right to obtain them; the explanation must identify actual principal reasons. A proprietary score or automated model does not replace that requirement. The 2022/2023 CFPB algorithm-related circulars were withdrawn in May 2025; this analysis relies on the current regulation, not those circulars as operative guidance. Regulation B notifications, current official interpretations, withdrawal register

FCRA adds disclosures when adverse action relies on a consumer report, including the reporting agency’s identity and applicable report/dispute rights. Score factors explain the score; the lender’s principal reasons explain its decision. Those are distinct objects, and the reporting agency does not itself make the lender’s approval decision. FTC consumer-report decision notices

9. Are lenders actually relaxing standards generally?

The July 2026 Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey reports mixed changes in residential real-estate lending standards, tighter credit-card standards, and broadly unchanged auto/other consumer standards over the preceding quarter. Banks reported standards at the tighter end of their historical ranges for most categories. This is a bank survey, not an all-lender census, and its observation period precedes September’s direction. It does not support a claim that all credit approvals are becoming easier at once. Federal Reserve July 2026 SLOOS

Our working interpretation is that the most important change is a broader choice of validated methods and admissible evidence for demonstrating creditworthiness. The potential borrower gain is access at sustainable terms. The empirical test must distinguish newly scorable people, newly eligible applications, favorable automated recommendations, lender approvals, closed loans, borrower costs and later performance.

The next evidence gaps are specific: broad-lender operating instructions; model-specific eligibility/pricing calibration; outcomes for comparable applicants; private lender overlays; FHA’s implementing score guidance; and the actual regulations/guidance implementing the broader mortgage-credit order. None is filled by adding together score-provider market-share and population estimates.

10. How judgment, administrative work and responsibility are changing

Follow-up scope, September 7, 2026: Katie's question is how reforms change the complexity of making and administering a sound credit decision. This pass uses Fannie Mae as the detailed operating case, with the joint GSE score playbook for model implementation. It does not generalize Fannie's procedures to all creditors or attribute every modernization to September's score announcement.

Finding: The changes redistribute work among risk models, verification systems, individual underwriters and lender management. Some prescribed tasks disappear; discretion, data controls and conditional contractual protections become more important. The sources establish changes to required work, not a measured industry-wide increase or decrease in total workload.

10.1 The credit decision and the validation result are separate judgments

DU assesses the risk represented by submitted information. The lender still decides whether the actual application warrants approval, checks completeness and accuracy, fulfills findings and investigates relevant facts the system did not analyze. DU also does not determine compliance with applicable lending law. These are continuing responsibilities, not newly granted human powers. B3-2-01, General Information on DU

Validation asks whether eligible source data supports a submitted component. Its outcomes include validated, not validated, and unable to validate. A failed data connection or insufficient report can require another documentation route; that technical outcome alone is not a credit denial. Vendor contracts, borrower authorization, identity matching, report access and retention, contradiction resolution, and fresh-report resubmission govern reliance on the service. The submitted data remains the basis of DU's underwriting recommendation. B3-2-02, DU Validation Service

Our interpretation: the underwriter must distinguish a risk problem, an evidence problem and a system-access problem. Treating all three as the same rejection would defeat the purpose of more precise assessment.

10.2 Income standards give concrete examples of structured judgment

Fannie's March 4 revision reorganized income requirements by source and introduced both policy changes and clarifications. It became mandatory for application dates on or after June 1, 2026. A relocated paragraph should not be mistaken for a new rule. SEL-2026-02

The revision reduced the standard base-income W-2 collection from two years to the most recent W-2 with a current paystub. Fannie's change comparison Current base-income policy also permits Form 1005, requires applicable employment verification, and distinguishes fixed pay from variable pay. Variable base income requires a 12-month history; declining income must have stabilized to qualify. A documented, nonrecurring interruption outside the borrower's control may be excluded from its calculation. B3-3.3-01, Base Income

Employment gaps in the last 12 months require analysis of whether current employment is likely to continue. Frequent job changes can coexist with reliable qualifying income. B3-3.2-02, Employment-Related Income

Illustration, not an observed borrower outcome: two applicants may show the same recent monthly receipts. One has guaranteed hours; the other has shrinking shifts. Classifying the income correctly changes the relevant calculation and supporting inquiry. Collecting fewer routine documents can coexist with careful interpretation of the facts that matter.

Where income has no defined expiration and the applicable history is documented, extra continuance evidence is generally unnecessary absent contrary information. If the lender cannot establish stability from the available evidence, the income must be removed and the file resubmitted. This makes sufficiency of evidence a substantive decision. B3-3.1-01, General Income Information

10.3 Validation can now divide a file more precisely

The April announcement set June 27, 2026 as the boundary for new casefiles using income validation by employer and income type, replacing the need to validate an income type across all employers. SEL-2026-03 The June integration specifications identify employer-specific validated and calculated amounts and revised findings messages. DU integration impact memo

Illustration: a borrower has income from two employers. Validating one employer's eligible income can provide relief for that component while the other still needs acceptable support. The administrative task becomes tracking exactly what was validated, what remains unresolved and which findings apply. The human-readable findings and the lender's software must preserve that distinction.

10.4 Quality control now delegates more process design to the lender

The April 1 changes removed the fixed 10% minimum for prefunding QC sampling, with implementation required for QC reviews conducted on or after July 1, 2026. They also reduced duplicate reverification and reporting requirements. This prefunding change must not be applied to the separate post-closing random-sampling rules. SEL-2026-03

The current prefunding guide requires a documented monthly selection process reflecting the lender's risk, channels, volume and products, with every production channel represented. Fannie can require adjustments or impose minimum samples. For validated income/assets, reviewers can omit recalculation but must check report identifiers, expiry and inconsistent information. D1-2-01, Prefunding QC

Post-closing rules remove specified repeat checks for approved automated verification and limit tax transcripts to returns required and relied upon for underwriting. Conditional DU validation relief also avoids specified reverification. Reconciliation remains required when evidence differs. D1-3-03, Post-Closing Reverifications

Our interpretation: judgment expands at management level in deciding where review resources belong; review quality cannot be assessed just by counting completed checklists.

10.5 Contractual error exposure influences the process

Fannie's representations and warranties are promises associated with delivery of a loan. Its limited waivers and enforcement relief specify where it will refrain from enforcing certain promises if conditions are met. This is contractual risk allocation, not a transfer of every legal duty or loss to Fannie.

For qualifying cases, undisclosed non-mortgage debt relief can protect against specified remedies, while known debts must still be identified and included and contradictions investigated. Mortgages, HELOCs and second liens are excluded. Validated components have their own conditions and closing deadlines. A2-2-04, Enforcement Relief

The non-mortgage liability feature dates to qualifying DU submissions/resubmissions from November 15, 2025; an April 2026 guide date does not establish its original launch. Undisclosed-liabilities operating FAQ

Analytical implication: reducing uncertainty about a later repurchase demand can reduce the incentive for duplicative defensive paperwork. Whether a particular lender changes its procedures requires evidence from that lender.

10.6 Score choice adds another operating decision

The July joint playbook permits participating lenders to choose a model per loan, requires consistent model use across its borrowers, retains tri-merge reporting and specifies separate pricing and delivery identification. Technology providers must support the specifications. The July document still describes limited participation; the September expansion and readiness gap discussed in section 2 remains applicable. Joint GSE playbook, interim phase and technology FAQs

Our inference is that model choice requires coordinated rules across report ordering, underwriting, pricing and loan delivery. These requirements do not establish that an individual underwriter may freely select any score or ignore an investor's instructions.

10.7 What to measure next

Location of work Useful evidence of the change
Borrower Document requests, repeated requests, consent steps and time resolving questions
Underwriter Time spent calculating, reviewing discrepancies, documenting exceptions and resubmitting
Lender management Sampling design, vendor monitoring, integration work and training
Quality control Duplicate checks avoided, material defects found, corrected data and repeat errors
Sale to investor Delivery exceptions, eligible relief, repurchase requests and contractual remedies

These are research measures, not established results. The evidence supports a redistribution of judgment and administrative duties. It does not yet quantify net cost, staffing needs or processing time across lenders. Score competition, income-policy revisions, validation enhancements and QC reform are related parts of the operating environment with separate authority and effective dates.
