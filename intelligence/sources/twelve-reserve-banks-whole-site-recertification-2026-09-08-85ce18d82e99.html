Delta boundary: after 2026-08-29 through the cutoff; older controlling documents admitted as role-map corrections
Scope: all twelve U.S. Federal Reserve Bank public sites plus the Board, OIG and Federal Reserve Financial Services control layer
Method: complete discoverable-site inventory where a sitemap or human site map existed, followed by a materiality-driven crawl of governance, bylaws, committee charters, minutes, financials, annual reports, OMWI/procurement, technology, payments, fiscal-agency, supervision, account, credit, cash, applications, leadership, archive and linked-PDF surfaces
Exclusions: foreign central banks; social posts; secondary reporting; astrology; private or authenticated systems; app or vault promotion

Question

What does each Reserve Bank actually do beneath the public-facing regional-research layer, what changed or remained operational through September 8, 2026, and what does that evidence do to the working “castration of the Fed” theory?

Bottom line

The deep pass materially changes the map. The twelve Banks are not merely regional economists attached to four national payment operators. They form a specialized internal operating network in which legal entity, balance-sheet booking, product leadership, application operation, technology hosting, procurement, customer service, risk support and Board oversight can sit in different places.

The newly recovered structure is denser than the headline pass showed:

  • Boston hosts System financial support and centralized accounting technology while operating FedNow and winding down the Main Street facility through a large private-vendor stack.
  • New York maintains Treasury’s primary operating account and a global official-dollar deposit, custody, gold and liquidity-services network in addition to SOMA and wholesale payments.
  • Philadelphia carries System collaboration, collateral-management and supervisory-data infrastructure.
  • Cleveland builds and maintains nationwide Treasury collections technology, including Pay.gov and Debit Gateway.
  • Richmond centralizes National IT, System procurement and FedCash currency technology.
  • Atlanta combines Check/FedACH operation, pension administration, national-business governance and cross-Bank emergency credit continuity.
  • Chicago combines electronic access with FRFS architecture, customer strategy, workforce, payments security and System credit-risk/collateral support.
  • St. Louis is the System–Treasury relationship and reimbursement nexus, a government-wide accounting node, and a national data/administrative-services Bank.
  • Minneapolis operates TreasuryDirect customer and technology support, a 24/7 System IT help desk and Systemwide payment-product testing.
  • Kansas City hosts System HR product strategy and important supervision-data/cloud functions while retaining a live institution-specific account gate.
  • Dallas hosts national credit-risk management, financial-management shared services, Treasury benefit-payment work and interdistrict Discount Window continuity.
  • San Francisco hosts FRFS and Statistics and Reserves, leads the Common Data Platform, houses FedCash leadership and acts for all Banks on major currency-equipment commitments.

This strengthens a narrower theory of functional unbundling and internal centralization. It does not establish abolition, Treasury absorption, private capture, loss of the dollar anchor, or removal of Federal Reserve settlement, account, credit, supervision or crisis functions.

The most defensible formulation is:

Ordinary routing, servicing, custody, execution, interfaces and technical components can move among private providers and specialist Reserve Banks while the Federal Reserve retains legal books, reserve balances, access conditions, discount credit, supervision, common standards, cyber recovery, fiscal-agency coordination and failure backstops. The daily Fed can become less visible while the operating and crisis Fed remains structurally dense.

What “whole site” means here

This is a bounded public-surface recertification, not a literal claim that every one of tens of thousands of research-article bodies was read. For each Bank the crawl began with robots.txt, sitemap or human-site-map inventory, archive indexes and current organizational routes. Every discoverable structural and operational lane was then inspected in depth, including linked controlling PDFs. Search/API/admin/authenticated routes, unpublished committee packets, redacted security material, vendor contracts and delayed minutes remain access gaps.

“Not found on reached official surfaces” therefore means exactly that. It does not mean “the Bank did nothing.” A sitemap lastmod, publication date, effective date, board vote, production start, accounting result and public disclosure date are treated as separate clocks.

Systemwide control layer found above the twelve Banks

1. Operating-circular modernization changed the common legal wrapper

Effective January 5, 2026, FRFS standardized terminology and consolidated cross-service terms into renamed Operating Circular 5, General Financial Service Provisions, while amending Operating Circulars 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8. Service-specific circulars prevail where they conflict with OC5. OC5 also permits a Reserve Bank to suspend, restrict or terminate an institution’s or service provider’s access for noncompliance or risk. FRFS modernization notice; summary of changes.

This is internal legal consolidation around still-separate Reserve Bank service relationships. It is not a statutory merger or transfer to a private operator.

2. Two fraud-data services are now operational

The FedNow Network Intelligence API launched for early adopters on April 28, 2026. It uses account-level patterns observed on FedNow to support a sender’s pre-payment risk decision; the sending institution still decides whether to proceed, hold or review. FRFS launch release.

Payee Name Verification is also offered as a current FedDetect service through FedLine Direct or FedLine Command. It compares a proposed payee name and routing/account number with twelve months of Reserve Bank transaction observations. Operating Circular 5 is explicit that the output is informational and does not stop or settle a payment. The public page says institutions can begin enrollment “today,” but no exact public launch date was located. PNV service page; OC5 Appendix E.

These services increase the Fed’s data-and-risk layer around transactions without transferring the customer’s decision or changing settlement finality.

3. The service perimeter both expands and contracts

In 2026 FRFS held FedNow and Accounting prices unchanged; changed Check, FedACH, FedCash, FedLine, Fedwire, NSS and Securities pricing; discontinued FedComplete packages; and added PNV fees. 2026 pricing summary.

FRFS also announced that FedGlobal ACH to Mexico and Panama and Foreign/Canadian Check services will end by year-end 2026, with forward-item cutoffs in November and December and customers directed toward alternatives. Discontinuation notice. This is genuine future service narrowing and private/other-provider handoff, but it does not end domestic FedACH, Check, reserve-account settlement or Reserve Bank authority.

The November 2026 Fedwire standards release was moved to November 2027 to maintain Swift interoperability. FRFS schedule notice. That is an adopted calendar change and a real external-dependency seam, not an outage or surrender of Fedwire authority.

4. Combined finances show asymmetric remittance recovery

The June 30, 2026 combined quarterly report was published August 28. The deferred asset tied to future Treasury remittances declined from $243.481 billion at December 31, 2025 to $235.498 billion. The Banks generated $14.071 billion in six-month net income before remittances, transferred $5.176 billion to Treasury, and reduced the deferred asset by $7.983 billion. The notes make clear that some Banks remitted while the combined deferred asset remained: separate-Bank results matter even inside a combined balance sheet. Combined quarterly reports; Q2 report.

The same report records $5.542 billion of six-month operating expenses, $271 million of service income, $461 million of reimbursable government-service income and $3.491 billion of net premises/equipment. In August, the Main Street vehicle sold its remaining participations for $516 million against about $582 million of principal and interest, implying about a $66 million third-quarter loss and leaving final wind-down work.

This does not show that Treasury “paid the Fed back.” The deferred asset is recovered from future Reserve Bank earnings; it is neither an ordinary payable due on demand nor one uniform Bank-by-Bank state.

5. OIG found live governance-control defects

On September 1, 2026 the OIG issued ten recommendations after contacting all twelve Banks. It found that Class C candidates were not uniformly prescreened or required to personally account for relevant family financial interests; only one Bank proactively provided the Board’s prohibited-holdings list during the search. Many Banks allowed Class A directors to participate in selecting Class C directors, potentially giving member banks undue influence. Board guidance also did not require disclosure of potential conflicts between executive candidates and outside search firms, and roles in selecting general auditors, senior supervisory officers and CFOs were insufficiently documented. The Board concurred. OIG report page; full report.

These are control weaknesses, not proof that a conflicted appointment occurred. They matter because the boards and executives govern the specialist functions mapped below.

Other ongoing OIG work expressly covers Reserve Bank president/FVP reappointments, bank-service-provider risk, examiner training and Federal Reserve System intelligence activity. OIG ongoing work. As of March 31, 2026, 63 Board recommendations remained open. Open recommendations.

6. Current public-budget transparency is stale

The Board’s public Bank-budget page still labels 2025 as current and was last updated October 31, 2025. Board Bank budgets. Current 2026 Bank and national-business-line budget detail was not publicly posted there, even though delayed local minutes show 2026 budgets being approved. This is a disclosure gap, not evidence that budgets were absent.

Corrected twelve-Bank operating map

1 — Boston: instant payments, accounting technology and crisis-facility administration

Current special role. Boston operates FedNow for the System and recognizes the Systemwide service revenue. Its 2025 audited statements also identify the Financial Support Office and Centralized Accounting Technology Services as major unreimbursed System services. Boston 2025 financial statements.

Boston remains managing member of MS Facilities 2020 LLC until closure. All Boston loans funding the facility were repaid by January 5, 2026, but the workout operation continued. The June 30 vendor report records $7.416 million of 2026 year-to-date vendor cost and $148.237 million cumulatively, including State Street, Guidehouse, PwC and FTI; FTI scopes were amended in March and April 2026. Main Street vendors; Q2 vendor-cost report.

The June 5, 2025 board minutes also approve a revised Inter-District Discount Window Backup resolution. Boston minutes.

Theory effect. Strong internal-centralization evidence and a clear public-vehicle/private-servicing case. It is also a counterweight to hollowing because instant settlement, accounting and crisis-tail administration remain inside a Reserve Bank.

Does not prove. Boston owns every FedNow application, vendor, production host, accounting policy or participant account; nor that private Main Street vendors held sovereign decision authority.

2 — New York: market execution, Treasury’s operating account and the global official-dollar node

Current special role. New York operates Fedwire Funds, Fedwire Securities and NSS; executes FOMC-directed SOMA activity; maintains the Treasury General Account, Treasury’s primary operating account; and provides deposit, payments, custody, settlement, investment, gold, FIMA repo and currency services to more than 200 foreign-official/international holders with more than 550 accounts. At year-end 2025 the TGA balance was $872.853 billion. New York 2025 financial statements; Central Bank and International Account Services.

The local Audit and Risk Committee must receive annual reports from FRFS and the System Chief Payments Executive, while the Management and Budget charter states that national-business-line budgets are approved by the Board of Governors. Audit and Risk charter; Management and Budget charter.

April 2025 minutes disclose development of a new Cash Services Center, but not its location, contractor, cost or completion clock. New York 2025 minutes.

Theory effect. Private dealers, CCPs and custodians can carry execution, but Treasury cash and the official international dollar network remain deeply embedded at New York. This is a major counterweight to a total-privatization reading.

Does not prove. New York independently sets Treasury or FOMC policy, owns customer funds, operates every custody component or controls FRFS as a whole.

3 — Philadelphia: collaboration, collateral and supervisory-data infrastructure

Current special role. Philadelphia’s current CIO title includes Information Technology Services and the Groupware Leadership Center. June 2025 board minutes show active briefings on National IT End User Services Digital Workplace—collaboration and AI—and the Securities Data Management Service; January minutes cover RADAR’s Bank and System functions. June minutes; January minutes.

Official lineage material identifies enterprise messaging/collaboration, Philadelphia-led Collateral Management System administration, collateral valuation/data functions, and RADAR securities/consumer-finance datasets used in supervision, CCAR and LISCC. 2022 OMWI report; 2018 officer release. Current leadership and 2025 minutes show institutional continuity, though the exact 2026 service catalog is not public.

The 2025 minutes also disclose live primary-credit use rising from 17 institutions in January to 25 in July before ending the year at 21; names and amounts are not public.

Theory effect. Philadelphia is a live System infrastructure and supervisory-data node, not a research shell. This supports distributed specialist execution beneath centralized policy.

Does not prove. Current application ownership, vendor/cloud identity, source-code control, production hosting or exact AI deployment.

4 — Cleveland: nationwide Treasury collections technology and cross-district cash

Current special role. Cleveland says Treasury Services designs, builds and maintains nationwide Fiscal Service products. Its disclosed stack includes product and business architecture, software development, DevOps, QA, site-reliability engineering, application security/fraud, incident response, settlement and operations. It reports approximately 245 million Pay.gov transactions exceeding $260 billion and 280 million Debit Gateway transactions exceeding $775 billion. Cleveland Treasury Services.

Cleveland also operates cash in Cleveland and Cincinnati plus depots in Pittsburgh, Buffalo and Louisville. Cash Services.

Its current bylaws draw an unusually sharp legal line: supervision and regulation are not formally a responsibility of the Bank; employees perform them under Board delegation and directors are excluded. Cleveland bylaws. The March 2026 Operations & Resource Committee charter separately requires review of material National IT/FRFS services and significant third parties. Committee charter.

Theory effect. Strong evidence that public Treasury collections infrastructure remains inside a specialist Reserve Bank while legal supervisory authority remains centralized.

Does not prove. Cleveland owns Treasury funds, decides fiscal policy, controls every historical application or vendor, or holds final settlement authority for every transaction.

5 — Richmond: System IT, procurement and cash technology

Current special role. Richmond hosts National IT and the System CIO/CISO structure. Its board approves the National IT budget after committee review and coordinates with the IT Oversight Committee. National IT Committee charter; 2025 minutes.

The current leadership map adds two omitted national roles: the National Procurement Office, which leads enterprise-wide sourcing and strategic procurement, and FedCash Currency Technology and Support, including the NextGen high-speed currency-processing program with the Board, BEP and Secret Service. Christin Patel; Niranjan Mowli.

Richmond’s July 23, 2026 bylaws exclude the System CIO from the president’s ordinary unilateral officer-employment authority and preserve a dual local/System governance chain. Richmond bylaws.

Theory effect. Shared technology and supply-chain control are centralized inside the Reserve Bank federation. Richmond is more than a server host but less than a technology sovereign.

Does not prove. Unilateral Richmond authority, complete vendor control, ownership of every application, ITOC reserved matters or account/service liability.

6 — Atlanta: retail rails, pension administration and emergency federation

Current special role. Atlanta operates Check and FedACH for all Banks and recognizes the Systemwide revenue; it also sponsors and accounts for the System pension plan through the Office of Employee Benefits. The 2025 statements record $256 million of service income, $72 million of reimbursable government-service income and a $1.119 billion net pension asset. Atlanta 2025 financial statements.

The Executive Committee charter covers nationally assigned businesses. Delayed 2025 minutes show board-level reporting on FRFS, System IT strategy, security, resilience, cloud, AI, cash modernization and OEB vendor risk. Most consequentially, the December minutes approved a mechanism allowing a “Buddy” Reserve Bank to extend credit and perform Credit and Risk Management functions during an emergency. Atlanta first-half minutes; second-half minutes.

The presidential search remains open; Allen Stanley has served as interim head of Supervision and Regulation since April 15, 2026. Presidential search; Allen Stanley.

Theory effect. A strong example of operational portability across separately incorporated Banks without merger. National retail and pension operations remain substantively inside the System.

Does not prove. Permanent authority transfer, Atlanta ownership of all retail-payment software, identification of the Buddy Bank, activation of the contingency or independent emergency-lending policy.

7 — Chicago: electronic access, FRFS architecture and shared credit-risk support

Current special role. Chicago operates electronic access/FedLine and recognizes System revenue, but its current role is much broader. The Chicago-based Credit Risk Management Support Office coordinates all twelve Banks’ credit-risk-management support for discount-window lending and intraday credit, including collateral data, technology and valuation support. Deborah Leonard.

Chicago also houses FRFS architecture and technology strategy, customer and industry relations, marketing, workforce strategy for roughly 1,800 FRFS employees, and System payments-security leadership. Pat Adler; Erik Van Bramer; Nokihomis Willis; Todd Aadland.

The System Activities, Bank Operations and Risk Committee receives annual FRFS and Chief Payments Executive reporting and reviews System-assigned responsibilities and material work performed by or for Chicago. SABOR charter.

Theory effect. A national payments enterprise is being constructed through distributed Reserve Bank staff. This supports internal modularization, not district disappearance.

Does not prove. Chicago makes other Banks’ lending decisions, owns collateral or every FedLine asset, sets service prices independently or controls final settlement.

8 — St. Louis: Treasury relationship, government accounting and public data

Current special role. St. Louis hosts the Treasury Relations and Support Office, which manages the Systemwide Treasury relationship and coordinates Reserve Bank commitments and technology strategy. Its Central Accounting Reporting System is described as Treasury’s system of record and processed nearly 15 million government transactions totaling trillions of dollars in FY2025. 2025 annual-report Treasury page.

The 2025 audited statements identify TRSO, the Subcommittee on Administrative Services Technology Center and the Center for Learning Innovation as major System services. St. Louis receives the other Banks’ Treasury reimbursement receivables and seeks reimbursement on their behalf; reimbursable government services were $256 million in 2025. St. Louis 2025 financial statements.

The July 16, 2026 bylaws allow another Reserve Bank to extend credit on St. Louis’s behalf and on St. Louis’s books during contingency conditions. St. Louis bylaws. This is unusually clear evidence that legal booking and operational performance can be separated without extinguishing the originating Bank.

FRED, FRASER, CASSIDI and the Bank On National Data Hub add national macroeconomic, historical, merger-analysis and banking-access data functions.

Theory effect. One of the strongest examples of specialist concentration inside the System and of functional unbundling without legal-role extinction.

Does not prove. St. Louis controls Treasury policy or appropriations, owns every Treasury application, or that its contingency authority is active.

9 — Minneapolis: TreasuryDirect, national IT support and transaction-product testing

Current special role. Minneapolis Treasury Services supports TreasuryDirect customers buying and redeeming marketable securities and savings bonds through customer service, fulfillment, claims processing and Treasury-system technology support. Financial Services; Treasury Services leadership release.

The Bank also describes a round-the-clock Systemwide IT help desk, and Minneapolis-based FRFS leadership runs Systemwide teams testing transaction products and services. Job profiles; Daniel From appointment.

The December 2025 Strategy and Risk Committee charter covers System activities, risks originating at or passing through other Banks and operations supporting extensions of credit. Strategy and Risk charter. March 2026 minutes separately approve the general Bank and S&R portions of the budget after ineligible directors leave. March minutes.

Theory effect. Minneapolis is not merely a billing node; it is a Treasury, IT-support and payment-testing specialist within the public System.

Does not prove. Control of Treasury issuance policy, ownership of a national rail or application, or power to make other Banks’ settlement decisions.

10 — Kansas City: account gate, HR product strategy and supervision-data cloud

Current special role. Kansas City’s March 4, 2026 Kraken Financial decision proves a one-year, tailored Tier 3 account and states that an individual Reserve Bank decides access based on applicant-specific facts and risks. The public release does not itself disclose the particular services and restrictions; those details remain confidential. Kansas City release.

The 2025 financial statements identify the Human Resources Product Strategy Center as a major unreimbursed System service. Kansas City 2025 financial statements. Current staff material also identifies Kansas City data-management support for Shared National Credit and accountable-change responsibility for the System Common Examination Platform and Common Data Platform cloud initiatives. Daniel Harbour; Shared National Credit.

The 2026 Jackson Hole symposium remains an agenda-setting and archival role; its theme was financial innovation, payments and policy. It is not an implementation authority. 2026 symposium.

Theory effect. Local conditional access coexists with centralized policy and cloud/data specialization. The public source supports a limited account, but not the earlier shorthand that all detailed restrictions were disclosed in the release.

Does not prove. A general new account class, Kraken exchange access, production use, independent national policy, sole cloud operation or System HR policymaking.

11 — Dallas: national credit risk, shared finance and Treasury benefit-payment work

Current special role. Dallas’s 2025 audited statements identify national credit risk management as a major unreimbursed System service. Dallas 2025 financial statements. The CFO/Risk Officer portfolio also includes the Financial Management Shared Services Center. Jeff Garrett.

June 2025 minutes say Dallas staff lead System efforts in Cash and Information Technology. April minutes approve an Inter-District Discount Window Operations Contingency Plan; the counterparties, triggers and liability allocation remain nonpublic. June minutes; April minutes.

Dallas Treasury Operations supports conversion of federal-benefit recipients from checks to direct deposit. Heidi Mitchell. Its supplier material also confirms use of System contracts negotiated by Richmond’s National Procurement Office. Dallas supplier information.

Fresh personnel delta. Benjamin Klein became vice president and deputy general counsel effective August 31, with responsibility for legal and ethics work; his prior portfolio included resilience, third-party risk and cloud matters. Dallas release.

Theory effect. Dallas is a live risk, continuity, finance and Treasury node as well as a public design/research shop.

Does not prove. Ownership of all Reserve Bank credit exposure, independent Discount Window policy, sole Cash/IT control, benefit-eligibility authority or private capture.

12 — San Francisco: FRFS host, reserves/statistics, common data and currency equipment

Current special role. San Francisco’s 2025 statements identify Federal Reserve Financial Services and Statistics and Reserves as major unreimbursed System services. They also show San Francisco, acting for all Banks, carrying $260 million of fixed unconditional commitments through 2034 for currency-processing-equipment maintenance and development. San Francisco 2025 financial statements.

Mark Gould, based at San Francisco, leads the full retail, wholesale and cash portfolio through teams spread across all twelve Banks. Mark Gould. Kathleen Young is Chief FedCash Services executive. Leadership directory.

Louise Willard leads the System Common Data Platform, San Francisco cloud transformation and coordination with the System CIO, district CIOs and FRFS Technology. Louise Willard. January and July 2025 minutes show board reporting on cloud migration, the data platform and System IT priorities. January minutes; July minutes.

Theory effect. A district Bank can be the corporate/accounting host for enterprise leadership, data infrastructure and all-Bank equipment commitments without becoming the legal operator of every rail.

Does not prove. Completion of cloud migration, a particular cloud vendor, ownership of every data object or cash site, or unilateral San Francisco control over the other Banks.

What the recertification does to the theory

Strengthened

  • Internal functional unbundling: legal booking, operations, technology, contracting, staffing and governance are demonstrably separable.
  • Common-machine centralization: FRFS, National IT, national procurement, common data/cloud platforms, shared-service offices and cross-Bank contingency plans reduce the explanatory power of a purely regional map.
  • Private ordinary execution/public tail risk: Main Street servicing/workout is the cleanest documented example; dealer/CCP, custody, identity and fraud-tool dependencies add narrower examples.
  • Opacity through specialization: no public application-level RACI connects product owner, software owner, production host, contract signer, incident commander, settlement Bank and participant remedy across the major services.

Weakened

  • A claim that the twelve Banks have become ceremonial shells.
  • A claim that private rails have displaced Reserve Bank accounts, final settlement, discount credit, fiscal agency, supervision, cyber recovery or physical currency.
  • A claim that the Banks are acting as twelve independent national central banks.
  • A claim that technology leadership or hosting equals legal ownership or sovereign policy authority.

Strongest rival explanation

The same facts can be explained as ordinary modernization of a federated public system: functions are concentrated to gain scale, professional specialization, common security and resilience while separate Banks retain legal books, local boards, district supervision, lending interfaces and account relationships.

Exact evidence that would strengthen the theory further

  • Named private providers gaining operating decision rights over high-volume routing, settlement, collateral, account access or participant remedy.
  • Service-agreement or contract text shifting finality, legal liability, emergency authority or failure losses outside the System.
  • Persistent Reserve Bank service-volume, revenue, staff or budget decline paired with private substitution.
  • Proof that local boards cannot see, review or contest material shared-service and third-party decisions charged to their Banks.
  • Crisis evidence in which private actors retain ordinary gains while losses reliably return to Reserve Bank/Treasury support.

Exact evidence that would falsify or sharply narrow it

  • Public application-level RACIs and reserved-matter schedules keeping accountable control inside the System.
  • Strong Fed-operated adoption, uptime, recovery, correction and participant-remedy evidence.
  • Transparent district participation in national-service budgets, vendor decisions and incident governance.
  • Private/tokenized systems remaining interfaces while Reserve Bank accounts, finality, collateral, liquidity and failure authority stay decisive.

Highest-value unresolved gaps

  1. FRFS Enterprise Principles, voting rules, reserved matters and contractor-Bank assignment schedule.
  2. Application-level RACI, contracts, SLAs, chargebacks and incident/remedy chains for FedNow, Fedwire, NSS, FedACH, Check, FedLine, PNV, Network Intelligence, CMS, RADAR, CARS, Structure Central, Common Data Platform and TreasuryDirect.
  3. ITOC charter, roster, votes, exceptions and its allocation of authority with Richmond, FRFS and local boards.
  4. 2026 Reserve Bank and national-business-line budgets; the Board’s public page remains at 2025.
  5. Delayed 2026 board minutes and the underlying committee packets, national-service reports and strategic-project evaluations.
  6. Buddy/backup/interdistrict Discount Window instruments: named counterparties, activation triggers, booking, collateral custody, liability and termination.
  7. Critical vendor registers, beneficial ownership, subcontractors, cloud providers, software ownership, security assessments and performance remedies.
  8. The exact launch clock, adoption, error rates, false positives, overrides and customer remedies for PNV and the FedNow Network Intelligence API.
  9. FedGlobal/foreign-check discontinuation migration outcomes and whether replacement providers change settlement, access, cost or remedy.
  10. Bank-by-Bank deferred-remittance recovery and the remaining Main Street wind-down result after the August sale.
  11. The next Board master-account database publication, Kraken’s actual term start/use/renewal and the authoritative source for any nonpublic account restrictions.
  12. New York’s Cash Services Center scope and San Francisco’s currency-equipment counterparties and milestones.
