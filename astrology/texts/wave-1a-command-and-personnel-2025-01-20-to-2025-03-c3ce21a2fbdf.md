# Wave 1A — Command and Personnel

**Research window:** January 20 through March 19, 2025.  
**Question:** How did the incoming regime begin changing the federal organism before most reorganizations, removals or operational outcomes could mature?  
**Finding:** It installed three linked command rooms and made the workforce increasingly legible to them. The early state change was less “the agencies disappeared” than “the new center could ask who occupied the chairs, stop or approve entry, demand future-state maps, reclassify policy-bearing positions, and prepare reductions.”

This is a factual and archetypal working synthesis. It does not claim that an ingress caused an order, that a lunation proves an outcome, or that a thematic fit can be pinned to the exact day.

## The chain in one view

```text
presidential doctrine
    ↓
three coordinating rooms
    ↓
inventories, reporting, approval and access gates
    ↓
voluntary-exit and performance machinery
    ↓
future-state organization charts and RIF plans
    ↓
later conversion, litigation, separation and implementation clocks
```

The three rooms were:

1. **National-security room:** NSPM-1 made the NSC and Homeland Security Council the principal policy mechanism, joined them through one staff and Executive Secretary, and routed work through Principals, Deputies and Policy Coordination Committees.
2. **Civil-workforce room:** the White House Domestic Policy Council, OMB and OPM set hiring, classification, performance, resignation, reorganization and reporting gates.
3. **Embedded technology room:** the former U.S. Digital Service was reflagged as the U.S. DOGE Service inside EOP, with agency DOGE teams intended to work beside agency heads and obtain lawful access to unclassified systems.

The rooms did not erase the departments' organic statutes. They made personnel, records and procedure the first surface on which the new regime could act across many still-existing entities.

## 1A.1 — January 20–27: command rooms and classification seeds

### January 20 — the command structure is named

**Mover.** The President; incoming EOP officials; Matthew Vaeth as acting OMB Director; Charles Ezell as acting OPM Director; Stephen Ehikian at GSA; White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles. The Senate did not confirm Russell Vought as OMB Director until February 6.

**Institutional change.** Several instruments landed together:

- [NSPM-1](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/organization-of-the-national-security-council-and-subcommittees/) organized a fused NSC/HSC policy ladder with one staff and Executive Secretary.
- The regulatory freeze placed pending, unpublished and selected recently published rules behind presidential-appointee and OIRA review.
- EO 14158 reflagged the U.S. Digital Service as the U.S. DOGE Service inside EOP, created a temporary organization inside it, and directed agency heads to establish DOGE teams.
- EO 14170 ordered a government-wide hiring plan; EO 14171 created the Schedule Policy/Career classification but listed no positions.
- The [Career SES memorandum](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/restoring-accountability-for-career-senior-executives/) began changing how the senior career layer would be evaluated and assigned.
- The [designation of acting leaders](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/01/designation-of-acting-leaders/) made the first operating carriers identifiable.

**Maturity.** The command rooms and legal directions were effective. Most downstream agency teams, classifications, reorganizations and terminations were not yet proved.

**Counterevidence and limit.** Agency heads retained the legal authorities assigned by statute. DOGE assistance or system access did not automatically transfer agency decision rights. Schedule Policy/Career did not yet contain a list, fire an employee or repeal merit hiring. A rescinded prior order did not by itself unwind every contract, grant, position or statutory duty previously operating beneath it.

**Plotline.** The control plane first moves through coordination and observability: who may review, who must report, and which objects can be seen before they are changed.

### January 20–22 — the hiring freeze becomes an executable gate

**Mover.** Vaeth and Ezell, with OPM's Amanda Scales named as the approval contact.

**Institutional change.** The [OMB–OPM hiring-freeze guidance](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/transmittals/2025/OMB-OPM%20Federal%20Civilian%20Hiring%20Freeze%20Guidance%201-20-2025%20FINAL.pdf) required agencies to remove or suspend job postings, certify compliance on a short clock, and seek approval before reinstating covered announcements. A broad presidential direction thus became an operational workflow in USAJOBS and agency HR offices.

**Maturity.** Operating administrative gate.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The guidance contained exemptions and did not abolish the civil service or every open position. Agency and OPM approvals remained part of the chain.

**Handoff.** The entry gate becomes the precursor to the February reorganization-and-RIF planning regime: stop inflow, inventory the workforce, then describe the proposed future state.

### January 21–24 — DEIA direction becomes inventories, leave and RIF preparation

**Mover.** OPM, OMB and agency heads; Amanda Scales appears again as the implementation contact.

**Institutional change.** The [January 21 implementation memorandum](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-PM-PURL-gpo237068/pdf/GOVPUB-PM-PURL-gpo237068.pdf) translated EO 14151 into dated tasks: office closure and administrative leave, public-media changes, contract and training review, employee and office inventories, and a January 31 RIF plan. The [January 24 RIF guidance](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/latest-and-other-highlighted-memos/guidance-regarding-rifs-of-deia-offices.pdf) told agencies to begin the notice machinery.

**Maturity.** Direction became a records-and-personnel workflow; individual outcomes remained agency- and position-specific.

**Counterevidence and limit.** Later [February 5 guidance](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/transmittals/2025/OPM%20Memo%20Further%20Guidance%20Regarding%20Ending%20DEIA%20Offices%20Programs%20and%20Initiatives%202-5-2025%20FINAL.pdf) expressly allowed statutory functions to migrate and required sufficient staffing for disability and accessibility obligations. Deleting or renaming an office was therefore not the same thing as eliminating every underlying legal function.

**Plotline.** The name and organizational box are pressured first; surviving statutory functions may be moved into a new carrier.

### January 27 — Schedule Policy/Career becomes a petition process, not yet a populated class

**Mover.** OPM and agency human-capital officers.

**Institutional change.** The [January 27 guidance](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/transmittals/2025/OPM%20Memorandum%20re%20Schedule%20Policy%20Career%20Guidance%20FINAL%27.pdf) required agency points of contact and petitions identifying policy-influencing positions. It made the classification seed administratively actionable.

**Maturity.** Identification and petition stage.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The memorandum preserved career status and prohibited partisan patronage. No 2025 petition list should be mistaken for the later 2026 appendix, and no classification alone proves a removal.

**Handoff.** This seed does not acquire its broad public list until EO 14410 in the 2026 Aries wave. Its long arc is classification → petition → rule/litigation → listed position → agency record change → only then any individual personnel action.

## 1A.2 — January 28–February 7: departure, senior control and data

### January 28–February 12 — the deferred-resignation program opens an exit lane

**Mover.** OPM, with agencies required to report participation.

**Institutional change.** The [January 28 memorandum](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/transmittals/2025/OPM%20Guidance%20Memo%20re%20Deferred%20Resignation%20Program%2001-28-25%20FINAL.pdf) offered eligible employees a deferred resignation effective September 30, generally with paid administrative leave and agency authority to reassign or eliminate duties. OPM's [Fork page](https://www.opm.gov/about-us/fork/) records the program's closing clock; OPM also published a [legality memorandum](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/transmittals/2025/OPM%20Memo%20Legality%20of%20Deferred%20Resignation%20Program%202-4-2025%20FINAL.pdf).

**Maturity.** A real offer and participation workflow, not yet the later separation event.

**Counterevidence and limit.** It was described as voluntary; acceptance totals and later OPM workforce summaries use differing definitions and must be reconciled before a definitive number is carried into the Spine. A resignation accepted in this chapter should not be dated as an organizational vacancy until its actual separation clock.

**Plotline.** The regime opens a consent-based personnel off-ramp while simultaneously preparing coercive reorganization machinery.

### February 4–6 — CIO, SES and performance records are recast as policy control surfaces

**Mover.** OPM, agency heads and new political leadership.

**Institutional change.** Three memoranda made the senior administrative layer more visible and more explicitly policy-bearing:

- [February 4 CIO guidance](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/transmittals/2025/OPM%20Memo%20Guidance%20Regarding%20Redesignating%20SES%20CIO%20Positions%202-4-2025%20FINAL.pdf) asked agencies to redesignate covered CIO positions from career-reserved to general positions.
- [February 5 SES-integrity guidance](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/transmittals/2025/OPM%20Memo%20Maintaining%20the%20Integrity%20of%20the%20Career%20Senior%20Executive%20Service%202-5-2025%20final.pdf) required review of positions moved in the opposite direction since 2021.
- [February 6 performance-data guidance](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/transmittals/2025/OPM%20Memo%20Request%20for%20Agency%20Performance%20Management%20Data%202-6-2025%20FINAL.pdf) requested records on low ratings, performance-improvement plans, actions, appeals and perceived barriers.

**Maturity.** Review, redesignation requests and an information return. These are genuine control-plane operations but not proof that every requested position changed class or that every low-rated employee was removed.

**Counterevidence and limit.** The SES remained a statutory personnel system with career-reserved requirements, appeals and prohibited-personnel-practice protections. The CIO instruction is especially important because it shows that control of systems, procurement, privacy, cybersecurity and AI was being treated as policy authority; it does not prove that a new central actor lawfully accessed every system.

**Plotline.** Information infrastructure and human-capital governance begin to fuse: the people who control records and systems are themselves reclassified as part of the policy perimeter.

## 1A.3 — February 11–March 19: the future-state machine

### February 11 — EO 14210 defines the reduction architecture

**Mover.** The President, OMB, OPM, agency heads and embedded DOGE teams.

**Institutional change.** EO 14210 directed a one-for-four hiring plan, DOGE-team consultation on career hires, monthly workforce reporting, and preparations for large-scale reductions in force. It also required agencies to identify whether components or functions could be eliminated or combined and to report their statutory bases.

**Maturity.** Authorized planning and a recurring reporting chamber. It did not itself execute a government-wide RIF.

**Counterevidence and limit.** Agencies still had to follow applicable statutes and personnel procedures. A target ratio is not a measured staffing outcome. A list of functions proposed for consolidation is not a consummated transfer.

### February 24–March 6 — the policy perimeter expands through SES, appraisal and HR

**Mover.** OPM, agency heads, Executive Resources Boards, Performance Review Boards and chief human-capital officers.

**Institutional change.** The [February 24 SES memorandum](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/transmittals/2025/Guidance%20on%20Career%20Reserved%20SES%20Positions%20and%20Agency%20Redesignation%20Requests%20FINAL.pdf) requested new redesignation packages. The [February 25 appraisal memorandum](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/transmittals/2025/New%20Senior%20Executive%20Service%20Performance%20Appraisal%20System%20and%20Performance%20Plan%2C%20and%20Guidance%20on%20Next%20Steps%20for%20Agencies%20to%20Implement%20Restoring%20Accountability%20for%20Career%20Senior%20Executives%20FINAL.pdf) required agencies to reconstitute review boards and prepared a new FY2026 appraisal system. The [March 6 CHCO memorandum](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/latest-memos/guidance-regarding-redesignating-chco-positions.pdf) applied the same policy-bearing logic to the senior HR layer.

**Maturity.** Board composition, reporting and redesignation machinery became operative. The new appraisal system's stated effective clock was October 1, 2025, outside this wave.

**Counterevidence and limit.** A future effective date must remain future. Reconstituting boards changed who participated in review; it did not itself determine every executive's rating or removal.

### February 26–March 13 — ARRPs convert the doctrine into agency blueprints

**Mover.** Confirmed OMB Director Russell Vought, OPM Director Ezell, agency heads and DOGE team leads.

**Institutional change.** The [February 26 Agency RIF and Reorganization Plan memorandum](https://www.opm.gov/chcoc/transmittals/2025/OPM_OMB%20-%20guidance%20on%20DOGE%20workforce%20EO%202.26.25%20FINAL.pdf) required a first-phase submission by March 13 and a second phase in April. The packages were to contain future-state organization charts, proposed function or component absorption, workforce data, direct-service justification and statutory constraints, followed by recurring progress reports.

**Maturity.** This is the point at which a general reduction thesis became an executable planning object inside each agency. It was still a blueprint, not a completed reorganization.

**Counterevidence and contest.** Later litigation supplies the most important factual limit. A May 9 district-court order described the chain from ARRP submission through approval, notices, leave and termination and found that RIFs had begun in several agencies; that is later operating evidence, not a fact to backdate into March. The Supreme Court later stayed the broad injunction and said the Executive Order and memorandum were likely lawful, while expressly declining to decide the legality of any particular ARRP. The architecture could proceed; each execution still had its own statutory and judicial clock.

Primary court records: [N.D. California TRO](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-cand-3_25-cv-03698/pdf/USCOURTS-cand-3_25-cv-03698-0.pdf), [Trump v. AFGE docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/24A1174.html), and [Supreme Court stay](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/24a1174_h3ci.pdf).

## What had changed by March 19

- A fused national-security policy ladder had been named.
- OMB and OPM could require government-wide personnel returns on short clocks.
- Hiring announcements, exemptions and selected reinstatements had acquired new approval gates.
- DEIA offices and positions were inventoried and placed into closure/RIF workflows, while some statutory functions explicitly survived by migration.
- The voluntary deferred-resignation lane was open.
- CIO, SES and CHCO roles were being reviewed as policy-bearing positions.
- Agencies had begun producing future-state organization charts and RIF/reorganization plans.
- DOGE teams were designed as embedded cross-agency adapters, though a complete public census of teams and actual system access was not established.

## What had not changed merely because these objects existed

- Departments and statutory bodies did not cease to be legal persons.
- DOGE did not become a Cabinet department or inherit every agency head's authority.
- Schedule Policy/Career had not yet populated a public government-wide position list.
- A plan, petition, inventory, ratio or redesignation request was not a firing.
- A deferred resignation was not yet a September separation.
- A future appraisal system was not yet effective.
- System-access language did not repeal privacy, tax, program-specific disclosure or records law.
- Later court stays did not adjudicate every agency plan on the merits.
