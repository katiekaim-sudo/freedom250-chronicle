Wave 4B — Trade Terms, Exception Books and Refunds

Research window: Exact 2025 Libra ingress through exact 2025 Capricorn ingress.
Evidence cutoff: June 20, 2026, 11:59 p.m. EDT. Later facts are quarantined as handoffs.
Question: When the Libra-period trade orders announced accommodation with China and narrower tariff scope for agricultural products and Brazil, where did the bargain actually acquire legal and operational body?
Finding: The relationship became real in layers. EO 14357 lowered the China fentanyl tariff from 20 to 10 percent. EO 14358 preserved the 10-percent reciprocal baseline while continuing suspension of the higher rate and naming Chinese commitments. EO 14360 and EO 14361 then converted broad political accommodation into large, executable HTS exception books, retroactive entry clocks and ordinary CBP refund paths. The books changed customs treatment; they did not prove that every negotiated commitment was performed or disclose the money ledger. USDA records show substantial soybean sales resumed, but the contemporaneous official record located here did not establish fulfillment of the entire 12-million-metric-ton commitment. In February 2026, the Supreme Court removed IEEPA as a tariff authority and EO 14389 terminated the relevant duties, so the operating chassis was later reversed even though the underlying relationship, product classifications and separate trade authorities survived.

The packet's ladder is:

counterparty bargain → presidential tariff term → HTS exception book → ACE/CBP entry treatment → refund eligibility → performance record → judicial survival or authority migration

4B.1 — November 4: China receives two coupled but nonidentical terms

EO 14357 reduces the additional duty imposed under the China fentanyl emergency from 20 percent to 10 percent, effective for goods entered or withdrawn for consumption at 12:01 a.m. EST on November 10. It attributes that change to Chinese commitments to stop shipment of designated chemicals to North America and control exports of other chemicals globally. Homeland Security, consulting the Secretary of State, is assigned continuing monitoring. The President reserves the ability to modify the order if China fails to perform.

That is an operative rate change with a conditional re-escalation gate. It is not a finding that the synthetic-opioid supply chain was ended.

EO 14358 carries a different tariff stack. Following the October 30 Trump–Xi meeting and the Kuala Lumpur arrangement, it continues suspension of the heightened reciprocal rate while leaving the 10-percent baseline in force through November 10, 2026. The United States said China committed to actions involving:

  • rare-earth and other critical-mineral export controls;
  • fentanyl precursor shipments;
  • retaliatory tariffs and other countermeasures announced since March 4;
  • agricultural purchases, including at least 12 million metric tons of U.S. soybeans in the last two months of 2025 and at least 25 million annually in 2026–2028;
  • semiconductor-related investigations and the flow of Nexperia products; and
  • other market-access and commercial measures.

The U.S. side also announced pauses or extensions concerning Section 301 exclusions, the affiliated-entity rule and shipbuilding-related measures. Treasury, Commerce and USTR were assigned monitoring roles.

These terms should not be collapsed into one “China tariff.” The fentanyl duty and reciprocal-tariff schedule had different predicates, instruments and monitoring questions. Nor should the published bargain be mistaken for bilateral performance. The executive order can change the U.S. rate. It cannot itself cause a Chinese exporter, ministry, state buyer or customs authority to perform every promised act.

Some transaction evidence appears, but the headline commitment remains larger

The USDA Foreign Agricultural Service reported that China resumed U.S. soybean purchases in late October after no purchases since May. Its late-November estimate was approximately 2.9 million metric tons ordered, including volume reported to USDA as an unknown destination that industry believed was China-bound. USDA's December outlook reported more than 3 million metric tons in commitments and flash sales through December 8, while actual September–November shipments to China remained below 0.1 million metric tons. A January transportation report later identified the first post-deal shipment—49,000 metric tons on November 29—and another 794,400 metric tons inspected for export through December 25.

That is real transaction and logistics evidence. It does not equal the full 12-million-metric-ton commitment, and sales, inspections, loadings, arrivals and final payment are separate clocks. A 2026 USDA attaché report described storage auctions as preparation for the anticipated arrival of the committed volume; it did not supply a closed fulfillment ledger.

Maturity. Operative U.S. tariff concessions, resumed private sales and initial physical shipments; incomplete public proof of the entire bilateral performance package.

Counterevidence and limit. China continued buying from other origins, and price competitiveness remained material. A product sale is not proof that export-control, precursor, semiconductor and retaliation commitments were all implemented. Conversely, absence of a single public omnibus ledger is not proof of nonperformance.

4B.2 — November 14: agricultural accommodation becomes a global product book

EO 14360 does not negotiate with only one country. It changes the global reciprocal-tariff scope after considering trade negotiations, domestic demand and domestic production capacity.

Its decisive artifact is the annex. The locally preserved canonical Annex II contains 1,087 eight-digit HTS rows, of which 248 are tagged as additions in the machine-readable comparison. The order makes the changes effective for goods entered or withdrawn for consumption at 12:01 a.m. EST on November 13—one day before signature—and directs refunds under ordinary law and CBP procedure.

The institutional mechanism is therefore not “food gets cheaper” or “farm trade wins.” It is:

presidential scope decision → annexed HTS code → importer classification → ACE entry → duty treatment → protest/refund process

The product code is the gate. An importer must still establish classification, origin, entry timing and any other applicable condition. Another duty imposed under a different authority can remain even when the reciprocal-tariff exception applies.

Maturity. Executable product-classification book with a retroactive entry clock and a refund path.

Counterevidence and limit. The order contains no entry-level register of importer savings, refunds paid, consumer-price transmission or domestic-producer effects. “Eligible for refund” is not “refund settled.” The annex changes legal treatment without resolving who ultimately bears or receives the economic benefit.

4B.3 — November 20: Brazil receives a separate exception book on a lunar boundary

EO 14361 follows an October 6 call between Presidents Trump and Lula and what the order describes as initial negotiating progress. It narrows the product scope of the additional 40-percent Brazil duty; it does not repeal the Brazil emergency or remove the whole tariff.

The updated Brazil Annex I contains 947 eight-digit HTS rows, compared with 694 in the original local book. The canonical CSV identifies 249 addition-tagged rows. Like the agricultural order, it applies retroactively to entries at or after 12:01 a.m. EST on November 13 and uses standard CBP refund procedure.

The Brazil and global agricultural books remain separate legal objects. Product overlap does not merge their authorities, predicates or entry tests. A Brazilian product may need to be tested across origin, classification, the Brazil-specific scope, the reciprocal-tariff exception book and any other surviving duty.

The signing-time gap matters

EO 14361 is dated November 20. The Scorpio New Moon occurred that day at 1:47 a.m. EST, but the public order supplies no signing time. It therefore cannot be assigned strictly to the closing Libra New Moon chapter or the opening Scorpio New Moon chapter.

This is not an astrological inconvenience to be repaired by assumption. It is a valid boundary object. Civil-date-inclusive views may show it in the Scorpio chapter, while the strict chronology must preserve unresolved membership.

Maturity. Operative, Brazil-specific product exception book and refund eligibility.

Counterevidence and limit. No public packet-level register identifies the affected entries, protests, refunds, importer savings or Brazilian export response. Negotiating progress is the stated predicate; it is not a complete bilateral settlement.

4B.4 — February 20, 2026: the IEEPA tariff body is severed

The Libra orders became administratively executable, but the authority clock did not stay still. In Learning Resources v. Trump, the Supreme Court held on February 20, 2026 that IEEPA does not authorize the President to impose tariffs. EO 14389 then terminated the listed IEEPA additional ad valorem duties as soon as practicable.

That later event does not make the November books fictional. For several months they were the operative instructions used by the customs system. It does mean the wave's tariff carrier did not survive intact. The legal state changed again:

IEEPA tariff authority claimed → rates and exceptions operationalized → judicial authority failure → duties terminated / customs system retuned

Other authorities such as Sections 232 and 301 were not erased by the decision, and product classifications remained meaningful. The bargain and its commercial consequences must therefore be separated from the life of this particular tariff chassis.

No complete public collection-and-refund ledger was located. The correct closing state is operative, later reversed, settlement record incomplete—not “never happened” and not “all money returned.”

The trade-interface theorem

Libra's relationship is not an abstract mood. Institutionally, it needs an interface through which two sovereigns, thousands of products and millions of entries can meet without becoming one body.

In this packet the interface is layered:

leader-to-leader understanding → executive implementation term → agency monitoring → annexed product identity → importer declaration → customs screening → payment or refund → court review

The bargain lives only as far as each carrier takes it. A communiqué can name the promise. A tariff order changes the U.S. legal term. An HTS annex decides which goods receive that term. ACE and CBP apply it to entries. USDA can observe sales and shipments. A court can later remove the authority beneath the entire tariff layer.
