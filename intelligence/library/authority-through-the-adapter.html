Authority Through the Adapter — Where Control Actually Moves

Are states and incumbent financial institutions being replaced by new digital machinery, or are they relocating authority into the machinery's legal, record, collateral and emergency layers?

Research through 2026-08-31T10:42:24-04:00

Home room: Crypto & Digital MoneyAlso in: Banking & the Dollar
The short version (draft, not yet reviewed by Katie)

Who really holds power in digital finance

New digital systems spread out who moves money and data, but the power to define the legal claim, keep the controlling record, accept collateral, supply final cash, fix failures and absorb losses stays with states, regulated issuers, central banks, clearinghouses and courts.

What we found

  • What moves most is the customer interface and transaction execution. Record-keeping moves only in limited ways, collateral moves selectively, and final liquidity and big systemic losses move least.
  • SWIFT's blockchain ledger shares payment commitments, but banks keep their assets and funding and settlement stays off-ledger; 17 banks were preparing live pilots as of July. At DTC, tokens can move entitlements, yet DTC's off-chain books stay official.
  • Europe's Pontes links blockchain platforms to the central bank's TARGET system, with cash settlement still in T2. The Eurosystem plans its own blockchain platform with native tokenized central-bank money: the state stepping into the new machinery. Planned, not yet built.
  • Japan's Project Agorá ran 30 real-value transactions (about CHF 800,000), but central banks and commercial banks still issued and managed the tokens with fallback procedures. A MUFG-led government-bond trial left the cash leg and production finality unresolved.
  • The trade: incumbents may lose the customer route, some fees and first sight of data, but keep claim definition, licensing, collateral rules and emergency cash. Result: a smaller everyday incumbent and a stronger crisis incumbent.

Why it matters

This is the thesis in action: states, central banks and utilities are not being torn down but are writing their core powers into the new machinery (SWIFT, DTC, Eurosystem, Japan). The open risk is that execution profits go private while crisis liability stays public.

What to watch

  • pending · Whether SWIFT's 17-bank blockchain ledger pilots move into live production, and where settlement actually happens.
  • pending · The Eurosystem's planned blockchain platform with native tokenized central-bank money and longer operating hours.
  • pending · The first serious failure in one of these systems, which will show whether old authorities kept real power or only the liability.

Research summary

The strongest selected cases support constitutional refactoring: transaction execution, interfaces and data movement can become more distributed while claim constitution, authoritative records, collateral eligibility, final liquidity, correction, emergency authority and loss allocation remain with or become more explicitly embedded in states, regulated issuers, central banks, market utilities and courts.
