Issuer transfer agent · SEC registration

Keep the authoritative record

Legal basis: Exchange Act §17A(c); appropriate-regulator registration

When it applies: The activity is an issuer transfer-agent function within the statutory registration perimeter.

Named firms: Securitize Transfer Agent, LLC (registered or chartered); Computershare Inc. (registered or chartered)

Who can use it: Transfer agents whose covered activities and entity category require registration with the SEC

ConditionsConfirm the covered security and statutory function. For entities assigned to the SEC, establish effective registration. Separately establish issuer appointment and the authoritative record.

The limitA chain or technology vendor does not inherit its client’s transfer-agent registration.

FDIC insured-depository resolution route

Recover or resolve a failed institution

Legal basis: Federal Deposit Insurance Act and the applicable FDIC receivership authority.

When it applies: A failed insured depository institution enters the statutory FDIC resolution/receivership framework.

Named firms: Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (public body)

Who can use it: Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation acting as statutory receiver; FDIC-appointed receivers; Assuming depository institutions under the approved resolution transaction

ConditionsConfirm the failed entity is the relevant insured depository institution. Identify the receiver, deposits/accounts, assuming institution or payout path, and claim class. Separate the bank from its parent, nonbank affiliates, custodial arrangements, and token issuer obligations.

The limitFDIC insurance/resolution is not SIPC protection, does not insure investment losses or token value, and does not automatically cover a bank affiliate or a customer claim against a nonbank issuer.

SIPA customer-property liquidation route

Recover or resolve a failed institution

Legal basis: Securities Investor Protection Act and the liquidation case.

When it applies: A financially troubled SIPC-member broker-dealer has missing customer cash or securities and a SIPA liquidation is applicable.

Named firms: Securities Investor Protection Corporation (public body)

Who can use it: Securities Investor Protection Corporation; Court-appointed SIPA trustees; SIPA liquidation estates

ConditionsConfirm SIPC-member broker-dealer status and the statutory trigger. Establish customer status, asset classification, account facts, and claim deadlines. Apply SIPA's limits and the trustee/court process.

The limitSIPC is distinct from FDIC deposit insurance; it does not protect market-value loss, ordinary investment losses, commodities/FX claims, or digital/crypto assets outside SIPA's security definition.

Federal bankruptcy court and estate-administration route

Recover or resolve a failed institution

Legal basis: Bankruptcy Code eligibility and the applicable chapter/court process.

When it applies: An eligible debtor's insolvency is governed by the Bankruptcy Code rather than a specialized bank, broker-dealer, insurer, or other resolution regime.

Named firms: U.S. Trustee Program (public body)

Who can use it: United States bankruptcy courts; U.S. Trustee Program; Court-appointed case trustees; Debtors in possession where the Bankruptcy Code permits

ConditionsIdentify the exact debtor legal entity and its eligibility. Identify the chapter, venue, court orders, estate fiduciary, and property/claim characterization. Test whether a specialized insolvency, receivership, or resolution statute applies instead.

The limitBankruptcy is not a catch-all: domestic banks are excluded by section 109, and specialized regimes can govern other regulated entities. A token ledger does not by itself identify estate property, perfection, custody, or priority.

Covered clearing-agency recovery and wind-down planning route

Recover or resolve a failed institution

Legal basis: Exchange Act clearing-agency framework and SEC Rule 17Ad-22.

When it applies: A covered SEC-registered clearing agency is within Rule 17Ad-22's applicable recovery and orderly-wind-down requirements.

Named firms: The Depository Trust Company (registered or chartered); National Securities Clearing Corporation (registered or chartered); Fixed Income Clearing Corporation (registered or chartered)

Who can use it: Covered SEC-registered clearing agencies; Default-management and recovery-plan actors named by an effective clearing-agency rulebook

ConditionsIdentify the registered clearing agency and whether it is covered by the cited provisions. Recover the current recovery/wind-down plan, default rules, liquidity arrangements, and participant obligations. Apply the exact service, product, member, and event rather than an organization-wide label.

The limitA recovery plan is not an observed resolution outcome, a customer guarantee, or a universal default waterfall for broker-dealers, banks, custodians, issuers, or blockchain networks.

CFTC swap reporting counterparty or registered-entity route

Report trades and retain regulatory records

Legal basis: CEA swap-data framework and 17 CFR Parts 45 and 49.

When it applies: The transaction is a CFTC-regulated swap and Part 45 assigns a reporting counterparty or reporting task to a SEF, DCM, or DCO.

Named firms: None named yet

Who can use it: swap dealer; major swap participant; other reporting counterparty as allocated by Part 45; swap execution facility; designated contract market; derivatives clearing organization; third-party reporting facilitator acting for the responsible reporter

ConditionsClassify the instrument as a CFTC-regulated swap and identify the reporting hierarchy or registered-entity rule. Select an appropriate SDR and meet its data, validation, timing, and access requirements. A counterparty may contract with a facilitator to submit data, but preserve the assigned reporting party's responsibility and evidence of successful acceptance.

The limitA delegate submits or facilitates only under the responsible party's arrangement; it is neither automatically the reporting counterparty nor an SDR. A generic blockchain log or oracle feed is not an SDR report.

CFTC swap data repository acceptance route

Report trades and retain regulatory records

Legal basis: CEA section 21 and 17 CFR Part 49; entity-specific CFTC repository status and service terms.

When it applies: A CFTC-regulated swap report must be accepted, maintained, and made available within the statutory SDR framework.

Named firms: Chicago Mercantile Exchange, Inc. (registered or chartered); DTCC Data Repository LLC · CFTC SDR (registered or chartered); ICE Trade Vault, LLC · CFTC SDR (registered or chartered); KOR Reporting Inc. · CFTC SDR (registered or chartered)

Who can use it: CFTC-registered or provisionally registered swap data repository within its approved scope

ConditionsConfirm the exact legal entity, CFTC status, asset-class scope, and current rules. Establish participant eligibility, repository selection, technical access, and successful data validation/acceptance. Keep repository maintenance separate from the counterparty or registered entity's reporting duty and correction duty.

The limitRepository listing is not proof that a particular transaction is reportable, accepted, correctly reported, or within a named repository's service scope.

SEC security-based swap reporting and registered SDR route

Report trades and retain regulatory records

Legal basis: Exchange Act section 13(n), Regulation SBSR, and the SEC security-based-swap data-repository framework.

When it applies: The instrument is a security-based swap subject to Regulation SBSR, rather than a CFTC swap or an ordinary tokenized security record.

Named firms: DTCC Data Repository (U.S.), LLC · SEC security-based swap data repository (registered or chartered)

Who can use it: security-based swap dealer; major security-based swap participant; counterparty or platform assigned a reporting duty under Regulation SBSR; SEC-registered security-based swap data repository

ConditionsClassify the instrument and responsible reporting person under the SEC regime. Confirm the registered SDR, asset class, reporting format, participant agreement, and compliance date applicable to the transaction. Do not merge the CFTC and SEC repository routes merely because service providers have related names.

The limitThis route does not govern ordinary securities-token recordkeeping, a transfer agent's master securityholder file, or CFTC-regulated swaps.
