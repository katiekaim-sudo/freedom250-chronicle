Government technology and the record overhaul

Which federal AI and digital-record systems actually change public decisions or service, and who authorizes and corrects them?

The braid so far

This is the government operator and record story, distinct from private AI and compute buildout. The October 4 Super Intelligence Force owner finds a White House coordination and recommendation body, without a public charter, budget or transferred agency authority. Its announced leaders retain their separate DNI, FTC, Defense and OPM powers. Coordination is a real political layer, not yet a shared operating system.

The October 5 NARA AI-records return gives a narrower live example: semantic search is operating on special-topic record-release pages, with public warnings that results may be incomplete or inaccurate and source documents must be checked. General Archives.gov AI search remains a future pilot in the saved inventory. NARA was considering general records-schedule coverage for AI policy and training data, but no approved general schedule or AI-records enforcement case was located at that cutoff.

The OPM structural audit finds some real electronic retirement and personnel-record operation, while a governmentwide HR-platform award and shared-service center still need agency migration, acceptance, decommissioning, error and result proof. AI hiring guidance is not a deployed, validated individual selection. These records show why “the government adopted AI” is too coarse: a search feature, staffing system, advice body and legally consequential decision have different owners and remedies.

The forward question is whether a named agency tool moves from authority and procurement to accepted use with an authoritative record, human decision right, accuracy test, privacy/retention rule and correction after an error. Constructively, shared technology makes public service faster and more legible without losing agency accountability. In shadow, a fluent interface becomes the public's only view while the underlying record, model and decision maker are hard to find. Ordinary IT modernization, piecemeal by agency, remains a strong rival to one federal technology constitution.

This braid begins without authored planetary strands. Rebuilding the Federal Machine's 2027–30 route hears its lawful work/record questions; Capacity or Spectacle cross-examines physical compute and private suppliers. A future chart relationship is earned only by a dated agency authorization, accepted system, consequential decision or correction, not by the word AI.
